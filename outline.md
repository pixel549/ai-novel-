# THE SWEETWATER YEARS — locked outline (v2)

Grimdark Victorian fantasy. 25 chapters, three acts, one POV per act.
~2,500 words per chapter, ~62,000 total.

**Frozen once drafting begins.** Amendments via `change_requests.md`.

**v3.** Change requests CR-001/002/003 applied.

**Changes from v1:** the Fourth Mercy is an ascension programme, not a quiet
euthanasia scheme, and its exposure is the climax of Act II. Crane is culpable,
not tragic. No foreign-policy thread — all corruption is domestic. Ch. 16 is
Lloyd delirious after the siege, not a lost day on the road.

---

## CANON — FIXED FACTS

### World

- **The Aventine Commonwealth.** Gaslight, rail, senate, empire in retreat.
- **The Attrition.** Four-year war, ended nineteen months before ch. 1. Won,
  technically. Trench and tunnel war. The enemy is never named or discussed
  beyond the fact of the war — this book is about what a country does to itself
  afterwards, not about who it fought.
- **Reconstruction.** Money exists. It does not reach the valleys.
- **The Larrow Valley.** Twelve villages on the river Larrow. Lloyd's home.
- **Carrowgate**, the capital. **Vantry**, the second city — mills, rail
  junction, and the largest Church presence outside the capital.
- **Arcane energy** is real, uncommon, unregulated. Undead and lesser horrors
  appear where the war killed enough people in one place. Treated as a pest
  problem.
- **Insight** is the capacity to perceive what is actually there. The narrative
  NEVER confirms whether Lloyd hallucinates or sees truly. Both readings must
  remain fully available at all times, including after ch. 14.

### The Salts

- A dream-state reached by immersion in heavy brine. Stillwell Hydro sells it
  as a nerve cure; the tanks are the door.
- Time inside does not match time outside.
- **Chalices** grant a permanent arcane ability shaped by the drinker, at a
  cost. Most divers never find one. Many don't surface.
- Injuries taken inside are real on surfacing and heal wrong.
- The Salts are not a secret to the powerful. The Church has been using them
  industrially for years, which is the discovery at the heart of Act II.

### The Church of the Mercies

- State-adjacent, wealthy, popular, and genuinely charitable at street level.
  Three public Mercies: **bread, shelter, absolution**.
- The **Fourth Mercy** is an ascension programme. The Church takes people
  nobody will miss — almshouse residents, the war-damaged, the unclaimed sick —
  and attempts to make something higher out of them. Homunculi grown in brine.
  Industrial use of the Salts on subjects who did not consent and cannot
  surface. Attempted extraction of the soul as a separable object. Arcane
  breeding programmes. Things bound out of the Salts and kept.
  None of it works. Six decades of failures, meticulously documented, each
  one a person.
- Doctrinally they believe they are performing a kindness: these are lives of
  no use being spent on the only project that matters.
- **Ascension House, Vantry** is the working laboratory. The **Sanctum at
  Carrowgate** is the headquarters and is never touched.

### Cast

**LLOYD HALE**, 31. Brawler. Broad, quiet, badly educated, not stupid. From
Callow Ford. Left at 22 for work in Carrowgate, sent money home, nine years
without visiting. High Insight before the Salts, worse after. He cannot
reliably tell which of what he sees is shared, and has organised his life
around not being alone with that. Left arm taken at the elbow in the Salts and
regrown wrong — two inches long, too many joints. Kept gloved.

**MARIA VESK**, 28. Former Sister of the Third Mercy. Minor arcane ability
since childhood: she can steady a thing, a body, a tremor. Six years ago she
emptied a poor box of forty-one crowns for **Anse Duley**, a widow with three
children refused on a paperwork technicality. She was never caught. She fled
anyway, in shame, because she took the Church at its word about itself.
Fundamentally kind. Lloyd sharpens her; he does not sour her.

**EDITH "EDIE" MARROW**, 34. Sapper, four years in the tunnels. Terse,
practical, as dangerous with a permit as with a knife. No Insight, and
grateful for it. In the last year of the war **Colonel Absolom Reyne**
collapsed a tunnel to seal a breach with ninety-one of his own sappers in it.
Nine got out. Edie killed him eleven weeks later and was never suspected.
Reyne had been in the Salts; it nearly cost her her life and it is how she
learned they exist. She has held a rigid bright-line morality ever since,
because if there is no line then what she did has no name.
She came to Stillwell to be strong enough to protect Senator Ferrick.

**SENATOR HALVARD CRANE**, 60s. Larrow Valley Reconstruction, and principal
shareholder in the **Larrow Consolidated Works** through two intermediaries.
He commissioned a groundwater survey before construction, received it, and
buried it. The **Larrow Indemnity** — compensation at year seven — was
actuarially designed around the mortality curve in that survey. Two
inspectors who pressed the matter have been reassigned. He is warm in public,
remembers names, is genuinely liked, and gives generously to the Church. None
of that is a front; he simply does not think of valley people as the kind of
people a decision happens *to*. He is not a monster. He is a man for whom
certain deaths are a line item, and he knew the number in advance.

**SENATOR ALONSO FERRICK**, 50s. Coastal district. Chairs the Reconstruction
ethics sub-panel. Genuinely good, incorruptible, and in eight years has
produced two reports and no wells. His decency is his ceiling. He believes his
hands are clean. By the end they are not, and he never learns it.

**SISTER SUPERIOR ONDINE VAIL**, 50s. Sennet Almshouse, then Vantry. Warm,
efficient, entirely sincere. She recruits Maria because Maria once broke a
rule for a person, which is the qualification. She is never cruel and never
raises her voice. She survives the book.

**IVET HALE**, 58. Lloyd's mother. Callow Ford. Sweetwater sickness, four
years in. Appears only in ch. 25, and not to Lloyd.

### Abilities gained from the chalice (ch. 5)

- **Lloyd — the Second Right.** His right arm, lost in the Salts and returned
  looking normal, passes through solid matter at will and can touch the
  eldritch things only he sees. There is also a second, ethereal, invisible one
  at the same shoulder, which he learns to work independently over the course
  of the book. Nobody is ever told about it. See canon for limits.
- **Maria — nothing.** She drinks only enough to return and takes no ability.
  Her Arcana is native and already far beyond anyone else in the book: healing,
  light, a five-metre fire burst, lightning, and a knack for steadying. Never
  war-winning, never a solution to a structural problem. See canon for limits.
- **Edie — the Still Mind.** She knows violence a half-second before it
  happens. No visions, no voices, and no extra speed — she frequently knows she
  is about to be hit and is hit anyway. She is enormously relieved it was an
  instinct and not sight.

### Motifs

- **Water that tastes sweet.** Brine, sweetwater, floodwater, the tanks.
- **Counting.** The knot-cord. Ninety-one. Forty-one crowns. Year seven. Six
  decades of case files.
- **Gloves and coverings.** What people put over the parts that changed.
- **Being told what is real by someone else**, and what it costs both of them.

# ACT I — EDIE (ch. 1–5)

Extended prologue. Establishes the Salts, the trio, and the dependency Edie
sees from outside. Claustrophobic. No world-building above ground.

## Ch. 1 — Stillwell Hydro
- A hydropathic sanatorium on a cold coast. Respectable, peeling, iodine over
  something older.
- Edie pays her entry with money drawn against Ferrick's household account
  without his knowledge. Stated plainly; she does not agonise.
- Divers go in threes. She meets Lloyd and Maria as an arrangement, not a choice.
- Lloyd asks whether she's ever been and accepts her answer. Maria checks it.
  The division of labour between them is visible before either speaks of it.
- She does not say what she wants a chalice for.

## Ch. 2 — Depth
- Inside. Salt-crusted architecture: stairs, corridors, a chapel-shaped thing.
- First temporal slip — one corridor takes two days by her own hunger.
- First combat. Something thin and many-elbowed. Lloyd goes at it bare-handed;
  Maria steadies him mid-fight, arcane and casual. Edie is the only one shaken.
- Maria has done many dives. Lloyd more.
- The knot-cord begins.

## Ch. 3 — The Long Search
- Weeks. Hunger, filth, boredom, terror in short bursts.
- The chapel is searched three times and differs each time. Maria says it's the
  same room. Edie's notes disagree with her own notes.
- Lloyd's arm is taken at the elbow. He doesn't scream. Maria talks him through
  the regrowth like she's done it before, because she has.
- It comes back wrong. He flexes it and says nothing about it, then or ever.
- The cord says nineteen. Edie doesn't believe it.

## Ch. 4 — What Is Real
- Lloyd narrates things Edie can't see: a woman at the corridor's end, a second
  Maria, a door where there is wall.
- The ritual: Lloyd describes, Maria rules, Lloyd obeys instantly and without
  argument, every time.
- Edie tests it once — lies about something small being there. Lloyd looks to
  Maria for the ruling, not to his own eyes. She doesn't do it again.
- She understands Maria is Lloyd's only interface with the world, that Maria
  needs to be needed that way, and that neither has said so aloud.
- She is grateful, for the first time, to be the dull one.

## Ch. 5 — The Chalice
- It is in the chapel they searched three times, on the floor, in plain sight,
  and all three know it was not there before.
- **Maria drinks only enough to return** and stops. She has Arcana already, she
  does not know what it would cost, and she is the only one of the three who
  walks away from the thing they came for. Neither of the others asks why.
- Cost: Lloyd surrenders a memory he can't afterwards name the shape of. Edie
  gives up sleeping without waking twice.
- Two abilities land — brief, physical, no mechanics explained. Lloyd's right
  arm looks and works like an arm, and he does not mention that it goes through
  the tank rail, or that there are two of them.
- Extraction. Nineteen days on the cord, four hours on the clock. A nurse asks
  if they enjoyed the treatment.
- Last beat: Lloyd, still half in the water, waiting for Maria to tell him the
  room is real before he'll get out.

# ACT II — LLOYD (ch. 6–16)

The world, then the thing under it. Ends with the siege and Lloyd's delirium.

## Ch. 6 — Overground
- On foot from Stillwell. Insight in daylight: the world above is worse because
  it insists it's fine.
- A rail guard with the wrong number of shadows. A dog that is not a dog. He
  says nothing; Maria's hand on his sleeve twice, no words.
- The arrangement: Maria has nowhere to go, Edie is going east eventually and
  won't say why, Lloyd says he's going home to get his mother out.
- Edie prices the journey and finds them work. She has the plan and the purse
  and neither of them asks about either.

## Ch. 7 — Wet Work
- **Hessel Bight.** Eleven drowned things in a flooded cellar under a burnt
  manse, left over from the war. Paid in food and a night indoors.
- The freelance economy: normal, unglamorous, badly paid.
- Lloyd's arm takes hold of something Edie's Still Mind had not registered as
  present. First seed that Lloyd's perception may be operative. Unresolved.
- Maria heals a boy's crushed hand and is thanked as a witch, not a sister.
  She lets it stand.

## Ch. 8 — Sweetwater
- **Ammet Ford**, a Crane works town. Rail spur, smelting works, new brick, a
  school with glass in the windows. Visibly better off than its neighbours.
- The water tastes sweet. Everyone mentions it. Nobody stops drinking it.
- The Larrow Indemnity introduced by poster: claims payable in the seventh year.
  Three people mention it approvingly.
- Lloyd is silent the whole chapter. Maria notices and doesn't ask.
- Edie thinks the works are, on balance, good. She has figures.

## Ch. 9 — Callow Ford, Told Not Shown
- Camp on the road. Maria asks about his mother. Lloyd gives the version he has
  decided to tell: Ivet Hale, sick four years, can't afford to move, he's going
  home strong enough to carry her out. Every fact true. The omission is the plan.
- Backstory: left at 22, nine years of sent money and no visits. Insight started
  at 19; he learned in Carrowgate that saying what you see gets you put somewhere.
- Edie says her charge is a good man and she'll leave them at Carrowgate. First
  mention of Ferrick, unnamed.
- Later, quietly, Lloyd asks Maria whether the fire is real. It is. He needed to
  hear it anyway.

## Ch. 10 — Two Priests
- Two priests of the Mercies on the north road, bound for an almshouse.
  Courteous, useful, they share food.
- Maria sees two men. Lloyd sees what is inside the vestments and does not eat
  what they give him.
- The first real fracture: she can't accept the accusation, he can't unsee it,
  neither can prove anything. Edie refuses to arbitrate, which helps nobody.
- Maria rules that they were real men. Lloyd accepts. The reader should feel,
  for the first time, that the ruling might be wrong.

## Ch. 11 — Permits
- **Tannery Reach.** A body in a canal; a question of jurisdiction and therefore
  of expense.
- Edie settles it with paperwork and one unpleasant conversation. Her competence
  is bureaucratic, and this is how the Commonwealth actually kills people —
  schedules, forms, whose problem it is.
- Lloyd is useless here and knows it. Violence and seeing are his only skills.

## Ch. 12 — Forty-One Crowns
- **Sennet Almshouse.** Maria goes in to confess. Lloyd waits outside and
  watches the building breathe.
- The reunion first, and it must be genuinely warm: Ondine Vail took Maria in
  at seven, raised her, taught her to read, and was the first person to tell her
  that what she could do was a gift and not a symptom. Six years of absence and
  Vail is simply glad. Nothing in this scene is sinister and nothing is staged
  as sinister.
- Then the confession. Six years of carried shame, out loud, to the only mother
  she has ever had.
- Vail is *delighted*. Not forgiving — pleased, the way a teacher is pleased.
  She has hoped for years that Maria would turn out to be someone who could set
  a rule aside for the person in front of her, because most people cannot and
  because the work requires it. She dismisses the theft as trivial in about
  fifteen seconds, which is its own kind of vertigo: Maria has organised six
  years of her life around it.
- Vail asks her to come to Vantry and help with something, because Maria has
  always had more of the gift than anyone she has trained.
- Maria comes out. Lloyd asks if she's alright. She says yes. First time in the
  book she lies to him — and the lie is not about the Church. It is that seeing
  Vail again undid her.


## Ch. 13 — Vantry
- The second city: mills, soot, rail, and the Church's largest house outside
  the capital.
- **The imbuing.** Vail brings Maria to a clean, warm, well-kept room. In it, a
  woman catatonic two years, eight months pregnant, cared for attentively and
  expensively. Everything Maria can see about the room is kind.
- Vail asks Maria to put her Arcana *into* her. Not to heal. To imbue.
- Asked what for, Vail tells the truth, warmly and at whatever length is
  wanted: that natural Arcana cannot be taught and can only be born; that this
  woman will not recover; that her child can be given something no one else
  could give it; that Maria herself was found rather than made, and that they
  have been trying for sixty years to stop relying on luck.
- No pressure. Maria may refuse, and Vail means it. **Maria refuses.** Vail
  accepts it instantly, without a flicker of disappointment, and takes her to
  lunch.
- That is the crack. Nothing threatening has happened, nobody has been unkind,
  and Maria now has a question she cannot stop asking.
- She asks the other two to help her look — framed as reconnaissance, because
  she wants to be told she is wrong about her mother.
- What they assemble over four days: an intake register with no corresponding
  discharges. Brine delivered by the ton to a building with no baths. Ascension
  House, with a chapel nobody worships in and a sub-basement older than the
  building above it.
- Vail receives Maria warmly twice more and answers every question honestly,
  and none of it helps.
- Lloyd has been describing the building since they arrived. Maria stops ruling
  on it, which is its own answer.


## Ch. 14 — The Fourth Mercy
The horror chapter. Restrained, catalogued, clinical — the register is worse
than the specimens.
- They get in at night. Edie's entry is a service door and a forged requisition.
- What is down there, in order of escalation: brine tanks with people in them
  who cannot surface and have not for years. Homunculi grown from parts of the
  above, in rows, most non-viable. A breeding ward with a stud book. An
  extraction theatre where they have been attempting to remove the soul as a
  separable object, with sixty years of case files documenting every failure by
  name and date. Something bound out of the Salts in the last room, kept, fed,
  and no longer whatever it was.
- It has never worked. Not once. The doctrine holds that it will.
- The theological reveal: this isn't a perversion of the Mercies. It is what
  the other three are for. Bread and shelter identify the surplus; absolution
  collects them.
- Maria finds Anse Duley's name in the intake register. The widow she stole the
  forty-one crowns for. Two years after Maria fled, refused again, and taken.
- Nobody says anything for a page. Then Edie says they're burning it.

## Ch. 15 — The Siege
The tension here is that **Maria's Arcana is the wrong tool**, not that it is
absent. Draft accordingly: she is the most dangerous person in the building and
can barely use any of it.
- The decision to destroy it *and* get the evidence out. Edie's plan is
  professional: documents first, fire second, and a route.
- Maria takes the six-decade case files and the intake registers. Edie takes
  the stud book and the requisition orders, because those carry signatures.
- **Why she can't just burn it.** Fire is a five-metre burst in a sub-basement
  with occupied tanks, with paper they came for, and with a route out behind
  them. The ward is full of people who cannot surface and cannot be moved. Any
  room worth burning has something in it she is not willing to burn.
- It goes wrong at the extraction theatre. The bound thing gets out. The viable
  homunculi are not passive.
- Maria uses lightning twice, precisely, and hates herself both times. She
  keeps light going for the whole retreat, which is the least dramatic and most
  useful thing anyone does that night.
- Lloyd holds the stair. The one straightforwardly heroic sequence in the book,
  earned and awful: the arm taking hold of things Edie cannot see, and being
  right about all of them.
- The real cost: Lloyd goes down and Maria has to choose between healing him
  where he lies — hours, and useless afterwards — or carrying him out through a
  building that is on fire. She carries him. He is worse for it and she knows
  it, and that decision is the seed of everything she does in Act III.
- Vail arrives during the fire and does not fight, argue or flee. She stands in
  the yard and asks Maria, without heat, whether she understands what she has
  just cost the world. She is not performing. She believes it.
- They get out with the papers. Lloyd does not get out intact: skull, ribs, and
  the right arm broken in three places.


## Ch. 16 — Delirium
POV Lloyd, interior, unreliable. The end of Act II.
- Days compressed into an unmarked interior. Salt corridors overlaid on the
  Vantry stair overlaid on Callow Ford. Ivet Hale in the tank. Crane's name
  spoken by someone who shouldn't know it.
- The buried memory from the chalice surfaces sideways and is still not
  legible.
- Foreshadowing, all deniable in retrospect: a courteous man asking after
  someone else's safety; a knot-cord with ninety-one knots; a room Maria does
  not tell him is real.
- Maria's voice comes through intermittently from outside, naming objects.
- Ends before he wakes.

# ACT III — MARIA (ch. 17–25)

Aftermath, the failure of the good act, and the choice she makes anyway.

## Ch. 17 — After
- Six weeks in a let room in a mill district. Maria nursing him. Physical,
  unromantic detail: bedsores, a bucket, the smell.
- She heals him in stages because that is all she can do — hours at a time,
  useless for a day afterwards, and it still leaves the arm crooked.
- Her faith is gone and her belief is not, and the two will not sit together.
  She still prays and is disgusted by it.
- **The wound is not theological, it is filial.** The woman who raised her from
  seven, who taught her to read and told her what she could do was a gift, spent
  those same years selecting people for the ward. Every kind thing Vail ever did
  for her is still true. That is the part she cannot get past.
- Anse Duley's name in the register: Maria stole forty-one crowns to save a
  woman the Church took two years later, and the theft is what qualified her.
- Lloyd wakes on the fourth day and asks whether the room is real. She can't
  answer for a moment, and that moment frightens them both.


## Ch. 18 — The Rumours
- The evidence goes out: three papers, Ferrick's sub-panel via Edie, a Vantry
  broadsheet that prints it whole.
- It lands. For eleven days it is the only story in the Commonwealth.
- Then the Sanctum's response. Ascension House was a rogue chapter. Two
  under-priors are surrendered to the courts. A bishop weeps convincingly in the
  Senate. Charitable expenditure is announced at a figure nobody can argue with.
- The intake registers are called forgeries by people who have not read them,
  and the story becomes a debate about the papers rather than about the ward.
- Nobody senior is charged. **Vail is transferred to the Sanctum at Carrowgate
  and promoted** — and writes to Maria, once, warmly, with no reproach in it at
  all, asking after her health. Maria does not reply and does not burn it.


## Ch. 19 — What It Was Worth
- Edie's reading: they walked into the worst place in the country and burned it
  down. Pure evil met with pure good, and the good won on the night. She is more
  certain than she has been since the tunnel, and briefly happy.
- Lloyd's reading: the world is exactly as horrific as he has always seen it,
  and he is now equipped to be horrific back on the right side. He has never
  been more comfortable in himself.
- Maria's reading, unspoken: the ward is empty and the programme is not, the
  people who ordered it are at dinner in Carrowgate tonight, and her mother has
  been promoted for it. The pure act cost her everything she had and bought
  eleven days.
- Edie leaves for Carrowgate. She gives them an address and no explanation, and
  Maria realises Edie has never once asked either of them for anything.


## Ch. 20 — Drifting
- Three weeks in Carrowgate. Casual work. Maria enjoys the rest and then stops.
- The pattern: every walk ends within sight of the Senate. Every conversation
  Lloyd strikes up is with someone from the Indemnity Office. He has been
  learning a building's habits, not a man's.
- She checks his coat while he sleeps: a session calendar, a floor plan, and
  the name Halvard Crane in his own handwriting.

## Ch. 21 — The Confession
- She confronts him and he tells it whole, and the relief of finally saying it
  is worse to watch than the plan.
- The reveal: he did not go to Stillwell for his mother's sake in the way he
  said. He went to become capable of reaching a senator.
- The case against Crane, laid out flatly: the buried survey, the shareholding
  through intermediaries, the year-seven schedule built on a mortality curve
  Crane had read, the two reassigned inspectors.
- Why not exposure — they have just watched exposure fail against a weaker
  institution than the Senate.
- He never told her because he needed her to tell him what was real, and a man
  who says he intends to kill a senator is a man you stop believing.

## Ch. 22 — The Argument
- Three days, across the chapter. Maria's case is real and she loses it fairly.
- She tries the courts, the press, Ferrick's panel, waiting, and the Vantry
  ledger as leverage. Lloyd has costed every one.
- Her last argument is the honest one: that it will destroy him, not Crane. He
  agrees, and it changes nothing.
- She stays. Explicit with herself about why: not because he convinced her, but
  because alone he will not survive a day of it, and she is the only reality he
  has. She notes, coldly, that this is the same reasoning the Church uses.

## Ch. 23 — Ferrick
POV Maria; the chapter's subject is Edie.
- Ferrick's house. Edie runs his security: four people and a locked cabinet.
- Ferrick in person: warm, tireless, principled, ineffective. He shows them the
  sub-panel's second report on industrial water. It is excellent. It has changed
  nothing. He is proud of it.
- He has had the Vantry evidence for two months and has convened a hearing for
  next spring.
- Edie has begun to notice that belief and results are separate things and says
  none of it.
- Maria says nothing about Lloyd. Edie notices her saying nothing.

## Ch. 24 — The Ledger and the Killing
- Edie does Ferrick's advance security for the Indemnity review session and
  finds Lloyd's reconnaissance, because it is the only thing in the building she
  did not put there. She reconstructs the plan from marks on a floor plan.
- The three of them in a room. Edie's position at full strength: she killed
  Reyne, it was murder, and she has spent nine years insisting on a line so that
  what she did stays on the wrong side of it and means something.
- She spends a night with the evidence and cannot construct a version in which
  clean hands produced the better outcome. Vantry was pure good and the
  programme survived it. Ferrick is pure good and has produced two reports. She
  has been guarding a man whose purity is the reason he is harmless.
- She returns at dawn with conditions, not permission. Crane dies during the
  session, in Ferrick's presence, and Edie's staging puts it on the Church — a
  reprisal for Crane's public support of the Vantry hearings. Ferrick's guard
  responds correctly. Ferrick inherits the constituency, the sympathy and the
  Indemnity chair.
  She is not helping Lloyd. She is making the murder productive, and using a
  true evil as the scapegoat for something they did, and she knows exactly what
  both of those things are.
- The killing: daylight, ninety seconds, squalid rather than dramatic. Crane's
  last twenty seconds are the hardest passage in the book — courteous, confused,
  asking after someone else's safety, exactly as ch. 16 foreshadowed.
- It does not go clean. Cornered in a stair with Lloyd down, Maria throws a
  light burst into a corridor of clerks — crude, blinding, and unmistakably
  Arcana in front of a dozen witnesses. It buys ninety seconds.
  **The irony is the point:** a public act of Arcana in the Senate is exactly
  what Edie's frame needs, because Arcana means the Church. Maria has just
  corroborated a lie about the institution she destroyed, by reflex, to save
  him. Edie did not plan it and does not correct it.
- Lloyd gets out with the right arm shattered again and a face four people can
  describe.


## Ch. 25 — Year Seven
Epilogue, three movements.
- **One.** Eleven days on. Ferrick takes the Indemnity chair on a wave of grief
  and immediately does what Crane would not, because the sympathy buys him the
  votes: schedule brought forward, works water tested, wells capped. It works.
  Lives are saved and the number is knowable. The Church, blamed for a murder
  it did not commit, loses the spring hearing it had been managing — the one
  thing the Vantry evidence could not achieve on its own merits.
- **Two.** Edie and Ferrick. He believes his hands are clean and thanks her for
  keeping them so. She lets him. Her final position: she has stopped trying to
  stay on the right side of a line and started counting outcomes, and this is
  not a fall — it is the first honest thing she has held since the tunnel. She
  keeps the knot-cord.
- **Three.** Lloyd cannot go north; he is described on every board in the
  valley and going home would undo all of it. So Maria goes to Callow Ford alone
  and meets Ivet Hale — alive, moved to clean water, asking after a son Maria
  has to describe carefully. Maria does not tell her.
  She goes back south. Lloyd is waiting where she left him, unable to be
  certain the room is real until she says so. The last line lands there: she is
  what he has instead of the world.

## Notes for the pipeline

- **The Insight ambiguity is a hard constraint**, including after ch. 14. The
  fact that Lloyd was right about Ascension House must never become proof that
  he is right about everything. Any draft that resolves it is rejected.
- **Crane is culpable and must never be a caricature.** Warm in public, liked,
  generous to charity, and he read the survey. His guilt is documentary. His
  manner is not.
- **Ch. 14 is catalogued, not lurid.** The registers and the case files carry
  the horror. Any draft that reaches for gore over paperwork is rejected.
- **Vail is never cruel and never raises her voice.** She is sincere. She wins.
