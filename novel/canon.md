# THE SWEETWATER YEARS — CANON

Reference sheet. Fed to every drafting pass alongside that chapter's beats.
Nothing here is optional and nothing here is a suggestion. Where this file and
a draft disagree, the draft is wrong.

---

# 1. THE THREE

## LLOYD HALE

**Age** 31. **From** Callow Ford, Larrow Valley. **Was** a labourer, then a
prizefighter of the unlicensed kind, then a man who did jobs.

**Body.** Six foot, heavy through the shoulders and going soft at the middle
because he eats when there's food and doesn't when there isn't. Broken nose set
badly twice. Hands wrecked — knuckles enlarged, two fingers that don't
straighten. Dark hair cut short by himself with a knife.

**Speech.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. He does not use metaphor and does
not understand people who do. When he doesn't know a word he says the long way
round rather than guessing. He answers questions literally, including rhetorical
ones. Under stress he gets shorter, not louder.

**Interior.** Constant low-grade triage: is that there, is that shared, will
saying it cost me. He has been doing this since nineteen and it is exhausting
and entirely invisible from outside. He is not tormented in a poetic way. He is
tired in a practical way.

**History.** Insight began at nineteen. At twenty-two he said what he saw to the
wrong person in Carrowgate and spent five weeks somewhere with locks on it. He
has never said what happened there and never will. Left the valley for work, sent
money home every month for nine years, never once visited, and cannot explain
why in a way that satisfies him.

**Never.** Never articulate, never wry, never self-analysing out loud. Never
describes his own condition in clinical terms. Never says the word "insight".

**Wants.** For his mother not to die of a thing a man decided was acceptable.

## MARIA VESK

**Age** 28. **Was** Sister of the Third Mercy. Taken into the Church at seven,
raised in it, and taught by Ondine Vail.

**Body.** Small, wiry, weathered from the road. Fair hair kept short for
practicality. Burn scarring on both forearms from twenty years of unschooled
fire-work, which she doesn't hide.

**Speech.** Warm, quick, funny — the humour of somebody who has spent a lot of
time around the dying and learned that jokes are load-bearing. Asks a lot of
questions. Deflects sincerity with lightness and then says the sincere thing
anyway, thirty seconds later, when nobody's braced for it.

**Interior.** She is good. This is not naivety and should never be written as
naivety — she has seen more death than either of the others and simply decided
what she is going to be about it. What she loses across the book is the
institution, not the goodness.

**History.** Seven years old, orphaned, taken in. Vail taught her to read, to
work, and to use what she could do. She has never known another mother and does
not think of the arrangement as strange. At twenty-two she emptied a poor box of
forty-one crowns for Anse Duley, a widow with three children refused on a
paperwork technicality. She was never caught. She fled anyway, in shame, and
has spent six years believing she is the corrupt one in the story.

**Never.** Never cruel, never cold, never sanctimonious. Never preaches. Never
loses her sense of humour, even at the worst of it — especially at the worst of
it.

**Wants.** At the start: absolution. By the end: to be the reason one specific
person can stand up in a room.

## EDITH "EDIE" MARROW

**Age** 34. **Was** a sapper, Fourth Tunnelling Company, four years.

**Body.** Lean, mid-height, grey coming in early. Deaf in the left ear from a
camouflet. Hands scarred flat across the palms from cable work. Moves without
wasting anything.

**Speech.** Flat, brief, unornamented. States conclusions and omits reasoning
unless asked. Uses trade vocabulary — spoil, shoring, face, breach — without
translating it. Swears functionally and without heat. Rarely asks a personal
question and answers them briefly when asked.

**Interior.** Everything is a survey: what's load-bearing, what fails first,
where the exit is. She thinks in costs and quantities. She is not cold, she is
economical, and the difference should be visible in what she does rather than
what she says.

**History.** Colonel Absolom Reyne collapsed a tunnel to seal a breach with
ninety-one of his own sappers inside. Nine got out. Edie killed him eleven weeks
later — a private killing, unwitnessed, never suspected. Reyne had been in the
Salts, which is why it nearly went the other way, and it is how she learned the
Salts exist. Since that night she has held to a hard bright-line morality, not
because she believes the world is simple but because if the line does not exist
then what she did has no name.

**Never.** Never has an epiphany. Never explains her own psychology. Never
mentions the ninety-one except in ch. 24. Never volunteers her plans.

**Wants.** For there to have been a right answer available at the time.

# 2. ABILITIES — HARD LIMITS

Every drafting pass is bound by these. A draft that exceeds them is rejected
regardless of quality.

## Lloyd — the Second Right

Lloyd lost his **right** arm at the elbow in the Salts. On surfacing it appeared
whole and normal, and it is: it works, it looks like an arm, it bleeds.

What is not normal is that he can push it through solid matter. And that there
is a second one.

- The physical arm can be **passed through solid objects** at will — wall,
  door, ribcage. It does not damage what it passes through and the object does
  not damage it. He can grip normally, or he can not-grip, and switching between
  the two is a decision.
- The **second arm** is ethereal and invisible, occupying the same origin at his
  right shoulder. Nobody can see it, including Maria and Edie. He learns over
  the course of the book to operate it independently of the physical one, so by
  Act III he is effectively working with two right arms at once. This is a
  learned skill and should be clumsy early and fluent late.
- It can touch **the eldritch elements he sees with Insight** — the things
  nobody else perceives. This is the crucial one and the reason the ambiguity
  survives: when Lloyd fights something invisible, the observable facts are a
  man swinging at empty air and then something happening. Whether he struck a
  real thing or a wall gave way on its own is never resolved on the page.
- **It cannot** lift, carry, throw, or apply force to ordinary matter while
  ethereal. It cannot be used at range — it reaches as far as an arm reaches.
  It grants no strength beyond his own. It does not heal him, protect him, or
  warn him.
- **Cost:** he cannot always tell which mode he's in until he tries, and the
  second arm's sensation bleeds into the first. He drops things.

## Maria — Arcana, untouched by the Salts

**Maria does not gain a chalice ability.** She drinks only what is needed to
return from the dreamscape, and no more. She is the only one of the three who
walks away from it.

Her Arcana is native, present since childhood, fostered by Ondine Vail and
trained continuously since. It may or may not have been strengthened by the
Salts — she doesn't know, she is also training it, and the book never resolves
which. Her repertoire is fixed and complete:

- **Healing.** Slow, effortful, physical. Sets bone, closes wounds, does not
  regrow, does not cure disease, does not raise the dead. Costs her hours and
  leaves her useless afterwards.
- **Light.** Reliable, cheap, sustained. The most useful thing she does.
- **Fire.** A single burst, roughly five metres across. Devastating in a room,
  irrelevant in a field. Takes several minutes to be able to do again.
- **Lightning.** A bolt, at a target she can see. Accurate, lethal, and she
  hates using it.
- **Steadying.** A minor, constant, almost unconscious knack — a hand, a
  tremor, a body about to fall.

This is far beyond any other natural practitioner in the book. It is not
war-winning. It does not solve sieges, armies, or institutions. She is a
dangerous individual, not a weapon, and every fight should make that distinction
obvious.

## Edie — the Still Mind

She knows violence a half-second before it happens. That is the entire ability.

- No visions, no voices, no images, no words. Just knowing.
- It does **not** make her faster, stronger, or harder to hit. She frequently
  knows she is about to be struck and is struck anyway. The gap between knowing
  and being able to act is the point of the ability and should be dramatised as
  often as its usefulness.
- It covers violence only. Not lies, not intentions, not danger in general. A
  collapsing floor does not register. A man deciding to hit her does.
- She is enormously, privately relieved that what she got was an instinct rather
  than sight. She watched what sight has done to Lloyd and she wanted no part of
  it, and this is the one thing she has ever admitted to being lucky about.

# 3. ONDINE VAIL — WRITING RULES

She is the hardest character in the book to write and the easiest to ruin. Read
this section before every chapter she appears in.

**Age** 40s. Sister Superior, Sennet Almshouse, later Vantry, later the Sanctum
at Carrowgate. She took Maria in at seven, raised her, taught her to read, and
was the first person to tell her that what she could do was a gift rather than a
symptom. Maria has never had another mother.

## What she is

Vail is **sincerely good at every level Maria has ever been able to observe.**
She feeds people. She sits with the dying. She does the unglamorous work
personally and has done for twenty years. Her affection for Maria is real, it
has always been real, and it does not stop being real at any point in this book.
She is not wearing a mask. There is no true self underneath.

She also believes the Fourth Mercy is the most important work in the world, and
she believes this **for exactly the reasons she says out loud**. The horror is
that she is consistent.

## Hard behavioural rules

1. **She never lies.** Every statement she makes is literally true. She does not
   mislead by implication either. If a question would be awkward to answer, she
   answers it.
2. **She never lowers her voice.** No confidential murmurs, no glancing at the
   door, no waiting until they are alone. She would say any of it in a full room
   and occasionally does.
3. **She never threatens.** Not directly, not by implication, not with a look.
   Nobody is ever punished for refusing her. Maria walks away from her twice in
   this book and is not pursued, not followed, not warned.
4. **She never gets angry** and she is never cold. Her warmth does not flicker
   when she is contradicted.
5. **She answers direct questions directly.** If Maria asks what the tanks are
   for, Vail tells her. The reason Maria doesn't learn everything in ch. 12 is
   that she doesn't ask, not that Vail conceals.

## Hard prose rules

The failure mode is writing menace into her delivery. **Do not.** Specifically,
never write:

- a smile that doesn't reach her eyes
- a pause before answering, or a long look
- her lingering in a doorway
- eyes that are cold, flat, hard, or unreadable
- the temperature of a room changing
- Maria feeling a chill, a wrongness, or the hair rising on her arms
- any sentence in which Vail "seems" to be something other than what she says

Every one of those is a cheat. **All menace lives in the content of what she
says, delivered warmly.** If a passage would still be frightening read aloud in
a kind voice, it is written correctly. If it needs the ominous staging, it is
written wrongly and gets rejected.

## The confession scene (ch. 12) — how it turns

Maria confesses the forty-one crowns expecting penance. She has carried it six
years.

Vail is **delighted**. Not amused, not forgiving — pleased, in the way a teacher
is pleased. She says, honestly, that she has hoped for years that Maria would
turn out to be the kind of person who could set a rule aside for a person in
front of her, because most people cannot, and because the work requires it.

She forgives the theft in about fifteen seconds and treats it as trivial, which
it is, and which is itself disorienting — Maria has organised six years of her
life around it.

Then she asks Maria to come to Vantry and help with something, because she has
always had more of the gift than anyone Vail has trained.

## The imbuing (ch. 13) — the peek behind the veil

At Vantry, Vail brings Maria to a clean, warm, well-kept room. In it is a woman,
catatonic for two years, eight months pregnant. She is being cared for
attentively and expensively. Everything Maria can see about the room is kind.

Vail asks Maria to put her Arcana **into** her — not to heal her, not to help
her. To imbue.

When Maria asks what for, Vail tells her the truth, plainly and warmly and at
whatever length is asked for: that they are trying to make something that can be
raised past what a person is; that natural Arcana cannot be taught and can only
be born; that this woman will not recover and her child can be given something
no one else could give it; that Maria herself was found, not made, and that they
have been trying for sixty years to stop relying on luck.

She does not pressure. She says Maria can say no, and means it.

**Maria says no.** Vail accepts this immediately, without disappointment, and
takes her to lunch.

That is the crack. Nothing threatening has occurred, nobody has been unkind, and
Maria now has a question she cannot stop asking.

# 4. THE CHURCH OF THE MERCIES

- State-adjacent, wealthy, popular. Genuinely charitable at street level and
  most of its people know nothing.
- **Three public Mercies:** bread, shelter, absolution.
- **The Fourth Mercy:** an ascension programme. Taking people nobody will miss
  and attempting to make something higher from them. Homunculi grown in brine.
  Industrial immersion of non-consenting subjects who never surface. Attempted
  extraction of the soul as a separable object. Arcane breeding, with a stud
  book. Things bound out of the Salts and kept.
- It has never worked. Sixty years, every failure documented by name and date.
  Doctrine holds that it will.
- The other three Mercies feed it: bread and shelter identify the surplus,
  absolution collects them.
- **Ascension House, Vantry** — the working laboratory. Destroyed in ch. 15.
- **The Sanctum, Carrowgate** — headquarters. Never touched. Everyone senior
  survives the book.

# 5. WORLD

- **The Aventine Commonwealth.** Gaslight, rail, senate, empire in retreat.
- **The Attrition.** Four years, ended nineteen months before ch. 1. Won,
  technically. The enemy is never named or discussed. This book is about what a
  country does to itself afterwards.
- **Reconstruction.** The money exists. It does not reach the valleys.
- **Carrowgate**, capital. **Vantry**, second city — mills, soot, rail junction.
  **The Larrow Valley** — twelve villages, Lloyd's home, Crane's works.
- **Arcana** is real, uncommon, unregulated, and mostly weak. Undead and lesser
  horrors appear where the war killed enough people in one place; this is
  treated as a pest problem, not an apocalypse.
- **The Salts.** A dream-state entered by immersion in heavy brine. Stillwell
  Hydro sells it as a nerve cure. Time inside does not match time outside.
  Chalices grant an ability at a cost. Injuries taken inside are real on
  surfacing and heal wrong. Not a secret to the powerful — the Church has been
  using them industrially for years.
- **Insight** is the capacity to perceive what is actually there. The book NEVER
  confirms whether Lloyd hallucinates or sees truly, including after ch. 14.
  Being right about Ascension House must never become proof he is right about
  anything else.

# 6. WHO KNOWS WHAT, AND WHEN

The single most common continuity failure is a character acting on information
they don't have. Check every draft against this.

| Fact | Lloyd | Maria | Edie |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lloyd intends to kill Crane | ch. 1 | ch. 20 | ch. 24 |
| Edie works for Senator Ferrick | ch. 9 (unnamed) | ch. 9 (unnamed) | ch. 1 |
| Edie killed Reyne | ch. 24 | ch. 24 | ch. 1 |
| Maria's theft | ch. 12 | ch. 1 | ch. 12 |
| Lloyd has a second arm | ch. 5 | never told | never told |
| The Fourth Mercy exists | ch. 13 (suspects) | ch. 13 | ch. 13 |
| What it actually is | ch. 14 | ch. 14 | ch. 14 |
| Anse Duley was taken | ch. 14 | ch. 14 | never |
| Crane buried the survey | ch. 9 | ch. 21 | ch. 21 |
| Lloyd was institutionalised at 22 | ch. 1 | never told | never told |

Lloyd never tells anyone about the second arm. Maria never learns about the five
weeks in Carrowgate. Edie never learns about Anse Duley. These gaps are
deliberate and permanent.

# 7. STANDING PROSE CONSTRAINTS

- Third person limited, past tense, one POV per act, no head-hopping.
- The POV character's ignorance is binding: if Maria doesn't know it, the
  chapter can't say it.
- No character ever names their own arc, growth, or psychology aloud.
- Horror is catalogued, not lurid. Registers and case files over gore.
- Crane is culpable and never a caricature: warm in public, liked, generous to
  charity, and he read the survey.
- Maria's Arcana never scales up to solve a structural problem.
- Edie's ability fails as often as it helps.
- Lloyd's Insight is never vindicated as a system, only ever in specific
  instances that admit another reading.
