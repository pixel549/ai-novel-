# SYNTHESIS — full read-through, ch. 1–25

Written after batches 1–5 (`novel/readthrough/batch-1.md`–`batch-5.md`), against
`novel/canon.md`, `novel/outline.md`, and `novel/voice_ledger.json`. Per
RUNBOOK P1.6. Honest book-level verdict; no flattery intended or applied.

## The headline, stated plainly

Every one of the twenty-five chapters was independently rated **strong** across
five separate read-through sessions, with specific lines quoted as evidence
each time, not generic praise. Worth saying outright rather than letting it
pass unremarked: a clean sweep like that is itself a thing to be suspicious
of. Five friendly sessions reading a book their own pipeline produced is not
the same as one adversarial read looking for reasons to fail it. That said,
the verdicts aren't rubber-stamped — each one cites a specific sentence
against a specific canon rule, and the problems that did surface (below) were
caught and named, not smoothed over. The honest summary: this is a genuinely
well-executed draft against its own stated rules, with a short list of real,
specific problems, not a draft that got an easy grade.

## Ranked list of the real problems

1. **Ch. 7 — the ethereal arm lifts a solid object (R-1).** Canon's hard
   limit for Lloyd's second arm is unambiguous: no force on ordinary matter
   while ethereal, no strength beyond his own. Ch. 7 has it draw a three-foot
   iron rod bodily through solid lath into his other hand. The chapter
   lampshades the tension in its own prose but doesn't resolve it. This is
   the most serious open issue in the book, because it's a stated hard rule
   broken on the page, not a soft judgment call — and because the campaign's
   own surface-ability rules (S1) will eventually need to decide whether this
   is canon or an error. Needs Jake's call; see DECISIONS.md.
2. **Ch. 23 — the Still Mind registers a dropped tray (R-5).** Same category
   as #1: canon states explicitly that Edie's ability "covers violence only
   ... a collapsing floor does not register." The chapter has it flinch on an
   accidental dropped tray. Unlike R-1, this one isn't a drafting slip —
   `outline.md` specifies the beat deliberately, so the contradiction is
   between the outline and canon.md's own rule, not between the chapter and
   its outline. Still needs resolving before I1 (Dramatis Personae) writes
   up Edie's cameo rules, since those rules will need to state her ability's
   actual boundary.
3. **Ch. 9 — Lloyd breaks the fourth wall (R-2).** A genuine, isolated
   outlier: Lloyd explicitly names the act of narrating and "a page in
   whatever this is," against both his own "never articulate, never
   self-analysing out loud" rule and the book's blanket ban on characters
   naming their own psychology. It does not recur anywhere in the other
   twenty-four chapters — confirmed clean across batches 3–5 — which settles
   the open question batch 2 raised: this reads as a one-off overreach, not
   a device. Lower stakes than #1 and #2 because it doesn't touch any
   mechanic the campaign depends on; it's a voice problem in one paragraph.
4. **Ch. 16 vs. ch. 13 — three nights or four days (R-3).** Ch. 16's
   delirium gives three nights outside the tank-room window; ch. 13's own
   text, twice over, gives four days. Could be the chapter's deliberate
   unreliable-numbers conceit (the knot-cord is ninety-one instead of
   nineteen by design) extending one step further, or a plain slip — nothing
   marks this specific number as intentionally wrong the way the cord is
   marked. Minor; cosmetic; doesn't touch any ability or world rule.
5. **Ch. 12/13 — an undramatized Carrowgate stop (R-4).** Two lines imply a
   stop in or connection to Carrowgate between ch. 6 and ch. 12 that isn't on
   the page. Not a contradiction — room exists for off-page backstory — and
   only matters if the Gazetteer (C1) needs the geography to be exact.
   Lowest priority of the five.

None of these are chapter-wide failures; each is a single beat or line inside
an otherwise well-executed chapter. All five are logged in detail in
`campaign/DECISIONS.md` → Review findings, with the specific decision each
one needs.

## One thing the RUNBOOK's own example worried about, checked and dismissed

RUNBOOK's prompt for this file names "long-sentence rate since ch. 6" as the
kind of thing to look for independently, not just take on the production
ledger's word. Checked directly: `voice_ledger.json`'s long-sentence-run
tracker shows a real jump from near-zero in ch. 1–5 to a sustained 14–26
range from ch. 6 on, held for the rest of the book. On its own that would be
a red flag. In context it isn't one: canon Section 1 specifies this
exactly — Lloyd "runs long, then breaks," and Maria's voice is
"compound-complex sentences that loop back on themselves." Act I (Edie) is
the only POV canon specifies as short and declarative; Act II and III are
narrated by the two characters canon explicitly writes long. No batch
read-through flagged a long sentence as hurting a scene's legibility, and
several (the ch. 4 door, the ch. 15 fire-math, the ch. 21 confession) cited
long sentences as doing real characterization work. Verdict: not a problem,
it's the specified design, correctly executed. The tic-tracking in
`voice_ledger.json` (the-way-simile, a-thing-abstraction, and the rest) shows
a production process that caught and rewrote its own habits chapter by
chapter; by ch. 17 on, the ledger is tracking constructions in the single
digits, most "retired" or capped at one per chapter. That's the ledger
working as intended, not a lingering problem for this synthesis to re-flag.

## Which chapters, if any, deserve a hand revision

**Ch. 7 and ch. 23**, conditionally. Both contain a hard-limit breach
(R-1, R-5) that the production process itself flagged in the moment and left
unresolved rather than silently accepting or silently fixing. If Jake's call
on either is "ground it" rather than "accept the expansion," that chapter
needs a real rewrite of the specific beat (not the whole chapter) — the rod
needs to stay intangible to everyone but Lloyd, or Edie's sense needs to
re-target a person's reflex near the tray rather than the tray's fall. If
Jake's call is "accept it," neither chapter needs touching.

**Ch. 9**, conditionally and lower priority. If Jake wants Lloyd's voice
rule enforced strictly, the fourth-wall paragraph is the one passage in the
book that needs dialing back. Given it's a single, clearly deliberate
authorial swing rather than carelessness, and given canon itself permits
exceptions nowhere else in nine years of drafting, this is the easiest of
the three to simply leave as a one-time overreach.

**Ch. 16's "candidate for writing by hand" flag is resolved, not open.**
`outline.md` line 638 left this flag pending a look at what the finished
chapter actually reads like. Batch 4's independent read found deliberately
controlled disorientation, not vagueness standing in for craft — recommend
closing this flag rather than carrying it forward.

**No other chapter needs hand revision.** The craft patterns genuinely
worth naming as strengths rather than problems: the Lloyd→Maria POV handover
at ch. 16/17 (five chapters of Lloyd's least reliable narration handing
cleanly to a completely different sentence shape with no transitional
throat-clearing); Maria's "I did not" anaphora, which earns its repetition
rather than reading as a tic (ch. 18's two-beat close, ch. 19's four-in-a-row
list, ch. 25's echo of both); and Lloyd's counting-coins gesture, planted
unexplained in ch. 22 and paid off explicitly in ch. 25 — a planted detail
that resolves rather than one left hanging.

## Threads confirmed closed, for the record

Two threads tracked across every batch are now confirmed as permanent book
gaps rather than still-open questions, and should be read that way going
forward: **Edie's ch. 5 chalice cost never pays off anywhere in the book**
(outline.md specifies losing the ability to sleep without waking twice; it
never surfaces on the page, including in the two chapters — 24 and 25 — that
would have been the most natural places for it), and **the "19 DAYS
REMAINING" extraction timer is never explained**. Neither is a continuity
problem; both are good campaign hooks. See `campaign/hooks.md`.

**Maria's healing cost, by contrast, is a thread that resolved well across
the book** rather than staying open: softened in ch. 3, partially paid off in
ch. 7, landed as an explicit plot consequence in ch. 15 and dramatized at
length in ch. 17. Worth recording as a craft success, not a lingering
problem — the hard limit in canon Section 2 is honoured by the book's end
even though it wasn't honoured consistently along the way.

## Bottom line

The book does what canon.md and the outline asked of it, chapter by chapter,
with five specific, named exceptions, three of which carry real design
weight for the campaign (R-1, R-5, and the Carrowgate question for the
Gazetteer) and two of which are craft-only and low-stakes (R-2, R-3). Nothing
found in this read-through blocks Phase 2. `campaign/source-bible.md` and
`campaign/hooks.md`, written alongside this file, carry forward everything
the campaign needs from the book; this file is the one place the warts are
listed without varnish.
