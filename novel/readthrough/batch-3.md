## Ch. 11 — Permits

**What happens.** The trio passes through Tannery Reach, a tannery town on a
canal, where a drowned boy (Fenwick) is left floating at a lock because two
parishes are disputing who has to pay for his burial. Edie forces the local
relieving officer's hand with a measured threat (a public-health complaint to
the Canal Company) in under a minute; Lloyd does the physical labour of
pulling the body out; Maria sits with the mother.

**Craft verdict: strong.** This is Section 8's "cost test" and "take the
neglect over spectacle" instruction executed about as cleanly as the book
manages anywhere: a boy is dead and the obstacle isn't a monster, it's an
unconfirmed channel marker and a settlement clause — "Confirm the parish,
confirm the liability, move the body — in that order, not the reverse." Edie's
negotiation is the chapter's best material and matches her Section 1 voice
exactly (flat, trade-costed, no heat): "Channel's thirty-one feet at the
second lock. I paced it off the towpath stones walking in... Boy's lying
eleven feet off the Saint Aldhelm's bank." Lloyd's own uselessness in the
scene is handled well and stays in voice — "I had a use for my hands in a
fight... and there was nothing in me built for that" — rather than being
played as self-pity. One craft note for SYNTHESIS: the closing line ("I
didn't have an answer that wasn't the same thing said back to her, worse, so
I kept it") is a clean instance of the "narrator states what he did not say"
closer `voice_ledger.json` tracks (currently logged at ch. 8 only, with a note
flagging ch. 12–14 as "the longest run" on the pattern) — see Craft patterns
below; this chapter isn't in the ledger's list at all and arguably should be.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Edie's four-years-sapper background
and her ledger/cord props continue exactly as established (batch 1–2).

## Ch. 12 — Forty-One Crowns

**What happens.** At Sennet almshouse, Maria confesses the forty-one crowns to
Vail in person (off-page from Lloyd and Edie); Vail forgives it in roughly
fifteen seconds, calls it trivial, and invites Maria to Vantry. Maria relays
the whole scene to Lloyd and Edie that evening.

**Craft verdict: strong — near-exact execution of canon's scripted scene.**
Canon's "confession scene (ch. 12) — how it turns" section specifies Vail's
reaction almost line for line, and the chapter delivers it: "She laughed...
Delighted — that's the only word for it... She forgave the theft in about as
long as it takes to say it. Fifteen seconds, maybe less." Edie's one line —
"That's not a reckoning. That's a woman telling you the debt was never
real." — is the chapter's sharpest observation and correctly keeps Edie's
bright-line morality (canon Section 1) visibly uncomfortable with a debt
erased by fiat rather than weighed. Maria's reported voice (sideways, circling
back on itself, self-doubt named explicitly) matches Section 1's spec for her
even filtered through Lloyd's telling. Lloyd's own interiority — turning his
nine years of unvisited home over against Maria's six years of avoided
confession, "found no new angle on it I hadn't already found twice... and put
it down, because there's only so many times a man can weigh the same stone
before he's just holding it" — is a good, controlled instance of his
self-surveillance without tipping into stated self-analysis.

**Continuity flags.** Who-knows-what table honoured: "Maria's theft" lands for
Lloyd and Edie in this chapter exactly as specified. One geography question,
not a clear contradiction: Lloyd describes "the elbow still sat crooked no
matter how many times the bonesetter in Carrowgate had sworn it would
settle." Nothing in ch. 6–11 places the trio in or near Carrowgate since the
Salts run that caused the injury (ch. 3), and the capital reads, from ch. 8 and
ch. 10's references, as somewhere behind or south of their current road, not
obviously on it. It isn't impossible — there's room for an off-page stop
between ch. 6 and ch. 7 — but it's worth a question rather than a silent fix,
since changing it risks erasing intended backstory. Flagged below.

## Ch. 13 — Vantry

**What happens.** The trio reaches Vantry. Vail brings Maria to a catatonic,
pregnant woman and asks her to "imbue" her Arcana into the unborn child; Maria
refuses, and Vail accepts it without any sign of displeasure. Over the next
four days Lloyd watches Ascension House from the street and Edie works the
goods yard, turning up brine deliveries far beyond any bath house's need and
a service door doing all the building's real traffic. They decide to go in on
Thursday.

**Craft verdict: strong — second scripted canon scene landed cleanly.**
Canon's "imbuing (ch. 13)" section is matched almost beat for beat: the
catatonic woman two years gone, eight months pregnant, Vail's "you may say no,
and I mean that exactly as I say it," the refusal accepted "without
disappointment," and the walk to lunch immediately after. Vail's hard rules
hold under a genuinely monstrous ask — no lowered voice, no menace in the
delivery, the horror sitting entirely in content: "Most of them don't, dear.
That isn't a cruelty, whatever it sounds like from outside it. It's simply
where the work begins. Not where it ends." That line is as good an example as
the book has of Section 3's instruction that menace must survive being read
aloud in a kind voice. The old stone under the new brick — "a foundation cut
and dressed by other hands, long before anyone had thought to raise a chapel
over the top of it" — and the barred, green-lit basement window with
something moving behind it are strong, restrained hook material (see Lore
ledger / Open threads).

**Continuity flags.** Who-knows-what table honoured: "The Fourth Mercy
exists" lands for all three in this chapter, as suspicion rather than
confirmed fact, exactly as specified. The same Carrowgate question from ch. 12
recurs here in a different form: "her bundle still tied the same knot it had
been since Carrowgate" implies Maria's bundle was tied in Carrowgate before
the story's current road began — plausible (she may have come from there
before ch. 1), but worth noting alongside the ch. 12 item as the same
underlying open question about where Carrowgate sits relative to this route
and timeline, rather than two separate issues.

## Ch. 14 — The Fourth Mercy

**What happens.** Edie talks the three of them past the Ascension House
service door at night. They move through five rooms — occupied brine tanks,
shelves of grown "specimens," a stud book of Arcana bloodlines, an extraction
theatre with sixty years of failed-soul-extraction files, and a brass-ring
room holding something bound — and Maria finds Anse Duley's name in the
intake register, two years after the Church took her, two years after Maria
had already fled. Edie calls it: "We're burning it."

**Craft verdict: strong — exact match to canon's Fourth Mercy inventory.**
Canon Section 4 lists five things the Fourth Mercy does: homunculi grown in
brine, industrial immersion of non-consenting subjects, extraction of the
soul as a separable object, Arcane breeding with a stud book, and things
bound out of the Salts and kept. All five appear here, one room each, in the
same order the canon sheet lists them. The horror is catalogued rather than
lurid exactly as Section 7 requires — "Each shelf had a card. Each card had a
date and nothing else useful on it," the extraction files' closing lines
("result negative, subject retained" / "result negative, subject not
recoverable") doing all the work registers and case files are supposed to do
in this book instead of gore. Edie's one-word verdict on the stud book —
"livestock" — is the chapter's best line and stays exactly in her trade
register. The who-knows-what table is honoured with real care at the exact
moment it would be easiest to break: Maria finds Anse Duley's name, Lloyd
narrates knowing what it means because she'd told him and Edie the backstory
two nights before, and the text explicitly confirms Edie does not learn it —
"Edie, three feet away, cataloguing dockets by lamplight, never once asked
what had stopped her hand" — matching canon's "Edie: never" on this fact
exactly.

**Continuity flags.** None. The thing in the fourth tank that "was watching
me back that wasn't the man inside it" is new and unexplained — logged as an
open thread, not a contradiction, since nothing in canon forbids an
unidentified presence in the brine.

## Ch. 15 — The Siege

**What happens.** The three try to carry the files and register out before
burning Ascension House. The ring-bound thing gets loose and a "grown" thing
off the shelves attacks; Maria uses lightning twice rather than her
area-effect fire, which would kill the tank occupants; Lloyd takes a bad hit
covering a blind spot and goes down with a cracked skull, two broken ribs,
and his ordinary right arm broken in three places. Maria starts a full
healing and is talked out of it by the fire's clock; they escape with the
papers. Vail is waiting in the yard.

**Craft verdict: strong — this is the chapter canon pins, and it pays off two
open threads at once.** Canon Section 4 states flatly that "Ascension House,
Vantry... [is] destroyed in ch. 15," and that is exactly what happens here,
with no ambiguity about which chapter carries the event. More importantly,
this chapter does real, text-level work on two things batches 1 and 2 both
flagged as under-dramatized: Maria's healing cost and her power ceiling.
Section 2's hard limit on Maria ("healing... costs her hours and leaves her
useless afterwards") was softened in ch. 3 and only partly paid off in ch. 7;
here it is the explicit, stated reason the healing *doesn't happen* — "I
heard her start the words that open a healing... Edie say, flat, that the
fire... wasn't going to wait on hours," and Lloyd is carried out still
broken, "none of what was wrong with me got tended properly that night." That
is the cost landing as a plot consequence, not just a described ache. Her
fire limit gets the same treatment: she talks herself out of using it at all
because a five-metre burst in a room with twelve occupied tanks is "twelve
dead or near enough to it" — Section 7's "Maria's Arcana never scales up to
solve a structural problem" dramatized as her own reasoning rather than
authorial restraint imposed from outside. Vail's appearance in the yard
afterward keeps every one of her hard rules intact under real pressure — no
staged menace, the horror entirely in comparing burning a torture facility to
"mislaid a key or left a letter unanswered," and no pursuit of Maria when she
walks past without answering, exactly per "nobody is ever punished for
refusing her."

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. One new open thread: Vail "was
waiting in that yard. It should have been impossible." The text doesn't
resolve how she knew to be there or arrived that fast — logged below, in the
same family as Lloyd's unresolved Insight (deliberately unexplained rather
than contradictory).

## Craft patterns across the batch

- **The "narrator states what he did not say" closer.** `voice_ledger.json`
  currently tracks this pattern at ch. 8 only, with a note (written at the
  ch. 15/16 production stage) calling ch. 12–14 "the longest run" on it
  without adding them to the tracked list. Ch. 11's closer — "I didn't have
  an answer that wasn't the same thing said back to her, worse, so I kept
  it" — is as clean an instance of the same pattern as ch. 8's own logged
  example, and isn't mentioned at all. Read end to end, the real run looks
  like five chapters (8, then 11 through 14), not three. Worth a line for
  whoever next updates the ledger, and worth SYNTHESIS knowing the pattern is
  slightly under-counted in production's own tracking.
- **Lloyd wry out loud (minor, soft flag).** Canon's Section 1 "Never" list
  for Lloyd includes "never wry... out loud," which sits in tension with the
  same section's instruction that wry, deflecting humour is his main
  narrative defence — the "never" plainly governs his spoken behaviour, not
  his narration. Ch. 14's "I told her, low, that she'd have made a fine
  thief, and she said she'd have made a better one than this" is a small,
  genuinely borderline instance of Lloyd being wry out loud to Edie. It's
  gentle enough (a low, deadpan compliment, not a joke with a setup and a
  landing) that it doesn't read as a clear breach the way ch. 9's fourth-wall
  address did — but it's the same category of question, just much smaller,
  and worth having on record rather than silently let go.
- **The "the way" simile and the "patient" closer** (both tracked in
  `voice_ledger.json`, both already flagged "rest"/"spent" by production):
  holding exactly as recorded — one or zero uses per chapter in 11–15, no
  "patient"-word closers anywhere in the batch. Nothing new to add.
- **Ch. 9's fourth-wall break** ("a page in whatever this is," flagged in
  batch 2 as either a one-off slip or the start of a device) **does not
  recur anywhere in ch. 11–15.** Five chapters clean since it happened. On
  current evidence this is trending toward "slip" rather than "voice" —
  worth confirming one way or the other once batches 4–5 are read.

## Lore ledger (Batch 3)

- **Tannery Reach** — a tannery/canal town split between Saint Aldhelm's and
  Marsh Fenton parishes along the canal centerline; a man drowned at the lock
  (Fenwick, of Harker's yard) sits unclaimed for a day because neither parish
  will take liability for burial costs until a flood-destroyed boundary
  marker is restaked. Strong template for a "bureaucratic neglect" encounter
  or faction mechanic (contested jurisdiction as adventure fuel). (ch. 11)
- **The relieving officer / lock-house** — the parish office that decides who
  pays for an unclaimed body; backs down only when Edie threatens a public
  health complaint to the Canal Company superintendent. (ch. 11)
- **Sennet (almshouse)** — confirmed on the page as Vail's first posting
  (matches canon Section 1's biography); grey stone, bread oven, a poor box
  by the door ("FOR THE POOR"), a meal-bell rather than an hour-bell. (ch. 12)
- **Anse Duley** — the forty-one-crowns widow, now confirmed on the page (not
  just backstory) as taken into the Fourth Mercy two years after Maria fled,
  per the intake register Maria reads in ch. 14. (ch. 12, 14)
- **Vantry** — expanded: permanent smoke-haze that never clears day or night,
  gaslamps lit at noon, chimneys "ranked, dozens deep." (ch. 13, 14)
- **Ascension House** — full exterior described: new brick over older stone,
  a bell tower that never rings, an unused front chapel door with grass
  growing between its steps, and a cart-width service door around the side
  doing all the building's real traffic. (ch. 13)
- **The old foundation under Ascension House** — per parish deed records, the
  stone beneath the new brick predates the Church's ownership of the ground
  it stands on; a barred, below-grade window shows a pale standing gleam (not
  lamplight) and, once, a shape that "moved nothing like a man walks." Strong
  thin-site/source-bible candidate — whatever this site was before the
  Church built over it. (ch. 13)
- **Fourth Mercy facility, Ascension House** — five rooms: a tank room (12
  occupied brine tanks, tended by a nurse with a thermometer and clipboard);
  a shelved room of grown "specimens"; a cots room with a stud book of
  Arcana bloodlines going back decades; an extraction theatre (restraint
  chair, 60 years of files ending "result negative, subject retained" or
  "...subject not recoverable"); and a brass-ring room holding something
  bound that was once a person. Core material for `campaign/source-bible.md`
  and the G/H bestiary and compendium items. (ch. 14)
- **The fourth catechism line** — beneath "bread, shelter, absolution" at
  novice height in Ascension House, a fourth line has been added in a recent,
  ledger-style hand. Its exact wording is deliberately withheld by the
  narrator, echoing the ch. 9 Callow Ford reunion's withholding device. (ch.
  14)
- **The thing in tank four** — watches Lloyd specifically, unexplained, never
  reaches for him. (ch. 14)
- **Ascension House, destruction of** — burned the night of ch. 14–15, per
  canon's own pin ("destroyed in ch. 15"); confirms `campaign/DECISIONS.md`
  D-1's default (Vantry's Ascension House is "a burned shell" a year after
  ch. 25) is consistent with the book as written. (ch. 15)
- **Lloyd's injuries, end of ch. 15** — skull cracked (left side), two ribs
  broken, right (ordinary) arm broken in three places, untreated when they
  leave Vantry because Maria's healing was interrupted by the fire's clock.
  Worth tracking into batch 4 for when/how this gets addressed. (ch. 15)
