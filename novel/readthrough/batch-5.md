## Ch. 21 — The Confession

**What happens.** Maria confronts Lloyd with the floor plan she found in his
coat. He confesses the whole of it without resistance: he went to Stillwell
not only for his mother but to become capable of reaching Senator Crane, and
lays out the case — the buried groundwater survey, the shareholding hidden
behind two intermediaries, the Larrow Indemnity's seventh-year schedule built
on Crane's own mortality curve, two inspectors reassigned to the coast. He
explains he hid it because a man who says he'll kill a senator is a man you
stop trusting to rule on what's real, and asks her, in the chapel's own
ritual, to tell him if he's wrong about Crane.

**Craft verdict: strong.** This is the chapter canon's own who-knows-what
table requires ("Lloyd intends to kill Crane" lands for Maria here, and
"Crane buried the survey" lands for her in this same chapter — both exactly
on the table's pins) and it earns the reveal by staging it as relief rather
than confrontation: "I watched it land on him, and it did not land as a
blow. It landed as a door he had been holding shut with both hands, finally
let go." Lloyd's voice (canon Section 1: short sentences, concrete nouns,
literal answers to questions including the rhetorical ones) holds under real
pressure — "Yes" to "You're going to kill him," no hedge, no speech. The
chapel ritual's return ("Tell me I'm wrong about him... You'd know before I
would") lands as the Act I dynamic applied to its largest possible stake,
exactly the escalation canon's own arc implies rather than a retread.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Who-knows-what table honoured
precisely on both pinned facts.

## Ch. 22 — The Argument

**What happens.** Over three days, Maria tests every alternative to killing
Crane — the courts, a broadsheet, Ferrick's sub-panel, simply waiting out the
seventh-year schedule, pairing the Vantry evidence with Crane's figures for
weight — and Lloyd has costed each one months in advance. Her last argument
is the honest one: it will not save his mother or stop the next Crane, and
its only certain cost is Lloyd himself. He agrees completely; nothing
changes. She stays anyway, naming to herself, coldly, that her own reasoning
is exactly Vail's.

**Craft verdict: strong — the cost test (canon Section 8) run explicitly as
the chapter's own structure.** Each failed option is given a specific,
plausible institutional reason to fail (a clerk who needs more than "a
flooded well and a column from an actuary's table"; an editor burned once
already on an unnamed source; a docket "read in the order it was built") —
bureaucratic neglect over spectacle, exactly as Section 8 prescribes, and
none of the obstacles are a nameable villain. Maria's self-aware parallel to
Vail — "It is also, I noted, with no warmth in the noting at all, exactly
Vail's reasoning... The Church never once called that love. If I am honest
with myself... neither should I." — is squarely within her Section 1 voice
(self-doubt named explicitly, guilt attaching regardless of outcome) rather
than a narrator-intrusion; she is reasoning about her own choice the way she
reasons about everything, not stepping outside the story to editorialise.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon.

## Ch. 23 — Ferrick

**What happens.** Maria visits Edie at Ferrick's townhouse. Edie runs his
security with four unremarkable, drilled people and an iron cabinet holding
the real Vantry registers, untouched in two months. Ferrick is warm,
solicitous of Maria's old burns, and proud of the sub-panel's excellent,
damning second water report — four sessions held, no vote, a hearing
calendar ringed for next spring. Edie's Still Mind flinches a half-second
ahead of a dropped tray elsewhere in the house. Edie asks after Lloyd's arm;
Maria answers only "mending," and Edie, who never presses, notices the gap
anyway.

**Craft verdict: strong, with one real hard-limit tension — outline-mandated,
not a drafting slip.** Ferrick is drawn exactly as canon's own cast of
"institutions rot, the people inside them aren't uniformly hollowed out"
(Section 8) demands: sincerely decent, genuinely ineffective, and the
chapter never once mocks him for it — "He believed it as he believes
everything: completely, without any need to check" reads as diagnosis, not
contempt. Edie's silent arithmetic (four sessions, no vote, one excellent
report filed and admired and shelved) is a clean, wordless instance of her
Section 1 voice: she totals, she doesn't yet rule.

The tension: canon Section 2 states Edie's Still Mind "covers violence only.
Not lies, not intentions, not danger in general. A collapsing floor does not
register. A man deciding to hit her does." This chapter has it fire — "the
oldest tell I know in her, the one she's never once been able to argue her
own body out of... A fraction more warning" — on a dropped tray elsewhere in
the house, an accident with no person's intent behind it, which reads as the
same category of event canon's own example ("a collapsing floor") explicitly
rules out. This is not a drafting accident: `novel/outline.md` (line 552-555)
specifies the beat deliberately — "The Still Mind catches once in the
chapter, uselessly, on nothing worse than a dropped tray... the smallest
possible rehearsal of the gap she's lived in since the tunnel" — so the
chapter executes its outline exactly as written. The contradiction is
between the outline and canon.md's own hard limit, not between the chapter
and its outline. Per canon.md's own framing ("nothing here is optional...
where this file and a draft disagree, the draft is wrong"), this needs a
decision from Jake rather than a silent fix either way (loosening the hard
limit to cover any sudden, startling accident near her, or re-grounding the
scene so what she senses is a person's reflexive, sharp motion — someone
flinching or lunging to catch the tray — rather than the tray's fall itself).
Logged below and in DECISIONS.md.

**Continuity flags.** See above (R-5, logged in DECISIONS.md). No other
flags — the rest of the chapter (Edie's four-person security detail, the
sub-panel report, Ferrick's warmth) is new material, nothing it states
contradicts canon or earlier chapters.

## Ch. 24 — The Ledger and the Killing

**What happens.** Edie arrives with proof of Lloyd's reconnaissance — crosses
on her own floor plan that she didn't make. She states, flatly and at
length, that she murdered Colonel Absolom Reyne nine years ago after he
sealed ninety-one of his own sappers into a collapsed tunnel, and that she
has held a hard line since so the killing would still mean something. After
a sleepless night she returns at dawn with conditions rather than blessing:
Lloyd goes in with the kitchen staff, Maria as Ferrick's guest, the death
staged to implicate the Church. Crane dies in a service passage, courteous
and confused to the end. The escape goes wrong when Lloyd's old injury gives
out; Maria throws a crude burst of light into a corridor of clerks to buy
their way out, unknowingly handing Edie's frame its clearest evidence yet.

**Craft verdict: strong — this is the chapter canon explicitly carves out for
Edie, and it uses the carve-out fully.** Canon Section 1's "Never" list for
Edie includes "Never mentions the ninety-one except in ch. 24" — this chapter
is the named exception, and the confession lands in her own register rather
than breaking it: stated once, flatly, completely, no revision afterward
("I've never once let myself call it anything softer"), which is Section 1's
"states conclusions and omits reasoning unless asked" turned on her own
worst fact rather than a drafting indulgence. The who-knows-what table is
honoured exactly: "Edie killed Reyne" and "Lloyd intends to kill Crane" both
land for Edie in this chapter, matching the table precisely. Crane's death
— "Forgive me," he said, mild, apologetic... "Is the young lady quite all
right?" — is Section 7's "never a caricature" and Section 8's authorial
stance executed at the hardest possible moment: warmth and courtesy
delivered right up against death, no staged menace anywhere near it, which
is the exact move canon's Vail rules make mandatory for her and this chapter
extends to Crane. The killing method itself (the ordinary hand "flat and
unhurried," no mark, nothing a coroner could name) stays inside canon's
long-running ambiguity about what Lloyd's arm actually does — Section 2
reserves that ambiguity for exactly this kind of moment ("whether he struck
a real thing... is never resolved on the page") and the chapter doesn't
resolve it either, which is correct restraint rather than a gap. Maria's
light-burst is squarely within her stated repertoire ("Light. Reliable,
cheap, sustained. The most useful thing she does") used for cover rather
than harm, with no power creep.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. One worth recording as a positive
catch: Edie's conditions ("Nobody else in that room was to be hurt, under
any circumstance") and her insistence on documentary cover (a torn scrap of
vestment thread) match her established trade-costed, redundancy-minded
competence (canon Section 1, and her security work earlier in this same
chapter) rather than reading as a sudden new skill.

## Ch. 25 — Year Seven

**What happens.** Eleven days after Crane's death, grief gives Ferrick the
votes to bring the works-water schedule forward, test the water, and cap
wells before frost. The Church, blamed for the murder, loses the spring
hearing the real evidence could never win alone. Edie lets Ferrick thank her
for keeping his hands clean and leaves her knot-cord visible rather than
hidden — the first wholly honest position Maria has watched her hold since
the tunnel. Lloyd, wanted on every board in the valley, sends Maria north
alone to his mother, alive and moved to clean water; Maria tells her nothing
true. The book ends on Maria naming objects aloud until Lloyd believes the
room is real, and her hand closed over his.

**Craft verdict: strong — lands every one of canon's binding constraints on
the ending simultaneously.** Section 8's "shape of the ending, held at a
distance" rules out both the institution redeemed/toppled and the cast
ground to nothing, and demands "something person-sized surviving, at a real
price, with the rot substantially unhealed" — this is exactly what's on the
page: the Sanctum stands, the Church merely loses one hearing for the wrong
reason, Crane's death saves real children at a real human cost (a murder,
Edie's traded principles, Lloyd hunted and exiled from his own valley), and
nothing structural is fixed. Edie's final position — "she had stopped asking
whether she stood on the right side of anything and started only asking what
an action produced... It was not a fall" — is the book's cleanest statement
of Section 8's "clear-eyed, not cynical" distinction, delivered without the
narrator (Maria) either condemning or absolving her. The closing
grounding-ritual callback ("Cup... Window... Blanket... Your hand... Mine" /
"Still here") pays off ch. 4's established mechanism one last time, now
doing the entire emotional work of the ending rather than a single scene's
beat, and ends on the plain fact of presence rather than any narrator
statement of what it means — exactly Section 9's instruction and the
pattern `voice_ledger.json` has tracked clean for twelve straight chapters
(see Craft patterns below).

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Matches `campaign/DECISIONS.md`
D-1's defaults precisely: Crane dead and blamed on the Church, the
works-water schedule brought forward, wells being capped, Ferrick's
sub-panel with real momentum, the Sanctum untouched. Worth recording for
P1.6/source-bible: this chapter is the primary textual source for D-1's
"roughly a year after ch. 25" starting state and should be cited directly
rather than re-derived.

## Lore ledger (Batch 5)

- **Halvard Crane** — silver-haired, small round spectacles, soft unhurried
  manner; dies in a service passage during an Indemnity review session,
  courteous and confused to the end ("Is the young lady quite all right?").
  Confirms canon Section 5/outline's demand that he never read as a
  caricature. (ch. 24)
- **The buried groundwater survey, in full** — commissioned by Crane before
  the works broke ground, buried rather than acted on; his ownership of the
  works held through two intermediaries; the Larrow Indemnity's seventh-year
  payout schedule timed deliberately to the survey's own mortality curve
  (most claimants dead before they could collect); two inspectors who pushed
  to reopen the survey reassigned to the coast. Core Gazetteer/Factions
  material — the single clearest "bureaucratic neglect as villain" case in
  the book. (ch. 21)
- **Ferrick's household security** — four drilled, deliberately unremarkable
  people (an ex-quartermaster, two former constables, a young woman who
  checks deliveries against a list) plus an iron cabinet bolted into a
  former nursery floor, holding the real Vantry registers, untouched for two
  months. A ready-made template for a "competent NPC quartermaster" security
  detail (D1, F1). (ch. 23)
- **Ferrick's second water report** — forty pages, indexed, tests mill
  runoff from six towns against the valley's wells; states plainly the
  water will not be fit to drink inside a decade. Excellent, acted on by
  nobody for two months — strong Factions & Clocks (C2) material for how an
  honest institution can still produce zero outcome. (ch. 23)
- **Colonel Absolom Reyne, in full** — Fourth Tunnelling, sealed a breach
  with ninety-one of his own sappers still inside to save an hour; nine
  escaped through an unofficial second heading; Edie killed him eleven weeks
  later, alone, unwitnessed, never suspected. The single most important
  piece of Edie's backstory, now fully on the page rather than inferred.
  (ch. 24)
- **The vestment-thread frame** — a scrap of torn vestment cloth left
  caught on a window latch near Crane's body; "nothing any barrister could
  make stand up under oath," enough only to point the obvious question at
  the Church. Strong template for how a frame/cover story actually works at
  the table (J1, campaign factions). Never investigated further; the frame
  holds because Maria's own public light-burst corroborates it by accident.
  (ch. 24)
- **Ivet Hale, alive** — found by Maria at Callow Ford, recovered, moved to
  clean water (a new standpipe where the old well was), steadier than four
  years of the old water should have left her. Confirms D-1's "roughly a
  year after ch. 25" window is consistent with the book's own ending.
  (ch. 25)
- **The final state of all three leads, for I1 (Dramatis Personae)** — Lloyd
  wanted on boards across the valley under a wrong name, barred from Callow
  Ford, his right arm re-broken and healing crooked a second time; Maria
  with him in Carrowgate, their relationship the book's actual ending point;
  Edie inside Ferrick's household, trusted, her knot-cord worn visibly
  rather than hidden, her bright-line morality traded for pure consequentialism.
  (ch. 25)
