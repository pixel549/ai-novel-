# Read-through notes — Batch 1 (ch. 1–5, Act I, Edie POV)

Read in full against `novel/canon.md`. Per RUNBOOK phase 1.

## Ch. 1 — Stillwell Hydro

**What happens.** Edie arrives at Stillwell Hydro on a slip drawn against Senator
Ferrick's household account, pays her thirty-five shillings, and is sent to
Waiting Room 2. She meets Lloyd and Maria there as strangers thrown together
by the batches-of-three rule, trades first names only, and the three take
their first ten-minute orientation steam together before surfacing.

**Craft verdict: strong.** The chapter's trade is entirely in load-bearing
physical description — tide height, bolt corrosion, pipe gauges, batch
logistics — and it earns the strangeness of the premise by refusing to editorialize
about it. "He did not ask my name, my business, or my authorization" does more
characterizing work on the institution than any direct statement could. The
three leads are differentiated economically on first contact: Lloyd gives his
name and nothing else ("Nothing else. Maria didn't push for more, and neither
did I. A man who hands over a name like it costs him something isn't made to
pay twice in one sitting"), Maria instructs without being asked, Edie narrates
procedure. This matches canon Section 1's voice specs for all three exactly.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Clean introduction; no claims made
that later chapters contradict.

## Ch. 2 — Depth

**What happens.** The trio descends into the Salts proper — salt-crusted
stairs, a cracked chapel-shaped gallery, a timbered drift where distance and
hunger stop agreeing with each other. They meet and destroy a thin,
many-jointed wire-frame creature: Lloyd grapples it bare-handed, Maria steadies
his arm with a beam of Arcana and then floods the thing with light, Edie's
Still Mind warns her of a ceiling collapse a half-second too late to dodge it.

**Craft verdict: strong.** The temporal/spatial distortion ("I took ten paces.
Then twenty... but the end of the tunnel stayed sixty yards out") is staged
through Edie's own trade competence rather than told — she counts, logs, and
disbelieves her own count, which is exactly the right vehicle for a
sapper-surveyor narrator. The fight choreography is legible (rare for a
three-body combat scene) because every beat is tied to a measured distance or
a named structural feature. First on-page confirmation that Edie's Still Mind
"knows and is struck anyway" (canon Section 2) — textbook execution: she
knows the blow is coming a half-second out and is hit regardless. Good,
unforced delivery of a hard ability limit as lived experience rather than
exposition.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. One thing to watch rather than flag:
this is the first of several chapters (see tics below) that reach for an
atmospheric/environmental image as its closing line — worth tracking whether
that becomes a crutch over the batch.

## Ch. 3 — The Long Search

**What happens.** Three weeks (subjective, inside the Salts) of searching a
chapel-shaped chamber that differs slightly on every pass — moved candles, a
column where there was none, a missing floor tile — while Maria insists the
room hasn't changed. Lloyd's right arm is sliced off at the elbow by an
invisible force; Maria heals the stump on the spot, and it comes back crooked.
A depth measurement that should read twelve feet reads nineteen.

**Craft verdict: strong, with one soft spot.** The three-pass search structure
is an efficient way to dramatize the room's unreliability without any
supernatural stage business — it's just Edie's notebook disagreeing with
itself, which is more unsettling than a jump-scare would be: "The mortar is
fresh grey dust... It was covered sixty seconds ago" against Maria's flat "It
is unchanged." The amputation-and-regrowth is played completely without
spectacle, in keeping with canon Section 7 ("Horror is catalogued, not
lurid") — no reaction shot, just procedure (tourniquet rod reached for,
Maria's hands already on the wound). The soft spot: canon Section 2 states
Maria's healing "costs her hours and leaves her useless afterwards," but the
healing on the page reads as a single continuous beat (a chant, "the golden
light flared once and died") with no signal that hours passed, and Maria is
back on her feet directing the search's next step (checking the drift behind
them) within the same scene. Time is already unreliable in this chapter by
design, so this may be intentional (hours could be passing off-page the same
way the shaft gained seven feet), but as written there's no textual cue that
the cost actually landed — it reads cheaper than the hard limit promises.
Worth a glance in later chapters for whether this gets paid off or stays a
loose thread.

**Continuity flags.** The regrowth itself is exactly within Maria's stated
hard limits per canon's resolution note (the Salts supplied the material, not
Maria's healing) — correctly ambiguous on the page, matching canon's
intention that neither Maria nor Edie ever learns the arm "wasn't, strictly,
hers." No contradiction. See "Small fixes" below for a one-line contradiction
this chapter creates for ch. 4.

## Ch. 4 — What Is Real

**What happens.** Lloyd narrates things only he can perceive — a woman at a
dead end, a duplicate Maria, a door in a load-bearing wall — and waits for
Maria to rule each one real or not before acting. Edie tests the arrangement
once, inventing a brass lever that isn't there; Lloyd defers to Maria's
verdict over his own eyes and over Edie's claim, and the door he opens by
hand turns out to be solid rock that swings like a greased hinge the instant
Maria says it's a door.

**Craft verdict: strong — the best-executed chapter in the batch.** This is
the chapter the whole Act exists to deliver, and it does it entirely through
action and withheld commentary rather than through Edie explaining what she's
watching. The turn — "He had not questioned Maria's verdict... He required
her voice before he would treat any of it as physical matter" — is earned
because we watch the mechanism operate twice (the door, the invented lever)
before Edie names it. "There was a quiet gratitude in being the dull one" is
the chapter's thesis line and it's held back to paragraph four of the essay-like
closing passage rather than front-loaded, which is the right call. No tics or
missteps; this chapter should be a reference point for how to dramatize a
relationship dynamic without a character ever saying it aloud.

**Continuity flags.** One self-contained contradiction, logged and fixed (see
Small fixes).

## Ch. 5 — The Chalice

**What happens.** The chalice appears on the chapel pedestal only once Maria
declares it present — lead, heavy, real the instant her hand would have
passed through empty air. Maria drinks "enough to return" and stops. Lloyd
draws something unnamed and memory-shaped out of a stone bench and lets it
dissolve; his arm briefly double-exposes, then passes clean through solid
schist on the way out. The extraction clock reads four hours elapsed against
nineteen days remaining. A nurse asks if they enjoyed the treatment. Lloyd,
slumped on the exit bench, won't move until Maria confirms the room is real.

**Craft verdict: strong.** The two-register payoff — the catalogued, procedural
voice applied to something as uncanny as a chalice materializing under a
narrator's own moving palm — is the chapter's whole trick and it lands: "I
measured the gap between the base of the cup and the outer edge of the stone
block. It sat dead center in the carving" is a sentence about a chalice that
didn't exist a breath earlier, delivered with the same flatness as a bolt
count. Lloyd's memory-cost is dramatized cleanly (the pale shape, "like the
corner of a room I had never seen," that dissolves before it can be read)
without naming what was lost, which is the correct choice — the loss should
be shapeless to the reader too. The closing image (Lloyd immobile until Maria
rules the bench real) is the Act's dynamic from ch. 4 compressed to its purest
form and is a strong final beat for the Act. One soft spot: outline.md's ch. 5
beat calls for "Edie gives up sleeping without waking twice" as her chalice
cost, paired explicitly against Lloyd's — nothing on the page in this chapter
visibly charges Edie anything. Not necessarily wrong (her cost may land later,
off-page or in a future chapter), but as of ch. 5 it's an asymmetry: two of
three named costs are dramatized, one is invisible. Flagged below as an open
thread to watch in Batch 2+.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon.md. The chalice location ("on the
limestone pedestal," in a carved depression) differs from outline.md's
summary beat ("on the floor, in plain sight") — immaterial, since canon.md
rather than outline.md's prose summaries governs, and nothing about the
staged reveal (object appears only once Maria speaks it) is affected either
way.

## Craft patterns across the batch (sentence-shape sameness, repeated closers, tics)

- **"Dead left ear / camouflet at the Red Redoubt" recap.** Some version of
  this reminder appears in every single chapter of the batch — ch.1 ("the
  dead side remained flat and empty... since the camouflet at the Red
  Redoubt"), ch.2 (twice: "My left ear was dead space... since the camouflet
  blew out four years ago" and "My left ear recorded nothing—just the same
  heavy, leaden silence"), ch.3 ("the dead silence sat in my left ear—the
  permanent souvenir from a camouflet at the Red Redoubt four years back"),
  ch.4 ("the lead-weight silence from the old camouflet kept that side of
  the passage dead"), ch.5 ("My left ear was dead from the camouflet at the
  Red Redoubt"). It's doing real work in ch.1–2 (establishing why Edie
  orients by sound and touch) but by ch.5 it's a checklist recap rather than
  new information — five chapters, five near-identical reorientations to the
  same backstory fact. Candidate for trimming or varying sharply if this
  pattern continues into Batch 2.
- **Palm-scar recap.** "Cable scars" / "flat scars... across my palm" from
  old winch-cable work recurs in ch.2, ch.3 (twice), ch.4, and ch.5 — almost
  always attached to the same gesture (bare palm pressed flat against stone).
  Same note as above: grounding motif early, repetitive by the fourth
  instance.
- **Environmental-image closers.** Four of five chapters end on a sensory/
  atmospheric image rather than dialogue or a character's choice: ch.1 (the
  flickering gas jet sign), ch.2 (the humming salt-stained walls), ch.3 (the
  out-of-place number in stale air), ch.4 (the glow swallowed by darkness).
  Only ch.5 breaks the pattern, ending on Maria's spoken line. All four are
  well-written individually and match Section 9's instruction to end on an
  object rather than narrator explanation — but four-for-four is a shape, not
  a choice, and is worth varying if Act II repeats it.
- **Craft strength, not a tic:** the batch never names "the Salts" on the
  page even once (checked directly) — the dream-state is rendered purely
  through sense detail and never labeled. Keep an eye on whether this
  restraint holds once the story surfaces in ch.6.

## Lore ledger (Batch 1)

- **Stillwell Hydro** — a hydropathic sanatorium on a cold tidal coast;
  granite-and-pine, run in batches of three ("Run one... no single entries, no
  pairs"); sells entry at thirty-five shillings per run, billed against a
  patron's account; staffed by an intake clerk and steam-room attendants. (ch.1)
- **Senator Ferrick** — named only via his household estate office's
  blind-stamped parchment slip; Edie draws against his account without his
  knowledge (unstated on the page but per outline.md's ch.1 beat). (ch.1)
- **Red Redoubt** — unnamed-elsewhere site of the camouflet that deafened
  Edie's left ear; war-era (Attrition) backstory, new place name not present
  in canon.md. (ch.1, recurs 2–5)
- **Fourth Tunnelling Company** — Edie's wartime unit, matches canon Section 1
  exactly. (ch.5)
- **The brass run-token** — numbered disk ("Run one"), bought once per entry,
  tracks which batch/session a patron belongs to. (ch.1)
- **The knot-cord** — hemp cord with brass wire ferrules at regular
  intervals, used throughout as a depth/route marker and improvised rigging
  line; becomes the trio's standing tool. (ch.2, recurs 3–5)
- **The chapel-shaped gallery / "the chapel"** — a recurring, unstable
  chamber inside the Salts that the trio searches three times in ch.3 and
  returns to for the chalice in ch.5; candles, a fractured sandstone column,
  and a missing floor tile appear between passes with no physical cause. (ch.2–3, 5)
- **The wire-frame creature** — thin, jointed, no face, moves by flickering
  rather than walking, dissolves to salt mist on structural failure; first
  combat encounter, no name given on the page. (ch.2)
- **Lloyd's right arm, severance and regrowth** — taken clean at the elbow by
  an unseen force; Maria heals it on the spot; it surfaces crooked (five
  degrees off true, misaligned elbow) and functions normally otherwise. (ch.3,
  recurs 4–5; governed by canon's "Second Right" resolution note)
- **The depth-measurement discrepancy** — a shaft surveyed twice at twelve
  feet reads nineteen feet by cord and clicker-gauge on the actual descent; no
  resolution offered on the page. (ch.3)
- **Lloyd's Insight-sightings in the drift** — a woman standing at a dead
  end, a duplicate Maria, a door in solid basalt; Maria's ruling is binding on
  Lloyd regardless of what Edie or his own eyes present. (ch.4)
- **The chalice** — lead, roughly a pint capacity, appears only once declared
  present by Maria; found (per this batch) on the chapel's limestone pedestal
  in a carved depression. (ch.5)
- **Chalice costs** — Lloyd surrenders an unnamed, unseen memory (dissolves
  before it can be read, described only as glimpse of "a high window with
  three iron bars"); Maria drinks only enough to return and gains no
  ability, by her own choice. Edie's stated cost (per outline.md: lost
  ability to sleep without waking twice) is not dramatized on the page in
  this batch. (ch.5)
- **The extraction timer** — a clockwork device at the hydro reads two
  figures on surfacing: hours elapsed outside (four) against a second figure,
  "19 DAYS REMAINING," whose referent is not explained on the page. (ch.5)

## Open threads (Batch 1)

- **The nineteen-foot shaft.** Never resolved within the batch — is this
  time/space distortion ordinary for deep Salts work, or a specific anomaly
  tied to this run? Table-usable as-is: a measured discrepancy with no
  explanation is good delve material.
- **Edie's uncashed chalice cost.** If it's never paid off on the page later
  in the book, this is worth a line in the eventual SYNTHESIS — two of three
  costs dramatized, one invisible, and worth knowing before citing "costs are
  always shown" as a pattern for the campaign's own Salts rules (S1/C3).
- **"19 DAYS REMAINING."** What the second figure on the extraction timer
  actually counts (a window the patron must return by? a Church-wide
  schedule? something being brewed elsewhere in the hydro?) is never
  explained. Strong hook: a countdown the protagonists never ask about is
  exactly the kind of background clock a Salts-adjacent thin site could use.
- **Maria's healing cost, softened on the page.** Per the craft verdict for
  ch.3 above — if later chapters never show this ability actually costing her
  "hours" and incapacity, that's worth flagging in SYNTHESIS as a hard-limit
  stated in canon but not consistently dramatized.
- **What the wire-frame creature actually was.** Dissolves to salt mist, no
  name, no explanation of why it was there or what (if anything) it guarded.
  Good source material for a Salts bestiary entry (G/T items) — "undefined
  horror, jointed, light-averse-adjacent" is usable as-is.

## Small fixes made this session

- **ch.4, line 5.** Fixed a one-line contradiction: the chapter (set
  immediately after Lloyd's arm is severed and regrown in ch.3, same
  continuous scene) described his newly-regrown elbow as having "knit crooked
  years back." Changed to "where the elbow Maria's healing had left crooked
  just that hour" — preserves the sentence's rhythm and Edie's clinical
  phrasing while removing the claim that an injury sustained minutes earlier
  in-scene is an old one.
