## Ch. 16 — Delirium

**What happens.** Lloyd, half-conscious, is moved and tended in the gap
between the Ascension House siege and the let room ch. 17 opens in. The
prose never fixes where "here" is: three stairs overlay each other (the
Salts stair, the Vantry sub-basement stair, the stair at his mother's house
in Callow Ford), his mother Ivet surfaces in a brine tank and asks mildly
why he never came home, an unnamed voice says "Crane" in passing, a
"courteous man" asks after someone else's safety, a knot-cord counts out to
ninety-one rather than the nineteen it should carry, and Maria's voice
cuts through from outside the slide naming ordinary objects (a cup, a
window, a blanket, her hand) as the only fixed points on offer.

**Craft verdict: strong.** This is the chapter the production notes
(`outline.md` line 632) flagged as "thin by design intent but too thin in
practice" before a 2026-10-03 pass added concrete imagery to every overlay —
and on the page, that work holds. The delirium reads as deliberately
engineered disorientation, not vagueness standing in for craft: "Every
single time I was certain the next step was the bottom. Every single time
I was wrong, and never once learned from it, because by the time I
understood the mistake I was three strides past it." Each of the outline's
required beats lands exactly as specified and in the specified register —
Ivet's question delivered as "a neighbour's interest in your excuse for
missing church," not grief; the knot-cord counted twice with both counts
agreeing and trusted by neither; the buried chalice-cost memory left
exactly as illegible as the outline demands ("a word... spoken in a voice I
have never since been able to put in any mouth I know... it wore down to a
sound with no shape left inside it"). Maria's grounding ritual from ch. 4–5
continues with real continuity of craft rather than just premise: "The
blanket," "My hand... that one's real," spaced exactly as earlier chapters
established. On the evidence of this chapter, the README's standing "ch. 16
— candidate for writing by hand" flag (referenced in `outline.md` line 638)
looks resolved rather than open — this reads like a chapter that got the
hand-level attention it needed, not one still waiting for it. Worth a line
in SYNTHESIS: the flag can likely be closed rather than carried further.

**Continuity flags.** One soft numerical question, not a clean
contradiction: Lloyd narrates the tank room as "the room I'd described to
the other two from outside a barred window three nights running before any
of us had the nerve to go in and confirm it." Ch. 13's own text has Lloyd
watching from the street and reporting "the same flat words" across **four**
days/evenings ("I counted that cart on both its days for four days
running"; "On the fourth evening I came back from the chapel wall with the
same report I'd given every night running"), not three. This could be a
plain one-off slip, or it could be the chapter's own unreliable-numbers
conceit doing exactly what it's designed to do elsewhere (the knot-cord is
explicitly ninety-one instead of nineteen, by outline mandate) — but unlike
the cord, nothing in the outline flags this specific number as intentionally
wrong, so it isn't safe to wave off as deliberate without a call from Jake.
Logged rather than silently fixed, since fixing it either way (changing
"three" to "four," or leaving it) is a judgment call about how far the
delirium conceit is meant to reach into background details versus the
cord alone.

## Ch. 17 — After

**What happens.** Six weeks in a rented room in a Vantry mill district.
Maria nurses Lloyd through bedsores, turning, and a three-stage healing
(skull, then ribs, then the arm, which sets slightly crooked because five
weeks pass before she reaches it). Her faith and belief come apart without
resolving into either. Edie visits twice, briefly, without comfort. Maria
reads Anse Duley's entry in the stolen intake register nightly. Lloyd
surfaces properly on the fourth day and asks whether the room is real;
Maria answers yes, after a silence that frightens them both.

**Craft verdict: strong — Maria's narrative voice (canon Section 1) arrives
fully formed on its first chapter.** The compound-complex, self-qualifying
sentence shape is there from the opening paragraph, and guilt attaching to
outcome regardless of result is dramatized rather than stated: "I did that.
I want to be exact about that, even now: a woman with my gift, doing my
best, left a mark on him that is going to be there for the rest of his
life, and calling it the best I could do doesn't make it not a mark." The
healing-in-stages beat does real structural work rather than being a
checklist item — "The skull first... Then the ribs... The arm came last" —
and pays off canon's stated hard limit (healing "costs her hours and leaves
her useless afterwards") as a concrete plot fact ("ask Edie, who found me
three separate times face-down at the table"). The filial-not-theological
wound (canon Section 3 / outline ch. 17) is handled with real restraint:
"She is not a liar standing in a good woman's shape. She is the good woman,
the whole time... I cannot get a knife in between them to tell you where
the one stops and the other starts." No menace-staging anywhere in how Vail
is remembered here, consistent with Section 3's hard prose rules even in
Maria's own private grief rather than a direct scene.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Who-knows-what table unaffected
(no new facts cross POV lines this chapter). The arm's final state — "a
place just above his wrist... a ridge you'd take for a scar" and a
half-second catch when he makes a fist — is a specific, trackable detail;
worth noting here so a later chapter's description of the arm can be
checked against it (ch. 19 already does, see below).

## Ch. 18 — The Rumours

**What happens.** Edie takes the intake register to a Vantry broadsheet,
which runs it as photographed plates rather than typeset copy, alongside
Ferrick's ethics sub-panel report. It is the only story in the Commonwealth
for eleven days. The Sanctum answers: Ascension House is called a rogue
chapter, two under-priors are surrendered to the civil courts, a bishop
weeps in the Senate, a large restitution figure is announced. The story
curdles into a debate about the register's authenticity rather than its
contents; nobody senior is charged. Vail is transferred to the Sanctum at
Carrowgate and promoted, and writes Maria a warm letter asking only after
her health.

**Craft verdict: strong — this is Section 8's "menace delivered warmly"
instruction extended past Vail's own scenes, exactly as the Authorial
Stance section demands ("never confined to Vail's own scenes").** The
Church's three-move PR response is laid out as content, not staging: "Grief
offered first, so there is nothing left over to accuse. Blame narrowed down
to the two names furthest from the top of anything. A figure large enough
that disputing it looks like disputing charity itself." Maria's realization
that she watched Vail train for exactly this her whole life, "and calling
it mothering because it was also that," lands the chapter's real wound
without a single staged-menace cue anywhere near it. Edie's register-handoff
scene keeps her voice exactly in canon's register (flat, trade-costed, no
persuasion): "They'll print it whole. Photographed, not set." The chapter's
closing beat — Maria folding Vail's letter back along its own crease and
filing it under the same floorboard the register used to occupy — is a
clean, concrete image doing the emotional work instead of a stated
conclusion, consistent with Section 9's "end on an action... not the
narrator explaining."

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. One worth flagging as a positive
continuity catch rather than a problem: the chapter's account of the
Sanctum's response (rogue chapter, two under-priors, no one senior charged)
matches canon Section 4 exactly ("The Sanctum, Carrowgate — headquarters.
Never touched. Everyone senior survives the book") and sets up
`campaign/DECISIONS.md` D-1's default (the Sanctum untouched a year later)
correctly.

## Ch. 19 — What It Was Worth

**What happens.** Eleven days after the story breaks, it has already moved
on. Edie arrives with a settled account of the six weeks' costs ("Paid
out") and reads the outcome as a clean win. Lloyd reads it as vindication —
publicly right after nine years of being disbelieved — and has never looked
less burdened. Maria's private reading is darker: the ward is gone, the
programme and the people who ran it are not, and her mother has been
promoted for it. She tells neither of them. Edie leaves for Carrowgate,
handing over an address with no explanation, and Maria realizes Edie has
never once asked either of them for anything.

**Craft verdict: strong — delivers the outline's three-readings structure
with each reading audibly in its owner's established voice.** Edie's
ledger scene is the chapter's best material and stays exactly in her
Section 1 register: "She had never once, in my hearing, cared what a thing
was worth. She had only ever cared what it cost. Those were not the same
question." Lloyd's one-word "Good" carrying "the whole of it" is a correct,
restrained instance of a character who (per canon) never narrates his own
psychology aloud. Maria's unspoken arithmetic is the chapter's real spine,
built from a genuine four-paragraph "I did not tell them" anaphora (the
other sum, her mother's promotion, the letter, what it cost) that reads as
earned structure rather than a tic — it is explicitly listing everything she
is choosing to carry alone, which is the chapter's whole point. Edie's exit
— "she had never once asked either of us for a single thing" — is exactly
the "no revelation scene, cumulative and mostly unspoken" treatment
`outline.md`'s prose-direction notes require for her, landed through what
she doesn't do rather than a stated feeling.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. One cross-check: Lloyd "opened
[the drawer] by reaching straight through the face of it rather than
fighting the wood, calm as a man checking a knife was still sharp" uses the
ethereal/physical-arm ability squarely within its canon hard limits (passing
through solid matter, no force applied to ordinary matter beyond the
pass-through itself) — consistent, no creep. The who-knows-what table is
untouched here: Maria watches the ability's visible effect, which she has
already seen repeatedly across the book, not the two-arm mechanism itself,
which stays "never told" per canon Section 6.

## Ch. 20 — Drifting

**What happens.** Three weeks in Carrowgate at Edie's address. Maria takes
in mending and sits with a labouring neighbour; Lloyd takes day work at a
coal yard. After a week of genuine rest, Maria notices a pattern: Lloyd's
evening walks always end near the Senate, and every man he "happens" to
talk with wears the Indemnity Office's pin. She overhears him quizzing a
clerk about door schedules and carpet-men days. That night, while he
sleeps, she searches his coat and finds a session calendar, a traced
building plan with three doors marked by crosses, and, in his own hand,
the name Halvard Crane.

**Craft verdict: strong.** The slow-build mystery structure is handled
economically — the chop-house exchange ("Chief clerk still leave his door
on the latch through the morning session?") does all its expository work
through clipped dialogue rather than narrated summary, and Maria's three
discarded, "respectable" explanations for Lloyd's behaviour (boredom, job-
hunting, comfort-seeking) are each tested against the facts and rejected in
turn rather than asserted away, which keeps her from reading as slow on the
uptake. The telling detail that triggers her suspicion — "a pale dust
ground into the toe of one boot that had nothing to do with coal" against
Lloyd's actual coal-yard work — is exactly the kind of concrete, physical
clue canon's prose direction calls for over exposition. The chapter ends,
per `voice_ledger.json`'s own tracking, on a bare physical image (her hands
unable to refold the plan steadily) rather than a narrator's gloss on what
the discovery means — consistent with the "narrator-explains-the-scene"
closer staying retired through this stretch, and with Section 9's
instruction to end on an action or object.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. The valley/Crane background Lloyd
has given Maria ("the mine, the river, the works, the twelve villages that
fed it, all of it Crane's") matches canon Section 5 exactly (Larrow Valley:
twelve villages, Lloyd's home, Crane's works). Per canon Section 6's
who-knows-what table, "Lloyd intends to kill Crane" is marked as landing for
Maria at ch. 20; this chapter gets her to the edge of that fact (a marked
building plan and a name) rather than an explicit statement of intent to
kill, which the text appropriately holds back for ch. 21's confession — a
faithful dramatization of the table's timing rather than a contradiction of
it.

## Craft patterns across the batch

- **The POV handover (Lloyd → Maria) lands cleanly.** Ch. 16 is Lloyd's last
  chapter on the page; ch. 17 opens in a visibly different narrating voice
  from its first paragraph, with no transitional throat-clearing. Worth
  recording as a craft success in its own right — a five-POV-chapter run for
  one narrator ending on his most compromised, least reliable chapter and
  immediately handing to a completely different sentence-shape is a real
  risk that didn't cost the book anything on the page.
- **The "narrator states what he did not say" closer** (`voice_ledger.json`'s
  `narrator-explains-the-scene`) stays retired across the whole batch —
  seven clean chapters by the ledger's own count through ch. 20, matching
  what this read independently found: ch. 16 ends on a bare image, ch. 17 on
  a plain physical fact (her hand on his chest), ch. 18 on a plain action
  (the letter filed away), ch. 19 on Edie's footsteps fading with no gloss
  on what her exit means, ch. 20 on unsteady hands. Holding this well into
  Act III is a genuine, trackable craft improvement over the ch. 8–14 run
  batch 3 flagged as under-counted.
- **Maria's "I did not" anaphora** is doing real structural work rather than
  reading as a repeated tic, at least across ch. 18–19: a controlled
  two-beat close in ch. 18 ("I did not write back. I did not burn it.") and a
  deliberate four-in-a-row list in ch. 19 cataloguing exactly what she keeps
  from the other two. Worth flagging to SYNTHESIS as a construction that
  earns its repetition rather than one to warn against, in contrast to
  several of the ledger's "watch" items from earlier batches.

## Lore ledger (Batch 4)

- **Carrowgate, expanded** — finer, lighter soot than Vantry's (a white
  collar lasts two days instead of one); gaslamps on the better avenues burn
  "a clean greenish white"; the Senate sits atop a hill, a long grey
  roofline with a central dome, built to read as permanent. (ch. 20)
- **The Indemnity Office** — a government office identifiable by a small
  brass pin (crossed quills); its people keep session-day hours, door
  schedules and "carpet men" maintenance days that Lloyd spends weeks
  mapping. Strong Gazetteer/Factions candidate — plausibly the office that
  would have adjudicated compensation or liability claims tied to Crane's
  works, which would connect directly to the buried survey. (ch. 20)
- **Halvard Crane** — full name confirmed on the page for the first time, in
  Lloyd's own handwriting on a stolen building plan, three doors marked with
  crosses. (ch. 20)
- **The Vantry broadsheet** — the specific outlet that ran the intake
  register as photographed plates rather than typeset copy, explicitly so
  no one could claim a compositor altered a word; strong "the press as a
  faction" material for Factions & Clocks (C2). (ch. 18)
- **The Sanctum's crisis playbook** — rogue-chapter framing, under-priors
  surrendered to civil courts, a large uncontested restitution figure,
  eleven days of news cycle before the story curdles into a dispute about
  the register's authenticity rather than its contents. A reusable template
  for how the Church manages any future exposure in the campaign. (ch. 18)
- **The Vantry assembly's standing archive** — where the intake register,
  the stud book and sixty years of case files end up, "to be asked for by
  date," almost never requested once the story stops being new. A strong,
  quietly horrifying location: the evidence that ended the ward still
  exists, filed and essentially unread. Good source-bible and Gazetteer
  material (bureaucratic neglect, not spectacle, per canon Section 8). (ch.
  19)
- **Ondine Vail — Sanctum, Carrowgate** — confirmed transferred and promoted
  to oversee intake across the Sanctum's three almshouses, after Ascension
  House's exposure rather than despite it. Directly supports
  `campaign/DECISIONS.md` D-1's default. (ch. 18)
- **The cost of six weeks' recovery, itemised** — rent, bread, an unpaid
  surgeon's fee, broadsheet printing costs, a second blanket, coal — closed
  out by Edie as "Paid out." A ready-made template for Travel & Downtime
  (F1) cost conventions and for how an Edie-type NPC quartermaster would
  actually account for a party's expenses. (ch. 19)
- **Lloyd's arm, final state** — healed crooked just above the wrist, a
  ridge under the skin, a half-second catch on a full fist. A specific,
  checkable physical detail for any later chapter or campaign cameo rule
  (I1) describing Lloyd. (ch. 17)
