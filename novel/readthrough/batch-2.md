# Read-through notes — Batch 2 (ch. 6–10, Act II opens, Lloyd POV)

Read in full against `novel/canon.md` and `novel/readthrough/batch-1.md`. Per
RUNBOOK phase 1. Also checked against `novel/voice_ledger.json`, which already

## Ch. 6 — Overground

**What happens.** Lloyd surfaces at Stillwell with Maria and Edie, privately
concludes (for the first time, to the reader only) that his regrown arm was
never Maria's doing, and the three split money and plans on the street before
walking north together — Lloyd toward Callow Ford, Maria and Edie toward a
rail junction job.

**Craft verdict: strong.** This is the chapter canon's own table says it has
to be ("The ch. 3 regrowth wasn't Maria's doing" — Lloyd, ch. 6) and it lands
the realization exactly as specified: no confrontation, no confession, just
"Some mercies you don't correct." The overground-Insight material (rotten
joists under fresh paint, a guard with three shadows, an iron dog) is a clean,
well-earned extension of the Salts' wrongness into daylight, and "the city's
whole trick is that those two facts look identical from the street" is a good
thesis line for the whole Act. One tic to flag on its own account and two
tracked by the production ledger already: the chapter ends on an atmospheric
image ("the extra shadow of the guard stretching ahead of us like a dark
omen") — batch-1 flagged exactly this closer pattern across 4 of 5 ch. 1–5
chapters, and it's back immediately in the first chapter of Act II.
`voice_ledger.json` separately logs a literal "patient"-closer tic for ch.
6–8 (different wording here, same shape) and a heavy run of the "the way
[clause]" simile (6 uses, tracked as "develop/rest" in the ledger) — both are
real and both are already on the production team's radar, which is worth
noting so the next SYNTHESIS doesn't re-discover something already caught.

**Continuity flags.** None. Who-knows-what table is honoured exactly (Lloyd
alone learns the regrowth truth, this chapter, told to no one).

## Ch. 7 — Wet Work

**What happens.** A paid "wet work" job in a flooded cellar: Edie prices the
space, Lloyd wades in and uses his arm-through-matter to open a stuck drain,
and his ethereal second arm pulls a length of iron rod out of a wall cavity
that Edie never reacts to. That night Maria heals a boy's crushed hand in the
street — slow, costly, visibly draining her — and takes "witch" without
correction.

**Craft verdict: strong, with one real mechanical problem.** Maria's healing
scene is the best writing in the batch and the first real payoff of the open
thread batch-1 flagged (canon: healing "costs her hours and leaves her
useless afterwards," softened on the page in ch. 3) — here the cost is
visibly dramatized: "the light in the alley had moved a hand's width along
the wall and she was grey herself, swaying... I got a hand under her arm
before she went all the way over." It's not quite "hours" and she's upright
and talking again within the scene, so the thread isn't fully closed, but
it's a real improvement worth noting rather than re-flagging as a miss.

The problem: the rod-from-the-wall beat. Canon's hard limit for the ethereal
arm is explicit — "It cannot lift, carry, throw, or apply force to ordinary
matter while ethereal... It grants no strength beyond his own" — with the
ambiguity reserved for *combat* ("whether he struck a real thing... is never
resolved on the page"). What happens here isn't combat: the ethereal arm
reaches into a stud bay, closes around a hidden object, and draws it bodily
through solid lath without a mark, after which it is "solid" and weighted in
Lloyd's ordinary hand. The chapter itself flags the tension — "shouldn't be a
thing that arm is allowed to touch if it's only ever the eldritch it gets and
nothing solid," "a weight to it that a thing made only of my own seeing has
no business having" — which shows this was written with eyes open, not missed.
But flagging the problem in-text doesn't resolve it: an object has been
lifted and carried through a wall by the ethereal arm, and that's the exact
thing Section 2 says it cannot do. This reads either as an early, unflagged
expansion of what the second arm can do (canon does say it goes from "clumsy
early" to effectively two working right arms "by Act III" — but this is ch.
7, very early for that) or as a hard-limit breach that should be walked back
or re-grounded (e.g., the rod turns out to still be intangible to anyone but
Lloyd, or he can't actually keep it). Listed below as structural; not mine to
fix by rewriting the scene.

**Continuity flags.** None beyond the above. Edie's non-reaction to the rod
is correct per the who-knows-what table (she's never told about the second
arm) and is good craft on its own terms.

## Ch. 8 — Sweetwater

**What happens.** The three pass through Ammet Ford, a model company town
built by Crane's works — glazed school, poured roads, a sweet-tasting
company well — and read a posted notice, the Larrow Indemnity, promising
compensation only "in the seventh year." Edie's arithmetic says the town is
genuinely good for its workers; Lloyd can't say why that doesn't settle it for
him.

**Craft verdict: strong.** This is the chapter's whole trick, and the title
pays off two chapters later: a town that is better than anywhere else in the
valley, built by the same company whose name Lloyd won't say yet, selling
water that "had a thickness under the sweet, a hush at the back of the
throat... make it sit still and agreeable in a way water doesn't sit on its
own." The Edie/Lloyd disagreement over the indemnity ("I can't make the
arithmetic come out badly just because the man's name I don't care for")
is the clearest statement yet of the gap between Edie's declared,
quantity-based ethics and Lloyd's Insight-adjacent unease, and it's handled
without either character being made to look foolish. Tracked by the ledger:
this is one of the three chapters (6–8) carrying the "patient"-closer
("The sweetness was still sitting... faint, and patient") — already flagged
and later retired per `voice_ledger.json`, and also one of the heaviest
"the way" chapters (18 instances, same ledger). Worth flagging for SYNTHESIS
as a real three-chapter run, not just a single instance, since it's the
second of three closers in a row using almost the same word ("patient") as
its pivot.

**Continuity flags.** None against canon. Matches Section 5 ("The Larrow
Valley — twelve villages, Lloyd's home, Crane's works") and extends it with
specifics the canon sheet doesn't contradict.

## Ch. 9 — Callow Ford, Told Not Shown

**What happens.** On the road to Callow Ford, Lloyd tells Maria a version of
his mother that's true but incomplete, Edie trades a fact of her own (an
unnamed "charge" in Carrowgate) for the first time, and Lloyd works out from
the Indemnity's seven-year clause that the valley's wasting illness — "the
Sweetwater" — has a mortality timeline the company priced the payout past on
purpose. The actual reunion with his mother is withheld from the page by the
narrator's explicit choice.

**Craft verdict: strong material, one real craft problem.** The title reveal
— "Sweetwater" as the valley's own name for the disease, distinct from but
deliberately homonymous with Ammet Ford's dosed well water two chapters
earlier — is the best piece of world-building in the batch and ties the
book's own title to both the illness and the company's product in one move.
Lloyd's arithmetic on the Indemnity ("nobody who'd drunk that water long
enough to need paying was left standing to collect it before year seven...
not a coincidence of arithmetic") is a strong, quiet indictment that never
needs to raise its voice, exactly per Section 8's instruction to prefer
bureaucratic neglect over a nameable villain.

The problem is the chapter's closing device. Lloyd withholds the Callow Ford
reunion with a direct address to the reader: "I've spent the whole of this
getting you to understand how I decide what gets shown, and the honest end
of all that explaining is to actually do it once, in front of you, instead
of just describing the practice... a story told to a woman on a hillside, or
a page in whatever this is." This is Lloyd being fully, deliberately
articulate about his own narrative practice — not just self-aware in the
reader's eyes the way all three narrators are, but naming the mechanism of
his own telling and acknowledging the existence of "a page." Canon's "Never"
list for Lloyd is explicit: "Never articulate, never wry, never
self-analysing out loud." Section 7 adds, book-wide, "No character ever
names their own arc, growth, or psychology aloud." Nothing elsewhere in
chapters 1–10 breaks the fourth wall like this, and nothing in canon
licenses a narrator to acknowledge being a narrator. It's a striking
paragraph on its own terms, but it's a genuine outlier against both the
specific Lloyd voice rules and the book's general restraint, and it isn't a
one-line fix — the whole point of the passage is the address — so it's
listed below rather than touched.

**Continuity flags.** None beyond the above. Who-knows-what table honoured:
Edie's "charge" stays unnamed to both Lloyd and Maria (matches "Edie works
for Senator Ferrick," ch. 9, unnamed, for both). Lloyd's five weeks in
Carrowgate at 22 are recounted to the reader only, never spoken to Maria —
consistent with "never told."

## Ch. 10 — Two Priests

**What happens.** Two Mercy brothers, Aldous and Tam, travel the road with
the trio for an afternoon. Lloyd catches Aldous's collar failing to move
with his throat when he laughs; Maria argues him out of it as a kindness
misread; Edie separately notices, and says nothing about, a discrepancy
between the marker post's travel time to Selwick and Aldous's own estimate.

**Craft verdict: strong — best dialogue in the batch.** The Lloyd/Maria
argument over Aldous is the most developed version yet of their recurring
disagreement, and it's an escalation from ch. 4's dynamic rather than a
repeat of it: in ch. 4, Lloyd deferred to Maria's ruling without resistance;
here he argues back ("It's not a feeling" / "It's a feeling with a reason
attached to it"), and she closes it with a ruling anyway ("They were real
men... I won't have you carrying the other thing all the way to Carrowgate
on my account"). That's real development of the Section 1 dynamic, not a
retread. Edie's role is the chapter's quiet highlight: "I don't have the
eyes for it. Either of your eyes. If it comes to a fight I'll have a
position on it. This isn't a fight" is exactly her voice, and her separately
noticing the day-count discrepancy on the cord — ordinary observation, not
her ability — and choosing not to arbitrate is a well-built parallel to
Lloyd's own habit of filing things away unspoken. The chapter breaks the
environmental-closer pattern (ends on action: "I caught them up and didn't
say anything else"), though that line itself is close in shape to the
"narrator states what he didn't say" closer `voice_ledger.json` tracks only
at ch. 8 — possibly a second instance the per-chapter drafting review didn't
catch, worth a glance at the next ledger pass rather than an urgent fix.

**Continuity flags.** None. Edie's ability isn't invoked or misused here —
she's explicit that this isn't a fight and she has "no eyes for it," which
correctly keeps the Still Mind scoped to violence per Section 2.

## Lore ledger (Batch 2)

- **Hessel Bight / Hessel crossing** — rail town four days north of
  Stillwell; site of the flooded-manse "wet work" job. (ch. 7)
- **The burnt manse** — a war-damaged house with a flooded cellar holding
  eleven war dead; cleared for a landlord paid in bread and a night's roof.
  (ch. 7)
- **Ammet Ford** — "LARROW CONSOLIDATED WORKS — AMMET FORD DIVISION," a
  model company town: new brick housing, slate roofs, a fully-glazed school,
  poured-concrete roads and culverts, a company-sunk well forty feet through
  clay. (ch. 8)
- **The Larrow Indemnity** — a posted compensation scheme; claims payable
  only in the worker's seventh year of employment/registration. Received
  gratefully by workers on the page. (ch. 8)
- **The Sweetwater (disease)** — the valley's own name (distinct from, and
  deliberately homonymous with, Ammet Ford's dosed well water) for a wasting
  illness that starts in the joints and moves to the chest; typical course
  five to six years. Gives the book its title. Lloyd's mother, Ivet Hale, has
  had it four years as of ch. 9. (ch. 9)
- **Ammet Ford's well water** — sweet beyond ordinary mineral sweetness,
  with "a thickness... a hush at the back of the throat"; produces an
  unconsidered compulsion to keep drinking it. Never confirmed on the page
  as the same substance as the valley Sweetwater, only named the same by the
  people who drink it. (ch. 8)
- **Ivet Hale** — Lloyd's mother's name, given for the first time. (ch. 9)
- **Lloyd's institutionalisation** — the room with no window in Carrowgate,
  five weeks, age twenty-two, after he told the wrong person what he saw at
  nineteen. Recounted to the reader only; never spoken to Maria or Edie on
  the page. (ch. 9)
- **The Callow Ford reunion** — deliberately withheld from the page by the
  narrator. All that's given: it cost something, and Lloyd paid it. (ch. 9)
- **Brother Aldous & Brother Tam** — two Mercy brothers travelling to the
  Selwick almshouse with blankets and seed corn; Aldous's collar does not
  move with his throat when he laughs. (ch. 10)
- **Selwick** — an almshouse two days up the north road per Aldous, one day
  per the morning's own marker post (country miles, which Edie does not get
  wrong). Discrepancy noticed, not resolved. (ch. 10)
- **Edie's cord** — the hemp knot-cord from batch 1 (there, a depth/route
  marker underground) reappears used for counting days on an overland
  journey — same prop, a new function. (ch. 9–10)
- **Edie's ledger** — an oilcloth account-book, soft and dark at the corners,
  used to cost every job, fare, and favour; established as a recurring prop
  from ch. 6 on. (ch. 6–9)

## Open threads (Batch 2)

- **The rod from the wall.** Does the ethereal arm's retrieval of a solid,
  weighted object through an unmarked wall (ch. 7) represent real growth in
  what it can do, ahead of the "clumsy early, fluent late" curve canon
  promises for later in the book, or is it a hard-limit breach that needs
  grounding? Flagged in the ch. 7 craft verdict above; the single most
  important continuity question in this batch.
- **The ch. 9 fourth-wall break.** "A page in whatever this is" is a one-off
  acknowledgment, by Lloyd, that he is narrating something written down —
  unprecedented in chapters 1–9 and against both his own and the book's
  stated restraint on self-analysis. Worth tracking whether this recurs in
  batches 3–5 (if it does, it's a voice; if it doesn't, it's a slip) and
  flagging either way for SYNTHESIS.
- **Aldous's collar.** The clearest "something is wrong with a Church
  agent" sighting since the rail guard in ch. 6 — and the first one
  attached to a named, speaking character rather than a background figure.
  Strong candidate for a Fourth Mercy construct (homunculus, something
  "bound... and kept," per canon Section 4) and a strong campaign hook as
  written.
- **The Selwick day-discrepancy.** Unresolved: a mundane lie, an honest
  mistake, or a small bleed of the Salts' own time-distortion into the
  surface, days' travel from Stillwell. Good delve or omen material either
  way — a table could use this as a seed for a thin site nobody's supposed
  to be near yet.
- **Maria's healing cost (continued from Batch 1).** Ch. 7's boy-in-the-alley
  scene is a real, visible partial payoff (greyness, swaying, real elapsed
  time) of the "hours and useless afterwards" hard limit batch-1 flagged as
  softened in ch. 3. Not fully to the letter yet — she's talking and
  walking again within the same scene — but worth noting as progress rather
  than re-flagging as a fresh miss. Keep watching batches 3–5.
- **Edie's uncashed chalice cost** (carried over from Batch 1, unaddressed
  this batch — Edie isn't the POV and no chalice material appears).
- **The "19 DAYS REMAINING" timer** (carried over from Batch 1, unaddressed
  this batch).
- **Sweetwater, the double meaning.** Whether Ammet Ford's dosed well water
  and the valley's wasting disease are the same substance in different
  doses, a deliberate palliative, or only linguistically related is left
  open on the page. This is likely the single best piece of campaign-bible
  material in the batch — "what the table could do with this" is substantial
  (a faction selling a controlled dose of the thing that's killing people
  slower elsewhere) and belongs in `campaign/source-bible.md` and
  `campaign/hooks.md` when P1.6 writes them.

## Structural issues (not fixed — listed for Jake / SYNTHESIS)

- **Ch. 7, the rod-from-the-wall beat.** See craft verdict and open threads
  above. Needs a decision: ground it (the rod stays intangible to anyone but
  Lloyd, or he loses it, or something else keeps it from being unambiguous
  ordinary matter) or accept it as an early, currently-unflagged expansion
  of the second arm's stated hard limits.
- **Ch. 9, closing paragraphs.** "A page in whatever this is" breaks the
  book's established first-person convention by having Lloyd acknowledge the
  existence of the text itself. Either this needs to be the first instance
  of a deliberate, book-wide device (in which case it should recur and be
  flagged when it does, or retrofitted into earlier chapters) or it's a
  single overreach that should be dialed back to match Lloyd's established
  "never self-analysing out loud" voice rule.
- **outline.md sync** (carried over note from Batch 1, unchanged — not
  re-verified this session since this batch's chapters don't touch Lloyd's
  arm-side description again).
