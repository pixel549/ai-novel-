I stepped from the doorway into the salt-crusted interior. The stairs descended into a dim corridor that arched like a chapel. The vaulting was rough masonry, white efflorescence thick along every mortar joint, furred like frost on wet brickwork.

I walked down the first corridor. The air grew thin, sour and stagnant like an unventilated drift. My stomach pangs made the short distance feel like two days under heavy load. Cold moisture seeped through the soles of my boots from the flagstones.

At the end of the passage, a thin, many-elbowed thing flickered into view. It coiled along the stone floor, sharp-jointed and pale grey, clicking against the flags like iron tongs.

Half a second before it struck, the air pressure shifted against my skin and I knew the blow was coming. Lloyd lunged at it bare-handed. His heavy fists snapped against the shifting mass, knuckles driving straight into flesh that didn't bleed.

Maria stepped forward. Her hand glowed briefly, a casual arc of pale light snapping out from her palm to steady Lloyd by the shoulder before he could lose his footing.

I kept my distance, staying five paces back where the arch held firm. I watched the creature twist on the flags. A sudden gust from the struggle knocked a cloud of dry, gritty dust from a nearby ledge, hitting my face and shaking me where I stood.

The thin thing collapsed into a heap of vapor and ash. The air cleared inside the chapel-shaped hall, leaving only the cold smell of burnt lime and wet iron.

Maria turned toward my right ear. She mentioned she had run this hydro many times, her voice flat but steady through the quiet.

"My count is higher," Lloyd said. He did not look back. He replied that his count of runs exceeded hers, his eyes scanning the stone walls for more signs of movement or weak mortar joints.

Near the altar, a thick knot-cord hung from a wrought-iron hook. Its rope was frayed at the ends like unlaid hemp line, stiff with old tallow.

I walked over, looped the cord around my pack strap, and pulled it taut. I felt the weight of the rope settle hard across my shoulder blade.

The cord snapped into place against the leather frame. It hummed faintly in my good ear as the stone walls seemed to pulse once in response, a low vibration rising through the soles of my boots.
