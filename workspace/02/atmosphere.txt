I stepped off the last stair into a salt-crusted corridor. Low gaslights sputtered inside caged wall brackets, casting a dull yellow wash over the stone arches. The vaults overhead caught the heavy scrape of our boots and threw it back down the passage.

I followed the drift down the gentle slope. The walls were dressed ashlar block, laid six inches by twelve without mortar, sweating cold condensation and thin white salt-efflorescence. The passage opened out into a chapel-shaped chamber with a vaulted head. At the far end, a tarnished brass door shut off the way to the lower level.

I pushed the door. It swung back on greased iron hinges. The corridor beyond stretched out the instant we crossed the sill. We walked for two minutes by my count, but the far wall stayed twenty yards ahead, fixed and unreachable. Two minutes felt like two days. A dry, hollow hunger gnawed at my stomach, and the edges of my sight blurred.

A thin, many-elbowed creature slid along the side wall with a quiet, wet hiss. Its jointed limbs clicked in sharp, unnatural angles against the stone.

Lloyd lunged. He went in bare-handed, his shoulders broad and heavy as he struck at the thing while its limbs writhed against the ashlar.

Maria raised a hand behind him. A hard pulse of white light flashed from her open palm, cutting through the damp shadow and driving the creature back two paces. The flash illuminated the wet stone floor, steadying Lloyd’s grip and keeping his footing firm on the salt glaze.

I stood aside against the solid wall and watched the fight. I knew the strike was coming half a second before it landed—a cold, sharp flick across my range. My body was too slow to clear it. The creature’s edge dragged across my left forearm, slicing through the wool coat and leaving a shallow cut.

Lloyd struck it again. The creature collapsed onto the flagstones in a pile of dark whorls, then dissolved into a cloud of sour steam.

Maria exhaled. She dragged the back of her sleeve across her damp forehead. "I've run this sequence dozens of times," she said. "I know the timing."

Lloyd nodded once, rolling his right shoulder. "Third encounter with that thing in the hydro," he said.

I looked up at the keystone overhead. A knot-cord hung from a rusted iron ring, its slack loop dropping as the steam thickened and the chamber pressure crept up.

I reached up and pulled the cord. The lead weight at the end resisted against my scarred palms, then clacked home into its iron housing as the system locked into the next phase.
