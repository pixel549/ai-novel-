I stepped off the final stair into a salt-crusted corridor. Low gaslight hung in caged wall brackets, throwing dull yellow light over the stone arches. The vaults overhead echoed with our boots against the floor.

I followed the stone-lined passage down the slope. The masonry was ashlar block, six inches by twelve, fitted tight without mortar and wet with cold vapor. The drift opened out into a vaulted, chapel-shaped chamber. At the far end, a brass door blocked the way to the next level.

I pushed the door. It swung back on heavy hinges. The corridor ahead seemed to stretch as soon as we crossed the sill. We walked for two minutes by my count, but the far wall refused to come closer. Two minutes felt like two days. Hunger gnawed at my stomach, dry and sharp, and time blurred around the edges.

A thin, many-elbowed creature slid along the walls with a quiet hiss. Its jointed limbs worked in smooth, unnatural angles against the stone.

Lloyd lunged. He went in bare-handed, his broad shoulders driving forward as he struck at the creature while its limbs writhed against the ashlar.

Maria lifted a hand behind him. A bright pulse of white light flashed from her palm, forcing the creature back two paces. The light steadied Lloyd’s grip and kept his footing firm on the slick floor.

I stood aside and watched the fight, keeping my back to the solid wall. I felt the strike coming half a second before it landed—a sharp flick of bone across my range. My body was too slow to clear it completely. The creature’s edge brushed my left forearm, leaving a shallow red cut through the cloth.

Lloyd hit it again. The creature collapsed into a pile of dark whorls on the flagstones and dissipated into steam.

Maria exhaled. She wiped her forehead with the back of her sleeve. "I've run this sequence dozens of times," she said. "I know the timing."

Lloyd nodded once, rolling his right shoulder. "Third encounter with that thing in the hydro," he said.

I looked up at the stone ceiling. A knot-cord hung from an iron ring in the keystone, its slack loop dropping lower as the steam thickened and the chamber pressure rose.

I reached up and pulled the cord. The weighted lead end resisted against my palms, then clacked home as the system locked into the next phase.
