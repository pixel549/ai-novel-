The door behind us was four inches of solid oak bound in iron straps, set into an ashlar wall three feet through. Rain had been coming down straight outside the entry, flat and cold off the hydro roof, smelling of soot and wet slate. Inside, the heat hit first—eight degrees warmer than the street, thick with the sour stench of damp sodium, boiled lard from the pipe packing, and old slate. The hall floor ran twelve feet across to the head of the stairs. Dry white salt lay three-eighths of an inch deep across the flagstones like crushed quartz, drifted to an inch and a half along the skirtings where the draft from the outer jamb didn't reach. My boots left flat, square heel-marks in the crust, the dried brine crunching under the leather with a sound like dry bone breaking underfoot.

Lloyd stood two feet to my right. His shoulders took up thirty inches of the passage width, his heavy coat dark and stiff at the seams where the rain had soaked through to the lining. He didn't look at the walls or the lime-washed vaulting overhead. He looked straight ahead at the dark below the top step, his hands hanging down at his sides—the right hand stiff, the index and middle fingers locked half-bent from old breakages that had healed thick and square.
