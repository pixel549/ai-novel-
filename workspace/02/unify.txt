I stepped off the stair into a salt-crusted corridor. Caged gaslights burned low, throwing a dull yellow wash across the stone arches. The vault caught our boot-strikes and echoed them down the passage.

I followed the line down the gentle slope. The walls were dry-laid ashlar, six inches by twelve, sweating condensation and thin white salt-efflorescence. The passage opened into a vaulted chamber shaped like a chapel head. At the far end, a tarnished brass door shut off the way to the lower level.

I pushed the door back on its iron hinges. The corridor stretched the instant we crossed the sill. We walked two minutes by my count, but the far wall stayed twenty yards ahead, fixed and unreachable. Two minutes felt like two days. A dry, hollow hunger gnawed at my middle, and the edges of my sight blurred.

A thin, many-elbowed thing slid along the side wall with a quiet hiss. Its jointed limbs clicked against the ashlar.

Lloyd went in bare-handed, heavy through the shoulders, striking at the center mass while the limbs writhed. Behind him, Maria lifted a hand. A hard pulse of white light flashed from her palm, cutting the damp shadow and driving the creature back two paces. The flash lit the salt glaze on the flagstones, steadying Lloyd’s footing so he could hold his grip.

I stood aside against the wall and watched. Half a second before the strike came, I knew it—a cold, sharp flick across my range. The body was too slow to clear it. The creature’s edge dragged across my left forearm, cutting through wool and leaving a shallow line.

Lloyd struck it again. The thing collapsed onto the flagstones in a heap of dark whorls and dissipated into sour steam.

Maria exhaled, wiping her forehead on her sleeve. "I've run this sequence dozens of times," she said. "I know the timing."

Lloyd rolled his right shoulder. "Third encounter with that thing in the hydro."

I looked up at the keystone overhead. A knot-cord hung from an iron ring, its loop slackening as the chamber pressure rose.

I reached up and pulled. The weighted end resisted against my scarred palms, then clacked home into its housing. The lock clicked and the chamber sealed, trapping us for the next run.
