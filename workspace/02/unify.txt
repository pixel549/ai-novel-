I stepped down the salt-crusted stairs into a corridor whose arches curved like a chapel. 

Dressed granite, sixteen steps dropping eight feet below the intake room. White salt crusted four inches deep in the corners where the broom missed. The vault overhead rose seven feet, brick arches springing from square pillars every four paces. Salt crystals bled through the lower mortar joints. Lloyd walked three paces ahead, broad through the shoulders. Maria took my deaf side, two paces back. Iron dishes in wall brackets held small, yellow oil flames that threw our shadows long down the flags.

The main line ran straight toward a timber bulkhead, but a narrow branch opened on the right. I stepped through the turn first. Three feet wide, six foot two overhead. I had not eaten since noon yesterday—four ounces of salt beef and a rye crust in the yard. Empty stomach, belt tight. We kept moving down the branch. Step after step, forty paces, eighty, one hundred and twenty. Cold air, heavy with wet stone. No side vents, no drain grates. Lloyd’s tread sounded right behind me, Maria behind him. Driven by hunger, the walk stretched until it felt like two days, pulling far past the surface footprint of the building before flattening out at a T-junction. Left dropped into dark; right was blocked by dead spoil.

Lloyd spotted a thin, many-jointed thing coiling along the far wall and lunged at it with bare fists. 

It clung to the damp mortar six feet up, directly opposite the turn—four feet of pale length, no thicker than three-inch haulage cable, six elbowed legs twitching against the brick. Lloyd did not call out. He took two long strides across the junction, right boot hitting the centre flag with a heavy thump, and drove his fist straight for the middle segment.

The creature darted, its limbs snapping, and Lloyd landed a punch that cracked one of its joints.
