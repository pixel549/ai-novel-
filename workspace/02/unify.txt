I stepped from the doorway into a salt-crusted interior, the stairs descending into a dim corridor that arched like a chapel. Efflorescence furred the joints like frost on wet brickwork.

I walked down the first corridor. The air grew thin, stagnant as an unventilated drift. Stomach pangs made the short distance feel like two days under heavy load.

At the end of the passage, a thin, many-elbowed thing flickered into view, coiling along the stone floor.

Half a second before it struck, I felt the air pressure drop and knew the blow was coming. Lloyd lunged at it bare-handed, his fists snapping against the shifting mass.

Maria stepped forward, her hand glowing briefly as she steadied Lloyd with a casual arc of light.

I kept my distance, watching the creature twist. A sudden gust from the struggle knocked dust from a nearby ledge, shaking me where I stood.

The thin thing collapsed into a heap of vapor and ash. The air cleared in the chapel-shaped hall.

Maria mentioned she had run this hydro many times, her voice flat but steady through the quiet.

Lloyd replied that his count of runs exceeded hers, his eyes scanning the stone walls for more signs.

Near the altar, a thick knot-cord hung from a wrought-iron hook, its rope frayed at the ends.

I looped the cord around my pack strap and pulled it taut, feeling the weight of the rope settle across my shoulder blade.

The knot-cord snapped taut, humming with latent pressure as the chapel's stone walls seemed to hold their breath.
