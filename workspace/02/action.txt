The pale grey material of its frame didn't bleed, but it clamped down with the force of an iron band, pulling his elbow flat against the masonry. Lloyd grunted once, his short neck tensing as he braced his left heel into the salt crust to keep from being dragged down.

Maria stepped forward.

She did not draw back or wait for an order. She moved into the three-foot gap between Lloyd and the left wall. Her grey shawl shifted over her shoulders, slipping back to expose her forearms. The skin from her wrists to her elbows was mapped with thick, flat burn scars—twenty years of unschooled fire-work that had left the flesh shiny and hairless.

She raised her right hand, palm outward, five inches from Lloyd's upper back.

A brief, hard arc of pale light snapped from her fingers. It was not a burst of fire and it made no heat. It was a single line of bright, steady radiance that clipped the air between her palm and Lloyd's shoulder. The arc settled across his shoulder
