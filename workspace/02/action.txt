I stepped off the final stone stair into a salt-crusted corridor. The floor underfoot was grey flagstone, covered in a two-inch layer of dry white salt crust that cracked beneath my boots. Overhead, three stone arches spanned the six-foot width of the passage at eight-foot intervals. The arches echoed with the sound of our boots under the low light of two gas lamps mounted in iron cages on the left wall. The flames burned blue and yellow, casting short, hard shadows across the salt. Lloyd walked three paces ahead of me. His broad shoulders took up most of the gap between the masonry walls. Maria walked two paces behind my right shoulder, keeping her footsteps light on the crust. My left ear registered nothing from the wall to my left, only the faint hum of steam vibrating through the stone under my soles.

We followed the stone-lined passage forty yards down a four-degree slope. The walls were ashlar blocks, six inches high and twelve inches long, fitted without mortar and slick with condensed vapor.
