I stepped off the coach onto the slick pier. The iron rails were wet from last night's rain. My boots left shallow marks in the damp earth. I walked toward the hydro's stone entrance. The stone was old and rough. Plaster peeled beneath strips of iodine‑stained cloth. The cloth hung from a rope. I paused at the threshold. The door was heavy. I pushed it open. The hallway inside was dim. I heard muffled voices. I moved toward the clerk's desk. A tall man in a uniform waited. He held a ledger slip in his hand. I handed him the slip. He stamped it with a thick stamp. He recorded a charge against Senator Ferrick's household account. He did not ask me anything. He pressed a brass token into my palm. The token was marked "Run 1". He told me the facility ran patrons in groups of three. I nodded. The clerk turned back to his paperwork. I followed a short corridor. A door led into a waiting room. The room was low and square. A wooden table sat in the middle. Lloyd leaned against the table. He was large and heavy. He inspected a coil of rope. The rope was thick and knotted. Maria was beside the table. She spread clean blankets on the bench. The blankets were white. They were folded neatly. The room smelled faintly of disinfectant. I stepped into the room. I stood beside Lloyd and Maria. Lloyd looked at me. He said in a low voice, "Never been here before?" I answered, "No." He nodded. Maria glanced at the token in my hand. She said, "First run," confirming my answer. The three of us left the waiting room. We walked toward the intake chamber. The chamber was wide and open. A heavy valve stood near a metal frame. Lloyd lifted the valve with his right hand. The valve was heavy. He pulled it to a position. Maria lit a gas lamp. She flicked a match. The flame steadied. I checked the pressure gauge. It read 120 psi. I noted the steam rising in the tank. The air grew hotter. A steel door opened. We filed into a steam room. The door closed behind us. The room filled with thick vapor. Steam drifted across the floor. The boiler hummed. The floor vibrated beneath our feet. The heat pressed against my skin. I held my breath. The timer on a clock ticked. The sound was faint. I felt the vibration in the wall. The timer beeped. The run ended. We stepped out into a cold corridor. The air was crisp. A fresh towel lay on a hook. I grabbed the towel. I wrapped it around my shoulders. Lloyd wiped his forearm with the towel. The forearm was damp. Maria wrapped a shawl around her shoulders. The shawl was thick. I pocketed the brass token. I slid it into my pocket. The token was warm. I felt its weight. The steam curled in the corridor. I stood in the doorway of the hydro. Steam still curled around my shoulders. The iodine‑stained sign flickered above the entrance. I breathed in the cool air. I breathed out the hot air. I checked the token again. It read "Run 1". I looked at Lloyd. He was standing beside the valve. He was ready. I looked at Maria. She was standing by the gas lamp. The three of us were ready for the next run. I turned to the clerk. He was at the desk. He was stamping a new ledger slip. He was busy. I walked back toward the exit. The door opened. The pier waited. I stepped back onto the pier. The hydro's stone entrance loomed behind me. The stone was dark. The plaster was peeling. I left the building. The coach awaited. I climbed back onto the coach. I boarded. The coach moved forward. The road stretched ahead. The journey continued.
