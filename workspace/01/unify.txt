I stepped off the coach onto the slick pier and walked to the hydro's stone entrance. Plaster peeled from the masonry beneath strips of iodine-stained cloth.

Inside, I handed my ledger slip to the clerk. He stamped the paper and recorded the charge against Senator Ferrick’s household account without asking a question. He passed me a brass token marked *Run 1* and told me the facility ran patrons in groups of three.

An attendant led me into a low waiting room. Lloyd leaned against a wooden table, inspecting a coil of rope, while Maria spread clean blankets across a bench.

Lloyd looked up. "Never been here before?"

"No," I said.

He nodded. Maria glanced at the token in my hand. "First run."

The three of us moved through to the intake chamber. Lloyd lifted a heavy iron valve into position. Maria lit a gas lamp against the wall, the flame brightening the tile. I checked the pressure gauge—one hundred twenty pounds—and noted the steam rising from the floor fittings.

We filed into the steam room. The steel door shut behind us, sealing tight, and thick vapor filled the chamber. The boiler hummed, vibrating through the floorboards beneath my boots. We stood together in the dark heat, breathing the hot air.

The timer sounded. We stepped out into the cold corridor, where an attendant handed me a fresh towel. Lloyd wiped his forearm, Maria wrapped a shawl around her shoulders, and I pocketed the brass token for the next run.

I stood in the doorway of the hydro, steam still curling around my shoulders, the iodine-stained sign flickering above the entrance.
