I walked up the cold coast road to Stillwell Hydro. The façade was respectable enough from fifty yards—a broad rectangle of brick and rusted iron—though up close the paint was peeling off the window frames. Beneath the smell of salt and old stone was a faint tang of iodine from the steam plants.

I stepped through the heavy timber doors. The lobby floor was tile, slick with condensation. I approached the desk, where a clerk sat behind a brass grate. I handed over a signed slip drawing our entry fees from Senator Ferrick’s household account. The clerk checked the signature, stamped the ledger page, and handed me a flat brass tag. I clipped it to my belt.

An attendant led me to the dive preparation area, a concrete bay pitched toward a central drain. Three divers lined up along the bench: a tall man in the middle with a nose set crooked from old breaks, a wiry woman to his left with pale burn marks on her forearms, and myself on the right.

Lloyd asked me directly, "Ever been here before?"

"No," I said.

Maria glanced at the log board by the door, nodded, and said, "First time for all of us."

We split the work. Lloyd checked the rigging, pulling the heavy cables to test the splices. Maria lit the brass lanterns and set them in the rack. I surveyed the shoring, checking the timber joints, the pitch of the iron beams, and calculating where the frame would fail if a breach took the wall.

We strapped on our gear, pulling the webbing tight across our chests. We stepped onto the reinforced platform. The platform lowered, steam fogging the view, and I stepped into the darkened tunnel as the hatch thudded shut behind me.
