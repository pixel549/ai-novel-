I walk up the cold coast to Stillwell Hydro and see the respectable façade, paint peeling, a faint iodine scent over older stone. The building is a rectangle of brick and rusted iron. The window frames are yellowed. The sign over the door reads “Stillwell Hydro Co.” in faded letters. I pause to gauge the distance. The railing is at shoulder height. I step onto the cobblestone path. The ground is damp from the mist. The air holds the smell of old water.

I step through the doors. The lobby floor is slick with condensation. A chill runs up my spine. I approach the clerk's desk. He is a thin man in a uniform. He looks at my ID. I present a signed slip. The slip is from Senator Ferrick's household account. I sign with a clear, quick line. The clerk stamps the page. He gives me a brass tag. The tag is small. It is flat. I clip it to my belt.

I am led to the dive preparation area. The hallway is long. It opens onto a room with metal walls. The floor is cold concrete. Three divers are in line. A tall man stands in the middle. He has a scarred nose. He lifts a heavy harness. A wiry woman stands to the left. Burn marks are on her forearms. She adjusts her goggles. I am at the right. I check my harness. I adjust straps. I listen to the hum of the ventilation.

Lloyd asks me directly, “Ever been here before?” I answer, “No.” The question is simple. I do not elaborate. He nods. Maria glances at the log board. She looks at the list. She confirms. She says, “First time for all of us.” The statement is brief. She does not add anything else.

The three of us split tasks. Lloyd checks the rigging. He lifts cables. He ensures the knots are tight. Maria lights the lanterns. She places them in the rack. She flips the switch. I survey the shoring. I calculate the breach points. I look at the steel beams. I note the angles. I measure the gaps. I mark the spots on my notebook. I keep the notes flat.

We strap on gear. I fasten my harness. I clip the rope. Lloyd secures his harness. Maria fastens hers. I tighten the straps. The weight is distributed. We stand in a line. We check the harnesses. The harnesses are secure. We do not shift. The harnesses hold.

I step onto the descending platform. The platform is made of reinforced steel. It is level. I place my feet. The platform moves. It lowers slowly. The hatch is behind me. I hear the hydraulic pistons. The hatch closes. The hatch is solid metal. It is heavy. I feel the vibration. The platform lowers further.

Steam begins to rise. The steam is thick. The air fills with heat. The steam fogs the view. The tunnel ahead is dark. The steam swirls around the platform. I feel the heat. I listen to the hiss. I hear my own breathing. I hear the hatch thud. I step into the darkened tunnel as the hatch thuds shut behind me.
