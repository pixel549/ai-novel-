I stepped through the stone arch of Stillwell Hydro. The limestone was grey and cold, the outer coat of paint peeling away in long strips. A sharp wind cut across the opening, carrying an iodine scent that clung to the damp air.

At the desk, the clerk sat behind a heavy oak rail with an open ledger. I handed over a signed voucher. He took it, checked the seal against his index, and debited Senator Ferrick’s household account without his knowledge.

The clerk pointed me toward the lounge. On the far wall, a framed board listed the schedule, showing that runs would start in threes. Three separate lines of patients waited along the wall.

Lloyd stepped out of the first line. He was broad-shouldered, with dark short hair and a nose set crooked twice across the bridge. Maria followed him—wiry, thin-built, with dark burn scars running flat across her forearms.

A staff member stepped between us with a clipboard. He assigned Lloyd and Maria to my group, stating it was the prearranged pairing for this run.

Lloyd turned his head toward me. "Never been here before?" he asked.

"No," I said.

He nodded once, accepting the answer, and looked back at the floor.

Maria looked up from her paper slate. She repeated the question: "First time?"

"No," I said.

She recorded my answer on her sheet with a stub of charcoal, confirming the pairing.

Staff escorted us into the preparation room, where two men were setting up the central steam basin, tightening the iron unions and fitting the copper feed pipes. Lloyd went to the doorframe and hefted the heavy iron door onto its pins. Maria moved along the wall brackets, arranging the glass lamps so the light fell square across the work floor. I inspected the harnesses on the timber rack, checking the canvas webbing for fraying, testing the iron buckles, and running my palm down every load-bearing seam.

An attendant held out the consent form on a wooden board. I took the pen and signed my name on the bottom line, noting only that I was entering. I left the lower space blank, stating no reason for wanting a chalice.

The door sealed shut into the jamb. The steam basin began to billow white vapour from the central grate, and the heat pressed hard against my skin as the run started.
