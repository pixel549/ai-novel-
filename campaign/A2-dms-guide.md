# A2 — DM'S GUIDE

*Reader: DM / AI DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md` (read first; this file
does not restate the premise, pillars, or timeline anchor) and `R1b`'s own
DM notes and antagonist writeup, used throughout as the worked example of
how the tone contract plays out at the table. Cites `PLAN.md`'s tone
contract and part template, `DECISIONS.md` D-7/D-8/D-10, and
`novel/canon.md` §§4, 7, and 8 throughout. This is the running-order and
behaviour layer; it does not re-derive the brief, the factions, or any
Part's content — see those files for what the table will actually
encounter.*

## 1. Running Order

**How a Part unfolds.** Follow `PLAN.md`'s template in the order it's
written, but let the table drive the pacing inside it: open with the
premise card and situation (what's true when the party arrives, who holds
what, which clocks are already running), then let the party choose which
keyed location to go to first rather than marching them through the lead
web's routes in the order they're written in the file. A lead web's three
routes exist so the party can find any of them in any order — treat "Route
1" and "Route 3" as equally valid starting points, never a sequence.

**Session shape.** One session is rarely a whole Part. Budget scenes, not
pages: a session that resolves one reveal and sets up the next, with one
social scene, one piece of travel with something happening in it (`S1` —
No Fast Travel), and room for a set piece if the table's heading toward
one, is a full session. Don't try to finish a Part in one sitting just
because the file fits in a single read. If a session ends mid-reveal,
that's fine — pick it back up next time from wherever the party actually
left off, not from the top of the file.

**Between sessions.** Advance clocks (`C2`) by calendar time whether or
not the party touched them that session — this is the mechanism, not an
afterthought, by which reconstruction money keeps not reaching the valley
in time. At the start of the next session, say out loud what moved: a well
got capped, a claimant didn't make it, a ledger got archived. The party
should feel time passing even in sessions that don't visit a clock's own
location.

**Continuity.** `K1` (Campaign Log & Continuity Index) is the eventual home
for this and doesn't exist yet (`PLAN.md`'s work queue). Until it's
written, whoever runs a session should keep a short running note — what
happened, what moved, what's now true that wasn't before — and read it back
before the next session starts, alongside `A1`, this file, and whichever
Part is live. **This matters more for an AI DM than a human one**: a
session run by an AI has no memory of a previous session unless something
written down carries it forward. Treat the absence of `K1` as a real gap to
patch with a plain text note, not a reason to re-derive continuity from
nothing each time.

**Failure states are fine.** `R1b`'s own DM notes say this for Part I
specifically; it's a running-order rule for every Part. A party that misses
a reveal, never finds an NPC, or loses a set piece moves on with that
unresolved — don't manufacture a fourth route or a do-over to rescue a
stalled scene. Check the Part's own reachability note first; if three
routes genuinely weren't enough, that's a sign the Part needs another keyed
site, which is a writing problem for the next Phase 2 session, not
something to patch live at the table.

## 2. The Tone Contract, at the Table

`PLAN.md` states the tone contract once; this is the version to actually
use mid-session, distilled to what changes a DM's next line.

- **Apply the cost test before and after every scene** (canon §8): what did
  this cost, and who paid it — the institution, or a person. If a scene's
  answer is "nothing, nobody," it's not pulling its weight yet.
- **Offer neglect over spectacle.** When a scene could go loud (a chase, a
  confrontation, a fire) or quiet (a filing cabinet, a clerk doing her job,
  a report nobody acted on), put the quiet option on the table as often as
  the loud one — not instead of it, alongside it.
- **Institutions are outlasted or escaped, not defeated.** No antagonist in
  this campaign has a secret boss underneath them to reveal, and no faction
  gets dismantled in one Part. `R1b`'s Corlett is the model: beating him is
  real and it still doesn't clean the valley's water on a timeline Pellick
  can use. Run every antagonist that way.
- **Church menace lives in content, delivered warmly** (canon §4, §3's
  writing rules for Vail, carried into `C2`). No ominous staging, no
  lowered voices, no portentous silence. A Church NPC who is doing
  something monstrous says so cheerfully, in the register of someone
  explaining a schedule.
- **Insight is mechanically true and socially disbelieved** (D-7). If a PC
  has it, what they perceive in-fiction is real; how surface NPCs react to
  them saying so is skepticism, not gaslighting disguised as mechanics.
  Being right once is never proof the table gets to cash in on later.
- **The reception clerk is never statted** (D-6), and neither is any other
  narrative fixture a Part names as one. If a scene needs that character to
  fight, something's gone wrong with the scene, not the character.

## 3. AI-DM Standing Instructions Addendum

Per `DECISIONS.md` D-8, this campaign is built to be run by an AI DM as
readily as a small human-run table. The rules above apply to both; this
section is only the extra discipline an AI DM needs that a human DM
mostly supplies by instinct.

- **Read before running.** At the start of a session: `A1`, this file, the
  live Part's files (overview + lead web/antagonist), `C2` for whichever
  clocks are in play, and whatever running note stands in for `K1` (see §1).
  Do not run a session from the live Part's file alone — the tone contract
  and the cross-references are load-bearing, not background reading.
- **Never invent a fact that contradicts `source-bible.md`, `novel/canon.md`,
  or an existing campaign file.** When a player asks something the files
  don't answer, check `source-bible.md` and `hooks.md` first. If it's
  genuinely undecided, make the same kind of defensible, in-register call
  a Phase 2 writing session would make — and say so to the table as a
  DM call, the way `DECISIONS.md`'s "session defaults" are logged for
  Jake, rather than presenting an invention as something the files already
  said. Write it down in the running note so it's a fact from then on, not
  a different answer next session.
- **Honour a telegraphed tell exactly, every time.** `R1b`'s Corlett (a
  true, irrelevant figure first, then a polite decline, then standing up
  to end the conversation) is the model: a tell is a promise to the table
  about how a character behaves, and breaking it to serve a scene is
  always the wrong call, however convenient. The same standard applies to
  any tell a later Part writes.
- **Generate stats live, archetype and tier only.** No `campaign/system/`
  ruleset exists yet (Q-1). Until it does, never invent a specific number —
  a DC, a damage die, an attribute score — mark it `(SYS?)` the way every
  campaign file already does, and resolve it with the lightest reasonable
  adjudication (narrative success/failure, or a flat coin-flip roll if the
  table wants dice) rather than quietly deciding on a number that will
  later contradict the real ruleset.
- **Preserve player agency over the lead web.** A Part's three routes per
  reveal are the DM's guarantee that *a* route exists, not a script. If
  players find or invent a fourth way to the same reveal that's consistent
  with the files, let it work. If they ignore all three and the reveal
  stalls, that's a failure state (§1) — don't railroad them back onto a
  route to avoid it.
- **Don't resolve the who-knows-what gaps or the novel's cameo limits.**
  Canon §6's permanent gaps (Lloyd's second arm, his five weeks, Anse
  Duley's fate as Edie knows it) stay closed regardless of what a PC
  investigates. Until `I1` exists, Lloyd, Maria, and Edie appear only as
  background mentions, per `A1`'s cameo rules — an AI DM asked to voice one
  of them in a full scene should decline and offer the background-mention
  version instead, the same way it would decline inventing a number the
  ruleset hasn't supplied yet.
- **When a player decision would retcon something fixed in `A1`'s timeline
  anchor,** say so plainly rather than quietly allowing it — "Crane is
  established dead before the campaign opens; that can't be undone" — the
  same way a rules question gets a straight answer instead of an
  improvised yes.

## 4. Session Zero

Run this before Part I, with the players, not just the DM. Four items:

1. **Pitch.** Share `A1`'s premise and one-line hook, the timeline anchor's
   headline facts (Crane dead, water schedule moved, wells being capped,
   the Church untouched), and campaign pillars 1–3 in plain language. Make
   clear up front that this is investigation-first, that institutions don't
   get toppled, and that the novel's three leads are history the party
   will bump into, not characters they'll play alongside or rescue.
2. **Character creation and hooks.** Per `A1`, PCs are new, ordinary people
   pulled in by something that could happen to anyone in this
   Commonwealth — a debt, a missing relative, a job that paid too well, a
   posting nobody wanted. Have each player name their hook in one sentence
   before mechanics; the mechanical build (six attributes, chargen) waits
   on `campaign/system/` (Q-1) and should be the competent-adult-with-a-
   trade placeholder `A1` describes until then.
3. **Lines and veils.** See §5 below — do this as its own conversation, not
   a rushed last five minutes.
4. **Table logistics.** Agree how long a session runs, how clocks and
   continuity get tracked between sessions (§1 — who keeps the running
   note), and that failure states are a feature, not a loss condition the
   table needs to be warned about mid-game.

## 5. Lines and Veils

A **line** is content the table agrees never appears on screen at all. A
**veil** is content that can happen but is not shown — acknowledged, then
cut away from. Ask about each item below specifically; don't rely on a
single "anything too dark?" question, because the source material's
register makes some of this easy to undersell until it's actually at the
table.

Likely to surface, given what this campaign is built from:

- **Non-consensual medical and bodily experimentation** (the Fourth Mercy —
  canon §4). The novel's own register treats this as catalogued, not
  lurid — case files and registers over gore (canon §7) — and that's the
  campaign's default regardless of what the table sets. Ask anyway; "we're
  fine with it as the novel wrote it" and "fine with the concept, not the
  specifics" are different lines.
- **Institutionalisation and involuntary confinement** (Lloyd's five weeks,
  canon §1 — a closed gap, never dramatised, but referenced).
- **War trauma and mass-casualty backstory**, including a character
  learning a superior deliberately killed subordinates to end a crisis
  (Edie's history, canon §1).
- **Child illness and death, and family grief** (the Indemnity Mortality
  Curve, Mirrin Tope's grandson — `C2`, `R1a`). This Part's named claimants
  are, by design, already past saving by the time the party meets them.
- **Body horror and eldritch transformation** (the Salts, mutation, any
  thin site — `C3`, `S1`). Confined to thin sites on the surface, but real
  when it happens.
- **Compulsion and addiction-like effects** (the Sweetwater double meaning,
  `R1b` — a pleasant, controlled dose of the same substance that causes a
  wasting disease elsewhere).
- **Institutional neglect and poverty** as ambient texture across every
  Part, not an occasional topic (`A1` pillar 5, canon §5).

**Default stance if nothing else is agreed:** table-time depiction follows
canon §7's own restraint — horror is catalogued, not lurid, violence and
its aftermath are handled through what it costs rather than through
spectacle — whatever the table's own lines and veils turn out to be. That
restraint is a floor, not a substitute for having the conversation: the
novel could rely on authorial control over exactly how far a scene went: an
improvised table or an AI DM cannot assume the same restraint will hold
without being told where the table's actual limits are.
