# C2 — FACTIONS & CLOCKS

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md` and `C1-gazetteer.md` — read
both first; this file does not restate the premise, the timeline anchor, or
the regions. Cites `source-bible.md` → Factions/Places, `hooks.md`, and
`novel/canon.md` §§4–5 throughout. Mechanics are archetype and tier only —
no `campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet (Q-1) — anything that would need a
hard number is marked `(SYS?)`.*

## How to use this file

Six factions, each with what it wants, what it has, how it moves, and where
it meets the party. Six clocks underneath, each tied to one or more
factions above. A clock advances in play — by calendar time, by what the
party does or fails to do, or both — and the DM should move it visibly
between sessions rather than springing its completion unannounced. None of
the six is built to resolve cleanly; per `A1`'s pillars, institutions are
outlasted or escaped, not defeated, and a clock completing should cost
someone something rather than close a plot off tidily.

## Factions

### The Church of the Mercies

**Wants:** the Fourth Mercy to work, eventually, after sixty years of
failure it still calls progress. Nothing else the Church does is anything
but infrastructure for that one goal. **Has:** the three public Mercies
(bread, shelter, absolution) running everywhere it operates, genuine
popularity, state adjacency, and the Sanctum at Carrowgate — untouched
across the novel and still untouched a year later (`DECISIONS.md` D-1).
**How it moves:** bread and shelter identify the surplus, people nobody
will miss; absolution collects them. When exposed, it runs a proven
sequence rather than panicking (`hooks.md` #5, the Sanctum's crisis-PR
playbook — see Clock 3 below): grief offered first so nothing is left to
accuse, blame narrowed to names furthest from the top, a restitution figure
large enough that disputing it looks like disputing charity. **Reach:**
unconfirmed on the page, but worth holding open at the table — Vail's
unexplained speed to the Ascension House yard (`hooks.md` #8) is either a
personal ability never otherwise shown or evidence the Sanctum sees further
than it admits to; the DM can run either reading without contradicting
anything, and switching between them session to session is fine. **Where
it meets the party:** anyone the party is trying to find, protect, or avenge
is exactly the population bread and shelter are built to notice first.

### Ferrick's Ethics Sub-Panel

**Wants:** the water tested and the wells capped before anyone else dies of
it — sincere, and two months late by the time Maria saw the second report
(`source-bible.md`). **Has:** Senator Ferrick's household in Vantry, a
staff that includes Edie running security, an iron cabinet holding the real
Vantry registers, and just enough post-Crane political will to move the
capping schedule forward. **How it moves:** town by town, ahead of frost,
underfunded and incomplete — the Capping Clock below is this faction's
clock. It does not move fast enough for every village (`C1` names Pellick
as the one likeliest to be reached too late) and the sub-panel knows it.
**Where it meets the party:** the single faction in this file that will
take help gladly and never has enough hands — contracts, escorts, testing
runs, and the occasional favour owed back.

### The Valley Trust (Larrow Consolidated Works, post-Crane)

Crane owned the works through two intermediaries and never put his own name
on the paper. After his death those same two intermediaries kept the works
running under a plainer name — no founder to rally around, no name worth
burning down. **Wants:** the mine and Ammet Ford's division profitable,
the buried groundwater survey staying buried, and just enough visible
cooperation with Ferrick's capping schedule that nobody asks why the survey
was never reopened. **Has:** the mine, the Ammet Ford showpiece, company
ledgers under whatever name now heads them, and the Larrow Indemnity's
seventh-year payout clause, still running on its own schedule
(`source-bible.md`). **How it moves:** quiet management, not dramatic —
cooperate on the parts that cost little (capping, the parts Ferrick would
win eventually anyway) and stonewall the parts that would cost everything
(the survey, the Indemnity's timing, whoever still profits from Ammet
Ford's showpiece well). **Where it meets the party:** company offices,
Indemnity paperwork, and anyone still employed at the works who remembers
too much. *(Named here as a session default per RUNBOOK's Phase 2
instructions — `DECISIONS.md` D-12. Jake can overturn it.)*

### Hydro operators

**Wants:** paying customers and no trouble. Each hydro is a private
business, not a movement — there is no guild, no central office, and that
absence is itself the fact: Arcana and the Salts are uncommon, unregulated,
and run exactly as unevenly as that implies. **Has:** Stillwell, confirmed
on the page, plus a small handful of further hydros assumed to exist
elsewhere to supply the Church's industrial use (`C1`'s Hydros entry,
`DECISIONS.md` D-11) — none named or placed below the Part level. **How it
moves:** the Stillwell genre repeated with variation — peeling
respectability, batches of three, a numbered token, a reception clerk who
is never statted (D-6) — some hydros selling only the nerve-cure trade,
others (not yet identified) quietly supplying the Fourth Mercy's brine
tanks. Which is which is a Part-level decision, not fixed here. **Where it
meets the party:** the first place anyone goes to take the salts
deliberately, and the first place a DM can seed a rumour that a specific
hydro's supply runs somewhere the owner won't discuss.

### The dead and their pests

Not an organisation; a standing pressure. The Attrition ended nineteen
months before ch. 1 and killed enough people in enough places that undead
and lesser horrors are a pest problem, not an apocalypse (canon §5). **Has
no wants** in the faction sense — it is weather, not an actor. **How it
moves:** worse where the ground is disturbed — mining, construction, a
thin site cracked open — and otherwise dormant. Red Redoubt, the camouflet
site that deafened Edie, is the one named example, backstory only, no
on-page location detail beyond the event (`source-bible.md`). **Where it
meets the party:** anywhere work breaks old ground, and especially the
Larrow Valley's mine, now under the Valley Trust's quieter management and
presumably still digging.

### Arcana practitioners

Real, uncommon, mostly weak, and unregulated — no guild, no registry, no
licence (canon §5). That is the whole faction entry for most of them: a
healer in a market town, a man who can light a candle without a match,
nothing anyone official has ever written down. **What's different here:**
the one place practitioners' bloodlines get tracked is the Fourth Mercy's
stud book at Ascension House, going back decades, built without most of
its subjects' knowledge or consent (`source-bible.md` → Places, Fourth
Mercy facility layout). An unusually capable practitioner risks exactly
that attention — recruitment into the "ascension" programme dressed as
charity, or worse, simple disappearance into absolution's intake. **Where
it meets the party:** any PC with native Arcana, or any NPC ally who has
some, should feel being noticed as a cost, not a flex — the opposite of how
the ability reads at a table that hasn't internalised this.

## Clocks

Each clock starts wherever the DM's current session sits in the timeline
(`A1`'s anchor: roughly a year after ch. 25) and advances from there. None
is a bomb with a fixed fuse; treat completion as a state change the table
has to live with, not a loss condition.

**1. The Capping Clock** *(Ferrick's sub-panel)* — tracks how many of the
twelve Larrow villages have clean water. Advances with calendar time
(slowly, town by town, ahead of frost) and with direct party help (faster)
or sabotage (stalled, or reversed if the Valley Trust quietly recontaminates
a capped well to protect the survey). At completion, the valley's water is
clean and the Sweetwater's supply cut off from this source — but the
disease's human cost up to that point does not undo, and other hydros or a
second source (per `hooks.md` #1, if the DM runs that thread) can keep the
double meaning alive even after the valley's own wells are capped.

**2. The Indemnity Mortality Curve** *(the Valley Trust)* — tracks named
claimants against the seventh-year payout clause, timed from the start to
Crane's own buried mortality curve. Advances on calendar time alone; the
party cannot speed or slow it, only witness it and choose what to do with
what they learn. At any checkpoint, a given claimant has either died
before collecting or is about to — a standing source of named NPCs whose
stories are already finished before the party arrives, the clearest
reusable instance of "the money exists, it does not reach the valleys."

**3. The Press Cycle** *(the Church / the Vantry broadsheet)* — starts
cold and spins up whenever the party causes, or nearly causes, a public
exposure. Eleven days of a single story dominating, matching ch. 18's
precedent exactly (`source-bible.md`), during which the Church runs its
crisis sequence (grief, narrowed blame, a restitution figure). At day
eleven the story curdles into an authenticity dispute regardless of how
solid the evidence was, and public attention moves on — a hard limit on
how long any exposure the party manages stays useful as leverage, by
design.

**4. The Hydro Network Map** *(hydro operators)* — tracks how many of the
assumed further hydros (`DECISIONS.md` D-11) the party has actually found
and identified by name. Starts at one (Stillwell; everything else is still
a rumour) and advances only through play — asking the right questions,
following a supply ledger, tailing a cart. Nothing forces this clock
forward on its own. At each new hydro found, the DM decides on the spot
whether it is a plain nerve-cure business or a quiet link in the Fourth
Mercy's supply chain — see hydro operators above — and that answer should
differ hydro to hydro rather than resolving into one tidy network.

**5. The Pest Pressure Clock** *(the dead and their pests)* — regional,
one per active region, tracking how disturbed the Attrition-scarred ground
there is. Advances when mining, construction, or a thin site breaks new
ground, and decays slowly when nothing disturbs it. At high pressure,
random-encounter rolls (see `J1`, not yet written) should draw from a
worse tier `(SYS?)` than at low pressure — this is weather, not a villain,
and should never resolve into a boss fight; it just gets worse where people
keep digging, which in the Larrow Valley is constantly.

**6. The Valley Trust's Consolidation** *(the Valley Trust)* — tracks how
thoroughly the post-Crane management has tightened its grip: buying out
smaller claims, replacing staff who remember too much, discouraging anyone
from reopening the Indemnity's underlying survey. Advances on calendar time
and faster whenever the party pokes at the survey or the Indemnity Office
without covering their tracks. At completion, the survey becomes
practically unreachable through ordinary channels — not destroyed, per
canon's pattern of evidence surviving while mattering less and less
(`hooks.md` #3), just buried one administrative layer deeper than the
party can dig through without a specific, costly way in.

## DM notes

**Nothing here is a villain roster.** Every faction above wants something
ordinary — money, water, safety, an unbothered institution — and none of
them is run by a mastermind twirling a moustache; the Church's menace in
particular lives in what it says, delivered warmly, same as in the novel
(`PLAN.md`'s tone contract). Play the Valley Trust's administrator, any
hydro proprietor, or Ferrick himself as sincere people doing a job, not as
disguised evil.

**Clocks are visible pressure, not a countdown to a cutscene.** Tell the
table where a clock sits when it's plausible their characters would know —
a newspaper date, a capped well they walk past, a claimant who didn't make
it. Let a clock complete off-page between sessions if nothing in play
touched it; that is itself the point the novel makes about reconstruction
money not reaching the valleys in time.

**Open threads this file deliberately leaves alone:** which specific
further hydros exist and whether any one of them supplies the Fourth
Mercy directly (Clock 4 exists to answer this through play, not in advance);
who specifically administers the Valley Trust day to day (inventable at
the Part level); and whether Vail's reach is personal or institutional
(deliberately left open above). None of these need deciding before R1a.
