# G3 — BESTIARY ADDITIONS, PART III

*Reader: DM. Builds on `R3a-carrowgate-overview.md` and `R3b-carrowgate-
leads-antagonist.md` §5 (Encounters) and §6 (Set Pieces) — read both first;
this file does not restate the encounters list or the set pieces, it
supplies the archetype write-ups those sections name and leave undetailed.
Cites `R3a`, `R3b`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`, `G1-bestiary-part-1.md`,
`G2-bestiary-part-2.md`, `source-bible.md`, `hooks.md`, and `novel/canon.md`
§§4/5/8 throughout. No `campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet (Q-1); every
entry below is behaviour and tells, never a stat block — numbers are
generated live at the table, archetype and tier only, marked `(SYS?)` per
`A2`'s standing instruction.*

## How to use this file

Same job `G1` and `G2` did for Regions I and II, now for Region III: the
unnamed or lightly-named faces a lead-web route needs consistent behaviour
for, and the capital's own instance of the Attrition-scarred ground (`C2`'s
"the dead and their pests" faction entry). Region III has no Fourth Mercy
site and no thin site (`R3a`, `R3b` §7, `C3`), so — unlike `G2` — this file
adds no new category for either; see "Fourth Mercy Products" below for why
that's a deliberate absence, not an oversight. Read an entry once, before
the session it's needed in; none of these carry a scene the way Sorrel,
Pell, or Vane do in `R3a`/`R3b` — this file supplies the texture around
them, not the leads.

**Session default — D-35 (`DECISIONS.md`).** Where `R3a` and `R3b` name a
category (the Mercy House's sister or brother, a Sanctum clerk or page, the
retained inquiry agent behind the Carpet Men's own surveillance, an optional
boarding-district Arcana spark, the Carrowgate instance of the Pest Pressure
Clock) without building it out, this file invents the behaviour and tells
needed to run it — logged here rather than decided silently, and
overturnable the same way `D-18` and `D-33` were for Regions I and II.

## People

### The Sanctum Clerk or Page

Covers every unnamed Sanctum-adjacent face a scene needs — the clerk who
answers the door, the page running a message between offices, whoever a
table asks about Crane's case before or instead of reaching Vail herself
(`R3a`'s own restraint on running her as a speaking NPC at all).

**Behaviour.** Calm, courteous, and entirely unbothered by the question —
per `R3b`'s D-23, the Sanctum has no operational stake in Crane's death or
the buried survey, and nobody here is performing that innocence. Asked
directly, says plainly that the matter is concluded and means it; this is
the institutional truth as the Sanctum itself understands it, not evasion
dressed as calm. Will not speculate past what they've been told, and
haven't been told to lie about anything because there's nothing here to
lie about.

**Tells.** Answers in the same unhurried register canon §3 sets for Vail
herself, scaled down — no lowered voice, no meaningful pause, nothing that
should read as sinister on sight (per the tone contract, Church menace
lives in content, never staging). Will, unprompted, mention the restitution
payment as a simple matter of record, the same way Sorrel discusses an
expedited filing (`R3b` Reveal B Route 1) — an institutional habit of
treating its own generosity as unremarkable, not a boast and not a
confession.

**Where this matters.** The single easiest way to reinforce `R3b`'s own
warning against playing the restitution as a twist: this archetype should
leave a table feeling like they asked a closed door a question and got a
straight, uninteresting answer, because that's what actually happened.
Never give this NPC a reason to be nervous — there isn't one.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

### The Capital Mercy Sister or Brother (the Sanctum's Shadow)

Covers the unnamed face at the Mercy House literally in the Sanctum's own
shadow (`R3a`) — the bread queue, the shelter roll, the same mechanism
`G1`'s Brother Aldous and `G2`'s Vantry Mercy sister or brother both run,
now closest of any Part to headquarters itself.

**Behaviour.** Sincere and genuinely moved by the work, same discipline
`G1`/`G2` set — most of the Church's people here, same as everywhere else
in the book, genuinely don't know what the fourth Mercy is. Per `R3a`'s own
note, this queue starts earlier than any other Mercy House in the
campaign, precisely because the Sanctum's presence draws people who've
already exhausted every other door; this archetype should read that
pressure as a fact about the city, not as something sinister about the
building next door.

**Tells.** A shelter roll crossed through for the same ordinary mix of
reasons `R2a` already names for Vantry (found work, nothing anyone wrote
down); genuinely brightens at a returning face, same as `G2`'s own version
of this archetype — the family resemblance between all three Mercy-House
entries (`G1`, `G2`, this one) is deliberate, not a reused description
without thought.

**Where this matters.** Region III's clearest demonstration that the
Church's charity and its menace share an address — this time literally
next door to each other rather than a city apart. Any horror a table finds
here lives several administrative layers back, same restraint `R3a`
itself sets; this archetype is not where that horror lives.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

### The Retained Watcher (the Carpet Men's own kind)

Covers `R3b` §5's Pursuit encounter — "a retained inquiry agent, paid by
the day, professionally unremarkable, reporting to Pell rather than
confronting anyone" — and the same class of hired, legitimately-covered
surveillance behind the "Carpet Men" set piece (`R3b` §6): the contracted
cleaning crew retained by the solicitor's firm that keeps turning up
wherever the party does. One archetype, two covers; a DM can dress it as
either depending on where the scene needs the party to notice it.

**Behaviour.** Never follows closer than a public street allows, never
confronts, never seems to be watching anything in particular — the
encounter, per `R3b`'s own framing, is entirely retrospective: the party
realising, later, how precisely Pell already knew what they'd been asking.
Whoever's wearing this cover today has an entirely ordinary, legitimate job
to point to if challenged, and genuinely is doing that job alongside the
watching — the two are never separable from the outside, which is the
entire point `R3b` makes with it.

**Tells.** None in the scene itself, by design — same discipline `G2`'s
Church-Adjacent Watcher set for Wick's own foreknowledge. The only tell is
retrospective: a session-day schedule that happens to put this crew
wherever the party was the day before, an inquiry agent's invoice that
surfaces later with dates that line up too neatly. A DM who gives this
archetype a meaningful glance or a reason to seem suspicious in the moment
has written it wrong.

**Where this matters.** Quieter and better-funded than Region I's company
runner or Region II's Church-adjacent gossip (`R3b`'s own comparison) —
this is what money buys at the capital instead of menace. Use it to make
the party feel surveilled after the fact, never during.

`(SYS?)` tier: non-combat; properly a Social/Pursuit hybrid per `PLAN.md`'s
taxonomy, filed here under Pursuit to match `R3b` §5.

### The Boarding-District Reader (optional)

Covers `R3b` §5's optional Arcana encounter — "a low-tier, unregistered
practitioner somewhere in the boarding district... worth a scene only to
dramatise `C2`'s point that being noticed here is a cost" — the Region III
instance of the archetype `G1` and `G2` each built their own version of
for Regions I and II.

**Behaviour.** Keeps the gift smaller and better-hidden than either
earlier region's version needed to, same escalating caution `G2`'s
Lodging-Row Spark showed over `G1`'s own practitioner — the capital's own
Indemnity Office files everything it touches, and this person has drawn
the plain, correct conclusion that a city built on paperwork is a bad place
to be noticed for anything unregistered. A clerk who reads a page faster
than anyone should; a tenant at the Waiting House who lights a stove
without a match and immediately turns to see who saw.

**Tells.** Minimises on instinct, not performance — stops mid-gesture
rather than finishing it if someone looks over; deflects "where did you
get that" the same uncertain way Maria's own repertoire works (canon §2),
scaled down and more guarded than either `G1`'s or `G2`'s version.

**Where this matters.** Same restraint `G1` and `G2` both held: skip
entirely if no PC has the hook, and never let a scene leave this person
better off for having been found out, even by a sympathetic party. The
Waiting House (`R3a`) is this archetype's natural home if a table wants a
specific location for it.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

## Pest-Class Horrors (the Attrition-scarred ground)

Per `C2`, `G1`, and `G2`: weather, not villains, with no wants in the
faction sense, worse wherever disturbed and otherwise dormant, never
escalating into a boss fight. Region I's version lived in the mine's older
workings; Region II's lived in Vantry's foundations and culverts. Region
III's lives wherever the capital's own paved, surveyed, maintained ground
still has an unpaved memory under it — an old cellar, an unused crypt under
a Mercy House, a stretch of ground the Senate's own groundskeepers avoid
without saying why (`R3a`).

### The Kept-Ground Lingerer

The same low-tier archetype `G1` built as the Collapsed-Tunnel Lingerer and
`G2` already re-dressed once for Vantry's Rail-Cut Lingerer — re-dressed a
third time here for Carrowgate's own ground, per `G1`'s own instruction
that a region doesn't need two pest archetypes to make the same clock
legible. This is the Carrowgate instance of the Pest Pressure Clock
(`R3a`; `C2`), not a new species, and its name is the joke `R3a` already
set up: the capital's ground is "kept" in both senses, maintained and
quietly avoided.

**Behaviour.** Identical to its two predecessors: doesn't hunt, doesn't
pursue past the cellar, crypt, or unmarked stretch it's bound to,
investigates sluggishly rather than attacking, and loses interest within
minutes of a party backing off. No plan, no grievance — a pest problem, not
a haunting (canon §5), exactly as `G1` and `G2` both held.

**Tells (re-dressed for Carrowgate).** Per `R3a`'s own texture: fewer
places to be bad in means worse wherever it is. A crypt under a Mercy House
that the sisters quietly stopped using for storage without ever explaining
why; a stretch of the Senate's own grounds that the groundskeepers tend
from a respectful distance, never quite stepping onto it; an old cellar
whose cold outlasts the building's own furnace by a margin nobody's
measured on paper.

**Function in the Part.** Per `R3a`'s own instruction, this clock "has
fewer places to be bad in Carrowgate and is correspondingly worse wherever
it is" — a DM running a scene near one of these three sites can use this
without inventing a second reason for anyone to avoid the ground. It
doesn't touch the Trust, the Sanctum, or the Senate's own business; it's
weather, same as its two predecessors, concentrated rather than spread.

`(SYS?)` tier: low; a contained scene, never a chase across the Senate
hill, per `C2`'s own instruction that this clock never resolves into a
boss fight.

## Fourth Mercy Products

**None.** Per `DECISIONS.md` D-23, the Sanctum has no operational stake in
this Part's lead web, and `R3b` §5 states this plainly: "the Sanctum is
clean in this file, deliberately and completely — no remnant, no echo,
nothing for a DM to half-confirm the way `R2b` left the tank-four
possibility open for Region II." Region III's horror, if a table ever finds
one, is administrative — Pell's tell, Vane's tell, the Carpet Men's own
unremarkable patience — never eldritch. This file does not invent a
category here the way `G2` did for Region II; doing so would reopen a
question `R3a` and `R3b` both close on purpose. If a future region needs a
genuine Fourth Mercy or Salts-adjacent bestiary entry, it belongs to `G4`
or `G5`, not here.

## DM Notes

**The Retained Watcher and the Kept-Ground Lingerer are this Part's only
two bestiary entries for a reason.** Region III's own restraint — no
Fourth Mercy connection, no thin site, institutional menace over eldritch
menace — means this file stays shorter than `G1` or `G2` on purpose. Don't
pad it; a DM who wants more texture for Carrowgate should reach for `R3a`'s
and `R3b`'s own NPCs (Sorrel, Caddock Lowe, Pell, Vane) before inventing a
new archetype here.

**Never let the Sanctum Clerk or the Capital Mercy sibling read as
nervous.** Both archetypes exist specifically to demonstrate that the
Church has nothing to hide in this Part — per `R3b`'s own warning, a DM who
plays either as evasive or uneasy has written the Sanctum wrong in exactly
the way canon §3's prose rules warn against for Vail specifically.

**None of this needs a fight to matter.** Per `A1` pillar 1 and the cost
test (canon §8): a clerk who answers plainly, a sister who brightens at a
returning face, a Watcher who's never actually there when you look, a
Lingerer that loses interest. Reach for combat because the table wants it
that session, not because an entry exists and needs using.

**How the novel's three appear.** Nothing in this file changes `R3a`'s own,
most restrictive note yet: Lloyd and Maria stay rumour and resemblance
only, and none of the archetypes above are a vehicle for giving either of
them a scene ahead of `I1`'s own rules. Edie does not appear in this Part
at all (`R3a`/`R3b`).
