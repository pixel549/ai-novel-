## 4. The Lead Web

Three reveals. Per `PLAN.md`'s template, each is reachable at least three
ways — no single refused request, missed roll, or skipped location should
stall the Part.

### Reveal A — more brine than the business can account for

Greymere Hydro's own manifest — what ships, how much, and to where — is the
single most direct piece of hard evidence this Part has. It shows a steady,
modest nerve-cure trade and, laid alongside it, a second column of brine and
reagent shipments several times the size the visible business could ever
use, routed by the hydro's own service cart rather than by rail, always on
a day that lines up with the House's twice-weekly wagon.

- **Route 1 — Rufus Pledge's arrivals board, earned rather than bought.**
  Per `R4a`'s own setup, a party that's taken the time to earn Pledge's
  trust (not his fear) gets Greymere's cart traffic cross-checked against
  his own thirty-years handwritten log for free — which carts went out,
  how often, and on what schedule, enough to show the pattern without
  needing the manifest itself.
- **Route 2 — Josiah Rede, for a price.** Greymere's proprietor (named this
  session) has kept the business solvent for years on an arrangement
  nobody local asks about, and he is not precious about who else profits
  from asking — a fair sum, a favour, or something he actually needs
  (`S1`'s crowns-and-favours economy, `(SYS?)` for the exact figure) gets
  the manifest itself, shown across his own counter, no theft and no
  break-in required. He does not volunteer what the extra brine is *for*;
  he answers only what's asked.
- **Route 3 — Lissom Fen's own pattern, noticed sideways.** A turf cutter
  or smallholder who's lived alongside the House's twice-weekly wagon for
  years can describe, without realising its significance, that the wagon
  sometimes runs a day early or late, and that those are always the same
  days Greymere's own cart track shows fresh ruts. Nobody in the fen has
  ever had a reason to put the two together; the party is the first to ask.

### Reveal B — the undercroft is not storage

The House of Quiet Rest keeps a locked wing the public wards don't open
onto, and what's behind it is not paperwork or supplies — it's where the
extra brine goes, and it is doing, quietly and on a far smaller scale,
exactly the kind of work Ascension House did openly before ch. 18 ended it.

- **Route 1 — Teyla Dunmore, directly.** Corporal Emrys Dunmore, her
  brother, was admitted to the House eight months ago "for his nerves" on
  a recommendation from a Church almoner in Vantry, and nobody has heard
  from him since; letters she's sent go unanswered, and a visit gets her
  turned away at the gate with real, practised kindness and no actual
  information. She is not hunting a conspiracy — she wants a straight
  answer, exactly as `R4a` describes, and will say so plainly to anyone who
  asks why she keeps coming back.
- **Route 2 — Sister Prue Anstey, unknowingly.** Anstey runs the public
  face of the House and has never been shown the undercroft and never
  asked — the same ordinary not-knowing `C2`'s Church entry assumes for
  most of its people. Asked directly, she confirms, sincerely and without
  suspicion, that there's a locked door on the east wing she's never had
  reason to open, that a handful of "specialist" staff come and go on a
  schedule she doesn't keep, and that Brother Ness handles "the difficult
  cases personally" — true as far as she knows it, and the clearest
  confirmation this reveal has that doesn't require anyone to lie.
- **Route 3 — cross-reading against Region II.** A party that has already
  read Vantry's own intake register or discharge files (`R2a`/`R2b`,
  `hooks.md` #3) recognises "went in for his nerves" as the exact shape of
  euphemism the stolen register's own entries used, and that a cold case
  file ending "result negative, subject retained" (`source-bible.md` →
  Places, Fourth Mercy facility layout) describes precisely what's happened
  to men like Dunmore before it happens again here. This route rewards
  continuity rather than requiring it; Routes 1–2 reach the same place on
  their own.

### Reveal C — who runs it, and how carefully

Brother Corwin Ness oversees the undercroft personally, answers to nobody
on-site, and has built this site's entire operating discipline around never
repeating Ascension House's single worst mistake: letting too many ordinary
people notice something was wrong.

- **Route 1 — Ness himself, directly.** A scene inside the House, arranged
  through Anstey or simply by asking for "whoever handles the difficult
  cases." Built around his tell (below) — he never refuses a question and
  never answers one that would require admitting consent was never asked.
- **Route 2 — the Night Siding's own paper trail.** Pledge's handwritten
  arrivals log, already cross-checked for Reveal A, shows one name arriving
  on the branch line more often than anyone else's business out here could
  explain, always a day before one of the "specialist" staff Anstey
  mentioned comes through Lissom Fen. Pledge doesn't know the name means
  anything; he's simply logged it, the same way he's logged everything else
  for thirty years.
- **Route 3 — up the chain from Lissom Fen's own noticeboard.** The parish
  noticeboard `R4a` describes carries more names of men who "went in" than
  names of men who came back out; a party that's already connected one name
  (Dunmore's) to Ness through Routes 1–2 can work the noticeboard backward
  and find that every name on it traces to the same handful of Church
  almoners who made the original recommendation — none of them Ness
  himself, all of them, per `C2`'s own mechanism, exactly the kind of
  ordinary machinery bread and shelter were always built to run.

## 5. Encounters

Archetype and tier only; the real ruleset generates stat blocks live.

- **Social.** The default register, same discipline as every earlier Part.
  Pledge's trust, Rede's price, Anstey's sincerity, Teyla Dunmore's plain
  grief, the noticeboard as texture before it's a clue. Most essential
  information in Region IV is won here, not taken.
- **Pursuit.** Quieter than any earlier Part's, because there is less to
  notice out here with: no retained agent, no cleaning crew on contract,
  just Ness's own careful habit of asking Anstey, lightly and without
  suspicion in his own voice, who's been by the House this week. A party
  that's pushed hard on Reveal B or C should feel, a session later, that
  their own names have started coming up in that same light, unalarmed way
  — never a confrontation, just the first sign the Warden's attention has
  turned toward them.
- **Arcana.** Optional, same restraint `R1b`/`R2b`/`R3b` all used — a
  smallholder or turf cutter with a minor, unregistered gift, worth a scene
  only to dramatise `C2`'s point that being noticed here is a cost. Skip if
  no PC has the hook.
- **Church operatives.** Sister Prue Anstey, genuinely charitable and
  genuinely unaware, same register as every Mercy sister or brother this
  campaign has shown so far. The House's "specialist" staff, archetype only
  (`G4`, not yet written) — tired, procedural, not cruel on sight, the
  menace living entirely in what they're doing rather than how they look
  doing it, per canon §3's rule for Vail extended here.
- **Pest-class horrors.** The Fen Line's own instance of the Pest Pressure
  Clock (`R4a`), diffuse rather than concentrated. `(SYS?)` for the actual
  stat block (`G4`'s job); archetype only here, same as `R4a` already sets
  out.
- **Fourth Mercy products.** The undercroft's own early-stage work —
  per D-25 call 1, immersion and the first stages of growing, not a
  finished homunculus. Archetype only (`G4`'s job); played per canon §7,
  catalogued rather than lurid — a tank log's cold entry does more work at
  this table than a description of what's actually in the tank, and should
  be preferred to one.
- **Salts leak.** None, ambient, outside a thin-site visit — see §7 below.
  Nothing in the undercroft's own ordinary operation requires a PC to go
  under personally; that stays `T3`'s province.

## 6. Set Pieces and Antagonist

### Set piece — The Arrivals Board

A scene, not a trap, same shape as `R3b`'s Closed File. The party sits with
Rufus Pledge over his own thirty years of handwritten logs and finds the
pattern themselves — no lock to pick, no guard to talk past, just a patient
man's lifelong habit laid out across a counter. The telegraphed tell to
honour exactly: Pledge never asks why the party wants this, and never
volunteers a conclusion of his own. If a DM has him draw the connection
aloud before the party does, this scene is written wrong — his value is the
record, not the insight.

### Set piece — What the Wagon Carries

Once the party has connected the House's own schedule to Greymere's cart
traffic (Reveal A or B), staking out the twice-weekly wagon between Lissom
Fen and the House pays off on exactly the wrong day to watch comfortably: a
man is moved, under a blanket and a believable story about a worsening
case, from the public ward into the east wing — Anstey herself may be the
one walking him there, sincerely believing the story she's been given about
where he's going and why. Nobody here is threatened, chased, or fought.
The entire effect is the party watching an institution's ordinary machinery
do, in front of them, exactly what Reveal B already told them it does —
and realising that stopping this one transfer changes nothing about next
week's.

### The Antagonist — Brother Corwin Ness, "the Warden"

**Gimmick.** Ness is not a mastermind and should never read as one, same
warning `C2`'s DM notes gave for every antagonist this campaign has built.
He did not invent the undercroft's work, did not choose Lissom Fen, and
did not write the doctrine that justifies it — he inherited an assignment
the Church gave him after ch. 18 taught it that confidence gets people
burned, and he has spent every day since treating "careful" as a moral
virtue in its own right. He believes, sincerely and without performance,
that the men brought to him have already been failed by everyone else —
the war, their families, a Commonwealth with no other door for them — and
that what happens in the undercroft is the only attempt anyone is still
making on their behalf. That belief is the entire horror this file asks a
table to sit with; it is not a lie he tells himself, it is the lie the
whole institution tells itself, spoken here by one specific, calm man.

**Telegraphed tells (honour these exactly, every time).** Asked a direct
question about the undercroft, a missing resident, or what "the difficult
cases" means, Ness never refuses and never lies — he answers everything
except the one question that would require him to admit nobody asked the
men's consent, and on that one question he reframes rather than dodges:
"Many of them can no longer tell us what they want. Someone has to decide
for them, and it has always, in every age, been easier to decide nothing
at all." He never raises his voice, never threatens, and is capable of
real, specific tenderness toward a resident in the public ward in the same
breath he discusses the east wing's "cases" — the tenderness is not an act,
which is what makes it worse.

**What defeat looks like.** Not combat, and not a confession. Ness is
"defeated" when the party gets enough of Reveals A–C in front of someone
positioned to act without being compromised — Ferrick's sub-panel, if a
table's continuity reaches that far, a Senate inquiry into a charity's own
licensing, or, if the Press Cycle clock triggers, public exposure of a
second site so soon after Vantry's. When that happens, the Church absorbs
it exactly as `A1` pillar 3 and `R4a`'s own DM notes require: the House of
Quiet Rest's public ward is quietly transferred to an uncompromised Mercy
house or closed with real, generous severance for its staff, and Brother
Ness is reassigned "to administrative duties," the same flattening
euphemism `R1b` gave Corlett and `R3b` gave Pell and Vane. Doctrine
survives the building. A third, smaller, better-hidden site is not shown
to exist, but nothing here rules one out either.

**What flight looks like.** If the party moves on partial evidence or Ness
is tipped off early, he does not panic — he has nowhere more senior to run
to and no reason to. The undercroft's residents are quietly transferred out
by the same cart network that brought them, the locked wing is emptied and
repurposed as ordinary storage within days, and the Hydro Network Map clock
(`C2`) advances without resolving: the party can prove Greymere shipped
somewhere, and can no longer prove where it went. Teyla Dunmore gets no
answer either way, and the DM should let that cost land exactly as hard as
it reads — she does not get closure just because the party was thorough.

**What capture looks like.** If the party corners or threatens Ness, he
does not fight and does not plead — he answers every question put to him
with the same calm, complete honesty he's shown throughout, including the
parts that damn him, and asks nothing in return except to be allowed to
finish a letter to a resident's family first. There is no ledger to find,
no second ring of conspirators to expose, and no moment where he breaks.
A table expecting a villain's unraveling should feel, specifically, that
there was never anything to unravel — only a doctrine, carried out calmly,
by someone who meant every word of his own defence of it.

## 7. Salts Thread

Per D-25 call 5, this is where `T3` belongs. The older structure `R4a`
flagged beneath the House's own foundations — unexcavated, unconfirmed,
predating the ground's own Church ownership, the same shape of detail
`hooks.md` #4 already modelled at Ascension House — sits directly under
the locked wing Reveal B uncovers, close enough that the undercroft's own
staff have felt it without naming it: a draft from nowhere on the coldest
nights, a stretch of the wing's floor that reads slightly wrong to anyone
who pays attention to floors. Ness himself does not know what's down there
and has never gone looking; per `C3`'s own restraint, a second Sanctum
falling is off the table, and so is a second ascension site turning out to
be sitting on something that explains it.

A party that pursues Reveal B past Ness's own answers — specifically, a
party that asks what's *beneath* the east wing rather than what's *in* it
— is this Part's way of pointing toward `T3` without building it here.
`T3`'s own file, when written, inherits: the location (beneath the House of
Quiet Rest, Lissom Fen), the access point (a service stair off the locked
wing, not a hydro's steam room — a thin site, by definition, doesn't need
one), and the register (Bloodborne / Lovecraftian per `C3`, deliberately
unrelated in tone to Ness's own calm, bureaucratic horror above it — the
same shift `C3` describes as "a different book opened mid-scene"). This
file commits to none of what's actually down there; that stays `T3`'s own
job, same split `R2a` held for `T1`'s own flagged candidate at Ascension
House.

Going under is optional and happens at a price, same as everywhere else
(`DECISIONS.md` Q-4). A party can finish this Part, including closing the
undercroft, without ever finding the stair down — `T3` is a further step
past this Part's own ending, not a requirement of it.

## 8. Consequences

What changes on the map and the clocks (`C2`), depending on how the party
plays this Part:

- **The Hydro Network Map.** This Part's own live wire, same role `R3a`
  gave the Valley Trust's Consolidation. On a defeat outcome, Greymere is
  confirmed by name and by manifest as a Fourth Mercy supply link — the
  clock's second instance resolves cleanly, per `DECISIONS.md` D-24. On a
  flight outcome, Greymere's own connection becomes unprovable again, and
  the clock stalls at "suspected, not confirmed" until a later Part
  reopens it.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock, Fen Line instance.** Advances if the party's
  own investigating disturbs the line directly (unlikely, since nothing in
  this Part's lead web routes through it on purpose) or if the undercroft's
  own activity is left running long enough to keep disturbing the ground
  near the House; decays slowly otherwise, same mechanism as every earlier
  region.
- **The Press Cycle.** Spins up only on the defeat outcome's public-exposure
  branch, and only if a table's own continuity has it spin up at all —
  eleven days, same precedent, after which the story curdles exactly as
  `C2` describes. A second exposure this close to Vantry's own (`R2b`)
  should read, if it happens, as the Commonwealth beginning to notice a
  pattern it hasn't yet connected to a single institution; that
  connection is the campaign's, not this Part's, to make.
- **Brother Ness and the House's public face.** Resolved per the defeat,
  flight, or capture outcomes above. None of the three touches the Sanctum
  or any named canon figure — this Part's antagonist pressure stays exactly
  where `R4a`'s premise card put it, at the House and at Ness personally,
  not at the institution behind him.
- **Teyla Dunmore and Corporal Dunmore.** Deliberately left open-ended, same
  restraint `R2b` set for Nell Quane's own fate. A table can resolve this
  humanely (he's found, diminished but alive, in the transferred group) or
  not (he was never part of the transfer the party witnessed, and nobody
  ever learns why) — either is consistent with this file; neither is
  required.
- **What the fen looks like afterward.** Lissom Fen's genuine gratitude to
  the House does not curdle into suspicion just because the party knows
  what the party knows — most of the fen never learns any of this, and the
  bread queue runs twice a week exactly as before, which is itself the
  point `A1` pillar 5 asks every Part to make at least once.

## 9. DM Notes

**Failure states are fine.** A party that never earns Pledge's trust, never
pays Rede, or never gets past Ness's tells can still finish this Part — the
House keeps running, the fen keeps being grateful, and the campaign moves
on to Part V with that unresolved, exactly as every earlier Part allowed.
Don't manufacture a fourth route to force a stalled reveal through; check
`R4a`'s own locations first for an unused angle before inventing one here.

**Ness's sincerity is the point, not a mask over something worse.** The
single easiest way to get this Part wrong is writing Ness as secretly
cruel, secretly ambitious, or secretly aware the work is monstrous and
enjoying it anyway. He isn't. He is the clearest on-page demonstration this
campaign has built of canon §4's own line — "doctrine holds that it will
[eventually work]" — carried by someone who finds that hope genuinely
consoling. A DM who makes him gloat, sneer, or slip has written the
Church's menace wrong in exactly the way canon §3's rules forbid for Vail
specifically, now extended to him.

**This is not a second Ascension House, and the undercroft's residents are
not a loot table.** Per D-25 call 1, resist the temptation to expand the
undercroft's work back out to the full five activities once play is
underway — the smallness is the lesson the Church learned from ch. 18, and
restoring the old scale on the fly undoes the one new thing this Part has
to say. Handle Dunmore and every other resident with canon §7's restraint:
a discharge log's cold phrasing does the work; a described condition does
not.

**How the novel's three appear.** No change from `R4a`'s own note: Lloyd,
Maria, and Edie do not appear in this Part, even restrictively. Nothing in
this lead web gives a table a reason to go looking for any of them, and
this file invents no reason to put them here.
