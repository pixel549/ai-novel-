# G1 — BESTIARY ADDITIONS, PART I

*Reader: DM. Builds on `R1b-larrow-valley-leads-antagonist.md` §5 (Encounters)
and §6 (Set Pieces) — read both first; this file does not restate the
encounters list or the set pieces, it supplies the archetype write-ups those
sections call for by name and leave undetailed. Cites `R1a-larrow-valley-

`novel/canon.md` §§4–5 throughout. No `campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet

are generated live at the table, archetype and tier only, marked `(SYS?)`
per `A2`'s standing instruction.*

## How to use this file

A D&D-style Monster Manual appendix exists so a DM doesn't have to invent an
encounter's behavior from nothing mid-session. This file does the same job
for Region I: two groups, **People** (the unnamed functionaries a lead-web
route or a pursuit encounter needs quick, consistent behavior for) and
**Pest-Class Horrors** (the Attrition-scarred ground, per `C2`'s "the dead
and their pests" faction entry). Nothing here is Fourth Mercy or Salts
material — Region I has neither a thin site nor an on-page Fourth Mercy
presence (`R1a`, `C3`), and this file doesn't reopen either question to pad
itself out. Read an entry once, before the session it's needed in; none of
these are meant to carry a whole scene on their own the way Corlett or
Mirrin Tope do in `R1b`.

**Session default — D-18 (`DECISIONS.md`).** None of the archetypes below
are named or detailed on the page. Where `R1b`'s Encounters section names a
category (a company runner, an unregistered Arcana practitioner, an
Attrition-scarred lesser horror) without building it out, this file invents
the behavior and tells needed to run it, the same way `D1` invented a
starting base and `R1a` invented Pellick's household — logged here rather
than decided silently, and overturnable the same way.

## People

### The Valley Trust Functionary

Covers the gatekeeper at Ammet Ford, the company runner who pays out the
bounty on Beddoe's slate (`R1b` → Reveal C, Route 2), and the mine's
foreman and company-office clerk (`R1a` → The Mine and the Works) — four
uses of one archetype rather than four separate NPCs, per `C2`'s own note
that the Valley Trust moves "quiet management, not dramatic."

**Behaviour.** Competent, unremarkable, and not unkind — nobody here is
playing a henchman. Their instinct when something goes wrong around them is
to report it up the chain, ultimately to Corlett, never to improvise a
confrontation themselves; none of them has the standing or the inclination
to take a risk the Trust hasn't authorised. They answer a specific,
informed question more readily than a vague one, the same tell `R1b` gives
Corlett himself, scaled down.

**Tells.** Consults a ledger, a delivery list, or a logbook before
answering anything that isn't memorised; uses the Works' own names for
things (the "Ammet Ford Division," not "the showpiece") without irony;
visibly uncomfortable being asked to speculate about anything above their
own job.

**Where this matters.** The company runner who paid the bounty genuinely
doesn't know what it's for (`R1b`); played straight, that ignorance should
survive even a sympathetic, favour-based approach (`S1` — Favours) — he can
be moved to say who gave him the instruction, never to explain a reason he
was never given.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat by default. Escalate only if a specific scene
calls for it; no lead-web route in `R1b` requires a fight with any of these
four.

### The Capping-Camp Tester

The sub-panel's own well-tester, wherever the mobile camp (`D1`) currently
sits — the NPC behind `R1b`'s Reveal A, Route 1.

**Behaviour.** Sincere, overworked, and quietly frustrated that an
off-schedule anomaly (Ammet Ford's well reading cleaner on paper than it
tastes) has nowhere to go once she's noticed it — per `C2`'s Ferrick entry,
underfunded and two months behind the problem even when she's right. She
will share her actual readings readily with anyone who asks a specific
question; she has no patience left for a vague one, not out of hostility but
because vague questions are exactly what she's spent a year not having time
for.

**Tells.** Carries the real numbers, not an impression — a tested figure,
a date, a comparison — and corrects herself aloud rather than guessing when
she isn't certain.

**Where this matters.** She is this Part's cleanest "institution sincere
and underfunded in the same breath" NPC outside Ferrick himself (`C2`); play
her disappointment as professional, not personal — she isn't owed a rescue
any more than Pellick is (`R1a`'s own DM notes), and the Part shouldn't
manufacture one for her either.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

### The Mercy Brother (Brother Aldous, Brother Tam, and any others met on the North Road)

Region I's only on-page Church presence this Part (`R1a`, `R1b`) — travelling
with blankets and seed corn toward Selwick, exactly as the three public
Mercies operate everywhere (canon §4).

**Behaviour.** Genuinely charitable, unhurried, and happy to talk — per
`A2`'s tone-contract rule for Church menace, anything unsettling about
either brother has to live in the content of what they say, delivered
warmly, never in staging (no lowered voice, no lingering, nothing canon §3's
hard prose rules for Vail already ban). Aldous and Tam believe in the work
exactly as much as any other Mercy brother in this book does; nothing about
either of them should read as performed.

**The tell.** Aldous's collar does not move with his throat when he laughs
(`source-bible.md`; `hooks.md` #9) — the clearest "something is wrong with a
Church agent" sighting in the whole book attached to a named, speaking
character rather than a background figure. Whether this means he's a
construct or homunculus the Fourth Mercy built and sent out walking as
ordinary charity, or simply an odd man with an odd collar, is left
deliberately open on the page and should stay open at the table by default —
see DM Notes below before deciding otherwise.

**Where this matters.** Never run Aldous or Tam as a threat in Region I —
`R1a` and `R1b` both scope this Part's Church presence to exactly this
encounter, nothing larger; anything more belongs to Region II (`R1b` §5).

`(SYS?)` tier: non-combat; no stat block is needed unless a DM specifically
resolves the construct question (see DM Notes) and a later Part calls for a
fight.

### The Unregistered Arcana Practitioner

Covers `R1b`'s optional Arcana encounter — "a candle-lighter, a minor
charm-worker" somewhere in the nine unnamed villages (`R1a`) — worth using
only if a PC has native Arcana of their own and the DM wants to dramatise
`C2`'s point that being noticed is a cost, not a flex.

**Behaviour.** Keeps the gift quiet and small on purpose — a candle lit
without a match, a headache eased, nothing that would draw the kind of
attention `C2`'s Arcana-practitioners entry warns about. Reacts to another
practitioner recognising what they are with wariness first, warmth second,
never the other way round; has heard enough rumour about the Fourth Mercy's
stud book to take the risk seriously without being able to name why,
specifically, beyond "people like us get noticed and then they don't."

**Tells.** Minimises the ability in front of strangers even when using it;
deflects a direct question about where the gift came from rather than
refusing to answer it outright — Maria's own "she doesn't know, she is also
training it" uncertainty (canon §2) is the model, scaled down to someone
with far less of it and far more reason to be careful.

**Where this matters.** This is the one archetype in this file explicitly
optional — skip it if no PC has the hook, per `R1b` §5. If used, it should
cost the NPC something to be noticed, even by a sympathetic party; a scene
that ends with them better off for having been found out is being played
against `C2`'s own point.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

## Pest-Class Horrors (the Attrition-scarred ground)

Not villains — weather, per `C2`'s "the dead and their pests" entry, which
has no wants in the faction sense and moves only in response to disturbed
ground. Two tells every instance of this archetype should share, regardless
of which site it's dressed for: it is worse wherever mining, construction,
or a thin site has recently broken new ground, and otherwise dormant; and it
never escalates into a boss fight (`C2`; `R1b`'s "Older Workings" set
piece). Region I's home address for the Pest Pressure Clock is the mine's
older, disused workings (`R1a`); Hessel Bight's flooded manse cellar
(below) is the only other keyed site that can plausibly host one.

### The Collapsed-Tunnel Lingerer

The low-tier instance built for `R1b`'s "Older Workings" set piece
specifically — a short, contained encounter in the mine's disused tunnels
when the Pest Pressure Clock is running high, not a dungeon crawl.

**Behaviour.** Doesn't hunt and doesn't pursue past the section of tunnel
it's bound to; registers warmth, movement, and light, and investigates
sluggishly rather than attacking on sight — a party that backs out of its
section loses its interest within a few minutes, every time. It has no plan
and no grievance; per canon §5, this is a pest problem, not a haunting with
unfinished business.

**Tells.** Cold air out of proportion to the tunnel's actual depth; a
dragging sound with no footprints to match it; old timber that's settled
wrong in a way that postdates the original cave-in by years, not decades.

**Function in the Part.** Makes the mine feel like a place the Valley Trust
has every reason to want left alone for reasons that have nothing to do
with the survey, which `R1b` already names as the point — Corlett's
stonewalling should read as multiply motivated, not single-mindedly
sinister, and this encounter is the mechanism that does that without adding
a second antagonist.

`(SYS?)` tier: low; a contained scene by design, never a chase across the
whole mine, per `C2`'s own instruction that this clock should never resolve
into a boss fight.

### Hessel Bight's Manse Cellar (a standing question, not a second creature)

Per `R1a`, whether the flooded manse's cellar — cleared once already as
paid "wet work," eleven of the Attrition's dead removed (`source-bible.md`)
— is actually clear now is left open on the page on purpose. If a DM needs
an encounter at Hessel Bight, this is where to put it: reuse the Collapsed-
Tunnel Lingerer's behaviour and tells above, re-dressed for a flooded
cellar rather than a mine tunnel (water instead of settled timber, a smell
instead of cold air), rather than inventing a second creature from nothing.
Region I doesn't need two pest archetypes to make this clock legible.

## DM Notes

**Nothing above is Fourth Mercy or Salts material.** The ch. 2 wire-frame
creature (`hooks.md` #11) and anything resembling the thing in tank four
(`hooks.md` #7) belong to a thin site or a later region's bestiary (`G2`
onward), not here — Region I has no thin site (`R1a`, `C3`) and this file
deliberately doesn't manufacture a Salts-adjacent entry a DM might reach for
out of habit. If a table wants to feel the Salts at the edges of this Part,
`R1b` §7 already names the only two things that close — Stillwell's own
unexplained extraction timer and Garrick Fen's willingness to talk there —
and neither is a bestiary entry.

**Resolving Brother Aldous is optional and, once made, should stay made.**
If a DM decides he's a construct, write it into the running note (`A2` §1)
so a later session doesn't quietly reverse it; leaving the question open,
the way the novel does, costs nothing and is the safer default.

**None of this needs a fight to matter.** Per `A1` pillar 1 and the cost
test (canon §8), every archetype above can resolve a scene without combat —
a functionary who reports up the chain instead of improvising a
confrontation, a tester who hands over her numbers, a Lingerer that loses
interest once the party backs out of its tunnel. Reach for combat because
the table wants it that session, not because an encounter entry exists and
needs using.
