# C3 — THE SALTS AS BACKGROUND

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md` → "How the Salts Fits" and
`C2-factions-clocks.md` — read both first; this file does not restate the
premise, the pillars, or the faction entries. Cites `source-bible.md` →
Places/Objects/Rules, `hooks.md`, and `novel/canon.md` §2 (abilities, hard
limits) and §5 (world) throughout. This is lore and DM rules, not a tier
ladder — mechanical numbers belong to `S1` (surface adaptation) and the
ruleset itself; anything that would need one here is marked `(SYS?)`.*

## What the Salts Are

A dream-state, not a place you travel to. Entered in a private, sauna-like
room at a hydro: reagent ("gothic") materials go into a steam basin at the
room's centre, the room fills with vapour, and everyone present settles
somewhere comfortable and blacks out in turn. No immersion, no equipment, no
diving apparatus — the only requirement is the room, the reagent, and
someone willing to breathe it in. Time inside does not track time outside;
nobody surfacing can tell you how long they were gone by how long it felt.

A run is taxing. Painful and exhausting enough that regulars doing serious
work stay on-site at a hydro and take one or two runs a day rather than
coming and going — this is why Stillwell has steam-room attendants and an
intake clerk rather than a single till. Injuries taken inside are real on
surfacing and can heal wrong (`source-bible.md` → Places, the chapel; the
ch. 3 regrowth is the one on-page example, and it is not what it looks
like — see canon §2's resolution of it before using it as a model for
anything).

It is not a secret from anyone with money or Church access. Canon §5 is
explicit: "the Church has been using them industrially for years." Stillwell
sells entry as a respectable nerve cure, thirty-five shillings a run, in
batches of three, tracked by a numbered brass token — the genre every
further hydro (`C1` → The Hydros) should repeat with variation, never
reskinned as something darker on the surface side of the counter.

**The dreamscape itself.** What's actually down there is a shifting,
unstable chamber — on the page it takes the shape of a chapel-shaped gallery,
different in some small way on every pass: a moved candle, a column where
there wasn't one, a missing floor tile, no physical cause offered
(`source-bible.md` → Places). Nothing says every dreamer sees the same
chapel, and nothing says a different hydro's Salts opens onto the same room.
Treat the specific shape as personal and unstable by design — a DM building
a thin site should invent its own version of "the room changes slightly and
nobody can say why," not reuse the chapel verbatim unless the scene is
deliberately echoing it.

## The Chalice

A lead vessel, roughly a pint, on a limestone pedestal in a carved
depression — visible only once someone present declares it there. That
detail is load-bearing, not flavour: the chalice is not a fixture waiting to
be found, it is a thing that exists because someone in the room decided it
should, which is as close as this file comes to a rule for how a thin site
should stage the moment a PC can choose to seek one out. Nobody searches
their way to a chalice by rolling well enough; they get there by deciding to
look at the room as though it's there.

The chalice grants **one ability, once, at a cost.** Not a currency, not a
point pool — a permanent, specific change to what the drinker can do and how
their body or mind now works. Lloyd's second arm is the only on-page
example and the model for every future one: a capability that reroutes or
extends some ordinary function (an arm that can pass through solid matter,
plus a second one nobody else can see) bought at a cost that is just as
specific (he can't always tell which mode he's in, sensation bleeds between
the two arms, he drops things). The gift and the cost are the same shape of
thing, scaled to fit the person. A chalice ability that reads like a clean
upgrade is being written wrong.

Drinking from the chalice is optional, and declining it is exactly as valid
a choice as taking it — Maria is the on-page model: she "drinks only what is
needed to return from the dreamscape, and no more" (canon §2), and the book
never treats that as a missed opportunity. A PC standing in front of a
chalice should feel that same weight: something is on offer, it will cost
something real and permanent, and walking away is not a wrong answer.

**Session default — D-13 (`DECISIONS.md`).** Chalice abilities are bespoke
per recipient, not drawn from a fixed list. Lloyd's arm is not "the chalice
option a PC can also pick" — it's his, permanently, and inventing a second
person with the same gift would cheapen it. When a PC drinks at a thin site,
the DM builds a new ability-and-cost pair to fit that character, following
the shape above (specific capability, specific permanent cost, no stat-block
upgrade), the way a chalice in the novel built one to fit Lloyd. `S1` can
propose a procedure for doing this consistently `(untested)`; until then, the
DM's judgement is the mechanism, same as it was for the book's author.

## What the Salts Explains, and What It Doesn't

The Salts explains chalice-granted abilities — a power bought once, at a
hydro, in the dreamscape, at a cost. It does **not** explain native Arcana.
Maria's repertoire (healing, light, fire, lightning, steadying) is fixed,
present since childhood, fostered by Ondine Vail, and the book never
resolves whether the Salts strengthened it — "she doesn't know, she is also
training it" (canon §2). That gap stays open at the table exactly as it
stays open on the page. No amount of play should close it: a PC with
native-Arcana-equivalent gifts (per whatever the ruleset calls it in `S1`)
never gets a scene that confirms a Salts origin for them, in either
direction. The uncertainty is the canon position, not a placeholder for a
future reveal.

**Session default — Q-2 (`DECISIONS.md`, existing).** The novel's Salts and
the ruleset's Salts are the same thing, not a quieter corner of it. The
hydro, the reagent, the chalice, and the anchor-chalice mechanic the
ruleset's own Salts loop uses all line up already; this file assumes that
identity rather than re-deciding it. Thin sites (`T1`–`T3`) run on the
ruleset's actual Salts-loop rules for exactly this reason.

## The Fourth Mercy's Use

The Church's institutional interest in the Salts is not the chalice — it's
scale. Sixty years of an "ascension programme," every attempt documented by
name and date, none of them successful, doctrine holding it will eventually
work (canon §4). Five documented activities, all at Ascension House before
its destruction in ch. 15: homunculi grown in brine; industrial immersion of
non-consenting subjects who never surface; attempted extraction of the soul
as a separable object; Arcane breeding tracked through a stud book going
back decades; things bound out of the Salts and kept (`source-bible.md` →
Places, Fourth Mercy facility layout). None of this is chalice work in the
sense above — nobody at Ascension House is being handed a gift at a cost.
It's industrial-scale attempts to manufacture or extract what the chalice
otherwise gives out one person at a time, by people the subjects never
consented to and mostly never meet.

This is background pressure for `C2`'s Church entry, not a dungeon the party
revisits. Ascension House is gone — a burned shell a year after ch. 25
(`DECISIONS.md` D-1) — and nothing says the pre-Church foundation underneath
it is active again (`hooks.md` #4 keeps it as an unresolved thin-site
candidate, not a working facility). Where this recurs in play is Region IV
(`C1`, unmapped, extrapolation), when a Part needs a second ascension site to
raise the stakes past "the one building we already burned down."

## How It Leaks Into the Surface

Three sanctioned channels, per `A1` → How the Salts Fits:

1. **Lore the party can learn** — this file, plus whatever a DM improvises
   consistent with it. Most Commonwealth citizens know the Salts exist and
   that hydros sell access to them the way they'd know a sanatorium sells a
   rest cure; almost nobody outside the Church or a chalice recipient knows
   what a chalice actually costs.
2. **Industrial use as a faction tool** — the Church's hydro supply chain
   (`C2` → Hydro operators, Clock 4) and the Fourth Mercy's facilities,
   whenever a Part needs the Church's reach to extend past "a building in
   Vantry."
3. **Thin sites** — three short, compact delves (`T1`–`T3`) using the
   ruleset's actual Salts loop rather than the surface adaptation `S1`
   builds for everything else. This is the only channel where a PC goes
   under personally.

What this file deliberately doesn't do is turn the Salts into a recurring
mechanic the party manages between sessions — no banked Tokens on a
character sheet, no "we go back in" as a standing option. A session can go
by without the Salts coming up at all (`A1` pillar 2), and that's the
intended default, not a gap to fill.

## Thin Sites and the Loop (DM rules, no numbers)

A thin site is a place where the Salts has worn close enough to the surface
that going under doesn't require a hydro's steam room — the pre-Church
foundation under Ascension House's ash is the clearest candidate already on
the table (`hooks.md` #4). Inside one:

- The ruleset's own Salts loop applies as written: anchor chalice, hub,
  bail-out, Token economy, no fast travel. This is the one place in the
  campaign where that loop runs unmodified — everywhere else, `S1`'s surface
  adaptation replaces it.
- The register changes with it. Bloodborne / Lovecraftian dream-logic:
  withhold lore rather than explain it, describe rather than signpost,
  treat every source as unreliable even when it's sincere (`PLAN.md`'s tone
  contract). A thin site should feel like a different book opened mid-scene,
  not a harder fight in the same one.
- Going under is optional and happens at a price (`DECISIONS.md` Q-4
  default: yes, but only here, and never free). A party can finish a Part
  without ever taking the salts; a Part that can only be solved by sending
  them in is a Part that needs rewriting (`A1`).
- A PC earns a chalice ability only this way, in play, at the cost D-13
  above sets — never as a starting-character freebie, and never twice for
  the same person without the DM deciding what a second drink would cost on
  top of the first `(SYS?)`.
- Injuries and changes taken at a thin site are as real and as liable to
  heal wrong as anything in the novel's chapel. This is not the place to
  soften the loop's actual stakes to make the detour feel safe.

## DM Notes

**A chalice ability is a cost wearing a gift's shape, not a reward.** If a
player leaves the table excited only about what they gained and not at all
about what it took, the ability was written wrong — go back to Lloyd's pair
(arm that reaches anywhere / can't trust which arm answers) as the standard.

**Don't let thin sites become the loop by the back door.** Three short
incursions across the whole campaign, not a rotating dungeon the party
returns to for gear. If a table starts treating a thin site as a resource to
farm, that's a sign the surface Part around it isn't giving them enough
reason to stay up top.

**Leave Maria's question open.** Whether native Arcana is Salts-descended is
permanently unresolved in the source material, and resolving it at the
table — in either direction — forecloses something canon deliberately left
loose. A DM can let a character wonder about it out loud forever without
ever answering.

**The Fourth Mercy's methods are not a menu for PC chalice abilities.**
Brine immersion, soul extraction, bred bloodlines, and bound things are what
an institution does to people who didn't consent, at industrial scale, over
sixty years of failure. A chalice-earned PC ability should never read like a
miniature version of one of these; it should read like Lloyd's — personal,
consented to, and costly in a way only the person who drank pays for.
