# R5b — PART V: THE OLD COAST — LEAD WEB, SET PIECES, ANTAGONIST & ENDINGS

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`,
`C3-the-salts-as-background.md`, and `R5a-old-coast-overview.md` — read all
four first; this file does not restate the premise, the factions, the Salts
lore, or Region V's geography and locations. Completes `PLAN.md`'s Part
template for Part V: sections 4–9 (lead web, encounters, set pieces and
antagonist, the Salts thread, consequences, DM notes), and — since this is
the campaign's last Part — the several endings `DECISIONS.md` Q-3 calls for
and the practical answer to Q-4 that `R5a`'s own DM notes flagged as
"actually coming due" here. Picks up the three things `R5a` deliberately
left open: whether Sister Edda Vance shows the First House's record to the
party and at what cost to her, what (if anything) the Church does once it
learns the party has been to Fallow Head, and which of several possible
endings a table's own choices point toward. Mechanics are archetype and
tier only; anything needing a hard number is `(SYS?)` (Q-1).*

## Session defaults this file sets (D-27)

Four calls, all logged in `DECISIONS.md` rather than decided silently:

1. **Vance will open the record to a party that earns it, and the cost to
   her is named.** She has kept the First House's ledgers private for
   sixty years on the belief that keeping the record complete is itself a
   kind of penance; this file decides she can be moved to break that
   silence, but only by patience and specificity, never by pressure or
   authority — see §4, Reveal C. The cost: opening the archive to an
   outsider for the first time is itself the end of the only role she has
   had since she was much younger, whatever the party does with what she
   shows them next.
2. **The antagonist is named: Brother Elias Prynne, "the Registrar,"**
   dispatched from the Sanctum's own records office once — and only once —
   the Church has reason to believe Fallow Head has been disturbed. Built
   institutional-first, personal-second, same discipline as Corlett, Wick,
   Pell/Vane, and Ness, but shaped to this Part's own stakes: his tool is
   never force, it's filing. See §6.
3. **The campaign's several endings (Q-3) are built here, ordered by cost,
   none clean**, per the existing default. Five are offered below; a table
   is free to land on a sixth the DM improvises in the same shape, and
   nothing here declares one of the five canonical for every table.
4. **Q-4's practical answer: this Part opens no new thin site, and that
   restraint is itself how Q-4 comes due.** `R5a` already ruled out a third
   tank room; this file goes further and confirms Region V has no Salts
   loop at all. The campaign's last Part proves its own thesis by *not*
   needing one — every ending below is won or lost entirely on the
   surface, with nothing a chalice could have made easier.

None of the four touches chapter text or contradicts canon: call 1 answers a
question `R5a` posed to this file directly; call 2 is an invention in the
register every earlier Part's antagonist used; call 3 exercises a standing
default (`DECISIONS.md` Q-3) rather than overturning it; call 4 is a
restraint, not an addition. Logged here per RUNBOOK's Phase 2 instructions
rather than decided silently in this file.

## 4. The Lead Web

Three reveals. Per `PLAN.md`'s template, each is reachable at least three
ways — no single refused request, missed roll, or closed door should stall
the Part, least of all its last one.

### Reveal A — Hollowmere knows exactly what it's paid to not ask about

The village doesn't need investigating so much as earning. Nobody in
Hollowmere is hiding a secret they understand; they're all quietly declining
to add up numbers that have never been theirs to add.

- **Route 1 — Catchpole's own account books, earned rather than taken.** Per
  `R5a`'s own Secrets entry, a party that's spent real time at the Anchorage
  being useful — mending nets, standing a round, not asking too fast — gets
  shown the ledger freely: a retainer, unbroken for longer than Catchpole's
  books go back, delivered by six or seven different hands across the
  decades, never once itemised as anything but "the usual."
- **Route 2 — the village's own oldest memory, sideways.** Someone older
  than Catchpole — a net-mender, a retired boat-hand, nobody this file names
  because the point is that it could be almost anyone in a village this
  small — remembers "the House up at Fallow Head" doing something before
  anyone currently living arrived: carts going up that didn't come back down
  the same day, a smell on the wind some summers that nobody born after a
  certain year ever smelled. They don't know what it means. They've simply
  never had cause to wonder past the edge of their own memory.
- **Route 3 — the campaign's own accumulated pattern, noticed by a party
  that's been paying attention.** A table that has already traced Greymere's
  manifest (`R4b`, Reveal A), read Vantry's own fragment of the record
  (`hooks.md` #3), or simply tallied how many quiet, well-funded, badly-
  explained places this campaign has put in front of them recognises
  Hollowmere's shape immediately: isolated, discreet, paid without being
  asked. This route rewards continuity; Routes 1–2 reach the same place
  without it, same balance every earlier Part's third route has struck.

### Reveal B — Fallow Head isn't an old ruin, it's the oldest record there is

What the party learns at Hollowmere (Reveal A) is that money moves to Fallow
Head. What they learn here is what's actually up there, and how long it's
been.

- **Route 1 — Vance herself, met plainly.** A party that simply walks the
  coast path and knocks finds an old woman tending a garden who answers the
  door herself and doesn't pretend to be anything but what she is: she'll
  say, without prompting, that she keeps records for the Church and has done
  for most of her life, and she'll say it in exactly the tone Ondine Vail
  would use for the same sentence — not a confession, a fact.
- **Route 2 — a memory older than Vance's own tenure.** The same old-timer
  from Reveal A's Route 2, pressed a little further, places the House on the
  headland as having been there, doing something, before Vance was the one
  doing it — which means whatever it is, it didn't start with her and won't
  end with her either. This is the thread that tells a careful party Fallow
  Head predates the entire rest of the Fourth Mercy's visible network.
- **Route 3 — cross-reading the campaign's own fragments.** A party carrying
  pieces from Vantry's archive, Greymere's shipping pattern, or the House of
  Quiet Rest's own undercroft (`R4b`) can lay a rough timeline against what
  Hollowmere and Vance both imply and notice the shape doesn't fit anywhere
  else the campaign has put a tank room or a growing bay — because Fallow
  Head was never rebuilt as one. It's the one site in sixty years that was
  tried, failed early, and simply kept, which is a different kind of thing
  than anything else the party has seen the Church run.

### Reveal C — what Vance will show, and what it costs her

This is the Part's real reveal, and the one `R5a` deliberately left for this
file to build: not what's in the record, which the party can't know until
they're shown it, but whether Vance shows it at all.

- **Route 1 — patience, held past the point of being comfortable.** Vance
  does not respond to urgency. A party that sits with her, lets her set the
  pace, and does not press "what's in the books" before she's ready to say
  it herself eventually gets exactly that question answered on her own
  terms. This is the slow route and the one that costs the party nothing but
  time, which, on this coast, is the one thing nobody's in a hurry to spend
  (`R5a` §4's own tide-timed pacing lever applies here too).
- **Route 2 — proof of good faith, specific rather than general.** A party
  that brings her something that independently checks against her own
  record — a name, a date, a detail from Vantry's archive or Greymere's
  manifest that she can confirm or correct against sixty years of entries —
  earns her trust faster than patience alone, because it proves the party
  already knows this is a record and not a story. She will correct them if
  they're wrong; she has never once, in sixty years, let an error into her
  own ledgers, and she won't start now out of politeness.
- **Route 3 — a name, not an institution.** A party chasing one specific
  person — Corporal Emrys Dunmore (`R4b`), Nell Quane (`R2b`), Anse Duley
  (canon, ch. 14), or an invented thread a table's own earlier Parts
  produced — and who says so plainly, by name, moves her faster than any
  amount of talk about the Church, the Fourth Mercy, or doctrine. In sixty
  years, per `R5a`'s own framing, nobody has ever come asking after one
  person rather than after the institution in the abstract. That is, to her,
  the one kind of question this archive was always actually for.

**What it costs her.** However the party gets there, showing the record to
an outsider for the first time in sixty years ends the only role Vance has
had since she was much younger than she is now — not because anyone makes
her stop, but because the thing she's protected by never showing it to
anyone no longer has that protection, whatever the party does next. She does
not regret the choice once made (per canon §3's own rules for every sincere
Church face this campaign has built, extended to her: she never lies, never
postures, and does not perform doubt she doesn't feel) but she does, for the
first time in sixty years, not know what happens now — and she says so,
plainly, which is itself the clearest sign of how much this has cost her.

**Her tell (telegraph this exactly, every time).** Before answering any
direct question about what the ledgers contain, Vance asks one question in
return, without fail: *"What will you do with it?"* She accepts any honest
answer — including "we don't know yet" — and she remembers it. If the party
returns, or acts, she holds them to whatever they said, not with anger and
never with a threat, simply by remembering precisely, the way her own
ledgers do.

## 5. Encounters

Archetype and tier only; the real ruleset generates stat blocks live.

- **Social.** The whole Part, almost without exception — Catchpole's slow
  trust, the old-timer's half-formed memory, and above all Vance's own
  question. This is the quietest Part in the campaign and should play that
  way; a DM reaching for a roll here is reaching for the wrong tool.
- **Pursuit.** None, until §6. `R5a`'s own DM notes are explicit that this
  region's danger is informational, not physical, and that any
  confrontation should arrive late and from outside the region. Nothing
  here waits for the party; nothing needs to.
- **Arcana.** Optional and minor, same restraint as every earlier Part — a
  Hollowmere fisherman's half-remembered ward against drowning, worth a
  scene only if a PC's own hook calls for it.
- **Church operatives.** Vance herself, who is a caretaker and a record,
  not an operative in any sense `C2`'s Church entry means by the word; and
  Brother Elias Prynne (§6), the only operative this Part has, arriving
  late and alone.
- **Pest-class horrors.** The Tide Flats' own instance of the Pest Pressure
  Clock (`R5a`, the *Aldermoor*) — weather, not a villain, `(SYS?)` for the
  actual stat block (`G5`'s job, not yet written). Archetype only here.
- **Fourth Mercy products.** None. Per `R5a`'s own DM notes, Fallow Head
  runs no active ascension work and never has within living memory; there
  is nothing here to encounter, only a record of what used to be tried
  elsewhere.
- **Salts leak.** None. See §7 below.

## 6. Set Pieces and Antagonist

### Set piece — The Named Ground

Not a trap and not a puzzle: a procedure. Once the party has read enough of
the record (Reveal C) to have a specific name in mind — their own invented
thread, or one carried forward from an earlier Part (Dunmore, Quane, Duley,
or any other loose end a table's own continuity has left open) — walking out
to the walled plot behind the First House and finding that name's marker, or
failing to, answers the question the record alone can't. `R5a` deliberately
left whether any specific thread resolves this way as the table's own
continuity to decide; this file fixes only the mechanism: the ledgers inside
cross-reference by number to the markers outside, Vance keeps both in the
same exacting order, and a party that has earned a look at one can be shown
the other. Honour the tell above exactly here above all — if Vance has
already been asked "what will you do with it" and answered honestly, she
walks the party out herself rather than making them search; this is not a
scene to gate behind a roll.

### Set piece — The Registrar's Visit

Whatever the party does with what they learn, word of it reaches Carrowgate
— not because anyone at Fallow Head reports it, but because the Church's own
reach (`C2`, the Vail-speed question left deliberately open) notices an
outside party has been asking after a site it hasn't had to think about in
decades. Brother Elias Prynne arrives on the next available rail connection,
alone, unarmed, and entirely unhurried, carrying a travelling case of blank
forms. This scene is a conversation, never a fight, and should be played
that way regardless of how the party arrives at it — see his tells below.

### The Antagonist — Brother Elias Prynne, "the Registrar"

**Gimmick.** Prynne is not sent to recover the record by force and would
find the suggestion a little strange — the Church's whole response to a
sixty-year liability being found is not to destroy it but to *properly file
it*, the same instinct that put Vantry's own exposed register "to be asked
for by date" in an archive nobody requests from (`hooks.md` #3), scaled here
to the institution's entire memory of itself. He is not a villain in any
register this campaign has used for one before; he is simply very good at
the one thing the Church has had sixty years of uninterrupted practice
doing, which is making a true, damning thing exist somewhere it will never
again inconvenience anyone who matters.

**Telegraphed tells (honour these exactly, every time).** Asked directly
what will happen to the record, or to Vance, he answers completely and
calmly, the same discipline canon §3 fixes for Vail and every sincere
Church face this campaign has built since: the record, wherever it currently
is, will be "properly filed" — recovered if it can be, catalogued if it
can't, and in either case moved one administrative layer further from
anyone who might ask for it twice. Vance will be "relieved of the
archive" and reassigned to a smaller house, "to rest" — never framed as
punishment, meant and delivered with real warmth, which is exactly what
makes it land as what it is. He never threatens, never raises his voice, and
is constitutionally incapable of being shocked by anything the party says to
him; he has read worse, filed under a worse year.

**What defeat looks like.** The record, or enough of it to matter, is
already outside Church hands before Prynne can act — the Public Exposure
ending below, reached before his arrival. He does not fight this and does
not deny it; he simply says, honestly, that this will need to be explained
upward, and the Church runs its proven sequence exactly as it has every
other time this campaign has forced it to (grief first, blame narrowed, a
restitution figure large enough that disputing it reads as disputing
charity — `hooks.md` #5). Prynne himself is "reassigned to administrative
duties," the same flattening euphemism `R1b` gave Corlett, `R3b` gave Pell
and Vane, and `R4b` gave Ness — the campaign's last antagonist absorbed by
the same machine every one before him was.

**What flight looks like.** The record is gone before he arrives — the Burn
It ending below. There is nothing here for Prynne to recover, contain, or
file, and for perhaps the first time in his career the procedure has no next
step. He leaves without incident, and the Hydro Network Map and Press Cycle
clocks (`C2`) both stall at exactly this point, permanently: the Church can
neither confirm what was destroyed nor act on it, which is its own kind of
ending and the only one in this Part where the institution is genuinely
denied something rather than merely inconvenienced by it.

**What capture looks like.** If the party corners or presses Prynne
directly — over the Quiet Leverage ending below, most likely, where
something has been taken but not yet used publicly — he does not fight and
does not plead. He answers every question with the same calm completeness
he's shown throughout, including the parts that damn the Church, and asks
only for time enough to finish a report of what's happened here, because
even his own failure, to Prynne, is a thing that belongs in a file rather
than nowhere. There is no larger conspiracy to expose through him and no
moment where he breaks; a table expecting a villain's unraveling should
feel, specifically, that there was never a mask to remove — only a man doing
competently and sincerely the one job this institution has always had
someone doing.

## 7. Salts Thread

None. Per D-27 call 4 and `R5a`'s own DM notes, Region V opens no new thin
site: `T1`–`T3` stay exactly where `R1b`/`R2b`/`R4b` already placed them, and
nothing at Fallow Head is a thin site candidate — the First House's horror,
per `R5a`, is entirely in what it has already recorded, and this file keeps
it that way through to the Part's close. A table that wants a Salts-side
coda to the campaign has three existing sites to use; this file adds no
fourth. If a PC earned a chalice ability earlier in the campaign, its cost
is still theirs to carry here — this Part changes nothing about what they
paid for it, and offers no occasion to pay it off.

## 8. Consequences — The Endings

Per `DECISIONS.md` Q-3 (several endings, ordered by cost, none clean), what
the party does with Fallow Head's record is the campaign's actual close.
None of the five below is the "right" one, and a table that lands between
two of them, or combines pieces of several, has not gotten this wrong.

**1. Walk away.** The party decides, for whatever reason, not to press
Vance past Reveal B, or decides on reflection that disturbing sixty years of
quiet isn't worth whatever it would cost her. Nothing moves on any clock.
Hollowmere keeps its retainer; Vance keeps her garden and her ledgers;
Prynne is never dispatched because the Church never has cause to be. The
cost is paid entirely by whichever name the party might have carried out to
the Named Ground and didn't — the campaign's own last word, if a table picks
this ending, is that evidence existing and evidence mattering stayed exactly
as separate as `hooks.md` #3 always said they were. A wholly valid ending,
not a failure state.

**2. Private reckoning.** The party gets what they came for — a name's fate,
confirmed or denied at the Named Ground — and takes nothing else. Vance
keeps the record; nobody outside this Part ever learns the party was here.
A private grief is answered; the institution's machine is entirely
unbothered, which is itself the point `A1` pillar 5 asks every Part to make
at least once, made here for the last time. Prynne, again, is never
dispatched — no clock moves, no faction notices.

**3. Quiet leverage.** The party takes specific extracts, not the whole
archive, to use privately — pressure on a Church figure encountered earlier
(Corlett, Wick, Pell and Vane, or Ness), protection for an ally, a favour
called in. This is the ending where Prynne is dispatched and actually meets
the party (§6, "capture"): the Church notices its own record has been
touched and sends someone to recover or neutralise what's missing, not to
punish anyone. The party gets what they wanted. The record itself, found
once, gets filed one layer further out of reach than it was before — the
Hydro Network Map clock, if it was ever going to connect Fallow Head to the
rest of the network, stalls here, permanently harder to reopen (`C2`'s own
mechanism for the Valley Trust's Consolidation, reused).

**4. Public exposure.** The party hands enough of the record to Ferrick's
sub-panel, the Vantry broadsheet, or both to make it a story — the single
largest exposure this campaign can produce, dwarfing even ch. 18's, because
this is sixty years rather than one ward. The Press Cycle clock (`C2`)
spins up exactly as it always has: eleven days of the biggest story in the
Commonwealth, grief offered first, blame narrowed to names the Sanctum can
afford to lose, a restitution figure large enough that disputing it reads as
disputing charity, and on day eleven the story curdles into an authenticity
dispute regardless of how solid the evidence was. The Sanctum is not
touched — per `A1` pillar 3, it never is. What *is* touched, and this is the
cost that makes this ending anything but a clean win, is Vance: the Church
reclaims what it can of the record "for safekeeping," and she is quietly
moved on, gently, exactly as Prynne's own tell promises, her sixty years of
private penance ending as one more line in the file she spent her life
keeping honest. The party wins the biggest exposure in the campaign's
history and loses the one person who trusted them enough to make it
possible.

**5. Burn it.** The party, or Vance herself as her own final act, decides
sixty years of documented failure should exist for no one — not the Church,
not the state, not the party's own future leverage — and destroys the
record rather than deliver, keep, or use it. This is Prynne's "flight"
outcome (§6): he arrives to find nothing to recover, and for the first time
in his career the procedure has no next step. No one is ever held
accountable for any of it, structurally or personally; every name in the
Named Ground loses its only remaining record, a second erasure stacked on
whatever erasure put them there. Nothing can ever be weaponised against the
Church by this record again — and nothing can ever prove, to anyone who
wasn't here, that any of it happened at all. The cleanest-feeling ending and,
on a second look, the one with the least clean a cost: the campaign's last
act is agreeing with the Church, for once, about what should be forgotten.

**Other clocks.** The Hydro Network Map does not extend to Fallow Head
under any ending (`R5a`); the Pest Pressure Clock's Tide Flats instance
advances only if the party's own travel disturbs the flats directly
(unlikely) and decays otherwise; the Indemnity Mortality Curve and the
Valley Trust's Consolidation are untouched by this Part entirely — Region V
sits too far from Region I/III's own geography for anything here to reach
them.

## 9. DM Notes

**Failure states are fine, including the quietest one.** A table that never
gets past Reveal A, or that gets everything and chooses Ending 1, has not
broken this Part — "walk away" is a listed ending, not a stall. Don't
manufacture urgency Vance's own tell (§4) is built specifically to resist.

**Prynne's sincerity is the point, same warning `R4b` gave for Ness.** He is
not secretly cruel, not secretly ambitious, and must never gloat, sneer, or
slip — if a DM plays him as menacing, this Part has been written wrong in
exactly the way canon §3 forbids for Vail and every face built in her
likeness since. He should be, if anything, slightly kind, and that is what
should unsettle a table more than anger would.

**Vance is not a villain, a victim, or a twist — she is the campaign's last,
largest demonstration of canon §3 applied to someone who was never once
asked a direct question by anyone, in sixty years, until this Part.** Her
cost is real and this file does not soften it under any ending but
"walk away." Play it exactly that plainly.

**How the novel's three appear.** No change from `R4a`/`R5a`'s own note:
Lloyd, Maria, and Edie do not appear in this Part, even restrictively, and
this file invents no reason to put them here at the very end of the
campaign either.

**This closes the Parts spine.** `R1a`/`b` through `R5a`/`b` are now
complete — five regions, five antagonists, three thin-site placements, and
the campaign's own set of endings, all built without a `campaign/system/`
ruleset existing (Q-1 is still open). What remains per `STATUS.md` is the
bestiary and compendium entries for Parts II–V (`G2`–`G5`, `H2`–`H5`),
`F1` (Travel & Downtime), `I1` (Dramatis Personae, including the cameo rules
`A1` has been deferring to it since its own first draft), `J1` (tables and
handouts), `K1` (the continuity index every later Part has assumed exists
and doesn't yet), and `Z1` (the final integration pass). None of them are
blocked. Flagging this milestone in the digest above the work queue, the
same way `H1`'s session flagged the vertical slice, since a table could
reasonably stop here and already have a complete, playable five-Part
campaign.
