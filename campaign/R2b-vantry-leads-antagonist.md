# R2b — PART II: VANTRY AND THE RAIL — LEAD WEB, SET PIECES & ANTAGONIST

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`,
`C3-the-salts-as-background.md`, and `R2a-vantry-overview.md` — read all
four first; this file does not restate the premise, the factions, the
Salts lore, or Region II's geography and locations. Completes `PLAN.md`'s
Part template for Part II: sections 4–9 (lead web, encounters, set pieces
and antagonist, the Salts thread, consequences, DM notes). Picks up the
three calls `R2a` deliberately left open — whether the ascension work
resumed anywhere, the archive clerk and broadsheet editor's names, and the
antagonist pressure — and builds the lead web and antagonist around them.
Mechanics are archetype and tier only; anything needing a hard number is
`(SYS?)` (Q-1).*

## Session defaults this file sets (D-21)

Three calls, all logged in `DECISIONS.md` rather than decided silently:

1. **The ascension work has not resumed anywhere in Vantry.** Ascension
   House's ruin stays exactly what `R2a` and `C3` already say it is — a
   burned shell, its pre-Church foundation unexcavated and unexplained.
   Nobody has reopened a facility there or anywhere else in the city. What
   has not stopped is the ordinary machine that used to feed it: bread and
   shelter still notice the surplus, people nobody will miss, the same way
   they always did (canon §4) — it simply has nowhere in this city to send
   what it notices now. This keeps Region IV's eventual second ascension
   site (`C3`) the first one the campaign actually builds, and gives this
   Part a genuinely unsettling status quo — a still-running identification
   system with no current destination — instead of a secret lab to raid.
2. **The Church's standing response to renewed interest in Ascension House
   has a face: Brother Calder Wick, the Vantry Almoner.** Installed after
   ch. 18's exposure, answering informally to Ondine Vail at the Sanctum
   rather than to any Vantry Mercy House directly, his actual job is the
   Sanctum's crisis-PR instinct (`hooks.md` #5) made into a standing office
   instead of a one-time sequence: grief and real charity offered first,
   attention steered gently away from the ruin and the archive, nothing
   false ever said. This is the institutional-first, personal-second
   antagonist `R2a`'s DM notes asked for, built the same way `R1b` built
   Corlett — a sincere person doing an inherited job, not a mastermind.
3. **A specific disappearance anchors the lead web: Nell Quane, a lodger at
   the Shuttle.** Invented here because the lodging rows (`R2a`) need a
   concrete case for the party to actually investigate rather than an
   abstraction, following `D1` and `R1a`'s own naming convention. Her
   profile — a minor, unregistered Arcana spark, no family, no one to file
   a complaint — matches the old intake register's selection pattern too
   well to be comfortable and not well enough to prove anything. Per `A1`
   pillar 5 and the lodging rows' own entry in `R2a` ("that ambiguity is the
   point, not a gap to close"), **her actual fate is deliberately left
   open** — debt, a better offer elsewhere, or something worse are all
   still true until a table's own play decides which, and this file does
   not pre-empt that.

None of the three touches chapter text or contradicts canon — call 1
extends C3's own restraint rather than reversing it; call 2 personifies a
faction behaviour `C2` already established; call 3 invents a case, not a
fact about the novel. Logged here per RUNBOOK's Phase 2 instructions rather
than decided silently in this file.

## 4. The Lead Web

Three reveals. Per `PLAN.md`'s template, each is reachable at least three
ways — no single missed roll, refused request slip, or skipped location
should stall the Part.

### Reveal A — the sixty years are all still filed, in the open, unread

- **Route 1 — the standing archive, in person.** **Hester Combe**, the
  archive clerk (named here; `R2a` left her unnamed and deferred the
  invention to this file, same deferral `R1a` used for Ammet Ford's
  administrator before `R1b` named Corlett). Orderly, sincere, and not
  hiding anything — she hands over the stolen intake register, the stud
  book, and sixty years of extraction files to anyone who fills in the
  request slip correctly, and will say, if asked why so few people ever
  do, that she genuinely doesn't know; the log just went quiet after ch.
  18's own flurry and never picked back up.
- **Route 2 — the broadsheet's own plates.** **Corde Ashby**, the Vantry
  broadsheet's editor (named here; `R2a` left this invention to this file
  too). Keeps the original photographed plates filed with more care than
  the city archive keeps its own copies, and will walk a party through
  them — the stud book's decades of entries, the extraction theatre's
  files, "result negative, subject retained" repeated hundreds of times —
  for the price of a promise to bring back anything newsworthy first.
- **Route 3 — the ruin itself, cross-read against the deed.** A surface
  walk through Ascension House's burned shell (not a descent — that's
  `T1`'s job, not this file's): the older stone foundation reading, in
  daylight, as a different and earlier building, matched against the
  parish deed records `source-bible.md` already cites as predating the
  Church's ownership of the ground. A party that visits the ruin first and
  the archive second (or the reverse) should find the two confirm each
  other without either one needing the other to make sense alone.

### Reveal B — a disappearance fits the old pattern too well to ignore

- **Route 1 — Ress Tannery, the Shuttle.** Worried in his own quiet way
  (`R2a`): Nell Quane stopped coming down for the shared stove three
  nights running, her room still paid through the week, nothing in it
  touched or taken. He'll say so to anyone who's stayed a night or two and
  earned the ordinary trust his common room runs on — he's not alarmed,
  exactly, just noting it the way he notices everything about his lodgers.
- **Route 2 — Corde Ashby, cross-referencing.** The editor, shown or told
  about Nell, lays her alongside the old register's selection pattern —
  unregistered Arcana spark (she lights candles without a match, known
  only to two or three people in the building), no family to ask after
  her, nobody positioned to file a complaint if she simply stopped
  existing to the city. He won't print a word of the comparison without
  more, and says so plainly: a wrong guess here is exactly the kind of
  story that curdles in eleven days.
- **Route 3 — a Mercy House sister or brother, kindly asked.** Confirms,
  without any sense of wrongdoing, that Nell came through the bread line
  more than once and was flagged — informally, the way the ordinary
  machine has always worked — as someone a sister might "look in on."
  Nobody ever did, not out of malice, just the unglamorous truth that the
  Mercy Houses are stretched thin and a flagged name is not the same as a
  followed-up one.

### Reveal C — Brother Wick, and the honest, procedural truth

- **Route 1 — Wick, directly.** A conversation at any Mercy House or
  wherever else Wick can be found, built around his tell (below). Asked
  plainly whether anything like Ascension House is running anywhere in
  this city, he will say, truthfully, that it is not — and will not
  elaborate further than the question actually asked, the same rule
  canon's own Vail follows (she answers direct questions directly; Maria
  doesn't learn more in ch. 12 because she doesn't ask more). A party that
  asks the right follow-up gets the right follow-up answer; a party that
  stops at the first reassurance stops there honestly, not because Wick
  concealed anything further.
- **Route 2 — the stud book's own dates.** Checkable without asking anyone
  a question at all: the Fourth Mercy's decades of bloodline entries end
  cold at ch. 18's exposure and never resume. A quiet, documentary
  confirmation of call 1 above, sitting in the same archive Reveal A
  already opened.
- **Route 3 — Ferrick or a Mercy sister, secondhand.** Either will confirm,
  sincerely and without suspicion, what Wick's actual job is and how far it
  reaches — Ferrick gratefully ("the Church's man for difficult things,"
  not realising or minding what that smoothness implies), a Mercy sister
  admiringly (he found her a placement once, after a bad year). Neither
  route requires confronting Wick to learn that he exists and what he's
  for.

## 5. Encounters

Archetype and tier only; the real ruleset generates stat blocks live.

- **Social.** The default register of this Part, same discipline as Region
  I. Wick's tell (below); Hester Combe's orderly cooperation; Corde Ashby's
  caution; Ress Tannery's unalarmed noticing; a Mercy sister's sincere
  admiration for Wick. Most essential information in Region II is won or
  lost here.
- **Pursuit.** Quiet and institutional, never violent — a Church-adjacent
  face noticing the party has been asking after Ascension House or the
  archive, and mentioning it to Wick rather than confronting anyone
  directly. The "encounter" is the party realising, later, that Wick
  already knew what they'd been asking before they ever sought him out.
- **Arcana.** A lodging-row resident with a minor, unregistered spark (Nell
  herself, if the party meets her before her fate is decided, or someone
  like her) — worth a scene only to dramatise `C2`'s point that being
  noticed here is a cost, not a flex, same restraint `R1b` used in Region
  I. Optional; skip if no thread needs it.
- **Church operatives.** A Mercy sister or brother running the bread line
  (`R2a`), genuinely charitable, genuinely unaware of anything past their
  own building — and Brother Wick himself, who is this Part's antagonist,
  not a combat encounter at all (see below). Nothing here escalates to a
  Fourth Mercy site; per D-21 above, there isn't one currently running to
  escalate to.
- **Pest-class horrors.** The Pest Pressure Clock's Vantry instance
  (`R2a`) runs on foundations, culverts, and the rail cutting rather than
  a mine shaft. A low-tier Attrition-scarred encounter near the lodging
  rows or the yards when digging or construction disturbs old ground —
  archetype: Attrition-scarred, not eldritch, same distinction `R1b` drew
  in Region I.
- **Fourth Mercy products.** Nothing active, per D-21. If a DM wants a
  faint echo specifically at Ascension House's ruin — a step past Brother
  Aldous's collar, since `R1b` reserved exactly that escalation for Region
  II — a surviving remnant of whatever was watching Lloyd from tank four
  in ch. 14 (`hooks.md` #7) is consistent with nothing on the page and
  ready for `G2` to build out properly; this file names the possibility
  without confirming it as fact or giving it a stat block. Anything larger
  than a remnant belongs to Region IV, not here.
- **Salts leak.** None that the party can reach without a thin site. The
  ruin is this Part's Salts thread (below); it is not a wandering-monster
  table entry.

## 6. Set Pieces and Antagonist

### Set piece — The Quiet Help

A scene, not a trap: wherever the party's Reveal B thread leads them to
Wick, he solves a real problem for someone — a debt covering Nell's unpaid
rent at the Shuttle so Ress isn't left holding it, a placement offered to
another lodger in earshot, a genuine kindness delivered without asking
anything in return — before, during, or instead of answering the question
the party actually came to ask. The telegraphed tell, honoured every time:
Wick never refuses a direct question and never lies, but he always finds
something real to give first, and the charity is never fake, which is the
whole discomfort of the scene. A DM who plays this as a bribe or a
distraction tactic has written him wrong; he would do the same thing if no
one were watching, and often has.

### Set piece — The Ruin in Daylight

A surface walk through Ascension House's shell with Reveal A's Route 3
(above): the bell tower that never rang, the front-chapel steps with grass
pushing through faster than the rest of the ash, the barred basement
window still there under char and fallen brick. Nothing moves, nothing
needs a stat block, and that's the point — the scene's tension comes from
confirming the foundation predates the Church exactly as the deed records
say, not from anything jumping out. If a table wants to go further than
daylight allows, that is `T1`'s job and this scene should end, cleanly,
at the edge of it rather than improvising a descent.

### The Antagonist — Brother Calder Wick, the Almoner

**Gimmick.** Wick is not a villain in the sense the table will expect one,
same warning `C2`'s DM notes gave for Corlett. He did not design the
Sanctum's crisis-PR sequence (`hooks.md` #5) — Vail's own standing
instinct, proven in ch. 18 — he is simply the person now stationed here to
run it continuously instead of once. He sincerely believes, because Vail
raised him to, that the kindest and most honest thing he can do for anyone
asking about Ascension House is to meet them with real help before the
question, because real help is the one thing he can always, certainly,
give, and the ruin's actual secrets are not his to dispense even if he
wanted to share them. He is not wrong that the help is real. That is the
horror, delivered exactly as warmly as canon §3 demands of every Church
menace.

**Telegraphed tell (honour this exactly, every time).** Asked a direct
question about Ascension House, the archive, or anyone taken by the old
programme, Wick always offers something concrete and immediately useful —
a debt cleared, a bed found, a name put forward for work — before he
answers. He never lies and he never refuses to answer once the help is
offered; he will not volunteer more than the question actually asked, the
same discipline Vail sets for herself in canon §3. A DM who lets him dodge
by vagueness, menace, or a flat refusal has written him wrong — the chill
comes from how much he is actually willing to say, and how little that
ends up mattering.

**What defeat looks like.** Wick is "defeated" when the party gets enough
of Reveal A–C in front of someone positioned to act on it without being
compromised — Ferrick's sub-panel, Hester Combe's own civic archive
asking the Church a formal question it has to answer, or (if the Press
Cycle clock triggers) the Vantry broadsheet. The Sanctum itself does not
move, per canon's own pattern (D-1; the Sanctum is untouched across the
whole book and stays that way here) — what moves is narrower: Wick's
office gets more oversight, a specific Mercy House's practices improve, or
one flagged name like Nell's gets an actual follow-up instead of a good
intention. The party can win this and still not touch the Sanctum, the
same flatness `R1b` built into Corlett's own defeat outcome.

**What flight looks like.** If the party moves on partial evidence or
tips Wick off early, he doesn't vanish or stonewall — he accelerates. More
charity moves through the lodging rows that week, genuinely, which makes
any later accusation read as ingratitude to people who were just helped.
The Press Cycle clock (`C2`) gets harder to trigger afterward, not because
evidence disappears but because the goodwill it would have to overcome is
freshly, honestly earned.

**What capture looks like.** If the party corners or threatens Wick, he
does not fight and does not plead. He offers restitution in the Sanctum's
own register (`hooks.md` #5) — an endowment, a placement, a debt cleared,
the same shape Corlett offered from the works' accounts in Region I — and
means it as sincerely as everything else he's offered. Holding or hurting
him gets the party nothing further: he has no hidden ledger and no bigger
name to give up, because per D-21 there genuinely isn't one running here
right now. A table that corners Wick and expects to uncover a secret
facility should feel, specifically, how little there was to find.

## 7. Salts Thread

Ascension House's ruin remains this campaign's standing thin-site
candidate (`hooks.md` #4; `R2a`; `C3`). This file does not build `T1` —
that is a separate work item, now unblocked by `C3` and `R1b` for whenever
a table's own run order reaches it — but it names why a story might send
the party there: Reveal A's Route 3 already puts them on the surface
above it, and a table that wants to feel the Salts at the edges of this
Part without a full descent can use the barred basement window's pale
gleam (`source-bible.md`) as exactly that — a thing seen, never
confirmed, never explained here. Going under is `T1`'s job alone.

## 8. Consequences

What changes on the map and the clocks (`C2`), depending on how the party
plays this Part:

- **The Press Cycle.** Cold at the Part's start unless Region I's own play
  already spun it up and let it cool. If the party gets Reveal A–C's
  evidence to Corde Ashby or to Ferrick publicly, it runs its eleven days
  exactly as `C2` describes and curdles into an authenticity dispute
  regardless of how solid the evidence was — the same hard ceiling R1b's
  Region I version hit.
- **The Hydro Network Map.** Untouched by this Part's lead web directly;
  nothing above requires tracing a supply chain. A DM who wants to move
  this clock here can route it through the rail yards (`R2a`) instead —
  this file doesn't need it to.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock, Vantry instance.** Advances if construction
  or digging near the lodging rows or the yards goes unaddressed; decays
  slowly otherwise, same mechanism as Region I's mine.
- **Wick's standing and the Almoner's office.** Advances toward "defeated"
  (above) if the party plays Reveal C all the way through to someone who
  can act; stays exactly where it started, quietly, if they don't — the
  office keeps running, the help keeps being real, and nothing about the
  machine's idle state (D-21) changes either way.
- **Nell Quane.** Resolved or not, by the table's own play, deliberately —
  per D-21, this file commits to no answer. Whatever a given table
  decides happened to her, that decision is theirs, not a retcon of
  anything fixed here.
- **What Vantry looks like afterward.** The ruin stays a ruin, the archive
  stays filed and mostly unread, and the Church's three ordinary Mercies
  keep being exactly as charitable and exactly as effective as `A1`
  describes — whether or not the party ever learns Wick's name. That
  flatness is the intended outcome, same discipline `R1b` held for the
  Valley Trust.

## 9. DM Notes

**Failure states are fine.** A party that never finds Hester Combe, never
connects Nell's case to the old pattern, or never gets past Wick's tell
can still finish this Part — the archive stays unread by one more table,
the question of what's under the ruin stays open, and the campaign moves
on to Part III with that unresolved, exactly as `R1b` allowed for Region
I. Don't manufacture a fourth route to force a stalled reveal through;
check `R2a`'s own locations first for an unused angle before inventing one
here.

**Don't resolve Nell's fate for the table.** Per D-21 and `R2a`'s own note
on the lodging rows ("that ambiguity is the point, not a gap to close"),
resist the urge to decide in advance whether she was taken, ran, or simply
isn't findable. Whichever the table lands on through play is the right
answer for that table.

**Wick is generous honestly, not as a tactic.** The single easiest way to
get this Part wrong is playing his charity as a performance that cracks
under pressure. It doesn't crack, because it was never a performance — see
`C2`'s own instruction not to run any faction here as a disguised villain.

**How the novel's three appear.** No change from `R2a`: Edie remains this
Part's only one of the three with a fixed, on-page presence, and remains
restricted to a name on the roster and a glimpse across a courtyard until
`I1` exists — nothing in this file gives her dialogue. Lloyd and Maria stay
out of Vantry entirely, per `A1`'s timeline anchor.

**Next in Phase 2.** `R3a` (Part III overview and locations) depends on
this file and is now the next runnable row per `STATUS.md`'s own order.
`G2` (Bestiary, Part II) and `H2` (Compendium, Part II) are also unblocked
by this file, including a proper write-up for the tank-four remnant above
if a future session wants to use it.
