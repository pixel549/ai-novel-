# T3 — THIN SITE 3: THE STANDING CISTERN

*Reader: DM. Builds on `C3-the-salts-as-background.md` (the thin-site rules)
and `T1-the-foundation.md`/`T2-the-second-day.md` (the first two files to
apply them — read both for the register even though this site works
differently again) and `R4b-fourth-mercy-leads-antagonist.md` (`STATUS.md`'s
formal dependency alongside `T2`) — read all four first. Also cites
`R4a-fourth-mercy-network-overview.md` for the House of Quiet Rest's surface
layout and `S1-surface-adaptation.md` for the chalice-ability build
procedure. No `campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet (Q-1); every mechanical
value below is `(SYS?)` — this file fixes what the site is and does, not
what it costs on a character sheet. Per `C3`, this is the third and last of
the three places in the campaign where the ruleset's actual Salts loop
(anchor chalice, hub, bail-out, Tokens, no fast travel, mutation) runs
unmodified; `S1`'s surface adaptation does not apply past the threshold
below.*

## How to Get Here

Per `R4b`'s D-25 call 5 and §7 (Salts Thread), this site is confirmed, not
merely flagged: the older structure `R4a` noticed beneath the House of Quiet
Rest's own foundations sits directly under the locked east wing Reveal B
uncovers, reached by a service stair off that wing that Brother Corwin Ness
himself has never gone down (`R4b`). A party that has already connected
Reveal B past Ness's own answers — specifically, a party that asks what's
*beneath* the undercroft rather than what's *in* it — is pointed here, same
restraint `R4b` set for exactly this handoff.

**There is a real stair here, unlike `T1`'s invented door or `T2`'s
committed count — and that is this site's own version of `C3`'s rule, not
an exception to it.** The stair is ordinary: stone steps down from the
locked wing to what the House's own handful of "specialist" staff, the few
who know the wing exists at all, call the old cistern — a disused
catchment for a well that predates the brine tanks above it by a margin
nobody on staff has ever asked about. Walked down with a lantern and
nothing more, it is exactly that: a cold, dry-footed sub-cellar, empty,
unremarkable, ordinary enough that Ness's own staff store spare tank
fittings on its upper shelf. **What makes it a thin site is a second kind
of looking, not a different door:** a party that stops at the cistern's own
standing water — there is always a little, even in a dry season, pooled at
the lowest point of an old stone floor — and chooses to look into it the
way `C3` says a chalice gets chosen into a room, expecting their own
reflection to show them something other than the lantern and the ceiling
above them, finds that it does.

Offer this moment plainly and once, the same discipline `T1` and `T2` both
use: describe the water, ask what the party does with it, and open the
site only on a deliberate choice to look for something in it, not an idle
glance. A party that takes fittings off the shelf and leaves, or never
looks down at all, has made a completely valid choice (`C3`; `A1` pillar 2)
and `R4b`'s own Part continues exactly as written without this file ever
coming into play.

## The Loop, As-Is

Everything below runs the ruleset's actual Salts-loop rules unmodified —
anchor chalice, hub, Token economy, bail-out, no fast travel, mutation —
the last of the three places in the campaign this is true (`C3`; `S1`
"Mutation and the Salts Loop"). None of `S1`'s surface replacements (crowns
and favours, safehouses, flight/capture/recovery, real travel) apply once a
party has looked into the water and found something looking back. `(SYS?)`
for every number this needs: anchor cost, Token count, bail-out trigger
conditions, and mutation thresholds all wait on `campaign/system/` landing.
Run the loop the way `T1` and `T2` already model it until the real ruleset
says otherwise: time inside doesn't track time outside, a run is taxing,
injuries taken here are real on surfacing and may heal wrong.

## What's Down There

Not the chapel, not `T1`'s foundation, and not `T2`'s road. `C3` is
explicit that nothing says two thin sites share a dreamscape, and this file
takes that as seriously as the first two did: where `T1` went down into
something architectural and `T2` stretched something horizontal, this site
holds still and looks back.

**Session default — D-30 (`DECISIONS.md`).** `R4a` and `R4b` both describe
this structure the same way `hooks.md` #4 describes Ascension House's own
foundation — unexcavated, unconfirmed, predating the ground's own Church
ownership — and `R4b` is explicit that a second ascension site turning out
to be sitting on something that explains it is off the table. This file
holds that line exactly: there is no name for what's under the cistern, no
founder, no doctrine, and — this is the part that matters, same as `T1`'s
own D-28 — **nothing here is Fourth Mercy work, and nothing here explains
or excuses the undercroft above it.** The brine tanks and the growing work
Ness oversees are entirely his own institution's doing, built on ground
that happened to already be strange, the same way Ascension House's new
brick went up over stone nobody at the Church ever asked about. Ness's own
sincerity (`R4b`) is real and untouched by what's below him; he has never
been curious about his own cellar, and this file gives him no reason he
should have been. **This file commits to no further resolution than that.**
A DM who wants more should invent it consistent with the above, not look
here for an answer `R4a`/`R4b` never gave.

Instead of a stable architecture or a stable distance, treat the water
itself as the site's own variety call: not a room that won't keep its size
(`T1`) and not a road that won't end (`T2`), but a reflection that won't
agree with what's actually above it. A lantern held over the cistern's
standing water should show, some of the time, the lantern, the party's own
faces, and the cellar's low stone ceiling exactly as expected — and, at a
moment a DM chooses rather than signposts, something else entirely: a
different ceiling, a different light, once, briefly, a shape that isn't
anyone standing there. This is a fresh device, not `hooks.md` #12's
distance-and-measurement principle reused a third time — `T2` already
named that as `T1`'s own device turned outward once; turning it a second
time would read as the same trick worn thin rather than a new one, and this
file chooses not to.

## The Site

Four nodes, the same compact shape `PLAN.md` sets for a thin site and `T1`
and `T2` both already used. Run it as a sequence a DM narrates through, not
a map a party needs to walk correctly — let the water's own wrongness
excuse any inconsistency in how long anyone thinks they've been standing
over it.

### 1. The Service Stair

The threshold: ordinary stone steps down from the locked wing, the air
turning colder and wetter the way a real sub-cellar's should, nothing wrong
with it yet. Spare tank fittings on an upper shelf, exactly as mundane as
the undercroft above. No decision point here, same discipline as `T1` and
`T2`'s own node 1: describe, never signpost. A party that stops here and
goes back up has seen nothing this file needs to account for.

### 2. The Standing Cistern

The cellar's low point: old dressed stone, a few inches of standing water
that never quite drains and never quite rises, the only sound a drip that
keeps a rhythm nobody can match by trying. This is where a party chooses
whether to look for something in the water rather than at it — the
decision point `C3` requires, offered exactly once per the rule above.
Declining it ends the scene here, ordinarily; a party already committed by
that choice finds the next three nodes waiting regardless of how they got
here.

**Encounter (optional).** Something stands in the water at the cistern's
far side the first time anyone actually looks for it — not moving, not
breathing, fully submerged to a height no sub-cellar's few inches of water
could actually cover, visible only because a party is already looking
somewhere a sub-cellar's water has no business reaching that high. It turns
to face whoever's watching and does nothing else. Struck, grabbed, or
simply ignored, it is gone the next time anyone looks, as if the water
itself had been holding the idea of it rather than the thing. `(SYS?)` tier:
low; a scene, not a set piece, same restraint `T1`'s wire-frame creature and
`T2`'s company on the road both set. It does not hunt and does not escalate
across a visit; a second sighting on the same delve should feel like the
same attention returning, not a restocked encounter.

### 3. The Watching Water

Past the cistern, a second, smaller pool set into the floor on its own,
too deliberately placed to be a drainage accident — a basin, not a puddle,
though nothing marks it as built rather than found. This is where the
reflection itself stops agreeing with the room above it: a lantern held
over this basin shows, unpredictably, a ceiling that isn't the cellar's, a
light that isn't the lantern's, once a shape that resolves into nothing
clearer before the water settles back to showing exactly what's actually
there. Nothing moves here except the reflection. If a party lingers, let
them feel looked at by their own image rather than show them what's looking
— the same restraint `T1`'s node 3 and `T2`'s node 3 both use for their own
unresolved elements. If a player asks what the basin is reflecting, the
honest table-time answer is that nobody — not Ness, not whoever built this
cellar, not the water itself if it could answer — knows there's a basin
here at all.

**This file commits to no further resolution than that.** A DM who wants
more should invent it consistent with the above, the same discipline D-28
and D-29 both set for `T1`'s gleam and `T2`'s tally wall — don't cash this
out just because it's sitting there unexplained.

### 4. The Dry Shelf

A ledge of the same dressed stone, raised just above the basin's edge, dry
in a cellar where everything else holds a little water. It holds still the
moment someone decides to look at it the way `C3` requires: the chalice,
lead, roughly a pint, sits on the shelf the way the spare fittings sit on
the stair's own shelf above — no pedestal, no carved depression, because
this isn't Stillwell's chapel, isn't `T1`'s shelf, and isn't `T2`'s
waystone either. It is there because someone in the party decided it
should be, exactly as `C3` states, nowhere else in this file.

## The Chalice

Per `C3`'s D-13 and `S1`'s build procedure, the same as `T1` and `T2`: this
file does not pre-build a gift. A PC who drinks at node 4 gets an ability
bespoke to them, built live, following `S1`'s five steps — name an
ordinary function; invert or extend it once, specifically; pair it with an
equally specific permanent cost using the same function; check it against
Lloyd's shape; never reuse Lloyd's own pair, and never reuse whatever was
built for `T1`'s or `T2`'s own drinker, if either happened at the same
table.

**Running the moment.** Ask what the character reaches for, physically or
otherwise, and build from there, not from a menu — `H1`'s "Sure Footing" is
still not available to assign, here any more than at `T1` or `T2`. Decline
is exactly as valid an outcome as acceptance (`C3`; Maria's own on-page
model); a party that leaves node 4 with nobody having touched the chalice
has made the same choice she did.

**Cost is not optional once a gift is taken.** `(SYS?)` for the exact
mechanical shape, but the content isn't negotiable: specific, permanent,
and built from the same function as the gift. A cost that reads as a minor
inconvenience has been written wrong — go back to Lloyd's own pair before
finalising anything.

## Mutation and Injury

Per `S1`'s D-14, mutation stays Salts-side everywhere in the campaign
except the thin sites. This is the last of the three sites where it can
trigger exactly as the ruleset defines it, `(SYS?)` for the actual
thresholds. Injuries taken past the threshold are real on surfacing and may
heal wrong, same as `T1`, `T2`, and the novel's own chapel — nothing about
a cellar a few steps below an infirmary makes this site feel lower-stakes
than a basement under a burned ruin or a stretch of open road, and running
it that way would be the mistake.

## Leaving

Bail-out applies, unmodified, `(SYS?)` for the trigger conditions — this is
not `S1`'s flight/capture/recovery rule; that rule doesn't reach past the
cistern. A party that bails out mid-delve surfaces back in the cellar,
dry-footed, at the bottom of the service stair, carrying whatever they went
in with and nothing more; node 4's chalice does not travel with anyone who
hasn't already drunk from it.

**No fast travel back in.** Per `C3`'s own standing instruction against
letting a thin site become the loop by the back door: once a party leaves
this cellar, either by finishing node 4 or by bailing out, the water goes
back to being a few inches standing in an old stone floor. Looking into it
again later, even deliberately, gets an ordinary reflection and nothing
more, unless a DM deliberately decides this specific party gets one further
visit for a specific story reason — the exception, never the rule, same
restraint `T1` and `T2` both hold for their own thresholds.

## Why This Isn't Ness's Problem

Per `R4b`'s own D-25, Brother Corwin Ness runs the undercroft's immersion
and growing work personally and answers to nobody on-site — and has never
been down his own cellar stair, because nothing about it has ever seemed
worth his attention. This file does not reverse that. What's under the
cistern predates the Church by a margin nobody can date and was never
Fourth Mercy work; Ness doesn't know this site exists any more than his own
doctrine does, and a party that goes looking for an institutional
conspiracy down here is looking for something this file deliberately
doesn't contain. Unlike `T1` (an institution's own dead ruin) and `T2` (a
stretch of road no institution patrols), this site sits directly beneath an
institution's own active work — and the Church's compartmenting, the
specific lesson it learned from ch. 18 (`R4a`; fewer people know the whole
shape of any one site), is exactly why nobody above has ever connected the
spare-fittings shelf to the water past it. If a DM wants Ness to eventually
learn of it, that's a choice for a later session, not something this file
assumes or needs; as written, going under changes nothing about Ness's
standing, his tell, or what defeat, flight, or capture look like for him
(`R4b` §6).

## Consequences

- **Region IV's clocks.** Untouched by a visit here (`R4a`, `R4b`) unless a
  DM specifically routes the Pest Pressure Clock's Fen Line instance
  through this site — plausible, since this is, in effect, the deepest
  disturbed ground Region IV has, but not required.
- **The chalice gift, if taken.** Permanent, per `C3`/`S1`, and belongs to
  the campaign going forward exactly as `T1`'s and `T2`'s own do — carry it
  into every later Part a DM runs for that character.
- **The basin at node 3.** Stays exactly as unresolved as it was found,
  unless a DM deliberately decides otherwise and logs that decision the way
  this file logs D-30.
- **Ness, the undercroft, and Reveal B.** Untouched. A party that goes
  under and comes back changes nothing about `R4b`'s own lead web, set
  pieces, or antagonist outcomes — this file is a further step past that
  Part's own ending, same as `T1` was for `R2b` and `T2` was for Region I
  and II's own travel.
- **What the surface notices.** Nothing, by default. Per the cost test
  (canon §8), this is the campaign's third and quietest case yet of a cost
  paid entirely by the people who went down — nobody above the stair so
  much as hears the water move.

## DM Notes

**Same register shift as `T1` and `T2`, same discipline.**
Bloodborne/Lovecraftian dream-logic, withhold lore, describe don't
signpost, treat every source — including a party's own reflection — as
unreliable. A DM who explains the basin, gives the submerged shape a
motive, or resolves why the water disagrees with the ceiling has written
this file the same way wrong as giving Vail a smile that doesn't reach her
eyes.

**This is the last thin site. Don't let it feel like the biggest one.**
`PLAN.md` sizes a thin site as a compact delve regardless of how late in
the campaign it falls; `T3` is not a finale dungeon and shouldn't be played
as this campaign's real last fight. The Old Coast's own several endings
(`R5b`) are the campaign's actual close — this file is optional texture a
table may never even reach, same as `T1` and `T2` both were for their own
Parts.

**Don't connect this to the undercroft's work.** The single easiest way to
get this file wrong is treating what's in the water as an explanation for
what Ness does above it, or as a reason the House of Quiet Rest was built
here specifically. Per D-30, it isn't either of those things — the ground
was simply already strange, and the Church built on it without knowing,
the same as it always has.

**Decline is not a failure state.** A party that walks away from node 4
having taken nothing — or that never looks into the cistern at all — has
finished this file exactly as successfully as one that drinks.
