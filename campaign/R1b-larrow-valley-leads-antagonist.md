# R1b — PART I: THE LARROW VALLEY — LEAD WEB, SET PIECES & ANTAGONIST

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`,
`D1-bases-safehouses.md`, and `R1a-larrow-valley-overview.md` — read all four
first; this file does not restate the premise, the factions, the bases, or
Region I's geography and locations. Completes `PLAN.md`'s Part template for
Part I: sections 4–9 (lead web, encounters, set pieces and antagonist, the
Salts thread, consequences, DM notes). Picks up the two calls `R1a`
deliberately left open — the Sweetwater double meaning and the Valley
Trust's administrator — and builds the antagonist pressure around both.
Mechanics are archetype and tier only; anything needing a hard number is

## Session defaults this file sets (D-17)

Two calls, both logged in `DECISIONS.md` rather than decided silently:

1. **The Sweetwater double meaning is real, not coincidence.** Ammet Ford's
   company well and the valley's wasting disease are the same contaminated
   groundwater at two different doses. The mine's runoff has been in the
   valley's water table since before the works broke ground — the buried
   survey said so. Ammet Ford's well draws from the same table but is dosed
   down: filtered, tested, and kept just clean enough that what residents
   feel is the pleasant, compulsive "thickness" rather than the wasting
   disease — a controlled exposure, not a cure. Everywhere else in the
   valley drinks the same water raw. Nobody at Ammet Ford is being poisoned
   on purpose; nobody there has ever had a reason to ask why their water
   tastes different from Pellick's. This is `hooks.md` #1's own suggested
   payoff, taken up rather than left open, because the Part needs a
   resolvable in-fiction truth to build a lead web around — the table can
   still disbelieve, misunderstand, or never fully prove it.
2. **The Valley Trust's day-to-day administrator is named: Hew Corlett.**
   Not one of Crane's original two intermediaries — he reports to them, and
   neither intermediary is named here either, per `C2`'s own restraint. He
   is the valley's actual public face: the man at Ammet Ford's company
   office who signs for deliveries, answers correspondence, and has held
   the post since before Crane died. He did not design the cover-up. He
   inherited it, found it already working, and has spent the year since
   maintaining it exactly the way he'd maintain a production schedule.

Both calls feed the lead web and the antagonist section below. Neither
touches chapter text or contradicts canon — the buried survey and the
Sweetwater naming overlap are both already on the page (`source-bible.md`);
this file only decides what they add up to.

## 4. The Lead Web

Three reveals. Per `PLAN.md`'s template, each is reachable at least three
ways — no single missed roll, refused favour, or skipped village should
stall the Part.

### Reveal A — the two waters are the same thing at two doses

- **Route 1 — the capping camp's own tester.** Whoever is running the
  well-testing for Ferrick's sub-panel that week (`D1` — the capping camp)
  has already noticed Ammet Ford's well reads cleaner on paper than it
  tastes, and will say so to anyone who asks a specific, informed question
  rather than a general one — she's sincere, underfunded, and has nobody
  to report an off-schedule anomaly to (`C2` — Ferrick's sub-panel).
- **Route 2 — Mirrin Tope, Pellick.** Her grandson's Sweetwater, described
  stage by stage, tracks the same "thickness... a hush at the back of the
  throat" Ammet Ford's water produces, just further along and without the
  compulsion to keep drinking — because by the time it's recognisable as
  the disease, nobody wants more of it (`R1a`, `source-bible.md`). Mirrin
  will make this comparison herself, flatly, if a PC describes what Ammet
  Ford's water does to them or their canteen.
- **Route 3 — the buried survey, in hand.** Found at the mine's company
  office (older ledger volumes, still in Crane's handwriting), through the
  Indemnity Office's travelling clerk (the job already on Beddoe's slate,
  `D1`), or from Garrick Fen (below, Reveal B) — any copy of the actual
  groundwater survey states the concentration gradient outright, in the
  dry language of a document that was never written to be read by anyone
  who'd be upset by it.

### Reveal B — the survey was buried on purpose, and still is

- **Route 1 — the mine's own ledgers.** Company-office paperwork, kept
  under whatever name now heads the ledgers, still shows Crane's signature
  authorising the original survey and, a volume later, its filing away
  rather than its submission to anyone outside the works (`source-bible.md`
  → Factions).
- **Route 2 — Garrick Fen.** One of the two inspectors who pushed to
  reopen the survey and was "reassigned to the coast" for it
  (`source-bible.md`). A year on, he's exactly where that reassignment put
  him — lodging quietly near Stillwell, working something beneath his old
  grade, afraid of the Trust in a specific and reasonable way rather than a
  dramatic one. He has nothing in writing he'll admit to keeping, but he
  remembers the survey's numbers precisely enough to repeat them, and will,
  to anyone who finds him and doesn't threaten him — Stillwell's own
  neutrality (`D1`) makes it the one place in the valley he'll talk at all.
- **Route 3 — the Indemnity Office's travelling clerk.** The same clerk
  named on Beddoe's slate (`D1`) keeps session-day hours and knows the
  seventh-year payout clause was timed off *something* — he was never told
  what, and saying so, to the right question, costs him nothing and tells
  the party exactly where to look next.

### Reveal C — who's keeping it buried now, and how

- **Route 1 — Hew Corlett, directly.** A scene at Ammet Ford's company
  office, or anywhere else Corlett can be found, built around his tell
  (below). He will not lie and he will not threaten; he will also never
  answer the actual question the first time it's asked.
- **Route 2 — the bounty on Beddoe's slate.** The standing two-crown bounty
  on whoever's been asking after the buried survey (`D1`) traces back to
  Corlett's office with minimal digging — a company runner paid it out, on
  Corlett's standing instruction, with no idea what it's actually for. This
  is the route that tells the party they've already been noticed.
- **Route 3 — the mine foreman or company clerk.** Either knows enough to
  confirm Corlett's role, and either can be moved with a favour rather than
  a threat (`S1` — Favours) — a cooper's debt called in, a kindness done at
  Beddoe's, a job finished for the capping camp. This is the quiet route,
  and per `A1` pillar 5 it should be on the table as often as the loud one.

## 5. Encounters

Archetype and tier only; the real ruleset generates stat blocks live.

- **Social.** The default register of this Part. Corlett's tell (below);
  Nessa's slate business; Mirrin Tope's flat refusal to be rescued; the
  capping-camp tester's off-the-record anomaly. Most essential information
  in Region I is won or lost here, not in combat.
- **Pursuit.** A company runner or gatekeeper, sent to see who's been
  asking questions at Ammet Ford, following the party rather than
  confronting them — reported back to Corlett, not acted on directly. No
  combat archetype needed; the "encounter" is realising you were followed.
- **Arcana.** A low-tier, unregistered practitioner somewhere in the nine
  unnamed villages — a candle-lighter, a minor charm-worker — worth a scene
  only if a PC has native Arcana of their own and the DM wants to dramatise
  `C2`'s point that being noticed is a cost, not a flex. Optional; skip if
  no PC has the hook.
- **Church operatives.** Brother Aldous and Brother Tam, travelling the
  north road toward Selwick (`source-bible.md`; `C1`) — this Part's only
  on-page Church presence, exactly as `R1a` scoped it. Play them as
  genuinely charitable and faintly wrong (Aldous's collar) rather than as a
  threat; nothing here escalates to a Fourth Mercy site this Part.
  `(SYS?)` for any construct/homunculus stat block if a DM wants to confirm
  what Aldous actually is — `G1` (not yet written) is the right place for
  that, not this file.
- **Pest-class horrors.** The mine is this clock's home address in Region I
  (`R1a` — Pest Pressure Clock). A low-tier undead or lesser horror
  encounter in the older workings or the slag terrace, triggered by new
  digging or a party poking around where the Valley Trust would rather they
  didn't. Archetype: Attrition-scarred, not eldritch — ground that's been
  disturbed, not a Salts leak (see below).
- **Fourth Mercy products.** None in Region I this Part, per `R1a` and
  `C3` — Ascension House is Vantry's. If a DM wants a faint echo, Brother
  Aldous's collar is as far as it should go; anything more belongs to
  Region II.
- **Salts leak.** None. Region I has no thin site (`R1a`, `C3`). If a table
  specifically wants one, that is a Part II (`hooks.md` #4) decision, not
  this Part's to make.

## 6. Set Pieces and Antagonist

### Set piece — The Testing-Team Escort

A scene, not a dungeon: the capping camp (`D1`) needs an escort for a
testing run to one of the unnamed villages, or to Pellick itself. Nothing
about the escort job is secretly dangerous by design — it's exactly the
kind of unglamorous, paying work `A1` describes as the party's ordinary
leverage. The telegraphed tell, honoured every time this scene runs: if the
party has already triggered Reveal C (Corlett knows they're asking
questions), the escort gets quietly delayed, rerouted, or short-staffed by
the Trust — never sabotaged violently, never anything that reads as an
attack, just enough friction that the schedule slips one more week and one
more village doesn't get reached before frost. This is the Part's clearest
dramatisation of `A1` pillar 3 and the cost test (canon §8): nobody has to
die for the valley to lose a week it doesn't have.

### Set piece — The Older Workings

If the Pest Pressure Clock (`C2`) is running high when the party goes near
the mine, a short, contained encounter in the older, disused tunnels: an
Attrition-era collapse disturbed by continued digging, a lesser horror
that's been quiet until now. Not an antagonist in its own right — it's
weather, per `C2` — and it should never escalate into a boss fight. Its
function here is to make the mine feel like a place the Valley Trust has
every reason to want left alone, for reasons that have nothing to do with
the survey, which makes Corlett's stonewalling read as multiply motivated
rather than single-mindedly sinister.

### The Antagonist — Hew Corlett, Administrator

**Gimmick.** Corlett is not a villain in the sense the table will expect
one, and running him as one is the single likeliest way to get this Part
wrong (`C2`'s DM notes apply directly). He inherited a working cover-up and
has kept it working the way he'd keep anything else on schedule: on paper,
through channels, without raising his voice. He sincerely believes — and
says so, if asked sincerely — that reopening the survey now would end the
works, throw several hundred people out of work in a valley with nothing
else to offer them, and change nothing for anyone already sick, since the
water in their bodies is already there. He is not entirely wrong about any
of this, which is the point: the horror is procedural, not personal.

**Telegraphed tell (honour this exactly, every time).** Asked a direct
question about the survey, the Indemnity's timing, or Ammet Ford's well,
Corlett always answers with a true, irrelevant figure first — this
quarter's output, how many families the works employs, how long the glazed
school has been open — before declining, politely and without heat, to go
further. He never lies. He never threatens. He will end a conversation he
finds unproductive by simply standing up. A DM who lets him dodge any other
way (vagueness, menace, a raised voice) has written him wrong; the chill is
supposed to come from how reasonable he sounds doing it.

**What defeat looks like.** Not combat, and not a confession. Corlett is
"defeated" when the party gets enough of Reveals A–C in front of someone
who can act on it and isn't already compromised — Ferrick's sub-panel, the
Indemnity Office, or (if the Press Cycle clock gets triggered) the Vantry
broadsheet. When that happens, the Valley Trust does not fight it in the
open. Corlett is quietly reassigned — "to the coast," echoing the two
inspectors who tried this before him (`source-bible.md`) — and someone else
takes the post within weeks, with no public admission that anything was
wrong. This is `A1` pillar 3 in miniature: the institution is outlasted,
not defeated. The party can win this scene and still not get the water
clean on a timeline that helps Pellick.

**What flight looks like.** If the party moves on partial evidence, or
tips Corlett off before they can act, he doesn't run — he has nowhere more
senior to run to that would help him, and running would itself be an
admission. Instead the paper trail moves: ledgers get "archived," the
travelling clerk's schedule changes, Garrick Fen gets a visit encouraging
him to remember less. The Valley Trust's Consolidation clock (`C2`) jumps
forward, and Reveal B's routes get harder, not impossible, to use again.

**What capture looks like.** If the party corners Corlett physically or
threatens him, he does not fight and does not panic. He offers money — a
restitution figure, in the same register as the Church's own crisis
playbook (`hooks.md` #5), but from the works' own accounts rather than the
Sanctum's — or a job, or both, and means it as a genuine olive branch, not
a bribe he expects to be refused. Holding him, hurting him, or forcing a
confession gets the party nothing further: he has no hidden second ledger,
no secret boss to name, because the whole point of this antagonist is that
there isn't a bigger villain underneath him to reveal. A table that beats
Corlett and expects the valley to change should feel, specifically, how
little that accomplished.

## 7. Salts Thread

None. `R1a` and `C3` already confirm Region I has no thin site; this file
doesn't reopen that. If a table wants to feel the Salts at the edges of
this Part, Stillwell's own unexplained extraction timer (`hooks.md` #2,

gets. The first thin site, when one is built, belongs to Region II

Part's job, not this one's.

## 8. Consequences

What changes on the map and the clocks (`C2`), depending on how the party
plays this Part:

- **The Capping Clock.** Advances on its own regardless of the party.
  Direct help (escorting, testing, pressuring the sub-panel) speeds specific
  villages; triggering the Testing-Team Escort's friction (above) slows it.
  Pellick can still be last, or late, or reached — none of those outcomes
  is wrong, per `A1` pillar 3 and `R1a`'s own DM notes.
- **The Indemnity Mortality Curve.** Unaffected by anything the party does
  here — it only advances on calendar time. Whatever claimants are named
  this Part are already past saving; the only choice is whether the party
  looks at that fact or doesn't.
- **The Valley Trust's Consolidation.** Advances faster if the party pokes
  at the survey, the mine's ledgers, or Corlett without covering their
  tracks (Reveal C, flight outcome above); slower, or briefly reversed in
  one visible way (a clerk reinstated, a ledger recovered), if the party
  wins the "defeat" outcome.
- **The Press Cycle.** Cold at the Part's start. If the party gets Reveal A
  and B's evidence to the Vantry broadsheet or to Ferrick publicly, it
  spins up for eleven days exactly as `C2` describes, then curdles into an
  authenticity dispute regardless of how solid the evidence was — a hard
  ceiling on how far this Part's exposure travels, by design.
- **The Hydro Network Map.** Untouched by this Part unless a DM specifically
  has the party ask where Stillwell's own supply chain runs — nothing here
  requires it, and `R1a` already keeps Stillwell itself secret-free.
- **What the valley looks like afterward.** Whatever the party does or
  doesn't resolve, the mine keeps running, the Valley Trust keeps a name on
  the office door, and the twelve villages are not meaningfully better off
  than `A1`'s opening state unless the party specifically bent a clock in
  their favour. That flatness is the intended outcome, not a failure of
  this file.

## 9. DM Notes

**Failure states are fine.** A party that never finds Garrick Fen, never
pieces together Reveal A, or never gets past Corlett's tell can still
finish this Part — Pellick stays uncapped, the survey stays buried one
layer deeper, and the campaign moves on to Part II with that unresolved,
exactly as the novel left its own loose ends (canon §8, the cost test).
Don't manufacture a fourth route to force a stalled reveal through; check
`C1`'s reachability note first — if three routes genuinely aren't enough,
that's a sign Region I needs another keyed site, not a contrivance here.

**What to withhold.** Corlett's name and role should not surface before
Reveal C's own routes earn it — a party that short-circuits straight to him
without any of Reveal A or B in hand should find him exactly as
unforthcoming as his tell promises, with nothing yet to leverage against
it. The Sweetwater double meaning (D-17 above) is DM-known ground truth,
not a thing the players are owed by default; if they never ask the right
question, the valley's water stays a rumour to them, same as it stayed to
the novel's own cast for most of the book.

**How the novel's three appear.** `I1` (Dramatis Personae, cameo rules) does
not exist yet. Until it does, do not run Lloyd, Maria, or Edie as speaking
NPCs in this Part — a name on a wanted notice (Callow Ford, `R1a`), a
mention in passing, nothing with dialogue. Ivet Hale is the one exception
`R1a` already permits, and only within the limits canon §6 sets for her
(she knows nothing of Lloyd's second arm, his Carrowgate institutionalising,
or his intent toward Crane). Treat Corlett, Mirrin Tope, Nessa Beddoe, and
Garrick Fen as this Part's actual cast — none of them need the novel's
three to carry a session.

**Nobody here is owed a rescue, including from Corlett.** Per `R1a`'s own
note and `A1` pillar 3: winning the "defeat" outcome above is a real win
and it still doesn't clean the valley's water on a timeline Pellick can
use. Play that straight rather than softening it with an off-screen bonus
fix.

**Vertical slice flag.** Per `STATUS.md`, once `R1b` lands, Part I's
remaining vertical-slice items are `A2` (DM's Guide), `B1` (Player's
Primer), `G1` (Bestiary, Part I), and `H1` (Compendium, Part I) — all
unblocked by this file. When those four are done, flag the vertical slice
complete to Jake per `STATUS.md`'s own marker row before continuing to
Part II.
