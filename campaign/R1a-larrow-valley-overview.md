## 1. Premise Card

**Region:** The Larrow Valley (`C1` Region I).

**Level band:** Starting characters — the campaign's vertical slice opens
here. `(SYS?)`: the exact numeric range waits on the ruleset's tier bands
landing in `campaign/system/` (Q-1); until then, build and run this Part as
"new characters, nothing earned yet."

**One-line hook:** the water still isn't clean, the schedule that's supposed
to fix it moves too slowly for at least one village left, and somebody at
Beddoe's just put the party's name against a job that walks straight at the
one subject the Valley Trust won't discuss.

**What the surface wants.** Ferrick's sub-panel wants every one of the
twelve villages tested and capped before frost, and knows, out loud, that it
won't make it to all of them in time (`C2` — Capping Clock). The Indemnity's
claimants want to live to their seventh year; most don't. Nessa Beddoe and
everyone else extending credit in the valley want their slates paid down.
The Valley Trust wants the capping schedule to succeed just enough that
nobody asks why the groundwater survey underneath it all was never
reopened.

**What the Church wants.** Nothing in Region I confirms a Fourth Mercy site
— Ascension House is Vantry's, not the valley's (`C1`). What the Church
wants here is smaller and exactly as described in `C2`: bread and shelter,
travelling the same roads as the capping schedule, quietly noticing the
valley's surplus — the grieving, the sick, the unclaimed, anyone a village
has already stopped asking after. None of it closes into a site a party can
raid this Part. It's the same pipeline that fed Ascension House for sixty
years, running exactly as patiently as always, one village over from
wherever the party is standing. A Mercy brother on the north road (`C1`,
Brother Aldous and Brother Tam's route) is this Part's only Church presence
on the page; treat anything larger as Region IV's problem, not this one's.

## 2. Situation

A year after ch. 25, the valley is being repaired the way `A1` describes
reconstruction generally: slowly, visibly, and not all the way. Five of
`C2`'s six clocks are live and moving in Region I now; the sixth (the Press
Cycle) is cold until the party causes, or nearly causes, an exposure.

- **The Capping Clock** is the valley's defining fact on the ground. Some
  villages have clean water and know it; some don't yet and know that too —
  this is not a secret schedule, it's a public one that simply isn't
  finished. Ammet Ford was never on it; its well was never the problem
  (`C1`). Harrow's Weir sits early-to-mid; Pellick (below) is last, by
  Ferrick's own sub-panel's admission, and knows it.
- **The Indemnity Mortality Curve** runs underneath every village with a
  claim filed, invisible to a passerby and completely legible to anyone who
  asks Nessa Beddoe, or any innkeeper like her, who's still owed for a
  funeral. It does not need the party to advance; it advances on its own,
  and the party's only choice is whether to look at it.
- **The Hydro Network Map** sits at one (Stillwell; everything else is
  rumour) until the party starts asking where a hydro's supply actually
  goes. Nothing in Region I forces this forward.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock**, Larrow Valley instance, sits at its quietest
  where nothing's been dug lately and climbs wherever the mine keeps
  working — which, per `C2`, is constantly. Treat the mine's own tunnels as
  this clock's home address in this Part.
- **The Valley Trust's Consolidation** advances on calendar time and faster
  the moment anyone pokes at the survey or the Indemnity Office without
  covering their tracks — which in Region I means Ammet Ford's company
  office and the mine's own ledgers, both below.

**Who holds what.** The Valley Trust (`C2`) holds the mine, Ammet Ford's
showpiece, and the company ledgers — quiet management, cooperating on the
parts that cost it little, stonewalling the survey. Ferrick's sub-panel
holds the capping schedule and, wherever its mobile camp currently sits
(`D1`), the valley's single best up-to-date read on which villages are
clean. Nessa Beddoe and Harrow's Weir's slate hold the valley's actual
gossip, debts, and leverage — more current than anything posted at the
Indemnity Office. Stillwell holds nothing but its own neutrality, which in
a valley this entangled is itself a kind of power. The current anchor sits
in early autumn; frost, and the Capping Clock's real deadline, is some six
to eight weeks off — close enough to feel, not close enough to force a
session-one crisis.

## 3. Locations

Keyed sites for Region I. Each gives purpose, NPCs, sensory tells, secrets,
and exits, per `PLAN.md`'s Part template. Geography, travel times, and
anything already fixed by `C1` or `D1` are cited, not repeated.

### Stillwell Hydro

**Purpose.** Neutral ground; a base candidate (`D1`); the valley's one
confirmed Salts access point, sold as a nerve cure — **not** this Part's
route to a thin site (`C3`). A good place for a scene nobody should
overhear.

**NPCs.** The reception clerk — a fixture, never statted, never developed
into a character arc (`DECISIONS.md` D-6). No other NPC here is worth
naming; per D-6, keep it that way.

**Sensory tells.** Granite and pine gone soft with damp; peeling plaster;
iodine-stained rags on the seaward wall; the extraction timer's clockwork
tick in the steam-room corridor, still reading some figure against "DAYS
REMAINING" that nobody on staff will explain (`hooks.md` #2 — leave it
unexplained here too).

**Secrets.** None at this site itself — Stillwell's whole function in this
Part is that it has none to find. If a DM wants a secret at Stillwell,
that's a sign the scene belongs at a different location instead.

**Exits.** Coast road south out of the valley (unmapped — Region IV's
problem); under a day on foot to the valley's edge, where the trunk rail
and coach road both begin (`C1`).

### Callow Ford

**Purpose.** Lloyd's home village; a complication generator, not a quest
hub. Any party that works with or near Lloyd as an NPC inherits the fact
that he's barred from here under a false wanted name.

**NPCs.** Ivet Hale — recovering, on a new standpipe, steadier than four
years of the old water should have left her (`source-bible.md`). She is a
person with her own life, not a Lloyd-information dispenser; she knows
nothing about his second arm, his five weeks in Carrowgate, or his intent
toward Crane, and nothing she says should imply otherwise (canon §6).

**Sensory tells.** The old well, capped now; the new standpipe still bright
where everything else in the village has weathered; a wanted notice for
Lloyd under his false name, posted at the same board as the capping
schedule.

**Secrets.** The village knows Lloyd didn't do what the notice says and
mostly keeps that to itself — not loyalty exactly, more the same instinct
that kept the old well's water quiet for years: outside attention has never
once helped anyone here.

**Exits.** River-adjacent; cart track to Harrow's Weir downriver, footpath
upriver toward Ammet Ford and the mine.

### Ammet Ford

**Purpose.** The showpiece — new brick, slate roofs, a glazed school, a
company well whose water nobody who drinks it can quite stop wanting more
of (`C1`). This Part's best site for the Sweetwater double meaning
(`hooks.md` #1) if R1b's lead web wants to use it; nothing below commits to
that reading, since the lead web itself is R1b's call.

**NPCs.** No administrator is named here — `DECISIONS.md` D-12 explicitly
leaves that invention to the Part level, which is `R1b`, not this file.
What exists for now: a schoolmistress who teaches the glazed school's
unusually healthy-looking children and has never once connected that to the
well; a company gatekeeper who logs who comes and goes without much
curiosity about why. Neither is named; name them only when a scene actually
needs to use one by name.

**Sensory tells.** Poured-concrete roads and culverts where the other
villages have mud; a thickness and hush at the back of the throat from the
water, the same the book describes (`source-bible.md`); children who look
better-fed than their parents did at that age, which nobody in Ammet Ford
reads as strange.

**Secrets.** The well's water is never confirmed on the page as the same
substance as the valley's Sweetwater disease — only named the same by
people who drink one and fear the other. Whether that's coincidence, cover,
or something the Valley Trust itself doesn't fully understand is R1b's
call, not this file's.

**Exits.** The valley's one rail halt sits here — a single platform the
Works built for its own convenience and never advertised past the valley's
edge, which is why nobody outside Region I thinks of the Larrow Valley as
having a station at all. Upriver footpath to the mine and works; downriver
cart track to Harrow's Weir.

### The Mine and the Works

**Purpose.** The valley's actual economy and the source of Crane's fortune;
lightly detailed on the page by design (`C1`) — the novel cared what the
works did to the water, not its floor plan. Left open for `R1b`'s set
pieces.

**NPCs.** A foreman and a company-office clerk keep the place running day to
day; neither is the Valley Trust's administrator (see Ammet Ford, above —
same deferral, same reason). Mine labour is older and plainer-housed than
Ammet Ford's showpiece workers, and knows it.

**Sensory tells.** A slag terrace visibly higher than it was a year ago;
worker housing that hasn't seen the Works' money the way Ammet Ford has;
company ledgers kept under whatever name now heads them, still Crane's
handwriting in the older volumes.

**Secrets.** The buried groundwater survey and the Larrow Indemnity's
seventh-year clause both trace back to this office (`source-bible.md`).
Whoever inherited the two intermediaries Crane owned the works through is a
loose thread `C2` already names as inventable at the Part level — `R1b`'s
to pick up or leave.

**Exits.** Upriver dead end past the works — nothing north of the mine
until the ground climbs out of the valley proper. Footpath down to Ammet
Ford.

### Harrow's Weir

**Purpose.** The starting base's village (`D1` — Beddoe's). Downriver of the
mine, where runoff settles first and the Indemnity's seventh-year claims
cluster thickest. This file does not restate Beddoe's interior — `D1`
already covers Nessa, the slate, the common room, and the three slate
entries in full.

**NPCs.** Nessa Beddoe (`D1`). No one new added here; Beddoe's own entry is
the complete picture of this village's people worth naming yet.

**Sensory tells.** Per `D1` — the cooperage's barrel racks still lining one
wall, the stove that's the warmest room in the village October to April.

**Secrets.** Per `D1` — the slate itself, and the standing two-crown bounty
on whoever's been asking after the buried survey, which the party may
discover describes them.

**Exits.** Cart track up to the mine and Ammet Ford; river crossing toward
Callow Ford; the long road north, unmarked on any village sign, that
eventually reaches Pellick and, beyond it, Hessel Bight.

### Pellick

**Purpose.** The furthest village from Stillwell, last on Ferrick's capping
schedule — the single clearest place in Region I to make "the money exists,
it doesn't reach the valleys" (canon §5) personal rather than statistical.

**NPCs.** **Mirrin Tope**, an old collier's widow, and her grandson —
invented here to give the capping schedule's lateness a face rather than a
statistic; logged as a new session default, `DECISIONS.md` D-16. The boy
has had the Sweetwater since spring; Mirrin knows exactly how many villages
are ahead of hers on the schedule and says so to anyone who asks, flatly,
the same register as Nessa mentioning her own husband's Indemnity math.
Neither of them is owed a rescue — per `A1` pillar 3, this Part should be
able to end with Pellick still waiting, and that should cost something, not
nothing.

**Sensory tells.** A well with a hand-chalked tally of days since the last
testing-team visit; a poorer, smaller version of every other village's
same complaints, with nobody local left with the energy to make them loudly.

**Secrets.** None invented beyond what's already true — Pellick's only
secret is that nobody with power has been looking at it closely enough to
find one, which is itself the point.

**Exits.** Northward road to Hessel Bight, the last leg before the valley's
edge.

### Hessel Bight

**Purpose.** Rail town, roughly four days north of Stillwell by road (`C1`'s
one hard-given travel distance) — the valley's practical northern edge and
this Part's exit toward the Northern Road and, eventually, Vantry.

**NPCs.** None named on the page or needed here; this is a transit location,
not a community the Part should spend much time characterising.

**Sensory tells.** A flooded, war-damaged manse with eleven of the
Attrition's dead cleared from its cellar once already, as paid "wet work"
(`source-bible.md`) — the kind of site that can need clearing again if the
Pest Pressure Clock climbs high enough locally.

**Secrets.** Whether the manse's cellar is actually clear is a standing
question this file leaves open on purpose; a DM short an encounter site has
one sitting here already, fully licensed by the book's own precedent.

**Exits.** The trunk rail and the coach road both run north from here
through Tannery Reach, Sennet, and the Selwick turning toward Vantry (`C1`
— Region II, Part II's territory); the half-day Carrowgate changeover
(`C1` → D-11) is reachable from the same junction without detouring through
Vantry first.

### The Other Villages

Per `C1`'s own restraint: the remaining eight villages stay unnamed and
unsketched beyond what's already fixed — smaller and poorer than Ammet
Ford, somewhere on the Capping Clock between Harrow's Weir and Pellick, no
reason given why one got reached before another. Name and detail one only
when `R1b`'s lead web actually needs a specific site this file doesn't
already provide; inventing all eight now would be exactly the kind of
structure `DECISIONS.md`'s "pick a defensible default" licence isn't meant
to cover.

## 4. The Valley's Reach — Travel Summary

Per `C1`'s Map and `S1`'s "Real Travel" rule (travel takes the time `C1`
says it takes, and something happens during it): Stillwell to the valley's
edge, under a day on foot. Ammet Ford's rail halt reaches the trunk line
directly, but the halt was built for the Works' own convenience, not public
service — a party using it is visibly travelling on the Valley Trust's
infrastructure, which is its own small complication, not a free pass.
Harrow's Weir, Callow Ford, the mine, and Pellick are cart-and-footpath
country, strung along the river and the north road in that order. Hessel
Bight, four days north of Stillwell by road, is the valley's exit toward
Region II. `F1` (not yet written) will set the exact per-leg crown costs and
encounter frequency; this file only fixes which locations sit where relative
to each other.

## 5. DM Notes

**Nobody here is owed a rescue.** Per `A1` pillar 3 and the cost test
(canon §8), Pellick's lateness, the Indemnity's dead claimants, and Ammet
Ford's uninvestigated well can all still be true at the end of this Part.
Resolving all of it would be a cleaner valley than the source material
built.

**Don't pre-empt R1b.** This file deliberately leaves the Valley Trust's
administrator unnamed, the Sweetwater double meaning unconfirmed, and the
other eight villages unsketched. Those are R1b's lead web's job to use or
leave; naming them here would take away a choice that session should get to
make with the actual investigation in hand.

**The rail halt at Ammet Ford is new geography, not canon.** Nothing on the
page says where the valley's trunk-rail stop sits; placing it at Ammet Ford
rather than, say, Stillwell or Harrow's Weir reinforces the showpiece
reading `C1` already set up and is logged as part of D-16 below. Move it if
a later session finds a better reason to put it somewhere else.

**Play the schedule's lateness as policy, not malice.** Ferrick's sub-panel
isn't failing Pellick out of cruelty — it's underfunded and moving in order,
exactly as `C2` already plays it. The frustration this Part should generate
is at the shape of the problem, not at a single villain withholding help.
