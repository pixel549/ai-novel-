# R4a — PART IV: THE FOURTH MERCY'S WIDER NETWORK — OVERVIEW + LOCATIONS

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C1-gazetteer.md` → Region IV,
`C2-factions-clocks.md`, and `C3-the-salts-as-background.md` — read all four
first; this file does not restate the premise, the pillars, the factions,
the clocks, or the Salts lore. `C1`'s own Region IV entry explicitly defers
locating this region until this file exists ("Build them when R4a is
actually written, not before") — that deferral ends here: this file extends
`C1`'s Map past Vantry and names the sites `C1` left blank, the same way
`R1a`/`R2a`/`R3a` keyed geography `C1` had already drawn. Also cites
`R3b-carrowgate-leads-antagonist.md` → §8 (Consequences) for how Part III's
clocks may have moved by the time this Part opens. Follows `PLAN.md`'s Part
template, sections 1–3 only (Premise card, Situation, Locations) — sections
4–9 (lead web, encounters, set pieces and antagonist, the Salts thread,
consequences, DM notes on failure states) are `R4b`'s job, next session, now
unblocked by this file. Mechanics are archetype and tier only; anything
needing a hard number is `(SYS?)` (Q-1).*

## 1. Premise Card

**Region:** The Fourth Mercy's wider network (`C1` Region IV), now located —
a branch line and the fen country at its end, forking off the Vantry
junction where `C1`'s own Map stopped drawing.

**Level band:** `(SYS?)` — one notch past Part III's own, same holding
pattern `R1a`/`R2a`/`R3a` used: build and run this Part for a party three
Parts into the campaign, or one step past Part III's own assumptions if a
table begins here instead. Exact numeric range waits on the ruleset's tier
bands landing in `campaign/system/` (Q-1).

**One-line hook:** a rumour that won't die down — more brine-cart traffic
passing through a railway siding than a dying nerve-cure trade could
possibly use — points the party past the last stop any map in this
campaign has bothered to name, to a Church charity nobody local has a bad
word for and a hydro nobody outside the fen has heard of.

**What the surface wants.** Oldbridge and Lissom Fen want nothing to do with
whatever brought the party out this far. The House of Quiet Rest is the one
unambiguously good thing either settlement can point to — it takes in men
the war broke and nobody else will keep, feeds them, and asks nothing back
— and most of the fen is sincerely, personally grateful to it in a way
`C1` Region I's Ammet Ford or Region II's lodging rows never managed for
their own versions of Church charity. A family with a relative who went in
and was never heard from again wants a straight answer more than they want
the place closed; closing it, to them, reads as taking away the only door
their son or brother had left. Greymere Hydro's proprietor wants paying
customers and no trouble, same as every hydro in `C2`, and is the person in
this Part most likely to know exactly what the extra brine is for and say
nothing, for a fee that's kept the business afloat for years.

**What the Church wants.** To never again have what happened at Ascension
House happen to anything it runs. The Vantry exposure (ch. 18; `hooks.md`
#5; `C2`'s Press Cycle) is this Part's standing lesson, learned the hard way
and never discussed aloud: fewer sites, each smaller, each compartmented so
that most of the staff — Sister Prue Anstey among them, see below — believe
exactly what the house's front door says and nothing more. Doctrine still
holds the ascension work will eventually succeed (canon §4); what's changed
since ch. 18 is operational discipline, not conviction. Per `C1`'s own
restraint on Region IV: nothing here gives the party a second Sanctum to
burn. A second site extends the institution's reach. It does not invert
"outlasted or escaped, not defeated" (`A1` pillar 3) — if `R4b` ever closes
the House of Quiet Rest, the Church absorbs it exactly the way it absorbed
ch. 18, and doctrine survives the building.

## 2. Situation

Call it a further two to three months past wherever a table's own Part III
ended — the same loose, generous, non-binding convention `R2a` set and
`R3a` extended, extended again rather than replaced. This file doesn't fix
Part III's outcome and doesn't need to: nothing below assumes Pell and Vane
were exposed, fled, or captured, or that the Indemnity Office's file on
Crane's death was ever opened. `K1` (not yet written) will eventually carry
the running continuity index `A1` calls for; until it exists, a DM carries
each Part's actual ending forward by memory or notes, and this Part is
written to work regardless of what any of the first three were.

Of `C2`'s six clocks, three matter here in a shape none of the first three
Parts gave them:

- **The Hydro Network Map** stops being a rumour clock and becomes this
  Part's actual spine. It started at one confirmed hydro (Stillwell;
  `C2`) and advances only through play — this file is where the second
  name finally lands. Per `C2`'s own deferral ("which is which is a
  Part-level decision, not fixed here"), this session decides it:
  **Greymere Hydro supplies the Fourth Mercy**, not just the nerve-cure
  trade. Logged as a session default rather than decided silently — see
  `DECISIONS.md` D-24.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock**, this region's instance, runs the opposite
  shape from Carrowgate's (`R3a`): the capital's ground was paved,
  surveyed, and maintained, concentrating pressure into the few places
  order couldn't reach. The fen is the reverse — unsurveyed, barely
  drained, criss-crossed by an old defensive line from the Attrition that
  nobody has filled in because nobody out here has the money to (see "The
  Fen Line" below). Pressure here is diffuse rather than concentrated:
  worse in more places, each instance individually weaker, `(SYS?)` for
  the actual numbers.
- **The Press Cycle**, if it is still spinning from whatever the party
  caused earlier in the campaign, is the one clock whose current state
  actually changes how guarded this site is. A Church still mid-crisis
  elsewhere runs this house more carefully than one that has gone quiet
  again; a DM should treat the Press Cycle's current position (wherever a
  table left it) as directly setting how much slack the House of Quiet
  Rest's staff have been given to bend their own compartmenting this
  month. If no exposure has happened yet, this site is simply operating at
  its normal, already-cautious baseline.

**Who holds what.** The Church holds the House of Quiet Rest itself, and —
per the lesson above — holds it more carefully than it held Ascension
House: fewer staff know the whole shape of it, and the ones who do are
Carrowgate appointments, not locals. The fen community holds goodwill
toward the House that is entirely genuine and entirely a liability for any
party that arrives looking to expose it; nobody here is lying to themselves
about what it's given their families. Greymere Hydro's proprietor holds
the one piece of hard, physical evidence that connects this charity to
anything else — a supply ledger, a cart's manifest, a destination that
doesn't match the business's own stated size — and has every reason to
keep holding it quietly rather than handing it over. Oldbridge holds the
only rail link in or out, which makes it this Part's chokepoint for
anyone's comings and goings, Church included.

## 3. Locations

Keyed sites for Region IV. Each gives purpose, NPCs, sensory tells,
secrets, and exits, per `PLAN.md`'s Part template. Geography invented here
extends `C1`'s Map rather than contradicting it — see §4 below for how the
pieces connect.

### Oldbridge

**Purpose.** Where the Vantry trunk's northern branch — one of the lines
`C1`'s Map already says meets at Vantry's own junction — stops being
through-traffic and becomes a local stub serving the fen country beyond.
The last town on any rail line in this campaign, and this Part's natural
gateway and chokepoint both.

**NPCs.** **Rufus Pledge**, a former signalman whose knees ended that
career before his nerve for the work did, runs **the Night Siding**, a
converted signal-cabin-and-yard-office turned lodging and taproom — this
region's invented foothold, following the naming convention `D1`, `R1a`,
`R2a`, and `R3a` all set (a specific, named proprietor rather than an
abstraction), logged as part of `DECISIONS.md` D-24. He watches the branch
line's traffic the way his old job trained him to and notices exactly the
kind of irregular cart manifest this Part's lead web needs noticed, without
ever drawing the conclusion himself.

**Sensory tells.** A platform too long for the traffic it actually gets;
a goods shed with more locked doors than the stated freight would need;
timetables chalked rather than printed, because printing one for three
trains a day isn't worth the ink.

**Secrets.** Pledge's own ledger of who got off here and where they said
they were headed is informal, handwritten, and more complete than anything
the Church would want kept — not because he's hunting anything, but
because a man who spent thirty years logging train movements doesn't stop
just because he's pulling pints instead. A patient party that earns his
trust (not his fear — `C2`'s DM notes on playing ordinary people sincerely
apply here as much as anywhere) gets Greymere's cart traffic cross-checked
against his own arrivals board for free.

**Exits.** The branch line back to Vantry's junction (half a day, per §4);
a cart track on to Lissom Fen and the House, no rail beyond this point.

### Lissom Fen

**Purpose.** A scattered fen-edge settlement — turf cutters, a handful of
smallholdings, nobody's showpiece and nobody's slum — whose entire visible
relationship to the Church runs through the House of Quiet Rest's charity.
This Part's clearest demonstration that bread and shelter identifying the
surplus (canon §4) can look, from inside the community it's happening to,
exactly like the only kindness anyone's shown them in years.

**NPCs.** No single administrator; invent smallholders and turf cutters per
scene, in the sincere register `C2`'s DM notes set for every ordinary face
in this campaign. A Mercy sister or brother keeps an informal bread-and-
shelter post here on the two days a week the House's own wagon comes
through — the same mechanism `R2a`/`R3a` describe for their own Mercy
Houses, scaled down to fit a settlement this size, and the point at which a
family with a relative who "went in" first learns to say the phrase.

**Sensory tells.** A bread queue two days a week instead of daily, the only
schedule in the fen anyone keeps exactly; turf stacks drying against
cottage walls; a parish noticeboard with more names of men who "went in for
their nerves" than names of men who came back out named on it.

**Secrets.** Nobody here is hiding anything on purpose — the secret, such
as it is, is that "went in for his nerves" has become the fen's own
euphemism for an outcome almost nobody actually follows up on, the same
shape of evidence-exists-evidence-doesn't-matter problem `hooks.md` #3
describes for Vantry's archive, just smaller and without a single filing
cabinet to point to. A party asking after a specific name gets sympathy,
a direction to the House, and no one who thinks to ask what happened next.

**Exits.** A cart track back to Oldbridge; a further walk, longer than it
looks on a flat fen road, out to the House of Quiet Rest itself.

### The House of Quiet Rest

**Purpose.** A Church-run convalescent retreat for men the Attrition broke
and nobody else will keep — officially, and genuinely, exactly that. Also,
per this file's own session default (`DECISIONS.md` D-24), the second
ascension site `C1` Region IV promised: smaller than Ascension House, far
better compartmented, built from the lesson ch. 18 taught the Church rather
than from the same confidence that built Vantry's. Where Ascension House
ran five rooms in a known order for a ward Maria toured in a single chapter
(ch. 14; `source-bible.md`), this house runs two — the public wards anyone
with a reason to visit can see, and a locked undercroft almost nobody on
staff knows exists.

**NPCs.** **Sister Prue Anstey** runs the public face of the house day to
day — sincere, warm, personally exhausted in the specific way of someone
who has spent years doing real, difficult, uncompensated work for people
nobody else wanted. She does not know what the undercroft is; she has
never been shown it and has never asked, the same ordinary not-knowing
`C2`'s Church entry assumes for most of its own people. Per `G1`'s own open
construct question (`hooks.md` #9), whether any specific Mercy figure here
is quite what they appear is this Part's call to make, not this file's —
`R4b` can build on Anstey or leave her exactly as human as she reads.
Whoever actually runs the undercroft is deliberately unnamed here, same
restraint `R1a`/`R2a`/`R3a` held for their own Parts' antagonists —
`R4b`'s to build.

**Sensory tells.** A front hall that is, without qualification, warm:
blankets, a fire kept lit, men playing cards badly with hands that shake.
Nothing here should read as sinister on sight — the menace, per canon §3's
rule for Vail and `C2`'s extension of it to the Church generally, lives
only in content, never staging. The undercroft's own single tell, for
anyone who goes looking, is mundane rather than eldritch: a door that's
locked when every other interior door in the building isn't, on a wing the
public wards don't open onto.

**Secrets.** The undercroft holds this Part's actual ascension work,
compressed and cautious compared to Ascension House's five-room tour:
`R4b`'s to detail, not this file's, per the same split `R1a`/`R2a`/`R3a`
used for their own Parts' antagonist content. What this file commits to:
nothing here changes canon's "everyone senior survives" or the Sanctum's
untouched status (`DECISIONS.md` D-1); whatever `R4b` builds, closing this
site is a local, costly, incomplete victory, not a second Sanctum falling.
This file also flags, without building, that an older structure beneath
the house's own foundations — unconfirmed, unexcavated, exactly the kind
of detail `hooks.md` #4 already modelled at Ascension House — is this
region's strongest remaining candidate for one of the two unplaced thin
sites (`T2`, `T3`); `R4b` can use or decline this, same restraint `R2a` used
in flagging Ascension House's own ruin for `T1`.

**Exits.** A fen road back to Lissom Fen; a service track, rutted and
little-used by anyone but the house's own cart, running the other way
toward Greymere.

### Greymere Hydro

**Purpose.** A hydro in the Stillwell genre — per `C1`'s Hydros entry,
peeling respectability, batches of three, a numbered token, a reception
clerk who is never statted (`DECISIONS.md` D-6) — run several sizes down
from Stillwell and several decades further from new paint. Its nerve-cure
trade is real and shrinking; its actual business, per this file's session
default, is the brine and reagent the House of Quiet Rest's undercroft
needs and the fen's own small customer base could never justify shipping
in this volume.

**NPCs.** A proprietor (unnamed; `R4b`'s to name if a scene needs one, same
deferral `R1b`/`R2b`/`R3b` used for their own Parts' administrators and
clerks) who has kept the business solvent for years on an arrangement
nobody local asks about out loud. Steam-room attendants and an intake
clerk run the visible trade exactly as Stillwell's do — ordinary, tired,
not party to whatever the back manifest actually says.

**Sensory tells.** Granite-and-pine gone soft at the edges, a worse version
of Stillwell's own iodine-stained seaward wall; a loading door at the back
that sees more cart traffic on a slow nerve-cure week than the front door
does on a busy one — Rufus Pledge's own noticing, writ larger here.

**Secrets.** The manifest itself — what's shipped, how much, and to
where — is the single most direct piece of hard evidence in this entire
Part, and per `C2`'s Hydro Network Map clock, finding and confirming it is
most of what that clock is actually for. Whether the proprietor hands it
over, sells it, or stonewalls is `R4b`'s lead web to build; this file only
fixes that the manifest exists and that it says what §2 above already
decided it says.

**Exits.** The service track back to the House of Quiet Rest; a longer,
little-used cart road the rest of the way out to the old coast, unmapped
past this point per `C1`'s own restraint on Region IV and V both.

### The Fen Line

**Purpose.** An abandoned Attrition-era defensive trench system cutting
straight through the fen a short walk from Lissom Fen — not a set piece in
its own right, a located instance of the Pest Pressure Clock (`C2`), the
same way Red Redoubt is backstory-only for Edie and Hessel Bight's manse
was a one-scene clearing job (ch. 7). Nineteen months' peace and no money
to fill it back in has left it exactly as disturbed as it was the day the
war ended.

**NPCs.** None fixed; this is weather, not a faction (`C2`), and any
encounter here is archetype-and-tier per `G4` (not yet written) rather than
a named cast.

**Sensory tells.** Collapsed duckboard still visible under standing water;
a rusted signpost reading a unit designation nobody local recognises
anymore; turf cutters who avoid the deepest stretch of line without being
able to say exactly why, the same unspoken avoidance `R3a` notes for the
Senate's own groundskeepers, scaled to a community with far less to lose by
saying so out loud if asked.

**Secrets.** Nothing keyed here beyond what the Pest Pressure Clock already
governs — at low pressure, nothing; at high pressure, an encounter drawn
from a worse tier `(SYS?)`, per `C2`'s own mechanism. This file does not
invent a monster to live here specifically; `G4` can, if a Part needs one.

**Exits.** A short walk from Lissom Fen in one direction; the fen simply
continues, unmapped, in the other.

## 4. Region IV's Reach — Travel Summary

Per `C1`'s Map: a day by rail from Carrowgate to Vantry, three or four by
road — unchanged by anything in this file. From Vantry's own junction,
where the lines from the north and east already meet (`C1`), the branch
this file names runs a further half-day by rail to Oldbridge, the last
stop any timetable in the fen bothers to print. Past Oldbridge, there is no
rail: a half-day's cart track reaches Lissom Fen, a further hour or so on
foot reaches the House of Quiet Rest, and the service track beyond that
reaches Greymere Hydro inside the same afternoon if the weather holds. The
Fen Line sits a short walk from Lissom Fen in the opposite direction from
the House. Nothing past Greymere is mapped — that stays Part V's problem,
per `C1`'s own restraint, not something this file resolves by extending the
line any further than this Part actually needs. `F1` (not yet written)
sets the exact per-leg costs; this file only fixes that the fen's own
geography is slower and poorer-served than any region played so far,
which is itself part of the point.

## 5. DM Notes

**This Part's antagonist pressure, if `R4b` builds one, is institutional
first, personal second — same discipline as Corlett, Wick, and Pell/Vane.**
Whoever actually runs the House's undercroft should read, if named at all,
as someone doing compartmented, careful work rather than a mastermind;
Sister Anstey's own genuine goodness is the point, not a disguise to see
through.

**Don't pre-empt R4b.** This file deliberately leaves the undercroft's
actual contents, its overseer, Greymere's proprietor, and the thin-site
candidate beneath the House all unbuilt — `R4b`'s lead web's job to use or
leave, same restraint every earlier Part's "a" file showed for its own open
threads.

**Region I–III's clocks are not this Part's business, except where this
file already says otherwise.** The Capping Clock, the Indemnity Mortality
Curve, and the Valley Trust's Consolidation stay exactly where the party
left them — none of the three has any Larrow Valley geography out here to
advance against. The Hydro Network Map, the Pest Pressure Clock, and
(conditionally) the Press Cycle are this Part's actual three, per §2 above.

**A second ascension site is not a second Sanctum.** Per `C1` Region IV's
own instruction, repeated here because it's the single easiest thing to
get wrong in this Part: nothing below, and nothing `R4b` should build,
makes closing the House of Quiet Rest equivalent to toppling the Church.
Doctrine survives the building, the same as it survived ch. 15's fire; the
institution is outlasted or escaped here too (`A1` pillar 3), not defeated.

**Lloyd, Maria, and Edie do not appear in this Part, even restrictively.**
`A1`'s timeline anchor places Edie in Vantry and Lloyd and Maria in or near
Carrowgate; nothing puts any of the three out on the fen line, and this
file invents no reason to put them there. Unlike `R3a`'s restraint around a
plausible sighting, this Part doesn't need that restraint exercised at
all — the question simply doesn't arise.

**Handle the House's population with canon's own restraint, not drama.**
`A2`'s lines-and-veils section already flags war-trauma backstory and
institutional neglect as sensitive content this campaign's source material
surfaces; this Part is where both come up hardest. Per canon §7, horror
here is catalogued, not lurid — a discharge register's cold "result
negative, subject retained" phrasing (`source-bible.md` → Places, Fourth
Mercy facility layout) does more work at this table than any description
of a specific man's condition would, and should be preferred to one.
