# R5a — PART V: THE OLD COAST — OVERVIEW + LOCATIONS

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C1-gazetteer.md` → Region V,
`C2-factions-clocks.md`, `C3-the-salts-as-background.md`, and
`R4a-fourth-mercy-network-overview.md`/`R4b-fourth-mercy-leads-antagonist.md`
— read all five first; this file does not restate the premise, the pillars,
the factions, the clocks, or the Salts lore. `C1`'s own Region V entry
deliberately left this region blank ("dependent on everything R4b decides
... filling this in before R4b exists would be the kind of invented
structure `DECISIONS.md`'s... licence isn't meant to cover") — that
deferral ends here, the same way `R4a` ended `C1`'s deferral on Region IV.
This file also picks up the specific thread `R4a` itself left dangling: "a
longer, little-used cart road the rest of the way out to the old coast,
unmapped past this point per `C1`'s own restraint on Region IV and V both."
Region V is that coast. Follows `PLAN.md`'s Part template, sections 1–3
only (Premise card, Situation, Locations) — sections 4–9 (lead web,
encounters, set pieces and antagonist, the Salts thread, consequences, DM

## 1. Premise Card

**Region:** The Old Coast (`C1` Region V, now located) — the stretch of
shoreline Greymere Hydro's own service track peters out toward, past every
line any map in this campaign has bothered to draw.

**Level band:** `(SYS?)` — one notch past Part IV's own, same holding
pattern `R1a`–`R4a` used: this is the campaign's finale region, built for a
party that has played through as much or as little of Parts I–IV as a
table actually ran. Exact numeric range waits on the ruleset's tier bands
landing in `campaign/system/` (Q-1).

**One-line hook:** the sixty years canon keeps score of — every failure,
named and dated — were all logged somewhere before anyone thought to build
a Vantry ward or a fen retreat to log them in, and the woman who has kept
that first, complete record since before most of the Sanctum's current
clergy were born is still there, still filing, in a village nobody's
reconstruction money has ever had a reason to find.

**What the surface wants.** Hollowmere, the one settlement this stretch of
coast still supports, wants exactly what it has had for longer than anyone
living there can date precisely: to be left alone, and to keep being paid
for it. A retainer comes down from Fallow Head every quarter, reliable as
weather, and has done since before the oldest person in the village was
born — the one place in this entire campaign where Church money *does*
reach where it's promised, precisely because discretion out here is worth
more to the Church than it is anywhere else (a pointed inversion of the
Capping Clock's chronic lateness, worth the DM making explicit at the
table). Nobody in Hollowmere asks what the retainer is for. Most genuinely
don't know; the ones who suspect have had sixty years to decide it isn't
their business.

**What the Church wants.** Nothing done about Fallow Head at all — not
defended, not reinforced, not even remembered by most of the Sanctum's own
current clergy as more than a line item. The site's danger to the Church
isn't operational, the way Ness's undercroft or Ascension House's tanks
were; it's documentary. Doctrine survives on "sixty years, every failure
documented... it will eventually work" staying a sentence rather than
becoming a read, itemised ledger. Nobody actively watches Fallow Head
because, until this Part, nobody has ever had a reason to.

## 2. Situation

Call it a further stretch past wherever a table's own Part IV ended — the
same loose, generous, non-binding convention `R2a` set and `R3a`/`R4a` each
extended rather than replaced. This file doesn't fix Part IV's outcome and
doesn't need to: nothing below assumes the House of Quiet Rest was closed,
fled, or never found, or that Ness answered for anything. `K1` (not yet
written) will eventually carry the running continuity index `A1` calls
for; until it exists, a DM carries each Part's actual ending forward by
memory or notes, and this Part, like every one before it, is written to
work regardless of what the first four were.

Of `C2`'s six clocks, two carry over here and one doesn't apply at all:

- **The Hydro Network Map** does not add a new instance here. Fallow Head
  was never a hydro in the sense that clock tracks — it sold no nerve cure,
  took no paying customers, and stopped doing active ascension work
  decades before the clock's own starting point (Stillwell, confirmed). A
  party that tries to fold it into that clock's tally is making a category
  error the DM should gently correct: this region's stakes are archival,
  not logistical.
- **The Press Cycle**, if still spinning from whatever a table caused
  earlier in the campaign, matters here the same way it mattered at the
  House of Quiet Rest — its current position sets how much attention,
  if any, the Sanctum has spared for its own oldest liabilities lately. A
  Church still mid-crisis from an earlier Part's exposure is marginally
  more likely to have someone quietly checking that Fallow Head hasn't
  also been found; a Church that's gone quiet again has spared it no
  thought at all, which is the default assumption if no table has
  triggered an exposure yet.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock** gets this region's own instance, and it runs
  differently again from every earlier one: not mining (Region I), not
  paved order (Region III), not an undrained fen (Region IV), but the sea
  itself. A troop transport, the *Aldermoor*, went down offshore during
  the Attrition with most hands aboard; nineteen months of peace and a
  coastline nobody dredges has left her where she sank, visible at the
  lowest tides, undisturbed except by what the war put in her. See "The
  Tide Flats" below.

**Who holds what.** Hollowmere holds nothing but its own quiet and its
quarterly retainer. Fallow Head holds the complete record — not the
fragment Vantry's assembly archive keeps (`hooks.md` #3; one exposed
ward's worth of files), but the whole sixty years, continuously
maintained since before Ascension House existed. Nobody currently active
in Church leadership holds any particular watch over it; that absence
*is* what holds it safe, and it stops being safe the moment this Part's
party starts asking the right questions in the wrong office.

## 3. Locations

Keyed sites for Region V. Each gives purpose, NPCs, sensory tells,
secrets, and exits, per `PLAN.md`'s Part template. Four sites, not the
five-to-eight of earlier regions — the Old Coast is a finale's worth of
geography, not a full twelve-village valley or a capital city, and
stretching it to look like one would cost this Part the focus it needs.

### Hollowmere

**Purpose.** A decayed fishing-and-turf hamlet on a cold tidal shore, the
region's entry point and this Part's invented foothold, following the
naming convention `D1`/`R1a`/`R2a`/`R3a`/`R4a` all set (a specific, named
proprietor rather than a fuller base treatment, since no Region V base
file is queued in `PLAN.md`'s work items).

**NPCs.** **Wren Catchpole** runs **the Anchorage**, a converted customs
house from a decade when this coast still saw enough shipping to need
one — now lodging, a taproom, and the closest thing Hollowmere has to a
noticeboard. Catchpole has heard every rumour this village has ever had
about "the House up at Fallow Head" and believes almost none of them,
mostly because believing any of them would mean admitting the retainer
that's kept the Anchorage's roof sound for thirty years comes from
somewhere worth worrying about.

**Sensory tells.** Boats drawn up well past the tideline, more of them
than the village has hands to crew; nets mended past the point of being
worth mending; a weathervane shaped like nothing anyone local can name
anymore, older than any other ironwork in the village.

**Secrets.** Catchpole keeps the Anchorage's accounts the way Edie kept
hers (`source-bible.md` → Objects, Edie's ledger) — every coin itemised —
and the retainer's own line has been paid quarterly, without a single
missed cycle, for longer than any account book Catchpole has ever seen
goes back. A party that earns trust rather than suspicion (`C2`'s DM
notes on playing ordinary people sincerely apply here as everywhere) can
see those books: the amount has never changed, the hand that delivers it
has changed six or seven times across the decades, and the word "House"
never once appears written down — only "the usual."

**Exits.** The service track back to Greymere and, beyond it, Oldbridge
and the rail (per §4 below); a coast path along the Tide Flats to Fallow
Head, passable only outside the highest tides.

### The Tide Flats

**Purpose.** The low, exposed shore between Hollowmere and Fallow Head —
not a set piece in its own right, a located instance of the Pest Pressure
Clock (`C2`), the same way the Fen Line was for Region IV and Red Redoubt
is backstory-only for Edie.

**NPCs.** None fixed; weather, not a faction, per `C2`.

**Sensory tells.** At the lowest tides, ribs of a hull break the surface
a half-mile out — the *Aldermoor*, lost with most hands during the
Attrition, never salvaged because nothing out here was ever worth the
cost of trying. Gulls that don't settle on that stretch of water even
when the fish are running.

**Secrets.** Nothing keyed here beyond what the Pest Pressure Clock
already governs — at low pressure, nothing; at high pressure, an
encounter drawn from a worse tier `(SYS?)`, per `C2`'s own mechanism, and
`G5`'s job to build (not yet written). This file does not invent a
specific horror to live here; it only fixes that the war's unhealed cost
reaches this coast too, same as everywhere else (`C1` → Scars of the
Attrition).

**Exits.** Hollowmere in one direction; Fallow Head in the other, reachable
only when the flats are passable.

### Fallow Head

**Purpose.** A headland above the tide flats, and on it, **the First
House** — per this file's own session default (`DECISIONS.md` D-26), the
site where the Fourth Mercy's ascension work actually began, decades
before Ascension House and longer still before the House of Quiet Rest.
Decommissioned as an active site within its own first years — whatever
happened here taught the Church enough about failure, early and badly,
to move the work elsewhere rather than repeat it on this ground — but
never abandoned. What it became instead, and has stayed for six decades,
is the doctrine's own complete memory: not a working facility, not a
third tank room (`R4b`'s own DM notes already warn against reusing that
shape a third time, and this file takes the warning), but an archive,
continuously kept, older and far more complete than the fragment Vantry's
assembly holds.

**NPCs.** **Sister Edda Vance**, close to ninety, has kept the First
House's record since she was a much younger woman — sincere, meticulous,
and the only person currently alive who has read the entire sixty years
in order rather than in the pieces this campaign's party has found so
far. She is built the same way every sincere Church face in this
campaign has been built (Anstey, Wick's own charity, Vail herself):
nothing about her is a mask, and nothing she says is a lie. She believes,
plainly and without performance, that keeping the record complete and
exact is itself a kind of penance the institution owes its own failures,
whether or not anyone ever reads it. Whether she would show it to the
party, and what it costs her to decide that, is `R5b`'s lead web to
build, not this file's — same restraint `R1a`–`R4a` all held for their own
Parts' pivotal figures.

**Sensory tells.** A building that reads, from outside, like nothing —
weathered stone, shuttered windows, a garden kept better than the house
around it. Inside: shelf after shelf of ledgers, bound by decade,
each spine lettered in a hand that changes only a few times across sixty
years of entries — Vance's own hand for the most recent third of them.
No tanks, no restraint chairs, no brass-ring room. The absence of
Ascension House's own machinery, in a building that invented the work
those machines later did, is itself the detail worth playing up.

**Secrets.** The full record: every name, every date, every documented
failure the Fourth Mercy has produced in sixty years, cross-referenced
against the stud book fragments Vantry's own archive only partially
preserved (`hooks.md` #3) and against whatever the party has already
learned at Greymere and the House of Quiet Rest. This is the campaign's
largest single instance of "evidence existing and evidence mattering are
different problems" (`A1` → Relationship to the Novel) — scaled up from
one exposed ward to the whole institution's own memory of itself, and the
central object `R5b`'s several endings (Q-4, Q-3 defaults) will be built
around. This file does not decide what the party does with it, or what
it costs them to get it, or what the Church does if it learns the party
has; all three are `R5b`'s to build.

**Exits.** The coast path back across the Tide Flats to Hollowmere; a
disused jetty below the headland, rotted past use, from the decades
before rail reached this coast at all — a detail for flavour, not a
travel route.

### The Named Ground

**Purpose.** A walled plot behind the First House, kept as carefully as
the garden — not a set piece, a single sustained image. Row on row of
plain markers, unmarked by any name a visitor would recognise, each
bearing only a number and a date. Sixty years of documented failure,
catalogued rather than lurid, exactly as canon §7 insists horror in this
world's register should be: a cold phrase in a register does more work at
this table than any description of what happened to any one person
would, and this is the single clearest place in the whole campaign to
prove that rule rather than just cite it.

**NPCs.** None; Vance tends it herself, the same quiet, personal
devotion she gives the ledgers inside.

**Sensory tells.** Grass kept short over ground that's otherwise entirely
untended by anyone but one old woman; no chapel, no shrine, no gesture
toward meaning beyond the markers themselves; a single bench, worn smooth,
facing the sea rather than the graves.

**Secrets.** Every number corresponds to an entry in the ledgers inside —
this is where a party that's already read the record can go to find out
that a specific name they've been chasing (Anse Duley, Corporal Dunmore,
Nell Quane, or an invented thread of the table's own, depending what a
given campaign has actually played) has, or hasn't, ended here. Whether
any specific thread from an earlier Part resolves this way is the table's
own continuity to decide, not this file's to assume.

**Exits.** Back into the First House; nowhere else — this is a dead end
by design, literally and otherwise.

## 4. The Old Coast's Reach — Travel Summary

Per `R4a` §4: a half-day's rail from Vantry's junction to Oldbridge, a
half-day's cart track beyond that to Lissom Fen, a further hour on foot to
the House of Quiet Rest, and a service track from there to Greymere Hydro
inside the same afternoon if the weather holds. This file picks up exactly
where that summary stopped: from Greymere, the "longer, little-used cart
road" `R4a` already named reaches Hollowmere in the better part of a day,
poorly maintained and poorly signed, the kind of road that exists because
someone still pays for its upkeep rather than because traffic justifies
it. From Hollowmere, the coast path across the Tide Flats to Fallow Head
is under two hours at low tide and impassable at high tide — the DM's own
simplest lever for pacing this Part, since the party cannot simply walk
there and back inside a single scene whenever the tide says no. Nothing
past Fallow Head is mapped, and nothing needs to be: this is the end of
`C1`'s own coastline, not a gateway to a Region VI that doesn't exist.
`F1` (not yet written) sets exact per-leg costs; this file only fixes that
the Old Coast is, if anything, slower and less reachable than Region IV's
own fen, which is itself part of the point — the easiest place in the
whole Commonwealth to forget about is also where the longest memory in
this campaign turned out to be kept.

## 5. DM Notes

**This region's danger is informational, not physical, until `R5b` says
otherwise.** Nothing at Fallow Head is staffed, guarded, or defended the
way Ness's undercroft was — the First House has had sixty years without
needing to be. If `R5b` builds pursuit or confrontation into this Part,
it should arrive late and from outside the region (a dispatched Church
presence reacting to the party having been here, not a standing defence),
never as something already waiting.

**Don't reuse Ascension House's or the House of Quiet Rest's shape a
third time.** `R4b`'s own DM notes already warned against restoring the
Fourth Mercy's full five-activity suite once play resumed; this file goes
a step further and gives Region V no active ascension work at all. The
First House's horror is entirely in what it has already recorded, not in
anything it is currently doing to anyone. If a table wants a tank room,
it has already had two.

**This is where Q-3 and Q-4 actually come due.** `DECISIONS.md`'s open
question on whether the campaign wants one ending or several (default:
several, ordered by cost, none clean) and its default on whether PCs can
take the salts at all (default: yes, only at thin sites, at a price) both
matter more here than at any earlier Part — not because this file resolves
either, but because `R5b`'s lead web and endings are the place both
questions get answered in practice. Flagging this now so `R5b` doesn't
have to re-derive it.

**A second ascension site's archive is not a second Sanctum, and finding
it is not defeating the Church.** Per `A1` pillar 3 and every earlier
Part's own restraint: whatever `R5b` builds, the institution is outlasted
or escaped here too, not toppled. The record existing, and the party
knowing it exists, changes what the party can do; it does not change what
the Church is.

**Lloyd, Maria, and Edie do not appear in this Part, even restrictively.**
Same restraint `R4a` held for Region IV, for the same reason: `A1`'s
timeline anchor places none of the three anywhere near this coast, and
this file invents no reason to put them here. If `I1` (not yet written)
later wants to use one of them at the very end of the campaign, that is
a call for `I1` or `R5b` to make deliberately, not something this file
pre-empts by accident.

**Don't pre-empt R5b.** This file deliberately leaves unbuilt: whether
Vance shows the record to the party and at what cost to her; what, if
anything, the Church does once it learns the party has been here; which
of the several possible endings Q-3 calls for the party's own choices
point toward; and whether any earlier Part's left-open thread (Dunmore,
Quane, Duley) gets a marker in the Named Ground. All of it is `R5b`'s
lead web and endings to build, same split every earlier Part's "a" file
held for its own "b" file.
