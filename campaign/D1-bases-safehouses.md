# D1 — BASES & SAFEHOUSES

*Reader: DM. Builds on `C1-gazetteer.md` (regions, Region I geography) and
`S1-surface-adaptation.md` (the Hub → Safehouses rule, Flight/Capture/
Recovery, Crowns and Favours) — read both first; this file does not restate
either. Cites `source-bible.md` → Places, `C2-factions-clocks.md`,
`hooks.md`, and `DECISIONS.md` D-6 (no single hub) throughout. Scoped to the
vertical slice: this file details the starting base and its alternates for
Region I, where `PLAN.md` puts the campaign's first Part. A later region's
Part can build its own bases the same way, using this file's conventions,
without this file needing to anticipate them now. No `campaign/system/`
ruleset exists yet (Q-1); upkeep and recovery figures below are marked
`(SYS?)`.*

## How to use this file

Per `S1`, a safehouse is wherever the party has standing, not wherever is
geographically central — there is no single hub (`DECISIONS.md` D-6). This
file names one starting base and three alternates, all reachable from Region
I's geography (`C1`), and sets how each is used, paid for, and lost. Treat
the starting base as where Part I (R1a/b) assumes the party already has a
foothold when play opens; the alternates are there to be found, earned, or
lost over the course of the Part, not all available from session one.

**Session default — D-15 (`DECISIONS.md`).** Nothing in the novel or in
`C1`/`S1` names a specific starting base; this file has to invent one to
open Part I from somewhere concrete, so it names Beddoe's (below) and logs
the choice rather than deciding it silently. Jake can swap it for any other
Region I location without touching anything else in this file.

## The Starting Base: Beddoe's, Harrow's Weir

Harrow's Weir sits downriver of the Larrow mine, where runoff settles first
and the Indemnity's seventh-year claims cluster thickest (`C1-gazetteer.md`
→ "The other nine villages"). **Beddoe's** is a converted cooperage on the
river side of the village — barrel racks still line one wall, repurposed as
shelving — run as a boarding house by **Nessa Beddoe**, a widow in her
fifties whose husband worked the mine for nineteen years and died in his
claim's sixth, one year short of the Indemnity's payout clause
(`source-bible.md` → Factions, "The Larrow Indemnity"). She knows exactly
why that number matters and has never once said so to a Valley Trust man's
face.

**Why the party starts here.** Beddoe's is where people with an unpaid debt,
a missing relative, or a job that pays too well to ask about end up sleeping
in this valley — the same unglamorous leverage `A1-campaign-brief.md` → "What
the PCs Are" sets as the party's own hook. Nessa fronts credit against work
nobody else will post openly (an escort for a capping-schedule testing team,
a cart that needs guarding past Hessel Bight, a letter that needs carrying to
someone who left under a different name) and keeps a slate of who owes her
what, in the same itemised, specific register as Edie's ledger
(`source-bible.md` → Objects). A new party owing Nessa for a bed, a meal, or
a debt inherited from whoever sent them to the valley is the default way
into Part I; the DM is free to substitute any other ordinary leverage that
fits `A1`'s pillar on how the three leads were pulled in.

**What it has.** Four lets-by-the-week rooms over the old cooperage floor, a
common room with a stove that's the warmest room in the village from
October to April, and a slate behind the bar that functions as the valley's
actual noticeboard — more current than the Indemnity Office's posted notices
and considerably more honest. Nessa buys nothing from Ammet Ford's company
store on principle, which costs her money and tells the party exactly how
she feels about the Valley Trust without her ever having to say it aloud.

**What it doesn't have.** No defences worth the name — a bar on the door and
neighbours who'd notice shouting, nothing more. No standing with the Church;
a Mercy brother passing through is a guest like any other, watched but not
turned away. No connection to Stillwell beyond the ordinary one anyone in
the valley has (below).

**What's on the slate, by way of example.** Three entries the DM can use or
swap for session-specific hooks without inventing from nothing: a cooper
two villages over who'll pay fair crowns for someone willing to ask the
Indemnity Office's travelling clerk an awkward question on his behalf; a
widow (not Nessa — a younger one, Ammet Ford side) who wants to know whether
her husband's name is on the capping schedule for this year or next, and
won't say why it matters so much which; and a standing two-crown bounty, not
Nessa's own idea, posted by someone at the Works who wants to know who's
been asking after the buried survey lately — which the party may discover,
on taking the job, describes them.

**The room over the stove.** Worth naming on its own: the common room is
where Harrow's Weir actually talks, in the unguarded way people talk near a
fire that costs nothing to sit by. A DM short on a rumour, a name, or a
reason for an NPC to recognise the party can generate it here rather than
reaching for a scene that has to be built from scratch — Nessa's common room
is this file's standing answer to "where would the party plausibly overhear
that."

**Where it sits on the clocks (`C2`).** Harrow's Weir is squarely inside the
Capping Clock's working radius — Nessa will know, village-gossip-fast,
whether the testing team has reached the Weir yet or skipped it again — and
inside the Indemnity Mortality Curve's reach: she is herself one of its
quiet data points, which she will mention exactly once, flatly, and never
raise again unless the party does.

## Alternates

Per `S1`, which of these counts as "the" safehouse can change as the party
moves through Part I; more than one can be live at once. None is free, and
losing good standing at one (a betrayed confidence, a job left undone, a
faction turning hostile) is as real a consequence as gaining a new one.

### Stillwell Hydro, and its clerk

Granite-and-pine on the cold tidal coast, Region I's western edge
(`source-bible.md` → Places). Sells Salts entry as a nerve cure, thirty-five
shillings a run, strict batches of three, tracked by a numbered brass token
— and, separately from any of that, sells ordinary hydropathic lodging the
way any sanatorium does: a bed, a steam bath, a kitchen that keeps hours.
**None of this is a thin site.** Stillwell is one base candidate among
several (`DECISIONS.md` D-6), not the party's route to the Salts loop — that
channel is T1–T3 only, per `C3-the-salts-as-background.md`. A party lodging
at Stillwell for the ordinary reasons (quiet, coastal, out of the Valley
Trust's immediate reach) never has to go anywhere near the steam room to use
it as a base.

**The reception clerk.** A fixture of the building, not a character arc and
never statted (`DECISIONS.md` D-6, reaffirmed here). He knows every patron's
account to the shilling, has seen every kind of person come through for a
nerve cure, and treats a party of investigators with the same unbothered
professional courtesy he'd give a senator's wife — which is itself useful:
nothing the party does at Stillwell under his eye reads as suspicious unless
they make it so. He will not discuss other patrons' business, on principle,
not menace; he is not hiding anything, he is simply the kind of man who
would say the same thing about anyone.

**What it has.** Genuine neutrality — the one base in this list with no
faction loyalty at all, which makes it the safest place in the valley to
have a conversation nobody should overhear, and the worst place to expect
help with anything that isn't a bed or a bath. Proximity to the coast road
south, away from the mine and the Valley Trust's day-to-day reach.

**What it costs to use.** Ordinary lodging rates, `(SYS?)` for the exact
figure; Stillwell takes crowns, not favours, and keeps no tab the way Nessa
does. A party that wants Stillwell to feel like standing rather than a hotel
bill needs to earn it the slow way — regular, unremarkable patronage, or a
specific favour done for the hydro itself (quietly handling a patron who
won't pay, say) — per `S1`'s favour rules.

### Ammet Ford — the company guest-rooms

The Larrow Consolidated Works' showpiece village: new brick, slate roofs, a
company well whose water nobody who drinks it can quite stop wanting more
of (`C1-gazetteer.md`). The Works keeps a small block of guest-rooms over
the company office for inspectors, visiting Indemnity clerks, and anyone
else the Valley Trust wants kept comfortable and watched. **This base is
compromised by design.** Using it means the party is, for as long as they
stay, visibly the Valley Trust's guests — which opens doors nowhere else in
the valley will (the company ledgers, an introduction to whoever now runs
the works day to day, cooperation on anything that costs the Trust little)
and closes others just as hard (Nessa Beddoe will not pretend she doesn't
notice; Harrow's Weir's slate will note it too).

**What it has.** Clean water, real heat, and the valley's best vantage point
on whatever the Valley Trust is doing about the buried survey and the
Indemnity's seventh-year clause (`source-bible.md` → Factions) — a party
staying here is positioned to overhear exactly the conversations `C2`'s
Valley Trust entry says it wants kept quiet.

**What it costs.** No crowns change hands — the Works comps its guest-rooms
to anyone it wants to watch — but every night here is a favour spent before
it's earned: the Trust will expect something back, named or not, per `S1`'s
rule that a favour costs the relationship rather than a resource tick. DM's
call, scene to scene, what the Trust asks for first.

### The capping camp (mobile)

Ferrick's sub-panel runs its well-testing and capping work "town by town,
ahead of frost" (`A1-campaign-brief.md`) out of a canvas-and-trestle field
camp that moves with the schedule — currently somewhere between Ammet Ford
and Harrow's Weir, per whichever village the Capping Clock (`C2`) says is
next. Unlike the other three bases, this one has no fixed address; it is
wherever the testing team currently is, and it moves without the party if
they don't move with it.

**What it has.** The sub-panel's own people — underfunded, sincere, glad of
extra hands (`C2`'s Ferrick entry) — and a direct, up-to-date read on exactly
which villages have clean water and which don't, which makes it the best
single vantage point in the valley on the Capping Clock's actual state.
Escort work, testing-run labour, and the occasional letter back to Ferrick's
household in Vantry are all standing, renewable favours here, not one-offs.

**What it costs.** Nothing to use, everything to keep current — a party that
doesn't move with the camp loses it as a base the moment the schedule moves
on, with no warning beyond what Nessa or the camp's own people would
plausibly mention in passing. This is the one base in this file built to
model `S1`'s point that a safehouse can stop being available for reasons
that have nothing to do with the party's own choices.

## Upkeep

`(SYS?)`: exact weekly crown figures wait on the real ruleset's economy
numbers. Until then, the shape is fixed here:

- **Beddoe's** runs on a tab, not a nightly rate — Nessa adds lodging, meals,
  and anything fronted to the same slate she keeps for everyone else in the
  village, and calls it in the way any Harrow's Weir creditor would: not
  urgently, but specifically, and usually tied to a job she already has in
  mind.
- **Stillwell** runs on crowns, paid as lodging is used, exactly like any
  sanatorium guest; no tab, no favour owed by default, per the clerk's own
  professional neutrality above.
- **Ammet Ford's** guest-rooms cost no crowns and build no tab — they cost a
  favour owed to the Valley Trust the moment the party checks in, whether or
  not the Trust calls it in that same visit.
- **The capping camp** costs labour, not money: showing up and being useful
  is the upkeep: missing a leg of the schedule without a good reason is the
  cost of not paying it.

A party holding more than one base at once is normal, not a strain on the
system — `S1` already expects standing at a prior region's base to lapse
once play has moved on, so the DM should let a Region I base go quiet rather
than inventing an ongoing rent the party has to track from three regions
away.

## How the PCs Recover

Per `S1`'s Flight, Capture, and Recovery section: recovery on the surface is
never instant and never free, mirroring the Salts loop's own "injuries taken
inside are real on surfacing and can heal wrong" rather than softening it.
A base is where that recovery happens, and which base the party is at when
it starts changes what it costs:

- **At Beddoe's**, recovery is cheap in crowns and expensive in attention —
  Nessa will know exactly what state the party came back in, will patch what
  she can herself (she has buried a husband and nursed half the village
  through one injury or another; she is not squeamish and not impressed),
  and will remember it the next time she's deciding who to send on a job
  that might end the same way.
- **At Stillwell**, recovery is the one place in this file that looks
  closest to a hydropathic rest cure — baths, quiet, competent ordinary
  care — and costs crowns on the clerk's ledger like everything else there.
  It is the best place in Region I to recover without anyone drawing a
  conclusion about why.
- **At Ammet Ford**, recovery is fast and well-supplied, because the Works
  keeps a company surgeon for its own injured labour and will extend that to
  comped guests — and it is watched, which means whatever hurt the party and
  whatever they say about it reaches the Valley Trust whether they mean it
  to or not.
- **At the capping camp**, recovery is field-grade: competent enough to stop
  something getting worse, not equipped for anything serious, and tied to
  whatever the schedule is doing that week — a party recovering here is a
  party not doing the job that keeps this base available.

`(SYS?)`: exact recovery-time bands and what, specifically, "heals wrong" on
the surface looks like in ruleset terms wait on the real system; this
section only fixes which base trades which kind of cost for which kind of
speed.

## Choosing a Base, Session to Session

Not a mechanic, a DM habit worth stating plainly: open most sessions by
naming, in one line, which of the four bases the party is currently using
and what that's costing them (a line on Nessa's slate, a favour owed to the
Trust, a leg of the capping schedule kept or missed) the same way `C2`
asks clocks to be made visible rather than sprung. A party that's let its
standing at every base lapse at once should feel that, concretely — nowhere
to sleep that isn't a cold camp by the road — before the DM reaches for a
new base to hand them for free. Four bases is enough to make the vertical
slice playable without the party ever being truly stranded, not enough that
losing one doesn't matter.

## DM Notes

**None of these four are safe in the way a Salts-loop hub is safe.** The
point of `S1`'s replacement is that the surface doesn't get a true safety
valve; every base in this file costs something to use, even the ones that
cost no crowns. If a base starts feeling free, something has gone
unaccounted for — check it against the upkeep table above.

**Ammet Ford is a trap shaped like comfort, not a trick.** Play the Valley
Trust's hospitality straight, per `C2`'s note that none of its factions run
on a disguised-villain logic — the guest-rooms are genuinely more
comfortable than Beddoe's, the favour genuinely isn't called in right away,
and that's exactly what makes it worth including rather than an obvious
bad deal.

**Don't let the capping camp become a fixed location by accident.** Its
whole point is that it moves; if a table starts treating it as "the camp at
such-and-such village," that's `C2`'s Capping Clock not being advanced
visibly enough between sessions.

**Nessa Beddoe is not a hook deployment device.** She has a dead husband, a
slate she keeps for reasons that are hers and not the party's business
unless she chooses to make them so, and a specific, quiet grievance against
Ammet Ford's well. Use her the way `C2`'s DM notes ask every faction to be
used — as a sincere person doing a job, not a quest-giver with a line of
dialogue.
