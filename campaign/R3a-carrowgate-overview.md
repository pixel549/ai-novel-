## 1. Premise Card

**Region:** Carrowgate and the Senate (`C1` Region III).

**Level band:** `(SYS?)` — one notch past Part II's own, same holding
pattern `R2a` used: build and run this Part for a party two Parts into the
campaign, or one step past starting-character assumptions if a table begins
here instead. The exact numeric range waits on the ruleset's tier bands
landing in `campaign/system/` (Q-1).

**One-line hook:** somewhere in the Indemnity Office's own index is the line
that closed the file on Halvard Crane's death — resolved, blamed, paid out —
and somebody just offered the party good money to find out exactly what it
says, because the two men who've run the works since him would rather it
stayed exactly where it is.

**What the surface wants.** The Waiting House's tenants — valley people in
the capital for a claim, a hearing date, or a relative's unanswered letter —
want the Indemnity Office to move faster than it ever does; most have been
waiting long enough to know it won't. Senator Ferrick, in the capital only
when the Senate sits (his household and the sub-panel's day-to-day work stay
in Vantry, per `R2a`), wants the same votes and funding `C2` already gives
him as a want, just pursued from a different building. The city's own
people mostly want nothing from any of this — Carrowgate's soot is lighter,
its gaslamps burn cleaner, and reconstruction looks, from here, exactly like
the office `C1` Region I already blamed for looking like progress from a
desk and nothing from a kitchen table. The Valley Trust's two still-unnamed
intermediaries (`DECISIONS.md` D-12) want the Indemnity Office's paperwork
to keep closing cases the way it always has, and are the one party in this
file with a real stake in nothing here changing.

**What the Church wants.** To keep being exactly what it looks like from the
Senate's own hill: popular, charitable, and untouched. The Sanctum has not
moved an inch on the Fourth Mercy (`DECISIONS.md` D-1) and has no reason to
expect it will need to; Ondine Vail's promotion here, not punishment, after
Ascension House's exposure is this Part's standing proof that the
institution outlasts rather than falls (`PLAN.md`'s tone contract; `A1`
pillar 3). Nothing below confirms the Sanctum has any operational stake in
the Indemnity Office or the Valley Trust's buried survey — that connection,
if a table wants one, is `R3b`'s call to make or decline, same restraint
`R2a` left over whether the ascension work resumed anywhere in Vantry.

## 2. Situation

Call it a further two to three months past wherever a table's own Part II
ended — the same loose, generous, non-binding convention `R2a` set,
extended rather than replaced. This file doesn't fix Part II's outcome and
doesn't need to: nothing below assumes Wick was exposed, Nell Quane was
found, or the standing archive was ever opened. `K1` (not yet written) will
eventually hold the running continuity index `A1` calls for; until it
exists, a DM carries both Parts' actual endings forward by memory or notes,
and this Part is written to work regardless of what either one was.

Three of `C2`'s six clocks matter here in a way the first two Parts'
clocks didn't; the rest sit wherever the party left them.

- **The Valley Trust's Consolidation** is this Part's live wire, more so
  here than anywhere it's been so far. The clock advances on calendar time
  and faster whenever the party pokes at the survey or the Indemnity Office
  without covering their tracks (`C2`) — and the Indemnity Office is a
  location in this Part now, not a reference to one elsewhere. A table that
  comes here looking is, by definition, advancing the one clock that can
  bury the valley's own loose thread one administrative layer deeper than
  they can dig through.
- **The Indemnity Mortality Curve** doesn't start over here — it's the same
  clock Region I already made legible through funerals and unpaid slates —
  but the Indemnity Office gives it a second, administrative face: the same
  curve, filed, dated, and closed out in a clerk's hand rather than counted
  at Nessa Beddoe's bar. Finding the office's own copy doesn't change what
  the clock has already done; it changes who the party can prove knew, and
  when.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock**, Carrowgate instance, runs quieter than either
  of the first two Parts' — the capital's ground is paved, surveyed, and
  maintained in a way the mine's tunnels and the rail yards' foundations
  aren't, and that order is exactly why pressure here concentrates rather
  than spreads: an old cellar, an unused crypt under a Mercy House, a
  stretch of ground the Senate's own groundskeepers avoid without saying
  why. `(SYS?)` for the actual numbers; the texture is that this clock has
  fewer places to be bad in Carrowgate and is correspondingly worse wherever
  it is.

**Who holds what.** The Senate holds the Commonwealth's actual money and the
actual vote to send more of it to the valley — and, per everything `A1` and
`C1` already establish about reconstruction, mostly doesn't spend either.
The Sanctum holds the Church's national reach and whatever protection that
reach buys the Fourth Mercy's survivors; nothing below says that protection
runs through a specific senator, and nothing needs it to. The Indemnity
Office holds the paper trail — claims, adjudications, and, if a table goes
looking, whatever closed the file on Crane's own death. The Valley Trust's
two intermediaries hold the survey itself, wherever it actually sits now,
and whatever capital-side legal and political cover keeps it there; `C1`
already notes nothing on the page specifies whether that cover runs through
Carrowgate specifically, and this file doesn't resolve it either. The
Waiting House holds this Part's actual population: valley people with
business here that the capital is in no hurry to finish.

## 3. Locations

Keyed sites for Region III. Each gives purpose, NPCs, sensory tells,
secrets, and exits, per `PLAN.md`'s Part template. Geography and anything
already fixed by `C1` are cited, not repeated.

### The Senate

**Purpose.** The Commonwealth's actual seat of power, and the literal
source of every reconstruction decision `A1` and `C1` describe as too slow,
too partial, or too late by the time it reaches a valley kitchen table.
Where Senator Ferrick sits when the Senate is in session, and the only
place in the campaign where the money itself — not its absence — is a
visible, nameable thing.

**NPCs.** **Senator Ferrick**, when the Senate sits (`source-bible.md` →
People; otherwise he's in Vantry, per `R2a`): sincerely decent, genuinely
ineffective, never mocked for it on the page and not to be mocked for it
here. Clerks, pages, and the capital's own layer of lobbyists and
petitioners work the halls and committee rooms; none are named here —
invent per scene, in the same register `C2`'s DM notes set for every
ordinary institutional face in this file (sincere people doing a job, not
disguised evil).

**Sensory tells.** The long grey roofline and central dome `C1` already
describes, built to look permanent and succeeding — up close, that
permanence is mostly paint and upkeep budget, visible in the parts of the
building petitioners don't get shown. A committee-room door with a queue
outside it that hasn't moved in an hour. Gaslamps on the better approach
avenues burning the cleaner, greenish white `C1` notes; a block over, where
petitioners actually wait, the ordinary yellow kind.

**Secrets.** Nothing here is hidden so much as slow — every vote that would
help the valley faster than it's currently moving is a vote somebody has to
want to spend, and `C2` already establishes that nobody with enough votes
wants it badly enough yet. If a table needs a specific senator to pressure,
bribe, or persuade, invent one here rather than reaching for Ferrick twice;
he already has his own faction entry and his own limits.

**Exits.** The Senate hill, above and apart from the rest of the city; a
longer walk or a short cab ride down to the Sanctum, the Indemnity Office,
and the boarding district below.

### The Sanctum

**Purpose.** Church headquarters, untouched across the novel and still
untouched a year-plus on (`DECISIONS.md` D-1). Ondine Vail was promoted
here, not punished, after Ascension House's exposure — the clearest single
proof in the campaign that the institution outlasts rather than falls
(`PLAN.md`'s tone contract; `A1` pillar 3). This Part's single largest
source of Church pressure, and the one site in it that should never be
stormed.

**NPCs.** Ondine Vail herself is here (`source-bible.md` → People) —
sincerely good at every level anyone has ever been able to observe, never
lying, never lowering her voice, never threatening (canon §3). Until `I1`
exists, do not run her as a speaking NPC with anything resembling a scene; a
name on a Sanctum roster, a figure glimpsed at a distance, nothing with
dialogue — the same restraint `R2a` set for Edie, applied here to someone
with far more institutional reach. Everyone else at the Sanctum — clerks,
almoners, whoever Brother Calder Wick answers to above Vail (`R2b`) — is
this Part's actual cast to invent and name as needed.

**Sensory tells.** A building that reads as warm rather than forbidding —
open doors, a bread queue that starts before the Senate's own offices do,
nothing that looks like what it is. The menace, per canon §3, lives only in
content, never staging: nothing here should read as sinister on sight, and
writing it that way is the single easiest way to get this location wrong.

**Secrets.** Whatever connects the Sanctum to Ascension House's ruin or to
the Fourth Mercy's current operations, if anything does, is `R3b`'s call to
make or leave alone — this file commits to nothing beyond what
`DECISIONS.md` D-1 already fixes (untouched, Vail promoted). Treat the
Sanctum as sincerely, demonstrably charitable at every level a party can
actually observe, same as Vail herself; the horror, if a table ever finds
it, lives several administrative layers back from this building's own front
door.

**Exits.** Below the Senate hill, nearer the avenues than the boarding
district; a Mercy House sits close enough to be reached on the same visit.

### A Mercy House, in the Sanctum's Shadow

**Purpose.** One of the Church's ordinary Mercies — bread, shelter,
absolution — running exactly as charitably and exactly as effectively as
`A1` and canon §4 describe, and running literally in view of the
institution it feeds. This Part's clearest demonstration that the Church's
menace and the Church's genuine goodness share a building, this time with
headquarters next door rather than a city away.

**NPCs.** A Mercy sister or brother (unnamed; invent per scene, same
sincere register canon §3 sets for Vail and extends to the Mercies
generally). Not a disguised villain — most of the Church's people here,
same as in Vantry, genuinely don't know what the fourth Mercy is.

**Sensory tells.** A bread queue that starts earlier here than anywhere
else in the capital, precisely because the Sanctum's own presence draws
people who've already exhausted every other door; a shelter roll crossed
through for the same mix of reasons `R2a` already names for Vantry's
Mercy House (found work, nothing anyone wrote down).

**Secrets.** Same mechanism as every other Mercy House in this campaign:
bread and shelter notice the surplus first, absolution collects it. What
happens to a noticed name here, with Ascension House gone and no confirmed
replacement site, is exactly as open as `R2a` left it for Vantry — this
file doesn't close that question either.

**Exits.** A short walk from the Sanctum; further from the Senate hill and
the boarding district both.

### The Indemnity Office

**Purpose.** The government office that adjudicates compensation and
liability claims tied to Crane's works — identifiable by a small brass
pin, crossed quills, and nothing else distinguishing it from any other
state building on the block (`source-bible.md` → Places). The most direct,
least dramatic route in the whole campaign to the paper trail behind the
Larrow Indemnity and the buried groundwater survey.

**NPCs.** A clerk or adjudicator (unnamed; `R3b`'s to name if a scene needs
one, same deferral `R1b` used for the Valley Trust's administrator and
`R2b` used for the archive clerk) keeps session-day hours and knows the
filing system better than anyone currently employed to ask questions about
it. Not hostile, not complicit beyond doing the job competently — the
obstacle here, same as Vantry's standing archive, is that almost nobody
knows to ask the right question, not that anyone's actively hiding the
answer.

**Sensory tells.** Session-day hours posted and kept exactly; a maintenance
schedule for "carpet men" that runs with more punctuality than the claims
themselves do; the brass pin, crossed quills, on a door otherwise identical
to every other one on the corridor.

**Secrets.** Whatever closed the case on Crane's own death — resolved,
blamed on the Church, nobody currently employed here likely to say more
than that out loud — sits somewhere in this office's files, filed the same
unglamorous way the Vantry archive files its own evidence (`hooks.md` #3,
same mechanism). Whether the Valley Trust's buried survey is reachable
through this office's own index, and how far the Trust's Consolidation
clock has already buried it, is `R3b`'s lead web to build; this file only
establishes that the trail runs through here.

**Exits.** Below the Senate hill, nearer the avenues than the boarding
district; a short walk or cab ride to the Sanctum.

### The Boarding District — the Waiting House

**Purpose.** Where valley labour actually lives while working the capital:
cheaper, further from the Senate's hill, soot closer to Vantry's than to
the avenues' (`C1`). Not on the page by name, same as Vantry's lodging
rows, but implied by everything canon says about Lloyd's own nine years
here and about where reconstruction money doesn't reach. This Part's
natural landing point for any PC with valley roots, and its best source of
rumour, contacts, and the occasional unwelcome recognition.

**NPCs.** **Caddock Lowe** — invented here to give the district a foothold,
following the naming convention `D1`, `R1a`, and `R2a` all set (a specific,
named proprietor rather than an abstraction) — runs **the Waiting House**, a
converted goods store three streets back from the Indemnity Office's own
queue. A former pump-man at the Larrow mine for over twenty years before
his knees went, he left the valley for Carrowgate work the better part of a
decade ago — roughly Lloyd's own span, unremarked by either of them — and
now rents rooms mostly to valley people in the capital for a claim, a
hearing date, or a relative's unanswered letter. Half his tenants are
waiting on the Indemnity Office in one way or another, which is where the
house's name comes from. Logged as a session default rather than decided
silently — see `DECISIONS.md` D-22.

**Sensory tells.** A common room with a slate by the door, same convention
as Nessa Beddoe's (`D1`), tracking who's owed what and who's still waiting
on which office; washing lines strung across a yard too narrow for the
avenues' cleaner light to reach; tenants who trade valley news faster and
more accurately than any broadsheet, because most of them still have
somebody back home writing to them.

**Secrets.** Per `C1`, this is where a PC with valley roots risks being
recognised by someone from home — not a plot device, an ordinary
consequence of a small world, and the single likeliest place in this Part
for Lloyd's or Maria's name to come up in conversation among people who
knew them before either left. This file does not place either of them
here, or anywhere else in Carrowgate beyond what `A1`'s own timeline anchor
already says ("in or near Carrowgate"); if a name comes up, it comes up as
a rumour, a resemblance, a secondhand account from someone who worked with
Lloyd nine years ago — never a scene, never dialogue, until `I1` exists.
See DM notes below.

**Exits.** A short walk from the Indemnity Office and the district's own
rail approach; further from the Senate hill, the Sanctum, and the avenues.

## 4. Carrowgate's Reach — Travel Summary

Per `C1`'s Map: a day by rail from Vantry, three or four by road; the same
leg from the Larrow Valley runs through the same half-day detour off the
valley-Vantry road and rail line that `DECISIONS.md` D-11 already fixes —
Carrowgate sits close enough to that route for an undramatized changeover,
not a scene, and this file keeps it exactly that minor. Within the city,
every location above is a walk or a short cab ride from every other one,
same as Vantry (`R2a`); what matters is the climb between them — the
Senate's hill above, the avenues and the Sanctum below it, the boarding
district further out and lower still, a geography that tracks the
Commonwealth's own money exactly as closely as it looks like it should.
`F1` (not yet written) sets the exact per-leg costs; this file only fixes
that nothing within Carrowgate itself needs one.

## 5. DM Notes

**This Part's antagonist pressure, if `R3b` builds one, is institutional
first, personal second — same discipline as Corlett and Wick.** The Senate
and the Sanctum both invite a mastermind; resist it until `R3b` actually
needs one. Nothing in this file is run by anyone twirling a moustache, and
the Mercy House above should read as sincerely charitable for as long as
the table is in it.

**Don't pre-empt R3b.** This file deliberately leaves the Indemnity
Office's clerk unnamed, the Valley Trust's two intermediaries unnamed (per
`DECISIONS.md` D-12, still inventable at the Part level), and the question
of whether the Sanctum has any operational stake in the survey or the
Indemnity Office completely open. Those are `R3b`'s lead web's job to use
or leave, same restraint `R1a` and `R2a` showed for their own Parts' open
threads.

**Region I and II's clocks are not this Part's business, except where this
file already says otherwise.** The Capping Clock, the Press Cycle, and the
Hydro Network Map stay exactly where the party left them. The Indemnity
Mortality Curve and the Valley Trust's Consolidation are the two
exceptions — already Region I's clocks, given a second, administrative
face here rather than a new one.

**The institution Lloyd was held in is not a location in this file, and
should never become one.** `C1` already declines to key it; this file
holds that line. It is one of three permanently closed who-knows-what gaps
(canon §6), not a mystery deferred to a later Part — no PC investigation in
this campaign, in Carrowgate or anywhere else, should ever uncover where it
was or what happened inside it. If a future Part needs a Church or state
institution of this general kind for an unrelated NPC, build a new one;
this file says so now so no later session reaches for this one by mistake.

**How the novel's three appear — more restrictively than any Part so far.**
`A1`'s own timeline anchor places Lloyd Hale and Maria Vesk "in or near
Carrowgate" at this campaign's opening, which makes this the one Part where
a table might plausibly expect to meet them. Resist that expectation. Until
`I1` exists, neither appears as a speaking NPC, a named location, or even a
confirmed sighting — rumour and resemblance only, exactly as the Waiting
House entry above allows and no further. This file does not place either
of them anywhere more specific than `A1` already does, and does not invent
a reason for a table to go looking for them; Carrowgate being where they
might be is not the same as Carrowgate being where the party finds them.
Lloyd's own nine years here (`source-bible.md` → People; canon §6) explain
why the capital isn't a stranger to valley people and why the Waiting
House exists; they are not an invitation to stage his reunion with anyone.

**Carrowgate is, if anything, safer for Lloyd than his own valley.** Canon
confirms his wanted notice posted "on every board in the valley" under a
false name (`source-bible.md`) — nothing on the page extends that posting
to the capital, and this file doesn't either. A party chasing a lead on
Lloyd here should find silence or a shrug before they find a wanted
poster; if a table wants that poster to exist in Carrowgate too, that's a
fresh call for whoever makes it, not a default this file sets.
