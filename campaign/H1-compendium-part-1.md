## How to use this file

A D&D-style magic-item appendix exists so a DM doesn't have to invent a
prop's weight mid-session. Region I has almost no magic items in that sense —
per `A1` pillar 1, this is an investigation, not a loot crawl, and per `C3`
there is no thin site here, so no chalice gift is in play. What this file
actually supplies is the ordinary and documentary objects the lead web and
the bases already assume exist (a slate, a survey, a sample kit, a bounty
notice), the valley's native-Arcana register (small, domestic, never
Maria-scale), a chalice-gifts section kept deliberately inert for this
region, and the weapons this setting's people actually carry — tools and
war surplus, not an armoury. Hand a PC-facing item or weapon description to
players directly; keep the Arcana and Chalice-Gifts sections DM-only where
noted, the same split `PLAN.md` draws between `B` (players) and everything
else (DM).

**Session default — D-19 (`DECISIONS.md`).** None of the specific items,
weapon archetypes, or the worked chalice-gift example below are named on the
page. `R1b`, `D1`, and `R1a` each name a category this file had to build out
(a sample kit implied by the capping-camp tester, a survey document implied
by Reveal B, ordinary gear implied by a valley this poor) — logged here
rather than decided silently, the same way `G1` invented its archetypes.

## Arcana in the Larrow Valley

Per canon §5, Arcana is real, uncommon, unregulated, and mostly weak — the
valley has no famous practitioner, only the kind `G1`'s Unregistered Arcana
Practitioner archetype already models: small, domestic, kept quiet because
`C2` is right that being noticed is a cost, not a flex. A PC with native
Arcana never approaches Maria's repertoire (canon §2; `A1` pillar 6) — these
are the valley's actual ceiling, not a PC's starting floor to grow past it.

- **Hearth-work.** Lighting a candle or a stove without flint or match;
  keeping a fire from guttering in wind that should have killed it; taking
  the worst of a chill out of a room for an hour or two. The single most
  common gift in the valley — half the "charm-workers" anyone's heard of
  turn out to do only this.
- **Ease-work.** Quieting pain without closing a wound, calming a frightened
  animal or a crying child, taking the edge off a fever for a night. Never
  a cure — the ache or the fever is still there in the morning, just
  survivable in the meantime. The gift Mirrin Tope's neighbours wish someone
  in Pellick still had.
- **Mend-work.** Closing a cut faster than it should close, setting a joint
  with less pain than the bonesetter's own methods. **Hard limit, inherited
  from Maria's own (canon §2):** no regrowth, no disease cure — a Mend-work
  practitioner cannot touch the Sweetwater, and every one of them in the
  valley already knows it and has had to say so, more than once, to someone
  who didn't want to hear it.
- **Find-work.** A knack for water, for a lost thing, for which way a
  missing person went. Unreliable and non-verbal — it registers as a pull
  or a wrongness, never a map reference. A PC with this gift gets no
  mechanical shortcut to Reveal A (`R1b` already gives three routes that are
  enough on their own); at most, it's colour — a character who's had this
  gift since childhood might say, once, unprompted, that Ammet Ford's well
  "pulls wrong" compared to any other water in the valley, which the DM can
  let land as atmosphere without it standing in for any of the lead web's
  actual routes.

`(SYS?)` tier: all four are low-tier, non-combat gifts with no mechanical
write-up until the real ruleset exists; none grants anything `C2`'s Arcana
entry would call worth a stud book.

## Chalice-Gifts

**DM-only.** Per `C3`'s D-13 and `S1`'s build procedure: a chalice gift is
bespoke, built fresh for one recipient, never drawn from a list. **Region I
has no thin site** (`R1a`, `R1b`, `G1` all confirm this independently) —
there is no chalice gift active, available, or pending anywhere in this
Part, and nothing below changes that. This section exists only to carry one
worked illustration of `S1`'s procedure forward for whichever DM eventually
runs `T1`, kept here rather than in `S1` because a compendium is where a DM
expects to find a worked example, not a procedure.

**Worked example — "Sure Footing" (teaching only; do not assign to any PC).**
Walking through `S1`'s five steps once, on a function neither Lloyd nor Edie
already uses:

1. *Ordinary function named:* balance — the inner ear's sense of up, down,
   and about to fall.
2. *Inverted or extended, once, specifically:* the recipient's balance
   simply does not fail. No slip, no stumble, no fall, on any surface, at
   any height, ever — one function, pushed exactly as far as Lloyd's arm
   was and no further.
3. *Cost, same function, equally specific:* the same inner-ear sense that
   never lets them fall now misfires on solid, level ground — a sudden,
   visible flinch and grab for a handhold that isn't needed, unpredictably,
   in front of whoever happens to be watching. It reads, to a stranger, like
   a nervous tic or a drinking problem.
4. *Checked against Lloyd's shape:* a capability (never falls) and a cost
   that is the same sense working, just wrongly and publicly — not a clean
   upgrade with a tax attached.
5. *Reused for nobody:* this exact pair belongs to this worked example only.
   A real PC's gift, when `T1` is eventually played, gets its own pair, built
   fresh from a different ordinary function.

`(SYS?)`: no stat block accompanies this on purpose — it is a demonstration
of the procedure's shape, not a pre-built option sitting in the Compendium
waiting to be handed out.

## Items

Ordinary and documentary objects the Part's scenes already assume exist.
Player-facing except where marked.

**The buried survey.** A company ledger volume, still in Crane's handwriting
in its older pages, stating the valley's groundwater concentration gradient
in the dry, unupsettable language of a document never written to be read by
anyone it would upset (`R1b` → Reveal B, Route 1; `source-bible.md` →
Factions). Whoever holds a copy holds Reveal B's cleanest single piece of
evidence. Weighs nothing in play beyond what it proves — this is a handout,
not a MacGuffin with stats.

**Nessa's slate.** The actual slab of chalked slate behind Beddoe's bar
(`D1`), the valley's working model for how a Favour is tracked at the table
— a short, specific, DM-visible list per person, never a number (`S1`).
Treat it as a physical prop a DM can describe updating scene to scene: a
line added, a line rubbed out, never a ledger a PC can read cold and know
everyone's business, since Nessa keeps her own shorthand.

**A quartermaster's ledger.** An oilcloth account-book, soft and dark at the
corners — the model is Edie's own (`source-bible.md` → Objects), not an item
she carries in this campaign, but the convention any party is free to adopt:
every job, fare, and favour itemised and closed out in one line, "Paid out"
style. `F1` (not yet written) will set the actual price list this ledger
would use; until then, a DM can let a PC keep one as flavour with no
mechanical weight.

**A knot-cord.** Hemp cord, brass wire ferrules at regular intervals — an
ordinary Commonwealth tool for counting depth underground or days overland
(`source-bible.md` → Objects), not unique to Edie. Cheap, unremarkable, and
genuinely useful: a party without a reliable way to track days on the north
road toward Pellick or Hessel Bight can be handed one instead of an
abstracted calendar note.

**The capping-team sample kit.** Glass vials, a dosage card, a tester's own
shorthand notation for "reads cleaner than it tastes" — the prop behind
`R1b`'s Reveal A, Route 1 and `G1`'s Capping-Camp Tester. Carries no
mechanical function; it exists so a DM has something concrete to describe a
PC examining, borrowing, or being refused a look at.

**A flask of Ammet Ford well water.** Sweet beyond ordinary mineral
sweetness — "a thickness... a hush at the back of the throat"
(`source-bible.md` → Places) — and, per `R1b`'s D-17, the same contaminated
groundwater as the valley's Sweetwater disease at a filtered, dosed-down
concentration. Narrative effect only: a PC who drinks it feels the
unconsidered pull to keep drinking described on the page, nothing more on a
single taste. **This is not a magic item.** No combat or healing function;
its weight is entirely in what the party does once they realise what it
is. `(SYS?)`: if the real ruleset wants a long-term compulsion or health
mechanic for sustained drinking, that number is not invented here.

**The standing bounty notice.** A plain handbill, two crowns offered for word
of whoever's been asking after the buried survey, paid out by a company
runner who genuinely doesn't know why (`D1`, `R1b` → Reveal C, Route 2). A
DM can hand this to players directly the moment they're told it exists on
Beddoe's slate — it's meant to be read, not just summarised.

**A travelling clerk's case.** The Indemnity Office's own clerk carries a
battered dispatch case between session-day stops — forms, a stamp, a
schedule book that knows the seventh-year payout clause was timed off
something he was never told what (`R1b` → Reveal B, Route 3). Prop only; its
function in play is that it exists and the clerk is willing to open it for
the right question.

## Weapons

This is a working valley, not an armoury. What follows is what people here
actually carry, archetype and tier only — no stat block until the real
ruleset lands.

**Mine tools (pick, sledge, draw-knife).** Improvised before they're ever
weapons. A pick or a cooper's draw-knife used in a fight reads as exactly
what it is — someone reaching for the nearest heavy thing, not a trained
combatant drawing a weapon. `(SYS?)` tier: low; favours intimidation and
improvised damage over any trained bonus.

**The company gatekeeper's cosh.** Standard-issue for the Valley Trust's
gatekeepers and runners (`G1`'s Valley Trust Functionary archetype) — short,
leather-wrapped, meant to discourage rather than injure. Per `G1`'s own
note, none of this Part's lead-web routes require a fight with anyone
carrying one; if it's drawn at all, that's the scene going wrong, not the
scene's intended shape.

**An Attrition-surplus revolver.** Four years of war left the Commonwealth
with more of these than it has uses for (canon §5 — the Attrition, four
years, won technically). Plain, worn, usually missing its original holster.
A PC or an NPC veteran carrying one is unremarkable in this valley; drawing
it is not. `(SYS?)` tier: the one weapon in this file capable of lethal
force at range — treat it as a serious escalation every time it appears,
never as flavour sidearm a character fires casually.

**A trench knife.** Attrition-era, double-edged, a knuckle guard worn smooth
— carried quietly by veterans who don't talk about where they got it, the
same register as Edie's own war-marked gear (`source-bible.md` → People)
without being hers specifically. `(SYS?)` tier: low-to-mid, close range
only.

**The capping-team's sampling rod.** A graduated steel rod for testing a
well's depth and drawing a sample — not built as a weapon, usable as a
improvised bludgeon exactly as readily as a mine pick, and exactly as poor a
choice against anyone actually trying to hurt the party. Included here
because `R1b`'s Testing-Team Escort set piece hands a PC this tool before it
hands them anything better, and a DM should know what it is if a player
asks to use it as one.

## DM Notes

**Nothing above needs a fight.** Per `G1`'s own standing note and `A1`
pillar 1: every weapon here is either explicitly non-lethal by design (the
cosh), an escalation the DM should treat seriously rather than casually (the
revolver), or something nobody in Region I's lead web is built to need
(everything else). Reach for combat because the table wants it, not because
an item entry exists.

**Items are props, not a reward track.** This file deliberately has no
loot — no item here is meant to make a PC mechanically stronger, because
this is an investigation, not a dungeon with chests in it (`A1` pillar 1).
A DM looking for a "found item" beat to reward a scene should reach for
information or standing (a favour, a route opened) per `S1`, not an object
from this list.

**The chalice-gifts section is inert on purpose.** If a player asks whether
their character can drink from a chalice this Part, the honest answer is
there isn't one here to drink from (`R1a`, `R1b`, `G1`, `C3` all agree). The
"Sure Footing" worked example exists to teach the procedure to whoever runs
`T1`, not to be handed to a PC early because the Compendium happened to have
something written down already — that is exactly the back door `C3`'s own
DM notes warn against.

**A flask of the water is a prop, not a gift and not a weapon.** Don't let
"drink it and see" become a resource a party farms for an edge; its entire
function in play is the realisation, not a mechanical effect worth
repeating.
