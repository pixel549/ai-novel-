# A1 — CAMPAIGN BRIEF

*Reader: DM / AI DM. Read `PLAN.md` first for structure and tone contract.
Cites `source-bible.md`, `hooks.md`, and `novel/canon.md` §§4–9 throughout;
this file states conclusions, not derivations — see `DECISIONS.md` D-1–D-10
for how each was reached and Jake's standing right to overturn any of them.*

## Premise

A year after a mine-owner died in a Church-run compensation office and the
Church walked away from it, the Aventine Commonwealth has not changed shape.
The works-water schedule got moved forward. Wells are being capped. A dead
man's name clears a room faster than a living senator's. Nothing is fixed;
some things are slightly less broken than they were.

The player characters are not Lloyd, Maria, or Edie, and nothing they do
will touch what those three already settled. They are new people, pulled
into the same machinery by the same kind of ordinary leverage — a debt, a
missing relative, a job that paid too well to ask why, a posting nobody
wanted — in a Commonwealth where the Church of the Mercies is wealthy,
popular, and still running a sixty-year experiment nobody with power is
looking for. The campaign is an investigation into institutions that do not
want to be investigated, played out across real towns, real rail lines, and
real bureaucracies, with the Salts sitting underneath all of it as the thing
that explains some of the strangeness and none of the rest.

**One-line hook:** a year on from the Larrow Valley, the water's still not
clean, the Church still hasn't been touched, and somebody just handed the
party a reason to look closer than the paperwork wants them to.

## Timeline Anchor

The campaign opens roughly one year after the novel's close (`DECISIONS.md`
D-1). Fixed at this point:

- Halvard Crane is dead, publicly blamed on the Church rather than on his
  own killer. Larrow Consolidated Works has new, quieter management.
- The works-water schedule has been brought forward under Ferrick's
  sub-panel; wells across the valley are being tested and capped, town by
  town, ahead of frost. This is slow, underfunded, and incomplete — exactly
  the kind of reconstruction money that doesn't fully reach a valley.
- The Sanctum at Carrowgate is untouched. Everyone who mattered there before
  ch. 18's exposure still matters there now. Ondine Vail was promoted into
  it, not punished.
- Ascension House, Vantry, is a burned shell. What is under it has not been
  excavated by anyone who'd admit to it.
- The Vantry assembly still holds the register, stud book, and sixty years
  of extraction files from the ward's exposure, filed and essentially
  unread.
- Lloyd Hale is wanted under a false name and barred from Callow Ford. Maria
  Vesk is with him, in or near Carrowgate. Edie Marrow runs Senator
  Ferrick's household security, her knot-cord worn openly for the first
  time. None of the three is a PC and none of their arcs is open for the
  party to resolve, continue, or undo — see `source-bible.md` → People and
  the who-knows-what table (canon §6) before using any of them as an NPC.

Advance this anchor as Parts are played; `campaign/K1` (not yet written)
will hold the running continuity index. Nothing above moves because a PC
asks it to.

## Campaign Pillars

1. **Investigation, not dungeon-crawl.** The default unit of play is a
   place, a problem, and a web of people who know pieces of it. Every
   essential reveal is reachable at least three ways. Combat happens, but
   it is not the point and it is rarely the fastest route to an answer.
2. **The surface is the campaign.** The Salts explain part of the world's
   Arcana and supply a handful of short, dangerous side-trips (the thin
   sites). They are not the loop the party returns to. A session can go by
   without the Salts coming up at all.
3. **Institutions rot; they are outlasted or escaped, not defeated.** The
   Church does not get destroyed in this campaign for the same reason it
   doesn't in the novel: that would be a cleaner world than the one the
   source material built. Victories are personal, costly, and real anyway.
4. **Apply the cost test to every scene.** Before and after running any
   scene, ask what it costs and who pays — the institution, or a person.
   This governs small scenes as much as set pieces; see canon §8.
5. **Offer neglect over spectacle.** When a scene could go loud (a chase, a
   fire, a confrontation) or quiet (a filing cabinet nobody emptied, a
   report nobody acted on, a clerk who just does her job), the quiet option
   is usually the more honest one and should be on the table as often as
   the loud one.
6. **Arcana is uncommon and mostly weak; the Salts is the only path to
   anything stronger, and it has a price.** No PC casually becomes as
   capable as Maria. Chalice abilities, when available at all, cost
   something real and permanent (Q-4, default yes — see `C3`).

## Relationship to the Novel

The novel is history the party will bump into, not a story they are
inside. Treat it the way a table would treat any recent, locally
significant event: known in outline to anyone literate who reads a
broadsheet, known in damning detail to almost nobody, and still live enough
that mentioning the wrong name in the wrong office gets a reaction.

- **What's public:** Crane is dead; the Church is blamed; the water
  schedule moved; wells are being capped. This is common knowledge, retold
  with the usual drift and exaggeration.
- **What's buried but recoverable:** the Ascension House register, stud
  book, and files, sitting in the Vantry assembly's archive, legally
  viewable and never requested (`hooks.md` #3). This is the campaign's
  model for "evidence existing and evidence mattering are different
  problems" — use it, or something built the same way, whenever the party
  wants proof rather than rumour.
- **What's permanently closed:** the who-knows-what gaps in canon §6 (Lloyd's
  second arm, his five weeks in Carrowgate, Anse Duley's fate as known to
  Edie). No PC investigation uncovers these. They are not secrets the table
  is meant to solve; they are facts about three specific people that stay
  theirs.
- **Cameo rules:** Lloyd, Maria, and Edie may appear as NPCs under the rules
  `I1` will set out. Until `I1` exists, do not run them as more than brief,
  off-page mentions — a name on a report, a face across a platform — rather
  than scenes with dialogue.

## What the PCs Are

New characters, unconnected to Lloyd, Maria, and Edie (`DECISIONS.md` D-2).
They are not chosen ones and nothing marks them as special before play
starts. What gets them into the campaign's business is ordinary: money
owed, a missing person, a job posting that pays too well for what it says
it is, a transfer nobody wanted, a debt to someone who works for the
Church. The novel's three were pulled in by grief, guilt, and work in that
order — the party should be pulled in the same unglamorous way, through
something that could happen to anyone in this Commonwealth, not through
destiny.

They are built on Jake's ruleset (d20 base, six attributes, limb targeting,
status effects, mutation, Insight, stopwatch parry) once it is available in
`campaign/system/`. Until then, treat the party as competent adults with a
trade or a service record behind them — exactly the kind of person the
Attrition left underemployed — and mark anything that needs a hard number
`(SYS?)`.

What they are not: agents of the Church, agents of the state, or already
in possession of Arcana rarer than the setting allows (see `C3` for what's
available and at what cost). A PC with a chalice ability earned it at a
thin site, in play, at the price S1/C3 sets — never as a starting-character
freebie.

## How the Salts Fits

The Salts is background, not the engine (`DECISIONS.md` D-9). It is real,
it is not a secret from anyone with money or Church access, and it explains
some of the Commonwealth's Arcana and not all of it:

- It explains chalice-granted abilities like Lloyd's second arm — a power
  bought at a hydro, at a cost, once.
- It does **not** explain native Arcana like Maria's. That stays
  unexplained, per canon, and no amount of play should resolve it.
- It surfaces in three ways at the table: as lore the party can learn
  (`C3`); as a faction tool the Church and others use industrially, same as
  in the novel; and as three short thin sites (`T1`–`T3`), compact delves
  using the ruleset's actual Salts loop (anchor chalice, hub, bail-out,
  Tokens, no fast travel) rather than the surface adaptation `S1` builds for
  everything else.
- Taking the salts is optional for the party and available only at a thin
  site, at a price (Q-4 default). It is never required to finish a Part,
  and a Part that can only be solved by sending the party into the Salts is
  a Part that needs rewriting.
- When the Salts does surface, the register changes: Bloodborne /
  Lovecraftian dream-logic, lore withheld rather than explained, sources
  unreliable even when sincere. This is a deliberate shift from the surface
  register and should feel like one — see `PLAN.md`'s tone contract.

## Using These Files

This brief is the top of the stack. Read it once per campaign, not once per
session. Everything else cites it rather than repeating it:

- `PLAN.md` — structure, part template, conventions, and the work queue's
  shape.
- `source-bible.md` — every established fact, organised by place, faction,
  person, object, and rule.
- `hooks.md` — the open threads worth building sessions around.
- `DECISIONS.md` — defaults, open questions for Jake, and anything flagged
  for review. Never loaded at the table.
- `novel/canon.md` §§4–9 — the Church, the world, who-knows-what, prose and
  authorial stance, for anything this brief compresses too far.

Later files (`C1` Gazetteer, `C2` Factions & Clocks, `C3` the Salts as
background, `S1` Surface Adaptation, `D1` Bases, the `R` Parts) build on
every pillar above without restating it. Where a later file would
contradict this brief, that is a bug — log it in `DECISIONS.md` and fix the
later file, not this one, unless Jake rules otherwise.
