# HOOKS

Open threads from the full read-through, ranked by campaign usefulness. Per
RUNBOOK P1.6. Cites `campaign/source-bible.md` for the underlying facts;
this file is just the "what the table could do with this" layer on top.
Design/continuity questions that need Jake's call (R-1–R-5) live in
`campaign/DECISIONS.md`, not here — these are story hooks, not open design
questions, even where the two overlap (noted below where they do).

1. **The Sweetwater double meaning.** Ammet Ford's company well water and
   the valley's wasting disease share a name and are never confirmed as the
   same substance, only linked by the people who drink one and fear the
   other. What the table could do with this: a faction (or Crane's
   successors) selling a controlled, pleasant dose of the exact thing that's
   killing people slowly elsewhere in the valley — a C2/C3 faction mechanic
   and a strong Part I (Larrow Valley) investigation spine, since the
   campaign's default first region is this one. (ch. 8–9)

2. **The "19 DAYS REMAINING" extraction timer.** Never explained anywhere
   in the finished book — confirmed a permanent gap, not a thread still in
   play (see SYNTHESIS.md). What the table could do with this: a
   background clock nobody at Stillwell questions, visible on every
   extraction readout — perfect for a Factions & Clocks (C2) entry that
   ticks toward something the PCs discover only by asking the question the
   novel's leads never did.

3. **The Vantry assembly's standing archive.** The evidence that ended the
   Ascension House ward — the stolen intake register, the stud book, sixty
   years of extraction files — still exists, filed under the city assembly
   and essentially never requested. What the table could do with this: a
   location where the PCs can find real, damning, perfectly legal-to-view
   proof of the Fourth Mercy, and the campaign's clearest demonstration that
   evidence existing and evidence mattering are different problems — strong
   for D1 (a base nearby) or a J1 handout.

4. **The pre-Church foundation under Ascension House.** Older stone,
   predating the Church's ownership of the ground, under a barred basement
   window that showed a pale standing gleam and, once, something that
   "moved nothing like a man walks." Never resolved on the page, even
   obliquely referenced once more in ch. 16's delirium and then dropped.
   What the table could do with this: Ascension House is a burned shell a
   year after ch. 25 (per `DECISIONS.md` D-1) — the foundation presumably
   still stands under the ash. Strong candidate for one of the three thin
   sites (T1–T3): whatever was on that ground before the chapel is still
   there.

5. **The Sanctum's crisis-PR playbook.** Grief offered first, blame
   narrowed to names furthest from the top, a restitution figure large
   enough that disputing it looks uncharitable, eleven days of news before
   the story curdles into an authenticity dispute. What the table could do
   with this: a reusable template for how the Church responds to *any*
   exposure the PCs manage to cause later in the campaign — not a one-time
   event but a faction behaviour pattern for C2.

6. **The Fourth Mercy's added catechism line.** A fourth line added beneath
   "bread, shelter, absolution" at Ascension House, in a recent hand, exact
   wording deliberately withheld by the novel. What the table could do with
   this: a genuine unanswered mystery the novel planted on purpose — good
   handout material (J1) precisely because Jake gets to decide what it says
   without contradicting anything already on the page.

7. **The thing in tank four.** Watched Lloyd specifically throughout ch.
   14, never explained, never escalated, never reached for him. What the
   table could do with this: a bestiary entry (G1) built around an entity
   whose motive is left genuinely open — it noticed Lloyd once; nothing
   says it won't again.

8. **Vail's "impossible" speed to the Ascension House yard.** She was
   waiting in the yard moments after the fire started, with no explanation
   offered anywhere in the book for how she knew or arrived that fast. What
   the table could do with this: either a personal ability of Vail's never
   otherwise shown, or evidence the Sanctum has surveillance reach well
   beyond what it admits to — either reading supports a Factions & Clocks
   entry for how much the Church actually sees.

9. **Brother Aldous's non-moving collar.** The clearest "something is wrong
   with a Church agent" sighting in the book attached to a named, speaking
   character rather than a background figure. What the table could do with
   this: strong direct material for a Fourth Mercy construct/homunculus
   bestiary entry (G1) — a Mercy brother who looks and talks like a person
   and isn't quite one, already demonstrated as walkable-past in ordinary
   daylight.

10. **The Selwick day-discrepancy.** Aldous's two-day estimate against the
    marker post's one-day reading, on a stretch of road Edie's own counting
    doesn't get wrong elsewhere. What the table could do with this: a seed
    for a thin site nobody is supposed to be near yet, or just a mundane lie
    worth a rumour table entry (J1) either way.

11. **The undefined wire-frame creature (ch. 2).** Thin, jointed, no face,
    moves by flickering, dissolves to salt mist, no name or explanation
    given. What the table could do with this: a ready-made bestiary entry
    for an early, low-tier Salts encounter (G1/T1) — "undefined horror,
    jointed, light-averse-adjacent" is usable close to as-is.

12. **The nineteen-foot shaft (ch. 3).** A shaft surveyed twice at twelve
    feet reads nineteen feet on the actual descent, with no resolution
    offered. What the table could do with this: a reusable Salts-delve
    device — distance and time inside the Salts don't have to agree with
    measurement, and a table can use that honestly rather than as a one-off
    flavour beat.

13. **Edie's uncashed chalice cost.** Confirmed in SYNTHESIS.md as a
    permanent gap — she never visibly pays the cost `outline.md` specified
    for her (losing the ability to sleep without waking twice). What the
    table could do with this: if Edie appears as an NPC (I1), this is
    available to use or not — a DM could decide it quietly did land and
    simply was never narrated, which costs nothing to introduce and gives
    her one more private, unstated price to have paid. Low priority as a
    PC-facing hook since it's about an NPC's backstory, not something the
    table directly investigates.

14. **Edie's itemised recovery-cost ledger ("Paid out," ch. 19).** Not a
    mystery hook so much as a mechanic template: rent, bread, an unpaid
    surgeon's fee, broadsheet printing, a blanket, coal, closed out in one
    line. What the table could do with this: the direct model for how an
    Edie-type NPC quartermaster would cost a party's downtime for real at
    the table (F1), rather than an abstracted "spend X gold."

15. **The Carrowgate geography/timeline question (ch. 12/13).** Two lines
    imply an undramatized stop in or connection to Carrowgate that doesn't
    fit cleanly into the on-page route between ch. 6 and ch. 12. What the
    table could do with this: lowest priority here — it only matters if the
    Gazetteer (C1) needs exact travel geography for the Larrow Valley to
    Carrowgate road; otherwise leave it as unresolved backstory the novel
    never had to dramatize either.

## Note for I1 (Dramatis Personae)

The leads' final states (ch. 25) are themselves hook material, not just
biography: Lloyd is a wanted fugitive under a false name, barred from his
own home village; Maria is with him in Carrowgate; Edie is inside Ferrick's
household, trusted, her knot-cord worn openly for the first time in the
book. A campaign set roughly a year later (per `DECISIONS.md` D-1/D-2) can
have the PCs cross paths with any of the three without touching the book's
own ending — I1 should write the cameo rules with that in mind, honouring
the who-knows-what gaps listed in `source-bible.md` → Rules.
