# G2 — BESTIARY ADDITIONS, PART II

*Reader: DM. Builds on `R2a-vantry-overview.md` and `R2b-vantry-leads-
antagonist.md` §5 (Encounters) and §6 (Set Pieces) — read both first; this
file does not restate the encounters list or the set pieces, it supplies
the archetype write-ups those sections name and leave undetailed. Cites
`R2a`, `R2b`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`, `G1-bestiary-part-1.md`,
`source-bible.md`, `hooks.md`, and `novel/canon.md` §§2/4/5/8 throughout. No
`campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet (Q-1); every entry below is behaviour
and tells, never a stat block — numbers are generated live at the table,
archetype and tier only, marked `(SYS?)` per `A2`'s standing instruction.*

## How to use this file

Same job `G1` did for Region I, now for Region II: two groups a DM can
reach for mid-session instead of inventing from nothing — **People** (the
unnamed or lightly-named faces a lead-web route needs consistent behaviour
for) and **Pest-Class Horrors** (the Vantry instance of the Attrition-
scarred ground, per `C2`'s "the dead and their pests" faction entry) — plus
one new category this Part introduces for the first time, **Fourth Mercy
Products**, built out from the possibility `R2b` §5 explicitly left for
this file rather than confirming itself. Read an entry once, before the
session it's needed in; none of these carry a scene the way Wick, Hester
Combe, or Ress Tannery do in `R2a`/`R2b` — this file supplies the texture
around them, not the leads.

**Session default — D-33 (`DECISIONS.md`).** Where `R2a` and `R2b` name a
category (the bread-line sister or brother, the Church-adjacent face that
notices and mentions it to Wick, a lodging-row resident with a minor spark,
the Vantry instance of the Pest Pressure Clock) without building it out,
and where `R2b` names a possibility (the tank-four remnant) without
confirming it, this file invents the behaviour, tells, and — for the
remnant only — the bounded scope those sections ask for. Logged here
rather than decided silently, and overturnable the same way `D-18` was for
Region I.

## People

### The Vantry Mercy Sister or Brother (bread line)

Covers every unnamed Mercy House face `R2a` and `R2b` leave to invent per
scene — the bread queue, the shelter roll, the confession booth — and
Reveal B's Route 3 specifically (the sister who confirms Nell was flagged
and never followed up).

**Behaviour.** Sincere, busy, and genuinely moved by the work, same as
Brother Aldous and Brother Tam in Region I (`G1`) but scaled to a
city-sized queue rather than a country road — per `C2`'s own note, most of
the Church's people here genuinely don't know what the fourth Mercy is,
and nothing about this archetype should play otherwise. Answers a direct,
specific question about a specific name readily; answers a general
question about "where people go" with the honest, slightly embarrassed
admission that nobody really tracks that past the shelter roll, which is
Reveal B Route 3's whole point, not a withheld fact.

**Tells.** Keeps the shelter roll in a hand different from whoever started
it years ago — names crossed through for a dozen ordinary reasons, nothing
sinister in the handwriting itself; recites the bread line's rules (one
ladle, no seconds before the queue's turned over once) without needing to
think about them; genuinely brightens, not performs brightening, at a
returning face they remember.

**Where this matters.** Region II's most legible version of `A1` pillar 3
(institutions rot without anyone in them being a villain) — per the tone
contract (`PLAN.md`), nothing about this archetype should read as staged
menace. Any horror the table finds here lives one step removed, in what
happens to a flagged name next, never in this room.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

### The Church-Adjacent Watcher

Covers `R2b` §5's Pursuit encounter: "a Church-adjacent face noticing the
party has been asking after Ascension House or the archive, and mentioning
it to Wick rather than confronting anyone directly." Not one NPC but a
category — a Mercy House regular, a shopkeeper near the ruin, a
lodging-row neighbour with a cousin in service at a Mercy House, whoever a
scene needs.

**Behaviour.** Never follows, never confronts, never seems to be watching
anything at all — the encounter is entirely retrospective, per `R2b`'s own
framing ("the party realising, later, that Wick already knew"). Whoever
this is has an entirely ordinary, non-sinister reason to have noticed and
mentioned the party in the first place: it's what they'd have said to
anyone, about anything, in the same unremarkable way gossip moves through
a city this size.

**Tells.** None, deliberately — the only tell is Wick's own foreknowledge
later, never a cue in the scene itself. A DM who gives this archetype a
meaningful glance, a lowered voice, or a reason to seem suspicious has
written it wrong; per the tone contract, Church menace lives in content,
never in staging, and this encounter has no content until Wick delivers
it.

**Where this matters.** The single easiest way to make Wick feel sinister
without ever having him do anything sinister — the unease is entirely the
party's own, built from how ordinary and untraceable the leak turns out to
be. Use sparingly; once is usually enough for a table to feel it.

`(SYS?)` tier: non-combat; properly a Social entry per `PLAN.md`'s own
Encounters taxonomy, included here because `R2b` filed it under Pursuit.

### The Lodging-Row Spark

Covers `R2b` §5's optional Arcana encounter — "a lodging-row resident with
a minor, unregistered spark... or someone like her" — for use when the
table meets this texture somewhere other than Nell Quane herself, whose
own case is `R2a`/`R2b`'s to run, not this file's to duplicate.

**Behaviour.** Keeps the gift smaller than it has to be, same discipline
`G1`'s Unregistered Arcana Practitioner set for Region I, now sharpened by
the Vantry archive's own existence: this person knows, in the vague way a
city knows things, that a register of exactly this kind of person exists
somewhere and was opened once already. Reacts to being noticed with the
same wariness-then-warmth order `G1` set, but faster to the wariness — the
stud book isn't a rumour here, it's a filed document three streets away.

**Tells.** Uses the gift only where a landlord, a foreman, or a stranger
won't see it — a candle lit behind a turned back, a held breath instead of
a visible effort; deflects "where did you get that" the same way Maria's
own uncertainty works (canon §2), scaled down, never refusing the question
outright.

**Where this matters.** Worth using only to dramatise `C2`'s point that
being noticed is a cost, same restraint `G1` and `R2b` both set; a scene
that leaves this person better off for having been found out is being
played against the Part's own grain. Skip entirely if the table's thread
doesn't need it — Nell's own case already carries this weight for the
Part.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

## Pest-Class Horrors (the Attrition-scarred ground)

Per `C2` and `G1`: weather, not villains, with no wants in the faction
sense, worse wherever digging disturbs old ground and otherwise dormant,
never escalating into a boss fight. Region I's version lived in the mine's
older workings; Vantry's lives in the foundations, culverts, and rail
cutting under the lodging rows and the yards (`R2a`), ground the war never
got properly cleared before the city rebuilt fast and cheap over it.

### The Rail-Cut Lingerer

The same low-tier archetype `G1` built as the Collapsed-Tunnel Lingerer and
already re-dressed once for Hessel Bight's flooded cellar — re-dressed a
second time here for Vantry's own ground, per `G1`'s own instruction that a
region doesn't need two pest archetypes to make the same clock legible.
This is the Vantry instance of the Pest Pressure Clock (`R2a`; `C2`), not a
new species.

**Behaviour.** Identical to the Collapsed-Tunnel Lingerer: doesn't hunt,
doesn't pursue past the stretch of foundation, culvert, or cutting it's
bound to, investigates sluggishly rather than attacking, and loses interest
within minutes of a party backing off. No plan, no grievance — a pest
problem, not a haunting (canon §5), exactly as `G1` held for Region I.

**Tells (re-dressed for Vantry).** A permanent cold patch in brickwork that
should be holding the yard's own residual warmth; the mill whistle's echo
coming back a half-second late from a culvert mouth that shouldn't echo at
all; coal dust disturbed in a short dragging track that starts and stops
mid-stride, with nothing at either end.

**Function in the Part.** Gives the lodging rows and the rail yards (`R2a`)
the same "this ground has more than one reason to be left alone" texture
the mine gave Region I's own antagonist pressure — a DM running a scene
near fresh digging or a disturbed culvert can use this without inventing a
second Church-adjacent reason for anyone to want the area quiet.

`(SYS?)` tier: low; a contained scene, never a chase across the whole yard,
per `C2`'s own instruction that this clock never resolves into a boss
fight.

## Fourth Mercy Products

A category Region I never needed (`G1`'s own DM Notes) — Region II is the
first Part with an actual, if burned-out, Fourth Mercy site on its map,
and `R2b` §5 names a possibility at that site without confirming it or
giving it a stat block, explicitly leaving the build-out to this file.

### The Tank-Four Remnant (optional)

**What it is, and isn't.** `hooks.md` #7's thing in tank four watched
Lloyd throughout ch. 14, in the working laboratory, before the fire that
destroyed it in ch. 15. Whether anything of it survived the burning is
never answered on the page and is not answered here either — this entry
exists so a DM who wants to use the possibility has behaviour and tells
ready, not so the possibility becomes a fact. **This is not `T1`.**
`T1-the-foundation.md`'s own entity (`DECISIONS.md` D-28, "the Gleam")
lives under the pre-Church foundation beneath Ascension House and is
explicitly, permanently unconnected to the Fourth Mercy's own work; this
remnant, if a DM uses it at all, is the opposite — Fourth Mercy work
specifically, confined to the burned footprint of the laboratory itself,
with no path down to `T1`'s site and no awareness of it. Running both at
the same ruin is fine; running them as the same thing is not.

**Behaviour.** Watches. Doesn't approach, doesn't flee, doesn't react
visibly to being watched back — the same unresolved stillness ch. 14 gave
it, carried forward rather than escalated. If a party searches the burned
tank room's footprint specifically (not the ruin generally), it may be
there, noticing them the way it noticed Lloyd once; nothing about it has
ever reached for anyone, and nothing here decides whether that's about to
change.

**Tells.** A patch of the ash that's colder than the rest and never seems
to warm by afternoon; a reflection in standing rainwater that holds a beat
too long after whatever cast it has moved on; nothing audible, ever — per
`hooks.md` #7's own silence, this entry invents no sound for it.

**Where this matters, and its limits.** Per `R2b` §5: "anything larger
than a remnant belongs to Region IV, not here." Use this only as a single,
wordless beat inside a scene already at the ruin (Reveal A's Route 3, or
`R2b`'s "The Ruin in Daylight" set piece) — never as a pursuit, never as a
fight, never as confirmation of what it is or what it wants. A DM who
gives it a name, a motive, or a second appearance has built something this
file didn't; that's a fine table decision, same restraint `G1`'s DM Notes
gave Brother Aldous's collar, but it should be made once and then kept,
not improvised scene to scene.

`(SYS?)` tier: none. Not a combat encounter under any circumstance this
file anticipates; if a future session needs it to be one, that decision
belongs with whichever file makes it, not this one.

## DM Notes

**The remnant is optional in a stricter sense than anything in `G1`.**
Region I's archetypes are all usable by default; this one is usable only
if a DM specifically wants the Part's single faint Salts-adjacent echo and
is prepared to keep it exactly as small as it's written here. Skipping it
entirely changes nothing else in the Part.

**Don't let the Rail-Cut Lingerer explain the lodging rows.** Per `R2a`'s
own DM Notes, most of what happens to people here is ordinary and sad, not
supernatural; this archetype is ground texture for the yards and culverts
specifically, not a reason for every missing lodger.

**Nothing above needs a fight to matter.** Per `A1` pillar 1 and the cost
test (canon §8): a Mercy sister who answers honestly, a Watcher who never
shows themselves, a Lingerer that loses interest, a remnant that only
watches back. Reach for combat because the table wants it, not because an
entry exists.

**How the novel's three appear.** Nothing in this file changes
`R2a`/`R2b`'s restriction on Edie — none of the archetypes above are her,
and none should be mistaken for a chance to give her dialogue ahead of
`I1`'s own rules.
