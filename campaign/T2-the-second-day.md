# T2 — THIN SITE 2: THE SECOND DAY

*Reader: DM. Builds on `C3-the-salts-as-background.md` (the thin-site rules)
and `T1-the-foundation.md` (the first file to apply them — read it for the
register even though this site works differently) and

`R2a-vantry-overview.md` for the road this site sits on, `C1-gazetteer.md`
for the Northern Road's own framing of `hooks.md` #10 as a "thin-site seed,"
`G1-bestiary-part-1.md` for the Mercy Brother archetype (used as-is, not

## How to Get Here

A party that travels the road between the Larrow Valley and Vantry, rather
than taking the rail — which clears the same stretch in under a day and
never puts anyone within sight of a marker post (`R1a`, `R2a`) — spends
several days on the north road through Tannery Reach, Sennet, and the
Selwick turning (`C1`; `R1a`; `R2a`'s travel summary). This file doesn't
need `F1`'s exact per-leg numbers, not yet written, to know that somewhere
on that walk a party reaches the stretch `source-bible.md` already names:
an almshouse at Selwick that a marker post puts one day off, and that
Brother Aldous — travelling the same road with Brother Tam, blankets and
seed corn, exactly as `R1a` and `R1b` already scope his Region I appearance
— puts at two, sincerely, without hesitation, the same register canon §3's
writing rules for Vail extend to every honest Mercy brother in this book
(`G1`'s Mercy Brother archetype).

If the party meets Aldous and Tam here, run them exactly as `G1` already
builds them: genuinely charitable, unhurried, happy to talk, never staged
as sinister. `R1b`'s restraint still holds — nothing here is a threat, and
nothing here resolves whether Aldous is a construct (`hooks.md` #9; `G1`'s
own instruction that the question "should stay open at the table by
default"). What this file adds is the half of the discrepancy neither `R1a`
nor `R1b` had reason to use: Aldous isn't guessing, and he isn't
misremembering. He has walked this stretch, by his own account, more times
than he can number, and it is two days every time, said with the exact same
unbothered sincerity he says everything else.

**There is no hydro here, no reagent, no basement window.** Per `C3`'s own
instruction that a thin site invent its own version of the trigger rather
than borrow another's, this file's version is the count itself. A party
that hears both numbers and then chooses one, out loud, and actually walks
the mile counting their own paces against it — not idly, the way anyone
paces out a stretch of road they're bored of, but committing to a specific
answer before the first milestone — finds the road answers whichever
number they picked. A party that picks the shorter, truer number (the
post's one day) gets exactly that: an unremarkable day's walk to an
unremarkable almshouse, `R1a`'s and `C1`'s Selwick precisely as written,
nothing to show for having asked. A party that commits to Aldous's number
instead — the extra day, the one the stone doesn't agree exists — gets the
extra day. That's this file.

Offer this moment plainly and once, the same discipline `T1` uses: ask what
the party believes, let them answer honestly, and open the site only if
they commit to the two-day count before anyone has taken a step on faith.
A party that picks the post, or that never engages with the discrepancy at
all and simply walks to Selwick, has made a completely valid choice (`A1`
pillar 2) and reaches the almshouse exactly as `C1` and `R1a` already
describe it.

## The Loop, As-Is

Everything below runs the ruleset's actual Salts-loop rules unmodified —
anchor chalice, hub, Token economy, bail-out, no fast travel, mutation —
the second of the three places in the campaign this is true (`C3`; `S1`
"Mutation and the Salts Loop"). None of `S1`'s surface replacements (crowns
and favours, safehouses, flight/capture/recovery, real travel) apply once a
party has committed to the second day. `(SYS?)` for every number this
needs: anchor cost, Token count, bail-out trigger conditions, and mutation
thresholds all wait on `campaign/system/` landing. Run the loop the way
`T1` already models it until the real ruleset says otherwise: time inside
doesn't track time outside, a run is taxing, injuries taken here are real
on surfacing and may heal wrong.

## What's Out Here

Not the chapel, and not `T1`'s foundation either. `C3` is explicit that
nothing says two thin sites open onto the same dreamscape, and this file
takes that instruction as seriously as `T1` did: where `T1` went down into
something load-bearing and architectural, this site doesn't go anywhere at
all. The road doesn't change shape, doesn't descend, doesn't open a door.
It simply doesn't end when it should.

**Session default — D-29 (`DECISIONS.md`).** `hooks.md` #10 leaves the
discrepancy itself unresolved — "a discrepancy noticed by Edie and never
resolved" — and this file keeps it exactly that unresolved rather than
deciding which number was ever true. What this file adds isn't an
explanation; it's a consequence: somewhere past the point a day's walk
should have ended, for a party that chose to believe it wouldn't, the road
keeps going anyway, and whatever happens on it from there isn't bound by
whether anyone back at the real marker post could measure it. This is
`hooks.md` #12's device — first used in `T1` — turned outward onto open
road instead of underground corridors: distance and time don't have to
agree with measurement. `T1` showed a DM how that works inside a building;
this file shows the same principle working on a flat, unremarkable stretch
of road, which should read as more unsettling for being less obviously
strange going in, not less.

## The Site

Four nodes, the same compact shape `PLAN.md` sets for a thin site and `T1`
already used. Run it as a sequence a DM narrates through, not a map a party
needs to walk correctly — let the road's own wrongness excuse any
inconsistency in how far anyone thinks they've gone.

### 1. The Second Milestone

The point where the walk should have ended doesn't announce itself. The
light doesn't change the way a day turning to evening should; it stays the
hour it was when the party committed to the count, for longer than an hour
has any right to last. The first concrete sign is a second milestone,
identical in cut and lettering to the real one the party passed that
morning — same moss, same chip on the same corner — reading a number that
matches neither the post's one day nor Aldous's two. No decision point
here, same discipline as `T1` node 1: describe, never signpost. If a player
asks whether they've actually been walking longer than it feels, that
question is the scene working.

### 2. The Company on the Road

Other travellers, ahead or behind, walking the same direction the party is.
At a glance they're nothing — coats, packs, the ordinary silhouette of
anyone on a long road — until someone notices that the distance between
the party and them never changes, no matter who speeds up. Closing on them
deliberately doesn't shrink the gap; it holds, exactly, like a held note —
until the party stops trying, at which point it closes to an ordinary
conversational distance in a single step. That's the frightening part, not
a chase.

Up close their faces resolve into something like boredom — the specific,
worn-out vacancy of someone re-checking a sum they already know comes out
wrong. They don't speak unless spoken to, and when they do, it's an
unfinished sentence about a day they were sure they'd already walked.
Struck or grabbed, they go to road-dust that the wind takes in a direction
there is no wind to take it.

**Encounter (optional).** `(SYS?)` tier: low, a scene, not a set piece. They
do not hunt with intent — like `T1`'s wire-frame creature, they notice
sluggishly and lose interest once a party puts real distance between
themselves and the group — but unlike that creature, they are a crowd, not
a single thing, and the crowd is larger the longer a party lingers on this
stretch, as if new travellers keep arriving at the same wrong count. Use
them once, as texture more than threat; a second appearance on a later
visit should feel like more of the same company catching up, not a
restocked encounter.

### 3. The Tally

Past the company on the road, a low drystone wall that wasn't there on the
way in runs alongside the verge for a stretch and then simply stops, the
way the road itself should have. Every stone is scratched with tally
marks — not ascending, not descending, no pattern a party can make sum to
anything, as if whatever has been keeping this stretch's count did it by
hand, badly, for far longer than anyone at the table has been walking.
Nothing moves here. If a party lingers, let them feel counted rather than
show them anything — the same restraint `T1`'s node 3 uses for its gleam.
If a player asks what's doing the counting, the honest table-time answer is
that nobody, not even Aldous, who has walked this road more times than he
can number, knows there's a wall here at all.

**This file commits to no further resolution than that.** A DM who wants
more should invent it consistent with the above, the same discipline D-28
set for `T1`'s own node 3 — don't cash this out just because it's sitting
there unexplained.

### 4. The Waystone

A flat stone at the road's edge, worn smooth the way a mile marker is worn
by hands resting on it between one count and the next. It holds still the
moment someone decides to look at it the way `C3` requires: the chalice,
lead, roughly a pint, sits on it the way a traveller's own cup might set
down for a rest — no pedestal, no carved depression, because this isn't
Stillwell's chapel and shouldn't borrow its furniture, and it isn't `T1`'s
shelf either. It is there because someone in the party decided it should
be, exactly as `C3` states, nowhere else in this file.

## The Chalice

Per `C3`'s D-13 and `S1`'s build procedure, the same as `T1`: this file does
not pre-build a gift. A PC who drinks at node 4 gets an ability bespoke to
them, built live, following `S1`'s five steps — name an ordinary function;
invert or extend it once, specifically; pair it with an equally specific
permanent cost using the same function; check it against Lloyd's shape;
never reuse Lloyd's own pair, and never reuse whatever was built for `T1`'s
drinker either, if that happened at the same table.

**Running the moment.** Ask what the character reaches for, physically or
otherwise, and build from there, not from a menu — `H1`'s "Sure Footing" is
still not available to assign, here any more than at `T1`. Decline is
exactly as valid an outcome as acceptance (`C3`; Maria's own on-page
model); a party that leaves node 4 with nobody having touched the chalice
has made the same choice she did.

**Cost is not optional once a gift is taken.** `(SYS?)` for the exact
mechanical shape, but the content isn't negotiable: specific, permanent,
and built from the same function as the gift. A cost that reads as a minor
inconvenience has been written wrong — go back to Lloyd's own pair before
finalising anything.

## Mutation and Injury

Per `S1`'s D-14, mutation stays Salts-side everywhere in the campaign
except the thin sites. This is the second site where it can trigger
exactly as the ruleset defines it, `(SYS?)` for the actual thresholds.
Injuries taken past the threshold are real on surfacing and may heal
wrong, same as `T1` and the novel's own chapel — nothing about an open road
makes this site feel lower-stakes than a basement, and running it that way
would be the mistake.

## Leaving

Bail-out applies, unmodified, `(SYS?)` for the trigger conditions — this is
not `S1`'s flight/capture/recovery rule; that rule doesn't reach past the
second milestone. A party that bails out mid-delve surfaces back on the
ordinary road, at the point the real first marker should have put them,
carrying whatever they went in with and nothing more; node 4's chalice does
not travel with anyone who hasn't already drunk from it.

**No fast travel back in.** Per `C3`'s own standing instruction against
letting a thin site become the loop by the back door: once a party leaves
this stretch, either by finishing node 4 or by bailing out, the road simply
goes back to behaving like a road. Walking it again later, even counting
it Aldous's way a second time, gets an ordinary two days and nothing more,
unless a DM deliberately decides this specific party gets one further
visit for a specific story reason — the exception, never the rule, same
restraint `T1` holds for its own stair.

## Why Nobody's Watching This Stretch of Road

Unlike `T1` (an institution's own ruin, where the site survives because
nobody at the Sanctum is looking under the ash) and the flagged `T3`
candidate (an institution's own active foundations), this site sits where
no institution has jurisdiction at all. Corlett's Valley Trust ends at the
valley's edge (`R1a`); Brother Calder Wick's entire job in Vantry is
managing attention toward Ascension House specifically, not patrolling a
multi-day stretch of road he's never been assigned (`R2b`). The Church's
own presence on this road is exactly Aldous and Tam, walking it for
charity, not reconnaissance — and if Aldous is in fact Fourth Mercy work,
as `hooks.md` #9 leaves open, this file still doesn't let that answer
anything: something sent out to hand round blankets has no more reason to
report an extra day on the road than an honest man would, and this file
does not decide which he is. The road has no owner, no patrol, no
institution's crisis-PR plan for anyone it swallows. This is the most
unsupervised thing in the campaign so far — a point about the stretch
itself, not an invitation for a DM to escalate it into one.

## Consequences

- **Region clocks.** `C1` scopes the Northern Road as connective tissue
  between Regions I and II, not assigned to either region's own Pest
  Pressure Clock instance. A DM running this mid-Part I or mid-Part II can
  route a visit through whichever clock is live for that session if they
  want a mechanical consequence, but nothing here requires it.
- **The chalice gift, if taken.** Permanent, per `C3`/`S1`, and belongs to
  the campaign going forward exactly as `T1`'s does — carry it into every
  later Part a DM runs for that character.
- **The tally wall at node 3.** Stays exactly as unresolved as it was
  found, unless a DM deliberately decides otherwise and logs that decision
  the way this file logs D-29.
- **Aldous and Tam.** Untouched. If the party reports the extra day back to
  Aldous, he receives it with the same sincerity as everything else he
  says — confirming, not explaining — and `hooks.md` #9 stays exactly as
  open as `G1` left it.
- **What the surface notices.** Nothing, by default, and more thoroughly
  than `T1` — that site at least sits under an institution that could, in
  principle, eventually learn of it; nobody is positioned to learn of this
  one at all. Per the cost test (canon §8), the campaign's quietest case
  yet of a cost paid entirely by the people who went in.

## DM Notes

**Same register shift as `T1`, same discipline.** Bloodborne/Lovecraftian
dream-logic, withhold lore, describe don't signpost, treat every source as
unreliable — including the road itself. A DM who explains the tally wall,
gives the company on the road a motive, or resolves why the numbers never
matched has written this file the same way wrong as giving Vail a smile
that doesn't reach her eyes.

**Don't resolve Aldous.** The two threads sharing this road — the Selwick
discrepancy and Aldous's collar — make it tempting to use this file to
finally decide `hooks.md` #9 since they're sitting right next to each
other. Resist it. `G1` already set the default that the construct question
stays open at the table; this file's whole restraint depends on not
cashing out every mystery it happens to touch, the same discipline D-28 set
for `T1`.

**Keep the horror in open space.** This site has no architecture and
nothing is "down" anywhere — if a DM runs it like `T1` with grass instead
of ash, both files start reading as the same trick twice. The company on
the road and the held distance are the point; don't add a building, a
cellar, or a door just because `T1` had one.

**Short means short.** Four nodes is the whole site. The waystone at node
4 is the payoff — spend the table's patience getting there, not wandering
an extra stretch of road first.

**Decline is not a failure state.** A party that walks away from node 4
having taken nothing has finished this file exactly as successfully as one
that drinks.
