# C1 — GAZETTEER

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md` — read that first for the
premise and the timeline anchor; this file does not restate it. Cites
`source-bible.md` → Places/Factions, `hooks.md`, and `novel/canon.md` §5
throughout; this file compresses them into regions, not the other way
round. The five regions below map directly onto the Part defaults already
set in `PLAN.md`'s work-item table (R1–R5). Reorder them if Jake wants a
different Part sequence, but update that table too.*

## How to use this file

One entry per region, each scoped to the Part it will become. Sites inside a
region keep the facts `source-bible.md` already established; what's added
here is geography, scale, and which towns go where. Two things in this file
are DM extrapolation beyond anything the novel states on the page, and are
marked as such where they appear: the shape of the rail network connecting
the regions, and the existence of Salts access points ("hydros," plural)
beyond Stillwell. Both are necessary for a campaign that travels; neither
contradicts anything in canon.md, and both are logged in `DECISIONS.md` as
defaults Jake can overturn.

## The Map

One rail trunk runs the length of the playable Commonwealth: from the coast
at Stillwell, inland and slightly north through the Larrow Valley, past
Carrowgate, and on to Vantry, where it meets the lines coming down from the
north and east — which is why canon calls Vantry the junction and not
Carrowgate, even though Carrowgate is the capital. A coach road shadows the
rail for the stretch between the valley and Vantry; most of ch. 6–13's
overland travel happens on this road, on foot and by cart, not on the train.

**Travel, loosely:** Stillwell to the valley's northern edge, under a day on
foot. Valley's edge to Hessel Bight, about four days overland (per ch. 7 —
"four days north of Stillwell" is the one hard distance the novel gives;
treat it as the anchor and scale everything else off it). Hessel Bight to
Vantry by road, another five or six days through Tannery Reach, Sennet, and
the Selwick turning. By rail, the same leg is under a day. Vantry to
Carrowgate, a day by rail, three or four by road. Nothing north or east of
Vantry is mapped yet — that's Part IV's and Part V's problem, not this
file's.

**On the Carrowgate question (R-4, `DECISIONS.md`).** Ch. 12's bonesetter and
ch. 13's "since Carrowgate" line imply a stop neither ch. 6–13 dramatizes.
Default here, consistent with everything on the page and nothing that
contradicts it: **the road and rail between the valley and Vantry pass
within a half-day of Carrowgate's outskirts**, close enough for an
undramatized changeover — a station, not a scene — rather than a detour.
This also fits Lloyd's own history (he left Callow Ford at twenty-two for
nine years of work *in* Carrowgate; the valley and the capital are already a
known labour corridor, not strangers to each other). Logged as D-11 in
`DECISIONS.md`. If a Part I lead web ever needs the exact road, this default
is where it starts; if it doesn't, leave it alone, same as the novel did.

## REGION I — The Larrow Valley *(Part I default, R1a/b)*

Twelve villages strung along the river Larrow, all twelve tracing their
ownership back to Halvard Crane's Larrow Consolidated Works, now under
quieter post-Crane management. Coastal at its western edge, where Stillwell
sits; inland and upriver toward the mine. A year on, this is a region being
repaired slowly and incompletely — the kind of reconstruction that looks
like progress from a Carrowgate office and like nothing from a kitchen
table.

**Stillwell Hydro.** The coast, cold and tidal. Granite-and-pine, peeling
plaster, iodine-stained rags on the seaward wall. Sells Salts entry as a
nerve cure at thirty-five shillings a run, strict batches of three, tracked
by a numbered brass token. One base candidate among several (`DECISIONS.md`
D-6); the reception clerk is a fixture, never a combat encounter, never
statted. A year on it is still open, still running the same batches — the
one piece of valley business the novel's events didn't touch, which is its
own quiet statement about how little reached the water's actual cause.

**Callow Ford.** Lloyd's home village, river-adjacent. Ivet Hale's house; a
new standpipe has replaced the old well as of a year after ch. 25, and she's
recovering on it. Lloyd is barred from the village under a false wanted
name — a standing complication for any PC who works with or around him as
an NPC, and a reason the village itself might quietly resent outside
attention.

**Ammet Ford.** "Larrow Consolidated Works — Ammet Ford Division." The
model company town: new brick, slate roofs, a glazed school, a company well
forty feet through clay whose water is suspiciously, compulsively sweet.
Visibly better-off than its eleven sister villages, which is the point —
it's the works' showpiece, kept nicer precisely so the other eleven don't
get asked why they aren't. The best candidate in the whole valley for a
Sweetwater-double-meaning plot (`hooks.md` #1): sell the dose here, let the
disease happen eleven villages over, and nobody outside the valley has to
connect the two.

**The mine and the works.** Upriver from Ammet Ford, the actual source of
the valley's economy and Crane's fortune. Lightly detailed on the page —
the novel cares about what the works did to the water, not its floor plan —
which leaves it open for R1b's set pieces: a pit head, a slag terrace
visibly higher than it was a year ago, worker housing older and plainer
than Ammet Ford's showpiece brick, a company office still keeping Crane's
ledgers under whatever name now runs it. The buried groundwater survey and
the Larrow Indemnity's seventh-year payout clause (`source-bible.md`) both
trace back here; so does whoever inherited the two intermediaries Crane
owned the works through, a loose thread R1b can pick up or leave alone.

**The other nine villages.** Unnamed on the page; name them when a Part
needs a specific one rather than inventing all nine now. Two worth having
ready: **Harrow's Weir**, downriver of the mine, where runoff settles
first and the Indemnity's seventh-year claims cluster thickest; and
**Pellick**, the valley's furthest village from Stillwell and the last on
Ferrick's capping schedule, which makes it the easiest place to show the
schedule not reaching someone in time. The rest can be sketched the same
way as needed: smaller and poorer than Ammet Ford, still waiting on the
well-capping schedule Ferrick's sub-panel is working through "town by town,
ahead of frost" (`A1-campaign-brief.md`). This is where "the money exists,
it doesn't reach the valleys" (canon §5) is most visible and most ordinary —
a capped well here, an uncapped one three villages over, no reason given.

**Hessel Bight.** A rail town roughly four days north of Stillwell by road,
on the trunk line. A flooded, war-damaged manse with eleven of the
Attrition's dead still in its cellar as of ch. 7 — cleared once already as
paid "wet work," and the kind of site that can need clearing again. Marks
the valley's practical northern edge; past it, the road starts climbing out
of Crane's territory.

## The Northern Road — waypoint towns

Connective tissue between the valley and Vantry, not a Part of their own.
Good for travel scenes, side jobs, and Part I's lead web reaching slightly
past its own region without becoming Part II early.

**Tannery Reach.** Tannery-and-canal town split between two parishes along
the canal centreline — a flood-wrecked boundary marker once left a drowned
man unclaimed for a day while the parishes argued liability. The
Commonwealth's bureaucratic cruelty in miniature; a reusable
contested-jurisdiction template (G1/J1 material, per `source-bible.md`).

**Selwick.** An almshouse two days up the north road by one Mercy brother's
count, one day by the road's own marker post — never resolved on the page.
Leave the discrepancy exactly that unresolved; it's a rumour-table entry
(`hooks.md` #10) or a thin-site seed, not a puzzle demanding a correct
answer.

**Sennet.** Grey-stone almshouse, Ondine Vail's first posting before Vantry
and the Sanctum. Where Maria confessed in ch. 12. Quiet, functional, nothing
sinister in its own right — a good contrast location for a Church site that
is exactly as charitable as it looks, to set against Ascension House later.

## REGION II — Vantry and the Rail *(Part II default, R2a/b)*

The Commonwealth's second city and its actual rail junction — permanent
smoke-haze, gaslamps lit at noon, chimneys ranked dozens deep. The largest
Church presence outside the capital (outline, world section). A year on,
this is a city that absorbed a scandal and kept running.

**Ascension House.** Burned out since ch. 15; a year later, still a shell,
unexcavated by anyone who'd admit to it. The pre-Church foundation beneath
it — older stone, a pale standing gleam behind a barred basement window, the
thing that "moved nothing like a man walks" — is never resolved on the page
and is this region's strongest thin-site candidate (`hooks.md` #4).

**The Vantry assembly's standing archive.** Holds the stolen intake
register, the stud book, and sixty years of extraction files from the
ward's exposure — filed, legally viewable, essentially never requested
(`hooks.md` #3). The region's best "evidence exists, evidence mattering is
a separate problem" location; strong candidate for a base nearby (D1) or a
handout (J1).

**Senator Ferrick's household.** Where Edie runs security now, her
knot-cord worn openly. Ferrick's own ethics sub-panel sits here too — the
body that forced the water schedule forward after Crane's death. A city of
mixed motive: the sub-panel is sincere and underfunded in the same breath,
exactly as the novel plays it.

**The broadsheet.** Ran the stolen register as photographed plates so no
compositor's word could be disputed — the only story in the Commonwealth
for eleven days before it curdled into an authenticity dispute. Usable as a
recurring press faction for any later exposure the party manages (see
`source-bible.md` → Factions, "the Vantry broadsheet").

**The lodging rows.** Not on the page by name, but implied by everything
canon says about Vantry as an industrial second city: streets of rented
rooms and boarding houses for mill and rail labour, the kind of
anonymous-by-necessity housing that absorbs people nobody will miss —
exactly the population the Fourth Mercy's other three Mercies are built to
identify (canon §4). A standing texture location rather than a keyed site:
useful anywhere R2b needs a lead to go cold because the person it points to
is one of thousands in a building with no register of who's actually
there.

## REGION III — Carrowgate and the Senate *(Part III default, R3a/b)*

The capital. Finer soot than Vantry's — a white collar lasts two days
instead of one — and gaslamps that burn a cleaner, greenish white on the
better avenues. The Senate sits on a hill: long grey roofline, a central
dome, built to look permanent and succeeding. A year on, the city has not
moved an inch on the Fourth Mercy.

**The Sanctum.** Church headquarters. Never touched across the novel and
still untouched a year later (`DECISIONS.md` D-1). Ondine Vail was promoted
here, not punished, after Ascension House's exposure — the clearest single
proof that the institution outlasts rather than falls (per the tone
contract in `PLAN.md`). Any Part III antagonist pressure from the Church
runs through here, at a remove; the Sanctum itself does not get stormed.

**The Indemnity Office.** Identified by a small brass pin (crossed quills);
session-day hours, maintenance schedules. The likely adjudicator of
Crane-works compensation claims and the probable link to the buried
groundwater survey — Lloyd surveilled it in ch. 20 but the connection is
his own inference, not confirmed on the page. A clean place for the party
to find the paper trail the novel's leads only watched from outside.

**Lloyd's nine years.** Not a location so much as a reason the capital is
not a stranger to valley people: Lloyd worked here from twenty-two to
thirty-one, which is also where and when Insight got him institutionalised
for five weeks (canon §6, never told to Maria or Edie — binding on any
cameo). Carrowgate is where valley labour goes, which is exactly why R-4's
undramatized stop above is the defensible default rather than a stretch.

**The institution.** Wherever Lloyd spent those five weeks is unnamed and
undescribed on the page beyond the fact of it. Leave it that way unless a
Part needs it keyed — it is one of the three permanently closed
who-knows-what gaps (canon §6), and the place itself staying vague protects
that better than inventing a building would. If R3b ever needs a Church or
state institution of this kind for an unrelated NPC, build a new one rather
than reaching for this.

**The boarding district.** Where valley labour actually lives while working
the capital — cheaper, further from the Senate's hill, soot closer to
Vantry's than to the avenues'. Lloyd's nine years of sent money started
somewhere like this. A practical location for any PC with valley roots to
have a contact, a landlord owed rent, or a reason to be recognised by
someone from home.

## REGION IV — The Fourth Mercy's Wider Network *(Part IV default, R4a/b)*

**Extrapolation, not canon.** Nothing on the page confirms a Fourth Mercy
site beyond Ascension House. But sixty years of a doctrine this patient,
feeding off three Mercies that operate everywhere the Church does, does not
plausibly run from one building, and canon §5 already states the Church
uses the Salts "industrially," which implies scale. This region is where
that scale gets a map: one or two further ascension sites, smaller or
better-hidden than Vantry's, in cities or Church holdings not yet named.
Build them when R4a is actually written, not before — this entry exists so
C1 doesn't silently promise a region it hasn't located yet. Nothing here
overrides canon's "everyone senior survives the book" or the Sanctum's
untouched status; a second site extends the institution's reach, it doesn't
give the party a second Sanctum to burn.

## The Hydros

**Extrapolation, not canon.** The novel names exactly one hydro —
Stillwell — and gives no indication it is unique; canon §5's "not a secret
to the powerful" line is about the Salts generally, not about Stillwell
specifically. For a campaign to use the Salts as background across five
regions (`A1-campaign-brief.md` → How the Salts Fits) without pretending
Stillwell is the Commonwealth's only access point, this file assumes a
small handful of comparable hydros exist elsewhere — the Church's
industrial supply chain for the Fourth Mercy has to come from somewhere,
and a nerve-cure sanatorium is exactly the kind of respectable front that
would run more than one location. None are named or placed yet; invent them
at the Part level where a thin site needs one; a new hydro should always
feel like Stillwell's genre (peeling respectability, batches of three, a
reception clerk who is never statted), not a reskin of Ascension House.
Logged alongside D-11 in `DECISIONS.md`.

## Scars of the Attrition

Not a Part, a texture that belongs everywhere. The war ended nineteen
months before ch. 1 and killed enough people in enough places that undead
and lesser horrors are a standing pest problem, not an event (canon §5).
Red Redoubt — the site of the camouflet that deafened Edie's left ear — is
the one named example, backstory only, no on-page location detail beyond
the event itself (`source-bible.md`). Use the Attrition's unhealed ground
the same way across every region: a collapsed trench here, a sealed tunnel
there, nothing that needs its own Part, everything that can seed a G-series
bestiary entry or a one-scene encounter. This is also where "institutions
rot, they are outlasted or escaped" (`A1-campaign-brief.md` pillar 3)
extends past the Church specifically — the war's own unresolved cost is the
same shape of problem, just older and quieter.

## DM notes

**What's confirmed vs. invented.** Everything under Regions I–III and the
Northern Road rests on the novel and `source-bible.md`; treat it as fixed.
Region IV and the Hydros entry are this file's own extrapolation, flagged
inline — safe to build on, but never cite them as if the novel said so.
Region V is deliberately empty.

**Reachability.** Per `PLAN.md`'s Part template, every essential reveal
needs at least three routes to it. This file's job was giving R1b–R3b
enough keyed geography that a lead web has somewhere to put those three
routes — a location, a person who knows a piece, a document that mentions
it — without the DM having to invent a new town mid-session. If a Part
still can't find three routes to a reveal using what's here, that's a sign
the reveal needs another site in this region, not a fourth route crammed
into one of the existing three.

**Don't over-fix the novel's own loose ends.** R-4 (Carrowgate) and the
Selwick discrepancy both got a working default here because a DM needs
*something* on the map, not because the novel asked to be tidied. Where a
hook (`hooks.md`) is marked low-priority or "leave unresolved," this file
has deliberately left it that way — Pellick and Harrow's Weir exist so the
DM has somewhere new to point to, not to force a resolution the source
material never wanted.
