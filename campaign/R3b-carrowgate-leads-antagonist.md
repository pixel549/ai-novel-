## Session defaults this file sets (D-23)

Three calls, all logged in `DECISIONS.md` rather than decided silently:

1. **The Indemnity Office's clerk is named: Winifred Sorrel.** `R3a`
   deferred this invention to whichever file first needed her to speak,
   same deferral `R1a` used for Corlett and `R2a` used for Hester Combe and
   Corde Ashby. She keeps the session-day hours and the filing system
   `R3a` already describes, and is this Part's primary Reveal A route.
2. **The Sanctum has no operational stake in Crane's death or the buried
   survey — the question `R3a` left open is answered no, not left open
   further.** Canon already settles who killed Crane and why (Lloyd, ch.
   24); nothing in this file reopens that. What canon also establishes is
   that the Church absorbed public blame for it anyway, via the
   vestment-thread frame (`source-bible.md` → Objects) and a restitution
   figure in the Sanctum's own crisis-PR register (`hooks.md` #5) —
   exactly the sequence `C2` already names as how the Church responds to
   *any* exposure, applied here to an accusation that happened to be
   false rather than one that was true. The Indemnity file's own closure
   (Reveal A, below) is built on this: the Church paid and didn't contest,
   not because it did anything, but because contesting costs it more than
   paying does. **This is not evidence of guilt and should never be
   played as a twist that the Church was secretly involved** — see DM
   notes.
3. **This Part's antagonist is named: the Valley Trust's two
   intermediaries, Osric Pell and Garrett Vane.** `DECISIONS.md` D-12
   named the Valley Trust as a faction without naming either of Crane's
   original two intermediaries, leaving that invention to whichever Part
   needed them in person; `R1b` named Corlett as their subordinate
   instead of naming them, since Region I's antagonist pressure sat at
   the works, not the capital. Region III's own premise card (`R3a`) —
   "the two men who've run the works since him would rather it stayed
   exactly where it is" — points straight at them, not at a new
   invention, so this file names them rather than building a third
   capital-level face. Pell handles the Trust's legal and financial
   side, including its standing relationship with the Indemnity Office;
   Vane handles its political and social side, including whatever
   goodwill the Trust still buys at the Senate. Neither reports to
   Corlett and neither is reported to by him in turn — he answers to
   them, same chain `R1b` already fixed, now given faces at the other
   end of it.

None of the three touches chapter text or contradicts canon — call 1 is an
invention this file needed to give Reveal A a direct route, same as every
earlier Part's clerk; call 2 extends C2's own crisis-PR mechanism to a
case it was never shown handling before, rather than inventing a new one;
call 3 completes a faction `C2`/`D-12` already named without putting a
name on an intermediary who should have stayed unnamed. Logged here per
RUNBOOK's Phase 2 instructions rather than decided silently in this file.

## 4. The Lead Web

Three reveals. Per `PLAN.md`'s template, each is reachable at least three
ways — no single refused request slip, missed roll, or skipped location
should stall the Part.

### Reveal A — resolved, blamed, paid out

What the Indemnity Office's own closed file on Halvard Crane's death
actually says: the matter is marked concluded, a restitution figure was
paid against it from Church funds, and no further inquiry was opened once
the payment cleared. Nothing in the file names a cause of death more
specific than "misadventure, Indemnity review session, service passage" —
the vestment-thread frame did the rest of the work in the court of public
opinion, and the file never had to.

- **Route 1 — Winifred Sorrel, through proper channels.** The Indemnity
  Office's clerk (named this session), orderly and sincere in exactly the
  register `R2a`/`R2b` set for Hester Combe: she hands the file to anyone
  who fills in the request slip correctly, and will say, if asked why so
  few people ever do, that a concluded case simply stops being anyone's
  job to reopen. The obstacle here is procedure and the time it costs a
  table, not secrecy.
- **Route 2 — the Waiting House's own tenants.** Caddock Lowe (`R3a`)
  rents rooms to several people with claims of their own moving through
  this same office; at least one has queued behind the Crane file often
  enough to describe its outcome secondhand — accurately, if not in the
  file's exact language — to anyone who's earned an evening's trust in
  the common room.
- **Route 3 — the Senate's own public record.** The restitution figure was
  large enough to touch an appropriations line somewhere, even if nobody
  in the chamber discussed why; a page, a clerk, or a petitioner who's
  spent enough hours in the queue outside a committee room (`R3a`'s own
  sensory tell) can point a party to the relevant mention without needing
  to understand its significance themselves.

### Reveal B — closed too fast, and somebody's hand was on the stamp

The file didn't just close. It closed quickly, and from two directions at
once: the Church's standing crisis-response (above) wanted it closed as
fast as any accusation it absorbs, and the Valley Trust's solicitors filed,
separately and for entirely self-interested reasons, to have their late
owner's own Indemnity matter expedited — the same instinct that buried the
groundwater survey, pointed now at a death rather than a disease.

- **Route 1 — Winifred Sorrel, asked the better question.** Not "what does
  the file say" (Reveal A) but "who asked for it to move this fast" — she
  answers this just as directly, because nothing about an expedited-closure
  request looks irregular to her: employers ask for these routinely, for
  payroll and insurance reasons that have nothing to do with anyone's
  motive. She names the filing solicitor's firm without hesitation.
- **Route 2 — the carpet men's own schedule.** `R3a`'s maintenance detail
  (a contracted cleaning crew that keeps better time than the claims do) is
  retained by the same firm that filed the expedited request — a detail
  visible only to someone who checks the office's own access log against
  who actually holds the contract, and the first sign that the Trust's
  reach into this building runs deeper than one filing.
- **Route 3 — cross-reading against Region I.** A party that already has
  Reveal B or C from `R1b` (the buried survey, Corlett's role) can lay the
  solicitor's firm's name alongside the Valley Trust's own company
  letterhead and find the same firm behind both — the Trust's lawyers
  didn't invent a new relationship with the Indemnity Office for Crane's
  death; they used the one that was already keeping the survey buried.
  This route rewards continuity rather than requiring it; a table starting
  fresh in Region III gets there through Routes 1–2 just as surely.

### Reveal C — who's actually invested, and why

The Valley Trust's two intermediaries, Pell and Vane, want the file to
stay exactly where Reveal A and B leave it — not because Crane's death
embarrasses them, but because any inquiry thorough enough to ask who
benefited from his death is thorough enough to ask what he was hiding
before it, and the survey is still buried one administrative layer short
of safe (`C2`'s Valley Trust's Consolidation clock).

- **Route 1 — Pell or Vane, directly.** A scene at the Trust's Carrowgate
  office, or wherever the Senate's own business brings Vane within reach,
  built around their tells (below). Neither lies and neither threatens;
  each answers in his own register, which together amounts to a
  conversation that goes nowhere twice as politely as Corlett's ever did.
- **Route 2 — the boarding district's own memory.** Caddock Lowe himself,
  or one of his longer-settled tenants, remembers which two names actually
  signed the paperwork when the works changed hands under Crane — not
  secret, just not asked about in twenty years, and Lowe has no reason to
  withhold it from a lodger who asks a plain question over the common-room
  slate.
- **Route 3 — up the chain from Corlett.** A party that has already met
  Hew Corlett (`R1b`) and knows who he reports to can simply ask him, or
  find his correspondence; he has never met either man in person and says
  so without evasion, which is itself the point — the distance between
  Corlett's valley office and Pell and Vane's capital one is exactly how
  far up the Trust's own hierarchy a table has to climb to find someone
  who could have stopped Reveal B's closure and chose not to.

## 5. Encounters

Archetype and tier only; the real ruleset generates stat blocks live.

- **Social.** The default register of this Part, same discipline as
  Regions I and II. Sorrel's orderly cooperation; Pell's tell; Vane's
  tell; Caddock Lowe's plain-spoken tenants; the committee-room queue as
  texture rather than obstacle. Most essential information in Region III
  is won or lost here.
- **Pursuit.** Quieter and better-funded than either earlier Part's
  version. Where Region I's pursuit was a company runner and Region II's
  was a Church-adjacent face mentioning the party to Wick, Region III's is
  a retained inquiry agent — paid by the day, professionally
  unremarkable, reporting to Pell rather than confronting anyone. The
  "encounter" is the party noticing, later, how precisely Pell already
  knew what they'd been asking before they asked him directly.
- **Arcana.** A low-tier, unregistered practitioner somewhere in the
  boarding district — a clerk who reads faster than anyone should, a
  tenant who lights a stove without a match. Worth a scene only to
  dramatise `C2`'s point that being noticed here is a cost, same
  restraint `R1b` and `R2b` both used. Optional; skip if no PC has the
  hook.
- **Church operatives.** A Mercy sister or brother at the Mercy House
  (`R3a`), genuinely charitable and genuinely unaware this file's lead
  web has anything to do with them. A Sanctum clerk or page, asked
  directly about Crane's case, will say plainly that the matter is
  concluded and mean it — not evasion, the institutional truth as the
  Sanctum itself understands it. Nothing here escalates to a Fourth
  Mercy site; per D-23 above, there is no operational connection to
  escalate to.
- **Pest-class horrors.** The Pest Pressure Clock's Carrowgate instance
  (`R3a`) — an old cellar, an unused crypt under a Mercy House, a stretch
  of ground the Senate's own groundskeepers avoid. `(SYS?)` for the actual
  stat block (`G3`'s job); archetype only here: Attrition-scarred, not
  eldritch, concentrated rather than spread because the capital's own
  paved ground gives it fewer places to be bad in, same logic `R3a`

- **Fourth Mercy products.** None, per D-23. The Sanctum is clean in this
  file, deliberately and completely — no remnant, no echo, nothing for a
  DM to half-confirm the way `R2b` left the tank-four possibility open for

  administrative, not eldritch.
- **Salts leak.** None. Region III has no thin site (`R3a`, `C3`); see §7

## 6. Set Pieces and Antagonist

### Set piece — The Closed File

A scene, not a trap: the party requests Crane's Indemnity file through
Winifred Sorrel's own proper channels and gets it — slowly, completely,
without obstruction. The telegraphed tell, honoured every time: nothing
about this scene should read as a lock to pick. The file's actual
weakness is that almost nobody has ever asked for it, not that Sorrel or
her office would refuse a correctly filed request; the tension comes from
how little anyone needed to hide once nobody was going to look. A DM who
puts a guard, a false front, or a withheld page in Sorrel's way has
written this scene wrong — if a table wants friction here, it belongs to
Reveal B's better question, which she also answers honestly, not to a
contest over Reveal A's plain one.

### Set piece — The Carpet Men

Once the party's asking has triggered Reveal B or C — specifically, once
they've connected the filing solicitor's firm to the Trust — the same
contracted cleaning crew that keeps the Indemnity Office's corridors
immaculate starts turning up wherever the party does: outside the Waiting
House, in the Senate's lower corridors, once at the Mercy House's own
door. Nobody is threatened. Nobody is followed closer than a public street
allows. The scene's entire effect is the party realising that a firm paid
to clean a government office has, for a few days, also been paid to watch
them, and that the distinction between the two jobs was never going to be
visible from the outside. This is Region III's version of the "company
runner" and the "Church-adjacent face noticing" — same mechanism, scaled
up to what money can actually buy at the capital.

### The Antagonist — the Two Intermediaries, Osric Pell and Garrett Vane

**Gimmick.** Pell and Vane are not villains in the sense the table will
expect, same warning `C2`'s DM notes gave for Corlett and Wick, now
applied to the two men standing furthest up the Trust's own chain. They
inherited the works from Crane the same way Corlett inherited its
day-to-day running — a going concern, already working, worth protecting
exactly as hard as several hundred jobs and a sizeable capital investment
deserve protecting. Neither personally buried the survey or arranged
Crane's own death's closure; both have spent a year since making certain
neither one ever needs to be reopened, because reopening either risks
reopening the other. They are sincerely, defensibly running a business.
That is the entire horror this file asks a table to sit with.

**Telegraphed tells (honour these exactly, every time).** Asked a direct
question about Crane's death, the survey, or the Indemnity's timing,
**Pell** always routes the question to a proper channel — a request slip,
a formal appeal, a solicitor's letter — that technically exists and
practically outlasts most tables' patience; he never refuses and never
lies, he simply never personally answers anything a form could answer
instead. **Vane**, asked the same question, offers something genuinely
useful first — an introduction, a door opened at the Senate, a word put in
somewhere that actually helps — and then, warmly, declines to go further
than the question strictly asked, the same discipline canon's own Vail
sets for herself (§3) and `R2b` already gave Wick. Between them, a party
gets real help and a real process and no real answer, and should feel,
specifically, how reasonable that is.

**What defeat looks like.** Not combat, and not a confession. Pell and
Vane are "defeated" when the party gets enough of Reveal A–C in front of
someone positioned to act without being compromised — a formal Senate
inquiry into the Indemnity Office's own closure practices, the Indemnity
Office's superior authority asking why Sorrel's office expedited a filing
at a private firm's request, or (if the Press Cycle clock triggers) public
exposure of the coincidence itself. When that happens, the Trust does not
fold. One of the two — whichever a table's own play has made more exposed —
steps back from the business "for reasons of health" or "to attend to
other interests," the same flatness `R1b` gave Corlett's reassignment, and
a third name takes his place within the season. The survey gets one
administrative layer easier to reach, not open; the Indemnity Office
tightens its own expedited-closure procedure slightly, a genuine, minor,
structural win that outlasts the session. The Sanctum does not move at
all — nothing here ever touched it, and nothing here should pretend
otherwise.

**What flight looks like.** If the party moves on partial evidence or
tips either man off early, neither panics or disappears — they have
nowhere more senior to run to and no reason to. The Valley Trust's
Consolidation clock (`C2`) jumps forward instead: Sorrel's filing system
gets a quiet procedural tightening that makes future requests slower
without ever refusing one outright, and the solicitor's firm's name
becomes harder to connect to the Trust on paper, though never impossible —
per `C2`'s own pattern, buried one layer deeper, not destroyed.

**What capture looks like.** If the party corners or threatens either man,
neither fights and neither pleads. They offer a settlement — formal,
legal, generously sized, drafted by the same solicitors who filed the
original expedited closure — structured specifically so that accepting it
closes the matter cleanly and pursuing it further afterward looks
litigious rather than righteous. This is Corlett's capture-outcome money
and Wick's capture-outcome charity, grown up into an actual legal
instrument; holding or hurting either man gets the party nothing further,
because neither has a bigger name above him to give up. Pell has no second
ledger. Vane has no better favour to call in. A table that corners them
expecting a confession should feel, specifically, how little there was
left to confess.

## 8. Consequences

What changes on the map and the clocks (`C2`), depending on how the party
plays this Part:

- **The Valley Trust's Consolidation.** This Part's live wire (`R3a`).
  Advances fastest of any clock yet if the party pokes at Reveal B or C
  without covering their tracks (the flight outcome above); on a defeat
  outcome, it ticks back one visible notch — Sorrel's procedure loosens
  slightly, the survey's own reachability improves by exactly the margin
  the defeat outcome above describes, no further.
- **The Indemnity Mortality Curve.** Unaffected by anything in this Part
  directly — it is Region I's clock, given its administrative face here
  (`R3a`), not a new one. Whatever the party learns about Crane's own file
  doesn't move the curve; it only lets them see, in writing, how the same
  office that closed his case has been closing theirs for years.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock, Carrowgate instance.** Advances if the
  party's own investigating disturbs old ground (a cellar check, a crypt
  visit) carelessly; decays slowly otherwise, same mechanism as the
  earlier two Parts.
- **Pell, Vane, and the Trust's public face.** Resolved per the defeat,
  flight, or capture outcomes above, depending entirely on how the party
  plays Reveals A–C. None of the three outcomes touches the Sanctum, the
  Senate's own composition, or Senator Ferrick's standing — this Part's
  antagonist pressure stays exactly where `R3a`'s premise card put it,
  at the Trust, not at either of the two bigger institutions surrounding
  it.
- **What Carrowgate looks like afterward.** The Sanctum keeps looking
  exactly as charitable and exactly as untouched as `A1` pillar 3
  requires; the Senate keeps moving exactly as slowly on the valley's
  behalf as `C1`/`A1` already describe; and the Valley Trust keeps a name
  on its own letterhead, whichever two men that name belongs to by the
  session's end. That flatness is the intended outcome, same discipline
  `R1b` and `R2b` both held for their own Parts.

## 9. DM Notes

**Failure states are fine.** A party that never finds Sorrel, never
connects the solicitor's firm to the Trust, or never gets past Pell's or
Vane's tells can still finish this Part — the file stays closed, the
Trust stays exactly as consolidated as it was, and the campaign moves on
to Part IV with that unresolved, exactly as `R1b` and `R2b` both allowed
for their own regions. Don't manufacture a fourth route to force a stalled
reveal through; check `R3a`'s own locations first for an unused angle
before inventing one here.

**The Church's restitution is not a confession, and must never be played
as one.** The single easiest way to get this Part wrong is letting a
table conclude, from how fast and how generously the Church paid out on
Crane's death, that it had something to do with it. It didn't — canon
settles this in ch. 24, and nothing above reopens it. What the Church did
is exactly what `hooks.md` #5 and `C2` already say it always does when
blame lands anywhere near it: pay, don't contest, let the story curdle on
schedule. A DM who uses this Part to imply Church guilt by implication has
written the Sanctum wrong in exactly the way canon §3's prose rules warn
against for Vail specifically — the menace, if a table finds any here,
lives in the Trust's paperwork, never in the Church's silence.

**Pell and Vane are not a bigger Corlett.** Resist making them more
sinister than he was simply because they're richer and further up the
chain — same discipline `R2b` used to keep Wick from reading as a
disguised villain once installed with more institutional weight than
Corlett ever had. The horror scales with what they can afford to spend
protecting the business, not with how willing they are to lie, which
remains exactly zero for both.

**How the novel's three appear.** No change from `R3a`'s own, most
restrictive note yet: Lloyd and Maria stay rumour and resemblance only,
never a scene, never a sighting, never a poster, and nothing in this
file's lead web gives a table a reason to go looking for either of them —
Reveals A–C are entirely about Crane, the Trust, and the Indemnity Office,
none of which intersects the who-knows-what gaps canon §6 protects. Edie
does not appear in this Part at all; she remains Vantry's own restricted
presence (`R2a`/`R2b`), not Carrowgate's.
