## How to use this file

Same job `H1`–`H3` did for Regions I–III, now for Region IV, with one
structural difference none of the three faced quite this way: Region IV is
the first region where a thin site sits *inside* its own geography rather
than behind or ahead of it. `T1` (Vantry) and `T2` (the Northern Road) are
both built, live, and behind a Part IV party exactly as `H3` already found
them; `T3-the-standing-cistern.md` is not merely reachable from here — it
*is* here, physically, beneath the House of Quiet Rest's own locked wing,
the site `R4b`'s own Reveal B points toward. That changes this file's
Chalice-Gifts section a third way again; see below. Everything else
follows `H1`–`H3`'s own split: the ordinary and documentary objects the
lead web and the locations already assume exist (Greymere's manifest, the
Night Siding's arrivals board, a resident's unanswered letters, the
House's own tank and growth logs — the last two built out fully by `G4`,
cited rather than repeated here), the fen's native-Arcana register (same
ceiling as every earlier region, `G4`'s own sharper caution), and the
weapons this region's people actually carry — turf and drainage tools, not
an armoury, same discipline `H1`–`H3` all held for their own regions. Hand
a PC-facing item description to players directly; keep the Arcana and
Chalice-Gifts sections DM-only where noted, same split `PLAN.md` draws
between `B` (players) and everything else (DM).

**Session default — D-38 (`DECISIONS.md`).** None of the specific items,
the weapons section's fen-specific entries, or the Chalice-Gifts section's
"it's here, not ahead" framing are fixed by an earlier file. `R4a`, `R4b`,
and `G4` each name or imply a document or object this file had to build
out (Greymere's manifest as a handleable prop distinct from the fact of
its existence, Pledge's own arrivals board, Teyla Dunmore's letters, the
fen's weapons register) — logged here rather than decided silently, the
same way `G4` invented its archetypes.

## Arcana in the Fen

Per canon §5 and `C2`'s Arcana practitioners entry, the fen's own ceiling
is identical to every other region's — the same four gifts, the same hard
limits, nowhere in the Commonwealth does the gift itself get stronger.
What changes, per `G4`'s own Unregistered Arcana Practitioner, Fen
Instance, is how little it takes to be found: no stud book, no city
archive, just an almoner with a good memory and a charity nobody local
will say an unkind word about.

- **Hearth-, Ease-, Mend-, and Find-work.** Identical in kind and ceiling
  to `H1`'s own entries; see that file for the full description of each.
  Mend-work's hard limit (no regrowth, no disease cure, inherited from
  Maria's own per canon §2) binds here exactly as it does everywhere else.
- **What's different here.** Per `G4`, a fen practitioner minimises faster
  and more completely than any earlier region's version — the escalating
  caution `G2`'s Lodging-Row Spark showed over `H1`'s own valley
  practitioner, and `G3`'s Boarding-District Reader showed over `G2`'s,
  reaches its sharpest point in Lissom Fen precisely because the danger
  here isn't a filed register, it's a recommendation, made sincerely, by
  someone the whole parish trusts.
- **Find-work and the lead web.** As in every earlier region, a PC with
  this gift gets no mechanical shortcut to any of `R4b`'s three reveals —
  Routes exist enough without one. At most, colour: a character with this
  knack might feel, once, that the House's locked east wing "sits wrong"
  the way a shut door sometimes does, a feeling a DM can let land as
  atmosphere pointing toward Reveal B without it standing in for any
  route `R4b` already built.

`(SYS?)` tier: all four stay low-tier and non-combat, same as `H1`–`H3`;
nothing in the fen raises the ceiling.

## Chalice-Gifts

**DM-only.** `H1` kept this section inert (no site). `H2` made it a
pointer to a site that was live and ahead of a Part II party. `H3` held
two live sites behind a Part III party and one, `T3`, still ahead. This
file is different a third time: **`T3` is not ahead of Region IV — it is
in Region IV**, under the House of Quiet Rest's own locked wing, reached
by the same service stair `R4b`'s Reveal B uncovers. A Part IV party that
pursues Reveal B past Brother Ness's own answers, specifically asking what
lies *beneath* the undercroft rather than *in* it, is pointed at `T3`
directly by that Part's own lead web (`R4b` §7). This file does **not**
duplicate `T3`'s own account of the stair, the cistern, the water, or the
chalice at its node 4 — read `T3` directly rather than looking here for a
second version.

What this section exists to do instead is keep four things straight for
whoever is running this Part with the Compendium open rather than `T3`
itself:

1. **A PC may already carry a chalice gift from `T1` or `T2`.** If so, it
   travels into Region IV unchanged. Nothing in the fen explains, revises,
   adds to, or costs it further — this file is not where that bookkeeping
   lives; read `T1`/`T2`/`S1` directly for how either gift was built and
   what it still costs.
2. **`T3` is this Part's own site, not a reward for finishing it.** Per
   `R4b` §7, a party can close the House of Quiet Rest, resolve Ness's own
   defeat, flight, or capture outcome, and finish the Part entirely
   without ever finding the service stair. `T3` is a further step past
   this Part's own ending (`T3`'s own framing), not a requirement of it —
   a DM should never dangle it as the "real" climax Region IV is actually
   building toward.
3. **`T3` is not this Part's own confirmed Fourth Mercy Products
   category, and the reverse.** `G4`'s Tanks and Early Growth entries sit
   directly above `T3`'s own service stair, separated by a locked door and
   a flight of steps, and `T3` itself is explicit that nothing under the
   cistern is Fourth Mercy work, explains the undercroft, or excuses
   Brother Ness's own sincerity (`T3` → "Why This Isn't Ness's Problem";
   D-30). A DM reading this Compendium entry while also holding `G4` open
   should keep that boundary exactly where both files already drew it —
   the same boundary `H2` held between `T1` and the Tank-Four Remnant,
   now a floor apart rather than a ruin apart.
4. **Nothing above requires a party to go under at all.** Per `C3`'s own
   standing instruction and `T3`'s own "Decline is not a failure state":
   a party that finishes Part IV without ever finding the stair has lost
   nothing this file considers a reward.

`(SYS?)`: no stat block accompanies any of this, for the same reason `H1`
and `H2` both gave — this is a pointer to where the mechanism lives, not a
second menu.

## Items

Ordinary and documentary objects the Part's lead web already assumes
exist. Player-facing except where marked.

**Greymere's manifest.** The hydro's own cart ledger, laid out in two
columns — a steady, modest nerve-cure trade, and, beside it, a second
column of brine and reagent several times the size the visible business
could use (`R4b` → Reveal A). Physically unremarkable: a cloth-bound
account book that looks exactly like what a small hydro's own books
should look like, until someone who knows what the numbers should be
reads the second column against the first. A DM can hand a page of it to
players directly once Josiah Rede produces it or a party talks its way to
the counter; it is meant to be read, not summarised, same instruction
`H1`–`H3` all give their own central documents.

**The Night Siding's arrivals board.** Rufus Pledge's own thirty years of
handwritten logs (`R4a`; the set piece "The Arrivals Board," `R4b` §6) —
this region's instance of the convention `D1` set with Nessa Beddoe's
slate and `H2`/`H3` continued with Ress Tannery's rooming book and the
Waiting House's slate. Unlike those three, this one is explicitly not a
tally of debts owed but of trains and travellers, kept for its own sake
rather than for any business reason — which is exactly what makes it
useful evidence nobody meant to create. Pledge's own tell (`R4b`'s set
piece note) binds any DM handling this prop: he never draws the
connection aloud, and a scene that has him do so has written the item's
one rule wrong.

**Teyla Dunmore's unanswered letters.** Several, returned unopened or
answered with the House's own form reply — "your brother's care continues
to require our full attention; we will write when there is news" — the
concrete object behind her plain, repeated request for a straight answer
(`R4b` → Reveal B, Route 1). Worth handing to a player directly the
moment Teyla is met; the form reply's own flattened kindness does more
work read aloud than any description of her grief would.

**The parish noticeboard.** Lissom Fen's own public record of who's "gone
in for their nerves," more names of men who went in than names of men who
came back out (`R4a`; `R4b` → Reveal C, Route 3). Not a document a party
takes away — it stays fixed to the fen, read in place, the same way
`R2a`'s shelter roll stays fixed to its own Mercy House. A DM can let a
party read it cold; nothing on it is coded or hidden, which is the point
`hooks.md` #3 already makes about evidence that exists without anyone
following it up.

**The House's tank log and growth log.** Built out fully by `G4` as the
Fourth Mercy Products entries' own tells — cited here rather than
duplicated, since `G4` already fixes their cold, discharge-register
phrasing ("result negative, subject retained") and this file adds nothing
to it. See `G4` → The Tanks, The Early Growth.

**A settled brine account.** Greymere's own version of a quartermaster's
ledger — the arrangement that's kept the business solvent for years,
never itemised past "account settled, as agreed," the physical half of
Josiah Rede's own tell that he sells access rather than giving it away or
stonewalling (`R4b` → Reveal A, Route 2). Prop only; a DM describing it
concretely ("he turns one page, taps a line, says nothing further") does
more work than narrating the arrangement in full.

**Turf-cutting tools.** A spade, a dyke pole, a hand-knife for trimming
peat — Lissom Fen's own ordinary kit, included here for the same reason
`H1` named mine tools and `H2` named mill and yard tools: something a DM
can put in a smallholder's or turf cutter's hands without reaching for a
weapon register that doesn't fit the fen. Prop only; no combat function
beyond what `H1` already established for improvised tools of this kind.

## Weapons

Same discipline `H1`–`H3` all held: the fen is poorer and quieter than any
earlier region, and most of what's listed here is a tool first.

**Turf-cutting tools (spade, dyke pole, peat-knife).** Improvised before
they're ever weapons, same register as `H1`'s mine tools and `H2`'s mill
and yard tools — someone reaching for the nearest heavy or edged thing,
not a trained combatant. `(SYS?)` tier: low; favours intimidation and
improvised damage over any trained bonus, identical to the earlier
regions' own entries of this kind.

**What the House carries: nothing.** Per `R4b`'s own discipline for
Brother Ness and his "specialist" staff, restated from `H2`'s and `H3`'s
own entries for their regions' Church faces: no one at the House of Quiet
Rest, from Sister Prue Anstey at the front hall to whoever Ness answers
to above him, carries or draws a weapon of any kind. The institution's
menace lives entirely in content, never in staging, same as everywhere
else in the campaign. A DM reaching for this file to give the undercroft
an edge should reach for Ness's own tell, not an item entry; there isn't
one here on purpose.

**What Greymere carries: nothing either.** Josiah Rede is mercenary, not
violent — `R4b`'s own register for him, sincere and transactional, never
armed and never needing to be. The same restraint `R3b` set for Pell and
Vane's paperwork extends here to a man whose only leverage is a ledger.

**An Attrition-surplus revolver.** The same Commonwealth-wide surplus
`H1`–`H3` already describe (canon §5) — plain, worn, and no less
unremarkable to own out past Oldbridge than in the capital. A Fen Line
veteran or a smallholder with a reason to keep one fits this region
exactly as well as it fits anywhere else. `(SYS?)` tier: the one weapon in
this file capable of lethal force at range — treat every appearance as a
serious escalation, never as flavour.

**A trench knife.** Attrition-era, double-edged, carried quietly by
veterans who don't talk about where they got it — identical to `H1`'s,
`H2`'s, and `H3`'s own entry, no regional variant needed. `(SYS?)` tier:
low-to-mid, close range only.

## DM Notes

**Nothing above needs a fight, same as `R4b`'s own warning.** Per `G4`'s
own standing note: Region IV's entire register of pressure is a sincere
charity, a ledger kept quietly, and a Pest Pressure Clock that's diffuse
rather than concentrated. A DM reaching for the revolver or the trench
knife in this Part should check first whether the scene actually called
for one, or whether an item entry just happened to exist.

**Items are props, not a reward track.** Same restraint `H1`–`H3` all
held: no item above makes a PC mechanically stronger. A DM looking for a
"found item" beat should reach for information or standing — a route
opened, a favour owed, per `F1` §6 — not an object from this list.

**The Chalice-Gifts section's "it's here, not ahead" framing matters.** If
a player asks whether their character can drink from a chalice this Part,
the honest answer is yes, but only at `T3`, and only by finding the
service stair beneath the House's own locked wing — this file supplies no
shortcut, no pre-built option, and no Region-IV-specific variant of the
procedure `T3` and `S1` already own. If that same player already carries
a gift from `T1` or `T2`, this file changes nothing about it.

**The Fourth Mercy Products entries and `T3`'s chalice stay on opposite
sides of a locked door.** Per `G4`'s own warning and `T3`'s own D-30: a
DM who lets the undercroft's tanks explain the water below them, or lets
whatever's in the water justify Brother Ness's own work, has blurred a
boundary both files drew on purpose.

**How the novel's three appear.** No change from `R4a`/`R4b`'s own note:
nothing above is a vehicle for giving Lloyd, Maria, or Edie a scene ahead
of `I1`'s own rules. None of the three appear in this Part at all.
