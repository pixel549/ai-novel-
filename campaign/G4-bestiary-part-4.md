# G4 — BESTIARY ADDITIONS, PART IV

*Reader: DM. Builds on `R4a-fourth-mercy-network-overview.md` and `R4b-
fourth-mercy-leads-antagonist.md` §5 (Encounters) — read both first; this
file does not restate the lead web, the set pieces, or Region IV's
geography, it supplies the archetype write-ups §5 names and leaves
undetailed. Cites `R4a`, `R4b`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`, `G1-bestiary-
part-1.md`, `G2-bestiary-part-2.md`, `G3-bestiary-part-3.md`,

No `campaign/system/` ruleset exists yet (Q-1); every entry below is
behaviour and tells, never a stat block — numbers are generated live at the
table, archetype and tier only, marked `(SYS?)` per `A2`'s standing
instruction.*

## How to use this file

Same job `G1`–`G3` did for Regions I–III, now for Region IV: the unnamed or
lightly-named faces a lead-web route needs consistent behaviour for
(**People**), the fen's own instance of the Attrition-scarred ground
(**Pest-Class Horrors**), and — unlike `G2`'s optional, unconfirmed
tank-four remnant — a **Fourth Mercy Products** category this Part's own
lead web confirms as real and reachable (`R4b` D-25 call 1), not a maybe a
DM is free to take or leave. Read an entry once, before the session it's needed
in; none of these carry a scene the way Rufus Pledge, Josiah Rede, Teyla
Dunmore, or Brother Ness do in `R4a`/`R4b` — this file supplies the texture
around them, not the leads.

**Session default — D-37 (`DECISIONS.md`).** Where `R4a` and `R4b` name a
category (the House's "specialist" staff, the fen's own smallholders and
turf cutters, an optional Fen Arcana instance, the Fen Line's own Pest
Pressure Clock instance) without building it out, and where `R4b` confirms
but does not build the undercroft's own early-stage Fourth Mercy work
(D-25 call 1: a small bank of immersion tanks and the early stages of
homunculi-growing, nothing further), this file invents the behaviour,
tells, and — for the Fourth Mercy work specifically — the bounded scope
those sections ask for. Logged here rather than decided silently, and
overturnable the same way `D-18`, `D-33`, and `D-35` were for Regions I–III.

## People

### The "Specialist" Staff (the Undercroft's Careful Hands)

Covers `R4b` §5's Church-operatives entry: "The House's 'specialist' staff,
archetype only... tired, procedural, not cruel on sight, the menace living
entirely in what they're doing rather than how they look doing it, per
canon §3's rule for Vail extended here." The handful who come and go on a
schedule Sister Prue Anstey doesn't keep herself, always on the same days
as the twice-weekly wagon (`R4a`).

**Behaviour.** Competent, quiet, and incurious in Brother Ness's own mode
(`R4b`), scaled down from overseer to hands — none of them invented this
work, none of them chose Lissom Fen, and none of them has asked a question
that would require an answer they'd rather not carry. Not furtive, not
cruel: they would describe their own day using the same flattened
institutional language the discharge files use (`source-bible.md` → Places,
Fourth Mercy facility layout) — "the difficult cases," "specialist care" —
because that is, as far as any of them examines it, exactly what the day
is.

**Tells.** Handle every task with the same practised, genuine care Anstey
shows the public ward, extended without any visible change in manner to
tasks the public ward would not recognise; never lower a voice, never
glance toward a door, never linger (per the tone contract — Church menace
lives in content, never staging). Keep to a schedule precisely, the same
unremarkable punctuality Pledge's own arrivals board already logs without
understanding.

**Where this matters.** Per `R4b`'s own warning against writing Ness's
sincerity as a mask, this staff should never read as secretly uneasy
either — they are the institution's ordinary machinery, the same
bread-and-shelter mechanism (canon §4) running one locked door further in
than Anstey's own public ward ever sees.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat. `R4b`'s own lead web routes no PC into a
fight with any of the House's own staff.

### The Fen Smallholder or Turf Cutter

Covers `R4a`'s own instruction: "No single administrator; invent
smallholders and turf cutters per scene, in the sincere register `C2`'s DM
notes set for every ordinary face in this campaign" — and, with it, Lissom
Fen's parish noticeboard and the generic "a family with a relative who
went in" texture (`R4a`) that isn't Teyla Dunmore's own, specific case.

**Behaviour.** Sincerely grateful to the House, not naive about it exactly
— grateful the precise way `R4a` names: "nobody here is lying to themselves
about what it's given their families." Answers a direct question about a
specific name warmly and completely, the same willingness `R4b`'s own
Route 3 for Reveal A assumes; answers a question about "where people go
after" with the same shrug the noticeboard itself gives — a name moves from
the bread queue to the list of men who "went in for their nerves," and
nobody's business what happened after, not from secrecy but because nobody
out here has ever had a reason to ask.

**Tells.** Turf stacked against a cottage wall whatever the season; the
bread queue kept to its twice-weekly schedule more exactly than anything
else in the fen gets kept; names the House's charity unprompted and
gratefully in conversations that have nothing to do with it, the same
reflex `R4a`'s own "most of the fen is sincerely, personally grateful"
line describes.

**Where this matters.** Region IV's own version of `hooks.md` #3 (evidence
existing versus evidence mattering), scaled down from an archive to a
parish noticeboard and a community's habit of not asking the next
question. Never let this archetype read as suspicious of the House — per
`R4a`, the goodwill here is entirely genuine, which is exactly why it's a
liability for a party that's arrived looking for a villain.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

### The Unregistered Arcana Practitioner, Fen Instance (optional)

Covers `R4b` §5's optional Arcana encounter: "a smallholder or turf cutter
with a minor, unregistered gift, worth a scene only to dramatise `C2`'s
point that being noticed here is a cost" — the Region IV instance of the
archetype `G1`, `G2`, and `G3` each built their own version of for Regions
I–III.

**Behaviour.** Keeps the gift smaller than any of the three earlier
versions needed to, for a reason none of them had: here the danger isn't a
distant stud book or a city archive, it's a two-day walk away and
genuinely charitable. A fen local who can ease a fever or light a damp
hearth without a match has watched a neighbour "go in for their nerves"
and drawn the plausible, quiet conclusion that the Fourth Mercy's own
recruitment doesn't need a filed register to find someone — just an
almoner with a good memory for who in the parish is a little strange.

**Tells.** Minimises on instinct, same discipline `G1`–`G3` all set,
scaled further down than any of the three — the fastest to the wariness of
any region's version yet, per the escalating caution `G2`'s Lodging-Row
Spark already showed over `G1`'s own practitioner and `G3`'s
Boarding-District Reader showed over `G2`'s.

**Where this matters.** Skip entirely if no PC has the hook, same
restraint `G1`–`G3` all held. If used, never let the scene leave this
person better off for having been found out — even, or especially, by a
party that closes the House down by the Part's end.

`(SYS?)` tier: low, non-combat.

## Pest-Class Horrors (the Attrition-scarred ground)

Per `C2`, `G1`, `G2`, and `G3`: weather, not villains, with no wants in the
faction sense, worse wherever disturbed and otherwise dormant, never
escalating into a boss fight. Regions I–III each gave this archetype a
single, concentrated home address (the mine's older workings, Vantry's
foundations and culverts, Carrowgate's own kept ground). Region IV's own
instance, the Fen Line, runs the opposite shape — per `R4a`'s own situation
section: "unsurveyed, barely drained... pressure here is diffuse rather
than concentrated: worse in more places, each instance individually
weaker."

### The Fen-Line Lingerer

The same low-tier archetype `G1` built as the Collapsed-Tunnel Lingerer,
re-dressed a second time by `G2` as the Rail-Cut Lingerer and a third time
by `G3` as the Kept-Ground Lingerer — re-dressed a fourth time here for the
Fen Line's own abandoned trench system, per `G1`'s own instruction that a
region doesn't need two pest archetypes to make the same clock legible.
This is the Fen Line's instance of the Pest Pressure Clock (`R4a`; `C2`),
not a new species.

**Behaviour.** Identical in kind to its three predecessors: doesn't hunt,
doesn't pursue past the stretch of collapsed trench or duckboard it's
bound to, investigates sluggishly rather than attacking, and loses
interest within minutes of a party backing off. No plan, no grievance — a
pest problem, not a haunting (canon §5), exactly as `G1`–`G3` all held.
**Diffuse, not concentrated, per `R4a`'s own instruction:** rather than one
strong instance keyed to a single site, run the Fen Line as several weaker
sightings scattered along its length — nineteen months of peace and no
money to fill the trenches back in (`R4a`) means the whole line carries a
little pressure rather than one stretch carrying a lot.

**Tells (re-dressed for the Fen Line).** Collapsed duckboard still visible
under standing water that never quite drains; a rusted signpost reading a
unit designation nobody local recognises anymore; turf cutters who avoid
the deepest stretch of line without being able to say exactly why, the
same unspoken avoidance `R3a` notes for the Senate's own groundskeepers,
scaled here to a community with far less to lose by saying so out loud if
asked.

**Function in the Part.** Gives the Fen Line the same "this ground has its
own reason to be left alone" texture the mine gave Region I and Vantry's
foundations gave Region II, usable without inventing a second,
Church-adjacent reason for anyone to want the fen quiet. It does not touch
the House, Greymere, or Ness's own undercroft; it's weather, same as its
three predecessors, spread thin rather than concentrated.

`(SYS?)` tier: low; several contained, scattered sightings rather than one
chase across the whole line, per `C2`'s own instruction that this clock
never resolves into a boss fight.

## Fourth Mercy Products — the Undercroft's Early Work

Unlike `G2`'s Tank-Four Remnant — optional, unconfirmed, a possibility a DM
could use or leave — this category is confirmed and real. `R4b`'s D-25
call 1 names the undercroft's actual work: "a small bank of immersion
tanks and the early stages of homunculi-growing," nothing else — no stud
book, no extraction theatre, no bound thing in a brass-ring room; those
stay Ascension House's own signature (`R4b`). `R4b`'s own Reveal B puts a
party within reach of this without ever requiring a fight with it, and this
file builds the two activities with canon §7's catalogued-not-lurid
restraint, the same register the discharge files themselves use
(`source-bible.md` → Places, Fourth Mercy facility layout).

### The Tanks

**What a party finds.** A small bank of brine tanks, tended, not
tormented — the same thermometer-and-clipboard attentiveness ch. 14 gives
Ascension House's own tank room (`source-bible.md`), scaled down to the
handful Region IV's own smaller, better-hidden site needs (`R4a`).
Corporal Emrys Dunmore, if a table's own continuity has reached him, may be
here; per `R4b`'s own D-25 call 4, his fate stays deliberately open,
whatever a party finds.

**Behaviour.** None, in the sense a bestiary entry usually means it — the
horror here is administrative stillness, not a creature. Nothing in a tank
reacts to being observed, threatens, or needs a tier. A log reading, in
the same cold hand as Ascension House's own files, "result negative,
subject retained," does more work at the table than any description of a
specific man's condition, and canon §7 asks for the log over the
description every time.

**Tells.** The same clinical furniture `R4a`/`R4b` already imply — a
thermometer, a clipboard, a log kept in the same institutional hand as
every other Fourth Mercy document this campaign has built (`H1`–`H3`).
Nothing eldritch; the wrongness is that this is ordinary, careful
institutional process, applied here.

`(SYS?)` tier: none. Not a combat encounter under any circumstance.

### The Early Growth

**What a party finds.** The early stages of homunculi-growing, per D-25
call 1 explicitly scoped short of a finished specimen — neither the
"shelved room of grown specimens" ch. 14 shows at Ascension House nor
anything further along than that. Inert, barely formed, closer to
undifferentiated material than to anything a party could mistake for a
person.

**Behaviour.** None. Doesn't move, doesn't react, doesn't reach for
anyone — the entry's entire point is that there is nothing here yet to
have a behaviour. A DM who gives it instinct, hunger, or motion has built
something `R4b`'s own D-25 call 1 explicitly declined to put in this
Part — that belongs, if it belongs anywhere in this campaign, to Ascension
House's own fully-grown specimens, not here.

**Tells.** Whatever a DM needs to make the room read as a working process
rather than a trophy case — a growth log with dates and measurements in
the same flattened hand as the tank log above, nothing displayed, nothing
staged for effect.

**Where this matters, and its limits.** Per `R4b` §9's own DM note — "this
is not a second Ascension House, and the undercroft's residents are not a
loot table" — this file holds that line exactly: immersion and early
growth only, nothing further, whatever a table's own instincts reach for
mid-session.

`(SYS?)` tier: none. Not a combat encounter under any circumstance this
file anticipates.

## DM Notes

**The Specialist Staff and the Fen Smallholder exist to keep Region IV's
horror institutional, not staged.** Per `R4b`'s own discipline for Brother
Ness, nothing here should read as secretly sinister on sight; a DM who
gives either archetype a guilty look or a nervous glance has written this
Part's menace wrong.

**The Fourth Mercy Products entries are not encounters to win, and not a
loot table.** Per `R4b` §9's own warning, a party that finds the tanks or
the early growth should come away with information and discomfort, not a
fight. Reach for the catalogued, discharge-file register every time
description is tempting, and never let the two activities expand past
what D-25 call 1 scoped.

**Never let the diffuse Fen-Line Lingerer concentrate into one boss
fight.** `R4a` is explicit that this region's pest pressure is "worse in
more places, each individually weaker" — treating it as a single climactic
creature writes Region IV's texture as though it were Region I's or III's.

**None of this needs a fight to matter, and the novel's three stay
offstage.** Per `A1` pillar 1 and the cost test (canon §8), every entry
above can resolve without combat; reach for it because the table wants it,
not because an entry exists. No change from `R4a`/`R4b`: Lloyd, Maria, and
Edie do not appear in this Part, even restrictively.
