# SOURCE BIBLE

The consolidated lore ledger from the full read-through (`novel/readthrough/
batch-1.md`–`batch-5.md`, `SYNTHESIS.md`) plus `novel/canon.md` Sections 4–5.
Every later campaign file cites this rather than re-deriving facts from the
chapters. Per RUNBOOK P1.6.

Chapter citations are the novel, not table-time flavour — cut them when a
line from here lands in a player-facing file. Where the book leaves a fact
genuinely unresolved, that's noted; it isn't this file's job to resolve it
(see `campaign/hooks.md` for the hooks, `campaign/DECISIONS.md` for pending
calls — R-1 through R-5 below).

## PLACES

**Stillwell Hydro.** A hydropathic sanatorium on a cold tidal coast in the
Larrow Valley; granite-and-pine. Sells entry to the Salts as a nerve cure,
thirty-five shillings a run, billed against a patron's account; run strictly
in batches of three ("no single entries, no pairs"), tracked by a numbered
brass run-token. Staffed by an intake clerk and steam-room attendants. Where
the novel opens; one base candidate among several per `DECISIONS.md` D-6.
The reception clerk is a fixture, never statted (D-6). (ch. 1)

**The Salts.** Entered through a private, sauna-like room: reagent
("gothic") materials into a steam basin, the room fills with vapour, everyone
present settles and blacks out in turn. No immersion, no equipment. Time
inside does not track time outside. A run is taxing — painful and exhausting
enough that serious users stay on-site and take one or two runs a day. The
Church uses it industrially; not a secret to the powerful. Chalices (below)
grant an ability at a cost. Injuries taken inside are real on surfacing and
can heal wrong. (canon §5; ch. 1–5 primary)

**The chapel-shaped gallery ("the chapel").** A recurring, unstable chamber
inside the Salts. Differs slightly on every pass — moved candles, a column
where there was none, a missing floor tile — with no physical cause offered.
Houses the chalice on a limestone pedestal, in a carved depression, visible
only once declared present. Searched three times in ch. 3; returned to for
the chalice in ch. 5. (ch. 2–3, 5)

**Red Redoubt.** War-era (the Attrition) site of the camouflet that deafened
Edie's left ear. Named only as backstory; no on-page location details beyond
the event itself. (ch. 1, recurring reference)

**Callow Ford.** Lloyd's home village, Larrow Valley. Site of Ivet Hale's
house and, a year after ch. 25, a new standpipe replacing the old well. Lloyd
is barred from it under a false wanted name as of the book's ending. (ch. 9,
25)

**The Larrow Valley.** Twelve villages, Lloyd's home ground, Crane's works.
The mine, the river, the works, and all twelve villages trace back to the
same ownership. Confirmed campaign Part I default per `PLAN.md`/R1a. (canon
§5; ch. 20)

**Ammet Ford.** "Larrow Consolidated Works — Ammet Ford Division." A model
company town: new brick housing, slate roofs, a fully-glazed school,
poured-concrete roads and culverts, a company-sunk well forty feet through
clay. Its well water is sweet beyond ordinary mineral sweetness — "a
thickness... a hush at the back of the throat" — producing an unconsidered
compulsion to keep drinking it. Never confirmed on the page as the same
substance as the valley's Sweetwater disease, only named the same by the
people who drink it. (ch. 8)

**The Larrow Indemnity.** A posted compensation scheme tied to Crane's
works; claims payable only in a worker's seventh year of
employment/registration — timed, per Lloyd's ch. 21 confession, to Crane's
own commissioned mortality curve for the disease the water causes. Most
claimants are dead before they could collect. Two inspectors who pushed to
reopen the underlying survey were reassigned to the coast. (ch. 8, 21)

**Hessel Bight / Hessel crossing.** Rail town four days north of Stillwell;
site of a flooded, war-damaged manse (eleven war dead in the cellar) cleared
as paid "wet work." (ch. 7)

**Tannery Reach.** A tannery/canal town split between Saint Aldhelm's and
Marsh Fenton parishes along the canal centreline. A flood-destroyed boundary
marker left a drowned man (Fenwick, of Harker's yard) unclaimed for a day
while the two parishes disputed burial liability. Strong template for
contested-jurisdiction encounters. (ch. 11)

**Sennet (almshouse).** Vail's first posting. Grey stone, bread oven, a poor
box ("FOR THE POOR") by the door, a meal-bell rather than an hour-bell.
(ch. 12)

**Vantry.** The Commonwealth's second city — mills, soot, rail junction.
Permanent smoke-haze that never clears day or night; gaslamps lit at noon;
chimneys ranked dozens deep. Campaign Part II default (R2a/b). (canon §5;
ch. 13, 14)

**Ascension House, Vantry.** The Fourth Mercy's working laboratory. New
brick over an older stone foundation that predates the Church's ownership of
the ground — per parish deed records. A bell tower that never rings; an
unused front chapel door with grass between its steps; a cart-width service
door around the side doing all the building's real traffic. A barred,
below-grade window shows a pale standing gleam (not lamplight) and, once, a
shape that "moved nothing like a man walks." Destroyed by fire in ch. 15; a
year after ch. 25, a burned shell (`DECISIONS.md` D-1). The pre-Church
foundation beneath it is never explained on the page — strong thin-site
candidate (T1–T3). (ch. 13–16)

**Fourth Mercy facility layout (Ascension House).** Five rooms, visited in
order in ch. 14: a tank room (twelve occupied brine tanks, tended by a nurse
with a thermometer and clipboard); a shelved room of grown "specimens"; a
cots room holding a stud book of Arcana bloodlines going back decades; an
extraction theatre (restraint chair, sixty years of files ending "result
negative, subject retained" or "...subject not recoverable"); and a
brass-ring room holding something bound that was once a person. Beneath a
novice-height catechism reading "bread, shelter, absolution," a fourth line
has been added in a recent, ledger-style hand — its exact wording withheld
on the page. Core material for the G/H bestiary and compendium items
(Phase 2). (ch. 14)

**The Vantry assembly's standing archive.** Where the stolen intake
register, the stud book, and sixty years of case files ended up after ch.
18's exposure — "to be asked for by date," almost never requested once the
story stopped being new. The evidence that ended the ward still exists,
filed and essentially unread. Strong bureaucratic-neglect location. (ch. 19)

**The Indemnity Office, Carrowgate.** A government office identifiable by a
small brass pin (crossed quills); keeps session-day hours and maintenance
("carpet men") schedules. Plausibly the office that adjudicated compensation
or liability claims tied to Crane's works — the likely link to the buried
survey. (ch. 20)

**Carrowgate.** The capital. Finer, lighter soot than Vantry's (a white
collar lasts two days there instead of one); gaslamps on the better avenues
burn a clean greenish white. The Senate sits atop a hill, long grey roofline,
a central dome, built to read as permanent. Home to the Sanctum (below).
Campaign Part III default (R3a/b). (canon §5; ch. 20)

**The Sanctum, Carrowgate.** Church headquarters. Never touched across the
book — everyone senior survives it. A year after ch. 25, still untouched
(`DECISIONS.md` D-1). Ondine Vail is transferred and promoted here after
Ascension House's exposure. (canon §4; ch. 18)

**Selwick.** An almshouse two days up the north road per Brother Aldous, one
day per the morning's own marker post — a discrepancy noticed by Edie and
never resolved. Good thin-site or omen seed. (ch. 10)

## FACTIONS

**The Church of the Mercies.** State-adjacent, wealthy, popular; genuinely
charitable at street level, and most of its people know nothing of the
fourth. Three public Mercies: bread, shelter, absolution. (canon §4)

**The Fourth Mercy.** An "ascension programme" — taking people nobody will
miss and attempting to make something higher from them. Five documented
activities: homunculi grown in brine; industrial immersion of
non-consenting subjects who never surface; attempted extraction of the soul
as a separable object; Arcane breeding via a stud book; things bound out of
the Salts and kept. Sixty years running, every failure documented by name
and date, doctrine holding it will eventually work. The other three Mercies
feed it — bread and shelter identify the surplus, absolution collects them.
Crisis response (proven in ch. 18, reusable as a faction template): grief
offered first so nothing is left to accuse; blame narrowed to names furthest
from the top; a restitution figure large enough that disputing it looks like
disputing charity. Eleven days of news cycle before any story about it
curdles into a dispute over evidence rather than contents. (canon §4; ch.
14, 18)

**Larrow Consolidated Works ("Crane's works").** Owns the mine, the
Ammet Ford division, and effectively the Larrow Valley's economy. Owned by
Halvard Crane through two intermediaries. Commissioned and buried a
groundwater survey before the works broke ground; the Larrow Indemnity's
seventh-year payout clause was timed to that survey's own mortality curve.
(ch. 8, 21)

**Ferrick's ethics sub-panel.** Senator Ferrick's investigative body. Its
second water report (forty pages, indexed, testing mill runoff from six
towns against valley wells) states plainly the water won't be fit to drink
within a decade — excellent, acted on by nobody for two months at the time
Maria sees it. After Crane's death (ch. 25), grief gives Ferrick the votes
to bring the works-water schedule forward, test the water, and cap wells
before frost. (ch. 23, 25)

**The Indemnity Office.** See Places. Functional link to Crane's survey
not yet dramatized past Lloyd's own surveillance of it. (ch. 20)

**The Vantry broadsheet.** Ran the stolen intake register as photographed
plates rather than typeset copy, specifically so no compositor's word could
be disputed. The only story in the Commonwealth for eleven days before it
curdled into an authenticity debate. Good "press as a faction" material.
(ch. 18)

**Fourth Tunnelling Company.** Edie's wartime unit; also Colonel Absolom
Reyne's. Background only; disbanded with the war's end. (canon §1; ch. 24)

## PEOPLE

**Lloyd Hale.** 31, from Callow Ford. Labourer, then unlicensed prizefighter,
then a man who did jobs. Insight since nineteen; institutionalised five
weeks at twenty-two in Carrowgate for naming what he saw to the wrong person
(never spoken of to Maria or Edie). Has a second, ethereal right arm granted
by the Salts (never disclosed to Maria or Edie; see Rules below). Final
state as of ch. 25: wanted on every board in the valley under a wrong name,
barred from Callow Ford, his right arm broken a second time and healing
crooked again. Killed Halvard Crane in ch. 24. (canon §1; ch. 24–25)

**Maria Vesk.** 28, raised in the Church from age seven by Ondine Vail.
Native Arcana (healing, light, fire, lightning, steadying — see Rules).
Stole forty-one crowns for Anse Duley at twenty-two, confessed it to Vail in
ch. 12, forgiven in roughly fifteen seconds. Refused Vail's request to
"imbue" her Arcana into an unborn child (ch. 13). With Lloyd at the book's
end, in Carrowgate; their relationship is the book's actual ending point.
(canon §1; ch. 12–13, 25)

**Edith "Edie" Marrow.** 34, former sapper, Fourth Tunnelling Company, four
years. Deaf in the left ear (camouflet, Red Redoubt). Killed Colonel
Absolom Reyne in a private, unwitnessed killing eleven weeks after he sealed
ninety-one of his own sappers into a collapsed tunnel to save an hour (nine
escaped; she was one of them). Has worked for Senator Ferrick since before
ch. 1 (unnamed to Lloyd and Maria until ch. 9, named to the reader from
ch. 1). Final state: inside Ferrick's household, trusted, running his
security; wears her knot-cord visibly rather than hidden for the first time
in the book — "the first wholly honest position" Maria has watched her
hold since the tunnel. Traded her bright-line morality for pure
consequentialism by the book's end, without the book calling that a fall.
(canon §1; ch. 24–25)

**Ondine Vail.** 40s. Sister Superior — Sennet, then Vantry, then the
Sanctum, Carrowgate. Raised Maria from age seven; the affection is real and
never revealed as false. Sincerely believes the Fourth Mercy is the most
important work in the world, for exactly the reasons she states aloud.
Never lies, never lowers her voice, never threatens, never gets angry,
answers direct questions directly. Transferred and promoted to the Sanctum
after Ascension House's exposure rather than despite it. Writes Maria a
warm letter after, asking only after her health. (canon §3; ch. 18)

**Halvard Crane.** Silver-haired, small round spectacles, soft unhurried
manner. Owns Larrow Consolidated Works through two intermediaries;
commissioned and buried the groundwater survey. Never a caricature — warm
in public, liked, generous to charity. Killed by Lloyd in a service passage
during an Indemnity review session, courteous and confused to the end ("Is
the young lady quite all right?"). (canon §7; ch. 24)

**Senator Ferrick.** Household estate funds Edie's first trip to Stillwell
(ch. 1, unstated on the page but per outline). Runs the ethics sub-panel.
Sincerely decent, genuinely ineffective; never mocked for it on the page.
Employs Edie's security detail: an ex-quartermaster, two former constables,
and a young woman who checks deliveries against a list, plus an iron
cabinet bolted into a former nursery floor holding the real Vantry
registers. (ch. 1, 23)

**Ivet Hale.** Lloyd's mother. Has had the Sweetwater (the disease) for at
least four years as of ch. 9. Found alive by Maria a year after ch. 25,
recovered, moved to clean water via a new standpipe, steadier than four
years of the old water should have left her. (ch. 9, 25)

**Anse Duley.** The widow Maria stole forty-one crowns for at twenty-two.
Taken into the Fourth Mercy two years after Maria fled the Church, two
years after the theft — confirmed via the Ascension House intake register
Maria reads in ch. 14. Edie never learns this. (canon §6; ch. 12, 14)

**Colonel Absolom Reyne.** Fourth Tunnelling. Sealed a breach with
ninety-one of his own sappers still inside to save an hour; nine (including
Edie) escaped through an unofficial second heading. Had been in the Salts,
which is how Edie learned the Salts exist, and why her private killing of
him eleven weeks later nearly went the other way. (canon §1; ch. 24)

**Brother Aldous & Brother Tam.** Two Mercy brothers travelling the north
road to Selwick with blankets and seed corn. Aldous's collar does not move
with his throat when he laughs — the clearest "something is wrong with a
Church agent" sighting attached to a named, speaking character. Strong
construct/homunculus candidate. (ch. 10)

## OBJECTS

**The chalice.** Lead, roughly a pint capacity; appears on the chapel's
limestone pedestal only once Maria declares it present. Grants an ability
at a cost (see Rules — chalice costs).

**The knot-cord.** Hemp cord with brass wire ferrules at regular intervals.
First a depth/route marker underground (ch. 2 on); reused overland as a day
counter (ch. 9–10); in ch. 16's delirium it counts out ninety-one instead of
nineteen (deliberate unreliable-narration device, not a continuity error).
Edie's cord specifically becomes, by ch. 25, the visible symbol of her
traded morality — worn openly rather than hidden.

**Edie's ledger.** An oilcloth account-book, soft and dark at the corners.
Costs every job, fare, and favour from ch. 6 on. Her itemised closing of the
six weeks' recovery costs in ch. 19 ("Paid out") is a ready-made template
for Travel & Downtime (F1) cost conventions.

**The extraction timer.** A clockwork device at Stillwell Hydro, read on
surfacing: hours elapsed outside against a second figure, "19 DAYS
REMAINING." Never explained anywhere in the book (ch. 5–25). See
`hooks.md`.

**The vestment-thread frame.** A scrap of torn vestment cloth left caught
on a window latch near Crane's body after the killing — not evidence that
would hold up under oath, enough only to point the obvious question at the
Church. Corroborated by accident when Maria's own public light-burst is
seen during the escape. Template for how a frame/cover story actually works
at the table. (ch. 24)

**Lloyd's stolen floor plan.** A traced building plan with three doors
marked by crosses, found by Maria in his coat alongside a session calendar
and, in his own hand, the name Halvard Crane. (ch. 20)

## RULES (world mechanics — binding on canon, not yet adapted for the table)

**Lloyd — the Second Right.** Physical right arm passes through solid
matter at will, undamaged either way; grip is a decision. A second,
ethereal arm occupies the same shoulder, invisible to everyone, learned
over the book to operate independently (clumsy early, fluent late — by Act
III, effectively two working right arms). It can touch eldritch elements
seen only through Insight — the deliberate ambiguity point: when Lloyd
fights something invisible, whether he struck a real thing is never
resolved on the page. **It cannot lift, carry, throw, or apply force to
ordinary matter while ethereal, grants no strength beyond his own, has no
range beyond an arm's reach.** Cost: he can't always tell which mode he's
in until he tries; sensation bleeds between the two arms; he drops things.
*Open question (R-1, see DECISIONS.md): ch. 7 has the ethereal arm lift and
carry an ordinary iron rod through a wall — a stated breach of this limit,
unresolved on the page. Treat as pending until Jake rules; S1 should not
build a surface analogue to this ability without that ruling.* (canon §2)

**Maria — Arcana, native, not Salts-granted.** Fixed, complete repertoire:
healing (slow, effortful, no regrowth, no disease cure, no raising the
dead — costs her hours and leaves her useless afterward); light (reliable,
cheap, sustained, her most useful ability); fire (one ~5m burst,
devastating indoors, several minutes' recharge); lightning (accurate,
lethal, a bolt at a visible target, she hates using it); steadying (a
minor, near-unconscious knack). Far beyond any other natural practitioner
in the book; explicitly not war-winning, does not solve sieges, armies, or
institutions. She declined a chalice ability outright — drank only enough
to return. (canon §2)

**Edie — the Still Mind.** Knows violence a half-second before it happens.
No visions, no voices, no images. Does not make her faster, stronger, or
harder to hit — she is frequently struck despite knowing it's coming; the
gap between knowing and acting is the ability's whole point. **Covers
violence only — not lies, not intentions, not danger in general. A
collapsing floor does not register. A man deciding to hit her does.** *Open
question (R-5, see DECISIONS.md): ch. 23 has it fire on an accidental
dropped tray, outline-mandated but contradicting this limit as written.
Pending Jake's call; I1's cameo rules for Edie should wait on it rather than
guess.* (canon §2)

**The Salts loop.** Entered via reagent-triggered steam-room blackout at a
hydro (not immersion or diving gear); time inside is decoupled from time
outside; a chalice grants one ability at a cost, once; injuries taken
inside surface real and may heal wrong; repeated serious use means staying
on-site and running once or twice daily. This is the loop `PLAN.md`'s S1
item has to adapt for a surface-based campaign rather than inherit as-is —
see `DECISIONS.md` D-10 for the proposed replacements (crowns/favours,
safehouses, real travel, a flight/capture/recovery rule). (canon §5)

**Who-knows-what (binding for any NPC cameo of the three leads — see I1).**
Lloyd alone knows about his second arm and that the ch. 3 regrowth wasn't
really Maria's doing; neither fact is ever told to Maria or Edie. Maria
never learns of Lloyd's five weeks in Carrowgate. Edie never learns Anse
Duley was taken by the Fourth Mercy. All three gaps are explicit in canon
as deliberate and permanent — any future scene using Lloyd, Maria, or Edie
as an NPC must honour them. Full table: canon §6. (canon §6)
