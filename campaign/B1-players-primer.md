# B1 — PLAYER'S PRIMER

*Reader: players — hand this out at session zero and read it before character
creation. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md` (premise, pillars, timeline
anchor) and `D1-bases-safehouses.md` (the starting base) — this file
restates only what a player needs to sit down at the table, not the DM's
working notes behind either. Nothing below gives away anything a DM would
want to keep for play: where the brief holds a fact back, this file holds it
back too. Cites `PLAN.md`'s tone contract and `S1-surface-adaptation.md`'s
Crowns and Favours in plain, player-facing terms. See `A2-dms-guide.md` §4
for how a DM should run the session this handout is meant to open.*

## Where You Are

You live in the Aventine Commonwealth: gaslight and rail, a Senate that
still sits in Carrowgate like it means something, an empire that stopped
getting bigger a long time ago. The war everyone calls the Attrition ended
a little under two years back. Nobody in this Commonwealth talks much about
who it was fought against — that's not evasion, it's just not a
conversation people have anymore, the way you don't discuss last winter's
weather. What people do talk about is what four years of it cost: villages
short a generation of men, mills running on half the hands they used to,
graveyards with a bad stretch of years in them.

Reconstruction is a real word, with real money behind it. It is not a word
that reaches very far. Everyone knows someone whose bridge, whose road,
whose well was promised and is still waiting.

Where the fighting was worst, the dead don't always stay that way. Nobody
calls it an apocalypse. It's a pest problem, the kind you report to the
right office and wait too long for someone to come clear — a sealed tunnel
nobody's opened since, a stretch of ground best not dug at night. If your
character grew up anywhere the Attrition touched, they grew up knowing
which ground that was.

## What Everybody Knows

**The Church of the Mercies** is everywhere, wealthy, and genuinely
useful — bread for the hungry, shelter for the homeless, absolution for
whoever asks. Most people who work for it believe in it completely, because
at street level there's nothing to disbelieve. It is also, like most big
institutions, the kind of thing you'd rather have as a friend than an
enemy, and most people arrange their lives accordingly without ever quite
deciding to.

**Arcana** is real. It's also rare, weak, and nobody's ever organised it —
no guild, no licence, no registry. A hedge-healer in a market town, a man
who can light a candle without a match: that's most of what Arcana looks
like in this Commonwealth. Nobody with real power takes it seriously enough
to control it, which is itself a kind of mercy, depending on who you ask.

**The Salts** are a dream most people have heard of and few have tried.
Certain hydros — Stillwell, on the Larrow coast, is the one anyone from the
valley could name — sell entry as a nerve cure: breathe in a prepared
vapour in a private room, black out, come back sometime later with no good
way to say how long you were gone. It's taxing. It's not cheap. The rumour
that sticks hardest is the chalice: that somewhere down there, something
can give you an ability you didn't have, permanently, for a price you'll
also keep permanently. Almost nobody your character has met has actually
done it. Everybody's heard the rumour anyway.

## The Last Year, As People Tell It

Public memory is never as clean as what actually happened, and this
primer isn't going to pretend otherwise. Here's the version a reasonably
well-informed person in this Commonwealth would give you, drift and
exaggeration included:

About a year ago, a scandal broke in Vantry. A Church building — Ascension
House — burned down amid a flood of ugly rumour: stolen registers,
photographs in the broadsheets, eleven straight days of a single story
dominating every front page in the Commonwealth. Then, the way these things
go, people started arguing about whether the photographs were even real,
and the story curdled into a fight about evidence instead of a fight about
what the evidence showed. Ask ten people today what actually happened at
Ascension House and you'll get four different answers and six shrugs.

Around the same time, a mine owner named Halvard Crane died in the Larrow
Valley, in circumstances the Church somehow ended up taking the public
blame for — depending who's telling it, that's either a grieving
institution shouldering what it shouldn't, or exactly what you'd expect
from people who burn down their own buildings when the questions get too
close. Since then, the valley's well-testing and capping schedule — a slow,
underfunded thing that's been limping along for years — got pushed forward.
Some villages have clean water now that didn't a year ago. Some still
don't.

Nobody famous came out of any of this. No hero got named in the
broadsheets. That's the honest, unglamorous shape of a year-old scandal in
a Commonwealth with a lot of other things to worry about.

## Starting Out: Harrow's Weir

The campaign opens in the Larrow Valley, in a village called Harrow's Weir,
downriver of the valley's mine — the kind of place runoff settles and bad
luck collects. Most parties will find themselves, one way or another,
staying at **Beddoe's**, a converted cooperage run as a boarding house by
a widow named Nessa Beddoe. She fronts credit against work nobody else
posts openly, keeps a slate of who owes her what, and runs the warmest
room in the village. If your character needs a reason to already be here,
or to arrive here first, "I owe Nessa for a bed and a meal" is as good a
starting point as any — it's the valley's most ordinary debt.

## Who You Are

Nobody at this table is playing Lloyd, Maria, or Edie — the three names
anyone who followed last year's news might recognise from whichever version
of the story they heard. Your characters are new people, with no connection
to any of that, pulled into the valley's business the same unglamorous way
real people get pulled into things: a debt owed, a relative who stopped
writing, a job that pays too well for what it claims to be, a posting
nobody else wanted, a letter that needs carrying to someone who left under
a different name.

You are not chosen ones. Nothing marks you as special before play starts.
You are not agents of the Church, not agents of the state, and none of you
begins play already holding an Arcana gift rarer than the setting allows —
if a character ever gets a chalice-granted ability, it will be earned in
play, at a real and permanent cost, not handed out at the table before the
first session.

## Your Hook

Pick one, adapt one, or bring your own — as long as it's the kind of thing
that could happen to anyone in this Commonwealth, not a destiny:

- Somebody in your life owes money, and you're the one who has to collect
  it, settle it, or run from it.
- A relative went quiet — stopped writing, stopped sending word — and the
  last anyone heard, they were headed this way.
- A job came through that pays better than it has any right to for what it
  says it is, and you took it anyway.
- You got posted here — by an employer, a creditor, an institution — and
  nobody you know would have chosen this valley for themselves.
- Somebody who works for the Church did you a kindness once, and this is
  the price of it.

Whatever you pick, it should be something your character would rather not
be doing. Nobody in this valley is here because it's glamorous.

## Why You Stay

This isn't a dungeon crawl, and nobody's handing you a map with an X on it.
Most of what you'll do is talk to people, follow a document, call in a
favour, and work out who's lying by omission rather than outright. Combat
happens. It is rarely the fastest way to an answer, and it is never free.

Two things keep score instead of a single currency:

**Crowns** are money, plain and simple — wages, fares, a bribe, a surgeon's
bill. You'll track them the itemised, specific way everyone in this valley
already does: rent, bread, a favour fronted, nothing abstracted into a
vague "gold total."

**Favours** are owed, not spent. The clerk who remembers you helped her
through a bad week, the runner who looks the other way because you did him
one good turn — that's not a resource on your sheet, it's a relationship,
and calling it in costs that relationship something. Spend a favour and you
may close a door you didn't mean to close.

Nowhere you stay is permanently safe. A safehouse is wherever you've built
standing — Beddoe's slate paid down, a hydro's quiet trust, a work camp
that'll vouch for you — and standing can lapse. If you stop showing up
somewhere, don't expect it to still be there for you later.

What actually keeps a party at the table, session to session, is the same
thing that kept the novel's own three people moving: the ordinary stakes
compound. A debt doesn't vanish because you ignored it for a week. A
missing relative doesn't turn up on their own. The valley's water schedule
moves whether or not you help it along, and somebody specific pays for
every week it doesn't reach them in time. Ask, honestly, what a scene costs
and who pays it — that question doesn't stop mattering just because you put
the controller down.

## What You're Not

You're not here to fix the Church, topple an institution, or get a clean
ending. Nothing in this world works that way, and nothing you do this
campaign is going to be the exception. Institutions here don't fall; they
get outlasted, or you get out from under them, and that's a real win when
it happens — just not the kind that makes the problem stop existing. Expect
victories that are personal, specific, and real, and expect most of them
to cost you something to get.

## Before You Roll Anything

The full character-building rules — attributes, limb targeting, status
effects, how Arcana and the Salts actually work on a sheet — aren't
finished yet. Until they are, build your character as a competent adult
with a trade or a service record behind them: the kind of person this
Commonwealth's reconstruction left underemployed, not a storybook
adventurer. Your DM will fill in hard numbers once the real rules land;
nothing about your character's story needs to wait on that.
