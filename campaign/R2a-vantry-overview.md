# R2a — PART II: VANTRY AND THE RAIL — OVERVIEW + LOCATIONS

*Reader: DM. Builds on `A1-campaign-brief.md`, `C2-factions-clocks.md`, and
`C3-the-salts-as-background.md` — read all three first; this file does not

file keys that geography into a Part, it does not re-draw the map. Also
cites `R1b-larrow-valley-leads-antagonist.md` → §8 (Consequences) for how

`D1-bases-safehouses.md` for the basing conventions this file borrows one

## 1. Premise Card

**Region:** Vantry and the Rail (`C1` Region II).

**Level band:** `(SYS?)` — one notch past Part I's "nothing earned yet." The
exact numeric range waits on the ruleset's tier bands landing in
`campaign/system/` (Q-1); until then, run this Part for a party that has
been through Part I and build a fresh party, if one starts the campaign
here instead, one step past starting-character assumptions rather than at
them.

**One-line hook:** the scandal is a year and a half old to everyone except
the people whose names are still in a drawer nobody's opened since the
story stopped being new, and somebody with an ordinary reason to care just
asked the party to go find out exactly what that drawer says.

**What the surface wants.** Ferrick's sub-panel, headquartered here, wants
the Larrow Valley's capping schedule to read as a win it can point to and
more hands and funding for the next underfunded thing after it — sincere,
chronically short-staffed, and still the faction in this file most willing
to take help and least able to pay for it outright (`C2`). The Vantry
broadsheet wants a story that isn't eighteen months stale; the Ascension
House exposure made it the paper of record for eleven days and it has not
had a story like it since. Mill and rail labour — the lodging rows' actual
population — want to be left alone, which in this city is itself a kind of
want: anonymity is the whole point of how that housing is built and used.
Nobody in Region II is waiting on a schedule the way Region I's villages
are; this is the Capping Clock and the Indemnity Mortality Curve's off
season — both still exist, both are Region I's problem, neither moves
because of anything that happens here.

**What the Church wants.** Centre stage for the first time in the
campaign. Ascension House is gone, but the Church's three ordinary
Mercies — bread, shelter, absolution — never stopped running in the
largest city it operates outside the capital, and canon's own mechanism
(bread and shelter identify the surplus, absolution collects it) didn't
stop working just because its one named destination burned down
(`source-bible.md` → Factions). What the Church wants here, concretely: the
standing archive to keep gathering dust unread; the burned lot to stay a
burned lot, unexcavated, un-discussed; and its ordinary charitable face —
genuinely charitable, genuinely popular, exactly as `A1` describes — to go
on being the first and friendliest thing a desperate person in this city
meets. Nothing below confirms where the ascension work itself resumed, if
it has; that's `R2b`'s call, same as Corlett's name was `R1b`'s to make, not
`R1a`'s.

## 2. Situation

Call it two to three months past wherever a table's own Part I ended —
generous enough that a long Part I still fits, specific enough that frost
has reached the Larrow Valley and Region I's own clocks have had time to
settle into whatever state the party left them in. This file doesn't fix
Part I's outcome and doesn't need to: nothing below assumes Corlett was
exposed, reassigned quietly, or never touched at all. `K1` (not yet
written) will eventually hold the running continuity index `A1` calls for;
until it exists, a DM carries Part I's actual ending forward by memory or
notes, and this Part is written to work regardless of which ending that
was.

Three of `C2`'s six clocks matter here; the other three are Region I's and
sit wherever the party left them, untouched by anything in Vantry.

- **The Press Cycle** is this region's live wire. If a table triggered it
  in Part I (getting Reveal A/B's evidence to the broadsheet or to Ferrick
  publicly), it has already run its eleven days and curdled into an
  authenticity dispute by the time this Part opens — the broadsheet's
  office (below) remembers exactly how that story died, and that memory is
  this Part's opening texture rather than a live fuse. If the party never
  triggered it, the clock is still cold, and the broadsheet is still
  hungry for literally anything, which is its own kind of opening.
- **The Hydro Network Map** sits wherever Part I left it — at one
  (Stillwell; everything else rumour) if the party never asked, or higher
  if they did. Vantry, as the Church's largest non-capital presence and the
  rail junction everything else passes through, is the single best place
  in the Commonwealth to move this clock forward: a supply ledger, a
  tailed cart, a rumour about where the brine for the tanks used to come
  from. Nothing here forces it; `R2b`'s lead web decides whether this Part
  touches it at all.
- **The Pest Pressure Clock**, Vantry instance, runs on different ground
  than the mine's. This city's unhealed Attrition scar isn't a tunnel —
  it's the lodging rows and the rail yards, built and rebuilt fast and
  cheap over ground the war never got properly cleared, same mechanism as
  Region I's mine (disturbed ground, worse where digging continues) with a
  different texture (foundations, culverts, a rail cutting) instead of a
  shaft.

**Who holds what.** The Vantry assembly holds the standing archive — the
stolen intake register, the stud book, sixty years of extraction files —
filed, legally viewable, almost never requested (`hooks.md` #3). Senator
Ferrick's household holds the sub-panel's own paperwork, the iron cabinet
of real Vantry registers, and Ferrick's own household security, which is
Edie Marrow's job now; see her entry under Ferrick's household below before
using her for anything. The broadsheet holds its own archive of plates and
sources, and the institutional memory of being right once and disbelieved
for it within a fortnight. The Church holds everything its three ordinary
Mercies touch, which in a city this size is most of the surplus population
the lodging rows house, and holds the burned lot itself, unclaimed by
anyone who'd admit to still wanting it. Nobody holds the rail junction
itself — it belongs to whichever company runs which line, a detail no
Part needs resolved — but everyone passes through it, which is this
region's whole geographic argument for being Part II.

## 3. Locations

Keyed sites for Region II. Each gives purpose, NPCs, sensory tells,
secrets, and exits, per `PLAN.md`'s Part template. Geography and anything
already fixed by `C1` are cited, not repeated.

### Ascension House (the ruin)

**Purpose.** The Fourth Mercy's former working laboratory, burned in ch. 15
and still a shell a year-plus on (`DECISIONS.md` D-1). This Part's
strongest single site, and its most dangerous to overuse — everything that
made the building matter is either ash or underneath it.

**NPCs.** None resident. A watchman employed by whoever nominally owns the
lot now (unnamed; invent only if a scene needs him to speak) keeps casual
trespassers out and has seen enough strange lights to not ask many
questions about the ones he hasn't reported.

**Sensory tells.** New brick gone black and sagging over an older stone
foundation that still reads, in daylight, as a different, earlier
building — the same foundation `source-bible.md` notes predates the
Church's ownership of the ground. The bell tower, never rung even when the
building stood, is the one part still mostly upright. Grass is coming back
through the old front-chapel steps faster than through the rest of the
ash.

**Secrets.** Whether anyone has gone back under that foundation since the
fire — and what, if anything, is still down there — is `R2b`'s call, not
this file's, same restraint `R1a` used for the Sweetwater double meaning.
`hooks.md` #4 is the standing candidate for this Part's thin site (`T1`);
nothing here commits to using it.

**Exits.** Inside the city proper, a short walk from the lodging rows; the
burned lot backs onto an alley the watchman doesn't bother walking at
night.

### The Vantry Assembly's Standing Archive

**Purpose.** A civic records office, not a Church one — where the stolen
intake register, the stud book, and sixty years of extraction files ended
up after ch. 18's exposure, filed under the city assembly, legally viewable
by anyone who asks and essentially never requested (`hooks.md` #3). The
Part's clearest demonstration that evidence existing and evidence
mattering are different problems.

**NPCs.** An archive clerk (unnamed; the Part-level invention this file
leaves to `R2b`, same deferral `R1a` used for Ammet Ford's administrator)
keeps the files in the same orderly, uninterested state they've been in
since the request log went quiet. Whoever she is, she is not hiding
anything and will hand the register to anyone who fills in the right slip —
the obstacle here is that almost nobody knows to ask, not that anyone's
stopping them.

**Sensory tells.** A request log with a long, undisturbed gap after ch.
18's own flurry of entries; the register's photographed plates, still
filed exactly where the broadsheet's own citation left them; a reading
room built for an audience that never came back.

**Secrets.** The files themselves are the secret, in the sense that nobody
currently in this file knows they're worth asking for — the archive has no
hidden second layer beyond what the register, stud book, and files already
plainly say. A party that finds this room and reads what's in it has done
the Part's hardest work by walking through a door that was never locked.

**Exits.** Civic quarter, a short rail-feeder ride or a longer walk from
Ferrick's household and from the lodging rows alike.

### A Mercy House

**Purpose.** One of several — the ordinary, unglamorous face of the Church
in Vantry: bread, shelter, absolution, running exactly as charitably and
exactly as effectively as `A1` and canon §4 describe, with no connection to
Ascension House visible from inside the building. The Part's standing
reminder that the Church's menace and the Church's genuine goodness are the
same institution, not two institutions wearing one name.

**NPCs.** A Mercy sister or brother (unnamed; invent per scene, in the same
sincere, warmly-delivered register `novel/canon.md` §3 sets for Vail and
extends to the Mercies generally — never menace in the staging, only, if
ever, in content) runs the bread line and keeps the shelter rolls. Neither
is a disguised villain; per `C2`'s DM notes, most of the Church's people
genuinely don't know what the fourth Mercy is.

**Sensory tells.** A bread queue that starts before dawn; a shelter roll
with names crossed through for reasons ranging from found work to nothing
anyone wrote down; the confession booth that absolution actually runs out
of, plain wood, no better furnished than anything else in the building.

**Secrets.** Per canon's own mechanism, the shelter roll and the bread
queue are where this city's surplus — people nobody's missing yet — gets
noticed first. Nothing here says where a noticed name goes next now that
Ascension House is gone; that's deliberately left open for `R2b`, same as
Ascension House's own foundation above. Playing this location as sinister
on its own terms is the single easiest way to get it wrong — the horror,
if the table ever finds it, lives one step removed from this room, never
inside it.

**Exits.** One of several Mercy Houses the city has; place others as a
scene needs them rather than inventing a second one here.

### Senator Ferrick's Household

**Purpose.** Where Ferrick's ethics sub-panel sits, and where its household
security — Edie Marrow's job since before the novel's end — is run.
Canon's model for a faction that will take help gladly and never has enough
hands (`C2`).

**NPCs.** **Senator Ferrick** himself: sincerely decent, genuinely
ineffective, never mocked for it on the page and not to be mocked for it
here (`source-bible.md` → People). Free to speak, to ask the party for
help, and to be wrong about how fast anything moves. His security detail is
canon-fixed: an ex-quartermaster, two former constables, and a young woman
who checks deliveries against a list (`source-bible.md`), none of them
named on the page and none needing names here unless a scene wants one. The
iron cabinet bolted into a former nursery floor, holding the real Vantry
registers, is itself worth treating as a location feature.
**Edie Marrow** runs this household's security and wears her knot-cord
openly for the first time in the book (`source-bible.md` → People). `I1`
(Dramatis Personae, cameo rules) does not yet exist. Until it does, do not
run her as a speaking NPC in this Part — a name on the household roster, a
figure glimpsed across a courtyard, nothing with dialogue, exactly the
restraint `R1b` set for Lloyd and Maria in Region I. The who-knows-what
gaps (canon §6) bind her exactly as they bind any future use of her: she
has never told Ferrick's household, or anyone, about Reyne, and nothing
here changes that.

**Sensory tells.** A household run tighter than its modest means should
allow; the iron cabinet's incongruous solidity in an otherwise ordinary
converted house; a knot-cord worn visibly by one member of staff, which
means nothing to anyone who doesn't already know what it is.

**Secrets.** The iron cabinet holds the real Vantry registers — the
unfiltered version of whatever the sub-panel reports publicly. What's in
it beyond that is `R2b`'s to decide if a lead web needs it; this file only
establishes that it exists and that Ferrick would show it to anyone he
trusted enough to ask properly.

**Exits.** Civic quarter, near enough to the standing archive that a
single afternoon can plausibly cover both.

### The Vantry Broadsheet

**Purpose.** Ran the stolen intake register as photographed plates rather
than typeset copy specifically so no compositor's word could be disputed —
the only story in the Commonwealth for eleven days before it curdled into
an authenticity dispute (`source-bible.md` → Factions). A press faction
this Part can use as leverage, an ally, or both.

**NPCs.** An editor (unnamed; `R2b`'s to name if a scene needs one) who
remembers exactly how fast a solid story turned into a dispute over
evidence rather than contents, and has been hungry for a follow-up ever
since without finding one worth the same risk.

**Sensory tells.** A newsroom that still keeps the original plates on file,
filed with more care than the city archive keeps its own copies; a wall of
old front pages with the Ascension House run given pride of place, eleven
days' worth, then nothing.

**Secrets.** Whether the editor still has sources inside the Church, the
assembly, or Ferrick's household who'd talk again is this Part's to decide
in play, not to pre-answer here — same principle `C2`'s Press Cycle clock
already applies: tell the table where this clock sits when it's plausible
their characters would know, never spring it.

**Exits.** Printing district, a short walk from the lodging rows; copies
ship out on the same rail lines everything else in this region does.

### The Lodging Rows

**Purpose.** Not on the page by name but implied by everything canon says
about Vantry as an industrial second city: streets of rented rooms and
boarding houses for mill and rail labour, the anonymous-by-necessity
housing that absorbs people nobody will miss — exactly the population the
Church's other three Mercies are built to notice first (`C1`;
`source-bible.md`). This Part's standing texture location, and the natural
place a new party ends up.

**NPCs.** **Ress Tannery** — invented here to give the rows a foothold
rather than leave the whole region without one, following the naming
convention `D1` set for Region I (a specific, named proprietor rather than
an abstraction) — runs **the Shuttle**, a rooming house over a dead loom
works, three to a room and a shared stove, favoured by workers between
postings and anyone who doesn't want their business known past the
landing. He lost two fingers to a loom before he bought the building with
a soldier's discharge payment and has never once asked a lodger what
they're running from, which is exactly why people tell him anyway, in the
same unguarded way Nessa Beddoe's common room works in Region I (`D1`).
Logged as a session default rather than decided silently — see
`DECISIONS.md`.

**Sensory tells.** Washing strung across a courtyard too narrow for the
sun to reach it most of the day; a mill whistle that sets the whole
street's schedule whether or not anyone there works that mill; doors that
don't lock so much as latch, and a landlord's instinct for who's new.

**Secrets.** People go quiet here in the ordinary way cities swallow
people, not a Fourth Mercy-specific one — most of a missing lodger's story
is debt, drink, or a better offer elsewhere. Occasionally it isn't, and
nothing here says how a table would tell the difference before it's too
late; that ambiguity is the point, not a gap to close.

**Exits.** Short walk to Ascension House's ruin, the broadsheet's district,
and a Mercy House; longer to the civic quarter and the rail yards.

### The Rail Yards

**Purpose.** Vantry is the Commonwealth's actual rail junction, not just
its second city — every line from the north and east meets the trunk line
here before it continues on to Carrowgate and, eventually, the Larrow
Valley (`C1`). Practical geography more than a scene location, but worth
keying on its own terms.

**NPCs.** Yard crews, signalmen, and a stationmaster's office that keeps
better hours than the Indemnity Office's equivalent in Carrowgate; none
named, none needed unless a scene wants a specific face.

**Sensory tells.** **Coal Drop Yards** — named here, a working stretch of
siding and sheds where fuel and freight change hands between lines, close
enough to the lodging rows that off-shift crews drink in the same streets
they work over. Permanent smoke-haze thicker here than anywhere else in
the city; gaslamps lit at noon and never fully off.

**Secrets.** Freight manifests pass through here for everything that
moves by rail in the playable Commonwealth, which makes this the Part's
best, least-dramatic place to trace where something came from or where
it's going — a supply ledger for the Hydro Network Map clock, a shipment
the Valley Trust would rather not have logged, a crate nobody wants to
claim. None of this is committed to any specific thread here; it's the
location's standing function, for `R2b` to use as needed.

**Exits.** Everywhere. South and slightly west to the Larrow Valley by the
trunk line (four days overland via Hessel Bight, under a day by rail);
south to Carrowgate, a day by rail, three or four by road (`C1`); north and
east, unmapped — Region IV's and V's problem, not this file's.

## 4. Vantry's Reach — Travel Summary

Per `C1`'s Map and `S1`'s "Real Travel" rule (travel takes the time `C1`
says it takes, and something happens during it): within the city, every
location above is a walk or a short rail-feeder ride from every other one —
Vantry itself doesn't need an internal travel table the way the valley's
spread-out villages did. What matters is the city's connections outward:
a day by rail to Carrowgate, three or four by road; under a day by rail
back to Hessel Bight and the Larrow Valley's edge, four to five days by
road through Tannery Reach, Sennet, and the Selwick turning. `F1` (not yet
written) sets the exact per-leg crown costs and encounter frequency for
those outward legs; this file only fixes that nothing within Vantry itself
needs one.

## 5. DM Notes

**This Part's antagonist pressure is institutional first, personal
second — same discipline as Corlett.** The temptation in a city this size
is to invent a mastermind running the Church's local reach. Resist it
until `R2b` actually needs one; per `C2`'s own notes, nothing in this file
is run by anyone twirling a moustache, and the Mercy House above should
read as sincerely charitable for as long as the table is in it.

**Don't pre-empt R2b.** This file deliberately leaves Ascension House's
foundation unexplored, the archive clerk unnamed, the broadsheet's editor
unnamed, and the question of where the ascension work resumed (if it has)
completely open. Those are `R2b`'s lead web's job to use or leave, same
restraint `R1a` showed for the Sweetwater double meaning and Corlett's
name.

**Region I's clocks are not this Part's business.** The Capping Clock and
the Indemnity Mortality Curve stay exactly where the party left them;
nothing in Vantry advances or reverses either one. A table that wants to
check on Pellick or Harrow's Weir is reaching back into Region I, not
asking this file a question it answers.

**The lodging rows are not a mystery delivery device.** Per `A1` pillar 5
and the cost test (canon §8), most of what happens in a city this size to
people nobody's watching is ordinary and sad rather than sinister. Reserve
the Fourth Mercy reading for the specific thread `R2b` builds around it,
not as a default explanation for every missing lodger.

**How the novel's three appear.** Edie Marrow is this Part's only one of
the three leads with a fixed, on-page presence in Region II (Ferrick's
household). Treat her exactly as `R1b` treated Ivet Hale, but more
restrictively still — Ivet could speak within canon's limits; until `I1`
exists, Edie should not, per the stricter rule `R1b` itself set for Lloyd
and Maria. Lloyd and Maria are not in Vantry at all as of `A1`'s timeline
anchor (they're in or near Carrowgate); nothing in this Part should place
them here even off-page.
