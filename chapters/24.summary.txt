The narrator, Maria, watches Edie arrive with proof of Lloyd's reconnaissance — pencil marks on Edie's own security floor plan that Edie did not make — and for the first time hears her state flatly and completely that she murdered Colonel Absolom Reyne nine years ago after he sealed ninety-one of his own sappers into a collapsed tunnel, and that she has held a hard moral line ever since so that killing would still mean something. After a night without word, Edie returns at dawn having concluded overnight that clean hands have never once produced a better outcome, not at Vantry and not in Ferrick's own stalled sub-panel, and sets conditions rather than blessing: Lloyd goes in with the kitchen staff, Maria as Ferrick's invited guest, and the death staged to point at the Church. Senator Crane dies in a service passage with Ferrick looking on, courteous and confused to the end, asking after Maria's safety rather than his own as Lloyd's hand finds his chest and leaves no mark a coroner could name. The escape goes wrong when Lloyd's old injury gives out on a stair under Edie's own correctly responding guards, and Maria throws a crude, public burst of light into a corridor of clerks to buy their way out, unknowingly handing Edie's frame against the Church its clearest evidence yet.
