The narrator, Lloyd, arrives with Maria and Edie at Vantry, the Church's largest house outside the capital, and spends his first days walking the grounds of Ascension House, noting a grand front entrance nobody ever uses against a service door that takes constant cart traffic. Vail takes Maria alone to a clean, carefully tended room holding a woman two years catatonic and eight months pregnant, and asks Maria not to heal her but to imbue her own native Arcana into the unborn child; Maria refuses, and Vail accepts the refusal instantly and warmly, then takes her to lunch as though nothing had happened. Shaken less by any menace than by how kind and sincere the asking was, Maria asks Lloyd and Edie to help her look into the building, hoping to be told she's wrong about the woman who raised her, and over four days they assemble damning circumstantial evidence: brine delivered by the ton to a building billed as a chapel, no discharges against years of intake names, masonry under the chapel far older than the Church's ownership of the ground, and — seen once by Lloyd alone, unconfirmed and unconfirmable — a barred basement window lit from within by something that was not lamplight. Vail receives Maria twice more, answering every direct question honestly and unhelpfully, and by the fourth evening Maria has stopped ruling on anything Lloyd reports; she sets a date to get inside before she can talk herself out of it.
