The narrator, Maria, gives up the one intake register she has kept hidden under the floorboards, with Anse Duley's name in it, when Edie comes for it two days after Lloyd first stands unaided, to complete the evidence going to Ferrick's ethics sub-panel and a Vantry broadsheet that will print it whole. It lands and becomes, for eleven days, the only story in the Commonwealth, while the mill-district room fills with newsprint and Lloyd walks farther than he should to buy every paper on the stand. Then the Sanctum answers with a practiced performance Maria recognizes from twenty-one years of watching Vail work: Ascension House declared a rogue chapter, two under-priors surrendered to the courts, a bishop weeping in the Senate, an uncontestable restitution figure, and the photographed register itself dismissed as a likely forgery by men who have not read it, until the story is about the papers rather than the ward and nobody senior is ever charged. Vail, transferred to the Sanctum at Carrowgate and promoted to oversee intake there, writes to Maria once, warmly and without a trace of reproach, asking only how her hands are; Maria neither answers it nor burns it, and folds it away in the exact space under the floorboard the register left behind.
