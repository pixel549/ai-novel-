The narrator, Lloyd, Maria, and Edie fall in with two Mercies priests, Brother Aldous and Brother Tam, bound for the almshouse at Selwick, who share bread and cheese and warn them off a washed-out crossing there, courteous and useful the whole afternoon. Watching Aldous laugh, Lloyd sees his collar fail to move with a throat that should have worked beneath it, sets aside the bread he's been given, and talks himself out of testing it with his hidden arm rather than risk being wrong in company. Once the priests are gone he tells Maria plainly what he saw; she can't accept an accusation against men who gave them bread and safety for nothing, Edie declines to arbitrate at all, and Maria eventually rules the priests were only tired, kind, and real, which Lloyd accepts the way he accepts all her rulings. Walking on, Lloyd notices that Edie caught a discrepancy of her own — Aldous's stated two days to Selwick didn't match the one day on that morning's marker post — and, like him, chose to say nothing about it.
