The narrator, Lloyd, camps with Maria and Edie two days north of Ammet Ford and gives Maria the carefully true-but-incomplete version of why he's going home, while his unspoken history — leaving at twenty-two, nine years without visiting, and the five weeks he spent locked away in Carrowgate at nineteen for saying what his Insight showed him — stays his alone. Pressed gently by Maria, Edie volunteers, for the first time, that she has "a charge" in Carrowgate, a good man she'll return to once the valley business is done, though she won't give his name. Alone by the fire with the Larrow Indemnity handbill from Ammet Ford, Lloyd privately works out that the seven-year payout schedule was built around exactly how long it takes the valley's Sweetwater sickness to kill — meaning Crane's people ran the mortality numbers before they built the scheme, not after — a realization he keeps to himself along with a quiet, late-night request that Maria confirm the campfire is only a fire. The chapter closes without showing what happens at Callow Ford itself; a week later the three are already back on the north road, Lloyd no longer carrying the weight of the handbill, when two priests of the Mercies appear ahead of them.
