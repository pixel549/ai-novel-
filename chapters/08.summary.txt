The narrator, Lloyd, and the others pass through Ammet Ford, a Larrow Consolidated Works town visibly better off than anywhere they've walked through since the war — new brick, a rail spur, a school glazed with real glass, and a standpipe whose water everyone praises as the sweetest in the valley. A company notice announces the Larrow Indemnity, compensation payable in the seventh year, and three separate townsfolk mention it approvingly within the same hour, never asking why the number is seven. Edie totals the wages and amenities and pronounces the works good on balance despite her dislike of Crane's name, while Lloyd says nothing to anyone from the school wall onward, drinking the sweet water without meaning to and half-convincing himself a stillness he saw in a cup was only his own hand shaking. The chapter closes on the road out of town at dusk, the works' clean smoke behind them and Callow Ford somewhere beyond the darkening hills ahead, the water's sweetness still sitting on his tongue.
