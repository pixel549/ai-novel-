The narrator, Lloyd, waits outside Sennet Almshouse while Maria goes in alone to confess the theft of forty-one crowns to Ondine Vail, the sister who raised her, and spends the afternoon watching the building's ordinary comings and goings for anything to confirm or deny while privately weighing his own nine years of avoiding his mother's door against what Maria is doing by choice. When Maria comes out she tells him she's alright, and Lloyd recognizes it — correctly — as the first lie she's told him in the book, though he lets it stand rather than press her on it. That night, in a rented room with Edie, Maria recounts the whole of it: a joyously warm reunion, a confession Vail dismissed as trivial in about fifteen seconds with open delight rather than forgiveness, and an invitation to come help at Vantry because Maria has more natural gift than anyone Vail has ever trained, which Maria accepted on the spot. Only once the story is fully told does Maria admit what the lie had actually covered — not the Church, not the theft, but the fact that simply seeing Vail again, before a word of confession passed, was what undid her.
