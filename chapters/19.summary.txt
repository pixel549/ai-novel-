The narrator, Maria, watches the Ascension House story die out in the Vantry press over its eleventh and twelfth days, while Lloyd grows physically easy with the arm and with himself in a way she's never seen, reading what happened as the world's horror finally met and beaten on the right side. Edie arrives to tally the whole job in her oilcloth ledger, a closed, even account that leaves her uncharacteristically close to pleased, and Lloyd's flat "Good" says he's reached the same sum. Maria keeps her own unspoken reckoning to herself: the ward is burned but the programme isn't, the people who ordered it are at dinner in Carrowgate, her mother has been promoted for it, and eleven days bought against sixty years of documented failure does not come out even. Two days later Edie leaves for Carrowgate with the ledger left behind and only an address and the word "Work" by way of explanation, and Maria realizes, watching her go, that in all their months together Edie has never once asked either of them for anything.
