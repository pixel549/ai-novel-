The narrator, Lloyd, Maria, and Edie break into Ascension House's sub-basement at night through the service door they've watched for four days, Edie's forged requisition getting them past the porter, and move through an escalating sequence of brine tanks holding people who have never surfaced, shelves of non-viable homunculi, an empty breeding ward with a stud book of recorded pairings, and an extraction theatre walled in sixty years of case files documenting every failed attempt to separate a soul from a body. A novice's catechism card pinned by the theatre door spells out the doctrine without concealment: the Fourth Mercy isn't a corruption of the Church's public charity but its actual purpose, with bread and shelter identifying the poor nobody will miss and absolution gathering them in. In the last room they find something once human, bound inside a ring of brass fittings, kept and fed and no longer nameable, and withdraw without testing what it is or what it might do. Back in the anteroom Maria finds Anse Duley's name in the intake register, taken two years after Maria fled for stealing on her behalf, and says nothing of it to Edie, who never learns why the name matters; after a long silence Edie declares, flatly, that they are burning the building down.
