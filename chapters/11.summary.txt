The narrator, Lloyd, Maria, and Edie reach Tannery Reach, a canal town built on tanning and lime, where a crowd stands back from the water watching a young man's body, Fenwick, drift in the channel because his landing it would make either of two parishes pay for the inquest and burial. Lloyd finds he has no use here — no fight to have, nothing for his hidden arm to find — while Maria steadies the grieving mother and Edie corners the local relieving officer, defeats his boundary and settlement-law excuses with paced measurements and the threat of a public-health complaint to the Canal Company, and forces the parish to claim the body. Once the officer yields, Lloyd does the one thing he's actually built for and helps haul the body in himself, checking it once with his unseen arm and finding nothing but an ordinary drowning, while privately measuring the town's small cruelty against the larger, colder arithmetic he already knows was run on his own valley. They leave before the paperwork is finished, and on the road Edie explains her philosophy plainly — finding the piece nobody's claimed and claiming it before it rots — as they walk on toward Sennet.
