The ash under my boot turned back to salt somewhere a long way down a stair I had by then gone down more times than there were stairs in the world to go down, and the rail under my hand ran on without a seam in it, straight into a half-inch gap of ordinary grey daylight, showing through a roof that had stood unmended, as far as I knew that night, for every one of the nine years I hadn't been home to climb up and mend it.
