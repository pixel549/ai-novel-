The narrator, Maria, spends three days testing every way out of Lloyd's plan to kill Senator Crane, trying the law courts, a Carrowgate broadsheet, Ferrick's ethics sub-panel, and the idea of simply waiting out the Larrow Indemnity's seventh-year schedule, while Lloyd — who has quietly costed each option months in advance — answers every one of them flatly and without any triumph in doing so. On the third day she takes Edie's old ledger and tries pairing the already-proven Vantry evidence with Crane's dry figures for weight, but Lloyd shows her that a story already called a forgery poisons the next one and would hand a motive to anyone who ever traces the first leak back to them. Her last argument drops strategy for honesty: killing Crane will not give his mother clean water, undo the years her illness has already cost, or stop the valley from getting a next Crane, and its only certain cost is Lloyd himself. He agrees completely and nothing changes, so Maria chooses to stay anyway — not because he convinced her, but because alone he cannot tell fact from fever, she is the only reality he has left, and she recognizes, coldly, that this is exactly the reasoning Ondine Vail once used to keep her.
