# 1. Stillwell Hydro

I stepped through the stone arch of Stillwell Hydro. The limestone was salt-bitten, the lead paint peeling in long curls. A sharp wind cut off the river, carrying coal ash and iodine.

The clerk sat behind an oak rail worn smooth by coat sleeves. An ink-stained ledger lay open before him. I handed over a signed voucher on heavy paper. He checked the wax seal against an index card and debited Senator Ferrick’s household account without the senator’s knowledge. The steel nib scratched twice into the paper.

He pointed toward the lounge. A pine board listed the schedule in chalk: runs started in threes. Three lines of patients waited along the damp wainscoting under hissing gas brackets.

Lloyd stepped out of the first line. Broad-shouldered, thick through the frame, dark hair hacked short, his nose set wrong twice across the bridge. Maria followed him—small, wiry, her grey sleeves turned back over flat burn scars running down her forearms.

A staff member with a slate clipboard stepped between us. He checked three names, assigned Lloyd and Maria to my group, and stated it was the prearranged pairing for the run.

Lloyd turned to me. "Never been here before?"

"No," I said.

He nodded once, accepting it, and looked down at his knuckles.

Maria looked up from her slate. "First time?"

"No," I said.

She marked the sheet with charcoal, confirming the pairing.

Two attendants brought us into the preparation room. The space smelled of boiled zinc and damp masonry. In the centre, two men fitted copper pipes to the steam basin, tightening iron unions with wrenches. Lloyd walked to the frame, bent his legs, and hefted the heavy iron door onto its pintles. Maria moved along the wall, adjusting the oil lamps until light fell square across the stone floor. I went to the timber rack to check the harnesses—testing canvas webbing, iron buckles, and the stitching along every load-bearing seam.

An attendant held out a consent form on a lapboard. The paper smelled of vinegar. I signed the bottom line, noting only that I was entering. I left the lower space blank, stating no reason for wanting a chalice.

The iron door sealed into the jamb with a thud. Vapour billowed through the floor grate, heavy with crushed minerals. Steam swirled around us, and the first pulse of heat pressed against my skin.
